# Chapter 13 - Spiritual Authority

<!-- p1 -->
Jesus is the supreme and perfect source of spiritual authority. As the triumphant Christ, He has ultimate authority over all things in heaven and on earth.

<!-- p2 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p3 -->
> **<u>Matthew 28:18-20</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Jesus came and said to them: "All authority has been given to Me in heaven and on earth.
>
> Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit,
>
> teaching them to observe everything I have commanded you; and behold, I am with you always, to the end of the age." Amen.
>

<!-- p4 -->
</span>

<!-- p5 -->
In this chapter we will examine the authority and power of Christ, what authority and power He has delegated to us (His church), and how to operate in the authority of the name of Jesus. We will also study angelic and demonic beings and consider their authority, power, and nature.

<!-- p6 -->
Authority means the right to act. Power is the ability to carry out an action. Both are necessary in spiritual life. Authority is different from power. Let’s look at the following table that compares authority and power, and consider a few examples.

<!-- p7 -->
[See Table 7](../tables/table7.md)

<!-- p8 -->
Here are a few examples to help understand the difference between power and authority.

<!-- p9 -->
Example 1: At work, an employee may have the talents and skills to carry out the sale of company assets (power). This power is legitimate if they also have the appropriate authorization (authority) from the owners of the company. Without this authority, the sale of company assets is theft.

<!-- p10 -->
Example 2: A police officer undergoes special training in handling firearms and hand-to-hand combat in order to be able to detain and physically compel a criminal (power). Police officers are given official responsibility, backed by laws (authority), to enforce criminal law.

## The source of Jesus' authority

<!-- p74 -->
Now that we have considered what authority is, we need to know who is the source of this authority. Jesus’ authority is both divine and delegated. It proceeds from God the Father, is confirmed by His death and resurrection, and operates in full harmony with the Father’s will. Though it is absolute, His authority respects human free will, manifesting God’s love rather than coercive control.

<!-- p75 -->
Jesus’ authority is rooted in His divine person as the Son of God and is bestowed by the Father. The Father has entrusted to Christ all authority, including judgment and dominion over heaven and earth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 11:27</span>). Jesus’ authority reflects the unity and love within the Trinity, where the Son perfectly carries out the Father’s plan (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="35" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:35</span>).

<!-- p76 -->
Although Jesus possessed authority inherent to Him as the Son of God, His victory over sin, death, and hell was fully affirmed through His death and resurrection. Christ’s obedience in His sacrifice led to His exaltation (seated at the right hand of the Father), establishing His authority over all creation. Having conquered death, Christ received absolute dominion over life and eternity.

## Laying on of Hands

<!-- p116 -->
Laying on of hands is a significant biblical practice used in both the Old and New Testaments to symbolize blessing, commissioning, healing, and the impartation of spiritual authority or gifts. It is more than just a physical action—it represents a spiritual connection and impartation under God's authority.

<!-- p117 -->
In the Old Testament, the laying on of hands was often associated with blessing and consecration. For example, Jacob blessed his grandsons, Ephraim and Manasseh, by laying his hands on them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="48" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 48:14</span>). Likewise, Moses laid hands on Joshua, commissioning him to be his successor, imparting to him leadership authority and wisdom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="NUM" data-chapter="27" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Numbers 27:18-23</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="DEU" data-chapter="34" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Deuteronomy 34:9</span>). This act publicly signified God's choice and empowerment for the task ahead.

<!-- p118 -->
In the New Testament, the laying on of hands is closely associated with the ministry of Jesus and the early church. Jesus regularly laid hands on the sick to heal them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 6:5</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 4:40</span>), demonstrating both compassion and authority over disease. The apostles continued this practice, using it to impart the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:17</span>), spiritual gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 4:14</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 1:6</span>) and blessings to believers. It also played a role in commissioning leaders and missionaries to ministry (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 13:3</span>).

<!-- p119 -->
The laying on of hands symbolizes an act of faith and obedience to God's Word. It is not a mechanical ritual, but a spiritual practice grounded in dependence on the Holy Spirit. For example, in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:6</span> the apostles laid hands on those chosen to serve as deacons, committing them to God's service and publicly recognizing their calling. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 19:6</span> Paul laid hands on believers, and they received the Holy Spirit, which shows its role in spiritual empowerment.

<!-- p120 -->
However, the Bible also warns regarding this practice:

<!-- p121 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p122 -->
> **<u>1 Timothy 5:22</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Do not lay hands on anyone hastily, nor share in other people's sins; keep yourself pure.
>

<!-- p123 -->
</span>

<!-- p124 -->
This underscores the importance of discernment and spiritual maturity when appointing people to leadership roles. As we studied in the chapter on the church, leaders must be tested and proven to avoid promoting unqualified or immature believers who could harm the Body of Christ.

<!-- p125 -->
A few more recommendations regarding the laying on of hands:

<!-- p126 -->
1.  Contextually appropriate - the person on whom hands are laid should be a willing participant.

<!-- p127 -->
2.  Physically appropriate - the laying on of hands does not involve sexual or sensual touching. In practice, touching a shoulder, arm, or forehead with a hand is generally considered acceptable (with permission) in the Western world.

<!-- p128 -->
3.  Culturally appropriate - in some cultures, men and women should not touch each other unless they are related by blood or marriage.

<!-- p129 -->
The laying on of hands remains a powerful expression of faith and obedience in the Church today. It serves as a means of blessing, healing, ordination, and empowerment, always pointing to the authority of Christ and the work of the Holy Spirit. Whether praying for the sick, sending out missionaries, or ordaining leaders, it reminds us that believers act not by their own power, but under the authority of Jesus Christ and in alignment with His purposes.

<!-- p130 -->
Now that we have studied the authority of Jesus and the authority of the believer, we will discuss other beings of the spiritual realm, angels and demons.

<!-- p131 -->
## Angels and Demons

<!-- p132 -->
### Angels

<!-- p133 -->
Angels are a significant part of God's creation, serving as His messengers and agents to accomplish His divine purposes. The Bible reveals a spiritual realm populated by these beings who are powerful, intelligent, and obedient to God's commands. Though angels are often invisible, their presence and actions influence human history and spiritual warfare. Understanding their roles, classifications, and activities gives us deeper insight into God's plan and the unseen realities that surround us.

<!-- p134 -->
The very term "angel" means "messenger," emphasizing their primary function. Angels act in obedience to God's will, whether delivering messages, providing protection, executing judgment, or engaging in spiritual battles. They are not objects of worship, but serve as examples of obedience and reverence, always pointing to the glory of God. This chapter explores the nature, classifications, and roles of angels, shedding light on their significance in Scripture and in our lives today.

<!-- p135 -->
In Scripture, angels are presented as **messengers**, **servants**, and **warriors**. Angels often act as messengers, bringing important announcements, warnings, and encouragement. They reveal God's plans and give guidance in times of need (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26-38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 1:26-38</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 2:13</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 1:1</span>). Angels are actively involved in protecting, strengthening, and delivering believers. They minister to God's people in times of distress and provide supernatural help (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="91" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 91:11</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 12:7-11</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 4:11</span>). Angels take part in spiritual warfare, fighting demonic forces to advance God's Kingdom and protect believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="DAN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Daniel 10:13</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:7-9</span>).

<!-- p136 -->
#### The Nature of Angels

<!-- p137 -->
Angels are created beings, brought into existence by God before the foundation of the world. They are distinct from humans, possessing unique characteristics that equip them for their heavenly roles. The Bible teaches that angels are spiritual beings, not bound by physical limitations, yet when interacting with people, they can take on visible forms.

<!-- p138 -->
Angels are said to be immortal, meaning they do not die or grow old. Their number is countless, and vast hosts fill the heavenly realms. Although angels are often referred to in the masculine, Scripture makes it clear that angels are sexless and do not marry or reproduce. These qualities underscore their design as servants of God, wholly devoted to His will and commands. Angels possess remarkable traits that reflect their divine purpose. They are obedient, reverent, wise, and powerful. These qualities equip them to carry out God's will effectively and faithfully.

[See Table 8](../tables/table8.md)

<!-- p140 -->
Although the Bible highlights several roles and titles of angels, it does not provide an exhaustive system of classification. However, some angels are identified by specific roles or functions:

<!-- p141 -->
- **The Angel of the LORD** - Often regarded as a pre-incarnate appearance of Christ, appearing with divine authority. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="23" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 23:20-23</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JDG" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Judges 6:11-24</span>*)

<!-- p142 -->
- **Archangels** - High-ranking angels, such as Michael, who leads the armies, and Gabriel, who delivers divine messages. (*Jude 9; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:7</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 1:19-26</span>*)

<!-- p143 -->
- **Cherubim** - Guardians of God’s glory and presence, for example in Eden and at the Ark of the Covenant. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 3:24</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 25:22</span>*)

<!-- p144 -->
- **Seraphim** - Worshiping God, continually proclaiming His holiness. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 6:1-6</span>*)

<!-- p145 -->
- **Elect angels** - Faithful angels who opposed Satan’s rebellion and remain devoted to God. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:21</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 25:41</span>*)

<!-- p146 -->
These classifications underscore the diversity and order within the angelic realm, however their primary focus is always the fulfillment of God’s purposes.

<!-- p147 -->
#### Interaction of People with Angels

<!-- p148 -->
People often encounter angels in Scripture, but we are nowhere instructed to command them or to pray to them. Instead, angels act at God’s direction. They serve believers as **God’s servants** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 1:14</span>). Their interactions include protection, guidance, and deliverance, always pointing us to God’s power and sovereignty.

<!-- p149 -->
- **Warnings against worshiping angels**: Scripture forbids worshiping angels or seeking encounters with them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:18</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 22:8-9</span>).

<!-- p150 -->
- **Prayer to God, not to angels**: Believers should pray to God, who sends angels according to His will (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="91" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalms 91:11</span>).

<!-- p151 -->
- **Encouragement and trust**: Angels remind us of God’s care, but our faith must rest entirely in God, not in angels.

<!-- p152 -->
Angels are awe-inspiring beings created to serve God and to minister to His people. Though their work is often unseen, Scripture gives glimpses of their activity, revealing their faithfulness, power, and reverence. Angels remind us of God’s provision and protection, but always point to His glory. As believers, we are called to trust God’s care, knowing that angels act as His servants to accomplish His divine purposes.

<!-- p153 -->
### Demons

<!-- p154 -->
Just as the study of angels is necessary for understanding the spiritual realm, so too is the study of demons. Scripture clearly describes their activity, influence, and defeat through Jesus Christ. Recognizing their role in spiritual warfare helps believers stand firm in faith without fear.

<!-- p155 -->
#### The Origin and Nature of Demons

<!-- p156 -->
Demons are fallen angels who followed Satan’s rebellion against God. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:7-9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 25:41</span>).* Satan, formerly known as Lucifer (“light-bearer”), an exalted angel, sought to exalt himself above God and was cast down from heaven along with other rebellious angels. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 14:12-15</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EZK" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ezekiel 28:12-17</span>).*

<!-- p157 -->
Demons are spirits without physical bodies, but they can oppress and torment people and animals. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 5:11-13</span>).* They seek to deceive, tempt, oppress, and destroy. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 11:14</span>). In contrast to God,* they have limits, and they are not omnipresent (present everywhere), omniscient (all-knowing), omnipotent (possessing all power)*.* Their ultimate defeat is certain, and they will suffer eternal punishment. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 20:10</span>)*

<!-- p158 -->
Scripture describes the deeds and activity of the devil as follows:

<!-- p159 -->
- Opposes the work of God *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Thessalonians 2:18</span>)*

<!-- p160 -->
- Deceives the world *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 4:4</span>)*

<!-- p161 -->
- Tempts and accuses believers *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 22:31</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:10</span>)*

<!-- p162 -->
- Oppresses and harms *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JOB" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Job 1:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 13:16</span>)*

<!-- p163 -->
The devil has many names and descriptions, each of which describes a different aspect of his work.

<!-- p164 -->
[See Table 9](../tables/table9.md)

<!-- p165 -->
The Gospels and epistles further clarify the work of the enemy. Evil spirits, being disembodied, mostly carry out their work through willing and open people. God works through people—and so does the devil. This oppression is often marked by torment, self-harm, and destructive behavior *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 8:27-29</span>).* Their attacks produce fear, temptation, and discouragement *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 12:7</span>).* The most common source of their power (not authority) in a person’s life is the influence of lies and emotional strongholds *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 10:3-5</span>).* 

<!-- p166 -->
It is important to note that not every lie a person believes, ailment in their body, or stronghold in their life is a direct result of demonic influence, but such wounds are places where the enemy can take advantage of us and keep us in a state of disappointment and oppression.

<!-- p167 -->
#### The Authority of Jesus over the Works of the Enemy

<!-- p168 -->
Do not be afraid: Jesus has full and absolute authority over the works of the enemy. Jesus demonstrated authority over demons during His ministry *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 1:34</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 5:1-20</span>).* The works of the enemy and Satan himself are defeated through the death and resurrection of Jesus *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:15</span>).* 

<!-- p169 -->
As believers, we are given authority delegated by Jesus to cast out demons in His name:

<!-- p170 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p171 -->
> **<u>Mark 16:17</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And these signs will accompany those who believe: in My name they will cast out demons; they will speak with new tongues;
>

<!-- p172 -->
</span>

<!-- p173 -->
Moreover, as heirs of salvation, we have authority to submit to God, resist the devil, and he will flee from us! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 4:7</span>).

<!-- p174 -->
To deal with an evil or unclean spirit, the pattern expressed in Scripture is to take authority over it in the name of Jesus and command it to leave. The Holy Spirit promises to give us wisdom and discernment if we ask! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:5</span>). We do not need to make a show or drama of it, like in the movies, or even talk with them—this is a trick of the devil to distract us. Satan’s power is established and maintained only by the lies he can get us to believe.

<!-- p175 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="18" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p176 -->
> **<u>Matthew 18:18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> "Truly I say to you, whatever you bind on earth will be bound in heaven, and whatever you loose on earth will be loosed in heaven.
>

<!-- p177 -->
</span>

<!-- p178 -->
We speak to them in the name of Jesus and command them to leave.

<!-- p179 -->
There are two principal cautions in Scripture when encountering the demonic:

<!-- p180 -->
1.  Only those who have a relationship with Jesus have authority over the demonic. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 19:11-20</span> speaks of the sons of a Jewish chief priest named Sceva. They tried to intervene in the life of a man oppressed and tormented by the devil, and the evil spirit refused to leave because they did not have a relationship with Jesus. The man who was oppressed leaped on them, stripped them naked, and beat them. Just as you cannot impersonate a police officer, do not impersonate someone who has a relationship with Jesus; instead, have a relationship with Jesus!

<!-- p181 -->
2.  When an evil spirit leaves, it leaves for a time and will try to return. If it finds the place where it dwelt empty, it will return in an even worse state. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="43-45" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 12:43-45</span>. When we minister to a person oppressed by the devil, that person needs to fill the void that the evil spirit occupied in their life. This means, at a minimum, establishing a personal relationship with Jesus and strengthening it through obedience to the Holy Spirit. Often an evil or unclean spirit gains authority to operate in someone’s life through the choices a person has made based on their beliefs. Everyone who is delivered from the devil needs to replace that old belief system and be transformed by the renewing of the mind (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:1-2</span>).

<!-- p182 -->
We serve an amazing and almighty God, and we are ‘more than conquerors’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="37" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:37</span>) over a defeated enemy. We have no need to fear our defeated enemy, for Jesus has given us His authority to crush his head! The devil’s time is short, and he will ultimately be condemned together with every principality and authority on earth that rejects the lordship of Christ.
