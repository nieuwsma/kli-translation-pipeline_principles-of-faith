# Chapter 6 - Faith in God

<!-- p1 -->
Faith — a cornerstone concept in Christianity. The word «faith» is used so often in Christianity that at times the meaning of this word is displaced by subtle misconceptions and misunderstandings. But it is not something you can hold in your hands—it's something that is formed in the heart as you begin to trust God. Conviction — that is faith.

<!-- p2 -->
The author of the Epistle to the Hebrews instructs us that we are to have ‘faith in God’.

### Definition of Faith

<!-- p10 -->
Faith — this is an often misunderstood and misrepresented aspect of Christianity. As they say, «a ditch on both sides of the road»! Since the word «faith» is so often used without proper explanation in modern Christianity, it can become overgrown with erroneous definitions reinforced by misunderstanding. Faith is often portrayed either as a generalized assertion of abstract belief or as an almost mystical force or «magic», something you have in a certain, measured, and limited amount.

<!-- p11 -->
#### What Faith Is Not

<!-- p12 -->
Faith presented <u>abstractly</u> lacks concrete conviction or confidence. This often becomes apparent with a bit of questioning, since such «faith» cannot be made specific. If you ask: ‘what do you believe’ or ‘why do you believe’, a person with this understanding of faith often cannot explain anything beyond ‘I believe’. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 3:15</span> says that we must be ready to give an answer for the hope that is in us.

<!-- p13 -->
Sometimes people think of faith as a magical power — but Scripture does not teach this. Faith — real relationship built on trust. In Scripture, faith is much more dynamic and pertains to the relational sphere — it is rooted in conviction, trust, and confidence in the character of God and His promises. It is not an impersonal force, but an active response to God that shapes thoughts, feelings, and actions.

<!-- p14 -->
Why is faith connected with conviction?

<!-- p15 -->
Our beliefs shape how we see the world around us and interact with it. Beliefs shape perception and generate our actions. That is how our thinking works — we live out what we consider possible.

<!-- p16 -->
We act based on what we perceive, understand, and consider possible. When we walk down a hallway, our brain recognizes the boundaries of the floor and walls and automatically cues our feet where it is safe to step and what should be avoided. This truth goes beyond merely physically navigating the world around us.

<!-- p17 -->
If you believe that it is impossible for you to become financially independent or to live a healthy and active lifestyle, then your actions will follow that belief, and true to your conviction, you will not do and will most likely not even attempt to do what you consider impossible.

<!-- p18 -->
If you think that God has an unloving and punitive character, such a view of Him will keep you from walking with Him as His child.

<!-- p19 -->
#### Faith — Conviction

<!-- p20 -->
So, what is faith? Conviction! The Greek word ***pistis*** means conviction, or the result of someone having been persuaded. We must have conviction toward God. In English we might say faith (conviction) ‘**in** God’. If you are married, then hopefully you have faith ‘**in’** your spouse. You believe that he will do what he said, and you believe that he is sincere and genuine. This trust should go beyond what is said and extend to him as a whole.

<!-- p21 -->
Forming faith, conviction, and trust in relationships takes time and experience. Hopefully, in life you have learned to build trusting relationships with another person. The principles you have learned that allow you to increase trust in someone you know will also allow you to increase your faith in God. Faith in relationships grows over time, through experience, consistency, and through small and large actions that strengthen your confidence in the character and nature of the other person.

<!-- p22 -->
«Faith toward God» is a relationship. Relationships are built by two parties, not one. The good news about our faith in God is that God takes care of His side of the relationship (made possible by the redemptive death of Jesus and the filling of His Spirit at rebirth), and He also helps us on our side too (through the working of the Comforter - the Holy Spirit). God does almost all the work; our part (as we will discuss later in this chapter) is to walk *With Him* in obedience.

<!-- p23 -->
### Jesus brings our faith to maturity

<!-- p24 -->
Jesus is the author and finisher of our faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 12:2</span>). Our faith is meant to continually grow and expand. We do not arrive at a single point where we have enough «faith», but rather, our faith increases. Think about the deep and personal relationships you have today. As with any relationship, we do not come to a specific milestone after which we «know» the person. We continue to get to know him and increase our knowledge of him, and this refines, shapes, and nurtures our relationship.

<!-- p25 -->
How do we grow in faith in God? By continuing to experience Him in the multitude of His revelations, manifestations, and attributes. Think again about the deep personal relationships in your life. You deepen a relationship by interacting with a person in different ways, in different formats, in different environments, in different settings. You talk with him, you text, perhaps you write letters. You see him one-on-one and in groups. You talk about what you value, and perhaps challenge one another. These numerous ways go on endlessly. Through all these expressions you get to know the person more deeply.

<!-- p26 -->
We must all know God, through Jesus, as our Savior. Remember the names of God (*see the appendix),* knowing God as Savior does not yet mean that we have already experienced Him as Deliverer, Healer, Counselor, Lord.

<!-- p27 -->
If you are in a position where you have been persuaded (faith) to know God as your Savior, building this relationship will give you confidence in His character to know Him in other capacities as well.

<!-- p28 -->
I am blessed to have several very deep relationships in my life. One such relationship is with my wife. In our relationship, thanks to the level of trust (faith) we have in each other, my wife can tell me about things she is going to do, although I have not yet seen them accomplished, and I have the confidence to believe her because of her proven character.

<!-- p29 -->
This example shows how we can experience our deep relationship with God—with trust and confidence! We may not yet have experienced Him fully (in fact, we will never fully experience Him in this life), but as we mature in our relationship, we can trust Him beyond what I personally know and what I have already seen happen.

<!-- p30 -->
Jesus increases our faith through experience with Him. A significant way we can and should experience Him is through His word.

<!-- p32 -->
Consider this passage from Romans:

<!-- p35 -->

<!-- p36 -->
When we replace the word *faith* with the word *conviction*, it becomes:

<!-- p37 -->
*‘Then conviction comes by hearing, and hearing — from the Word of God’*. This truth is naturally self-evident. One of the primary ways someone comes to conviction is to be persuaded, and we are persuaded when someone brings an ‘argument’-or gives a reasoned and clearly formulated explanation.

<!-- p38 -->
In the Bible there are two Greek words for «Word» — **logos** and **rhema**. Understanding the difference helps us see how faith grows.

<!-- p39 -->
**Logos**: generally refers to the **concept, idea, mind, or broader expression of God’s word**. It encompasses the universal, foundational truth or message and is often used to describe God’s eternal wisdom and Christ Himself as the living Word.

<!-- p40 -->
**Rhema (G4487)**: emphasizes the **specific, spoken word or utterance**. It usually refers to a specific word or message given at a particular moment, especially to a spoken or revealed word that directly relates to a person’s situation.

<!-- p41 -->
*Logos* is like the entire message of a book, whereas *rhema* is like a specific passage or sentence that, at that moment, addresses someone directly. While the *Logos* is the all-encompassing truth of God, *rhema* is when a part of that truth is spoken personally and applicably to a person. Together they illustrate the fullness of God’s communication: the *Logos* as the broad, foundational truths of God’s character and will, and *rhema* as the specific, applied word that brings that truth to life in a person’s life and situation.

<!-- p42 -->
### An example of a rhema word in action

<!-- p43 -->
God promises revelation and wisdom to all who ask. When He speaks to us, that utterance is a *rhema* word. Rhema words are from God and serve to confirm, lead, and guide us. They do not replace the *Logos* word and do not carry the authority of Scripture. *Rhema* words should be evaluated by the same standards as prophecy or the interpretation of tongues (see the chapter on the Holy Spirit for a detailed study of this topic).

<!-- p44 -->
At different points in my career I prayed for Divine guidance. A few years ago, I was considering starting to look for a new opportunity, and during prayer I heard God say to me: ‘*Just wait for Me; I have an opportunity for you.*’ I began to look at a new job because I was dissatisfied with my current role and felt discouraged. God’s directional word ‘wait for Me’ helped me find peace and confidence in an already anxious season.

<!-- p45 -->
Three or four months passed; I continued to work hard and diligently, maintaining a positive attitude. God reminded me, ‘wait for Me,’ then His promise was fulfilled. I was presented with a promotion opportunity, and I received a generous salary increase.

<!-- p46 -->
I could have continued the job search; I could have left. I could have started over somewhere else. Without a doubt, someone else would have gotten that promotion! But since I sought counsel from the Lord and was convinced by His **rhema word**, I obeyed and received benefit. God’s words are **living and personal**, and He promises to give us manifold and generous wisdom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:5</span>).

<!-- p47 -->
I have had dozens, if not hundreds, of instances when I prayed and asked God what to do, and He gave me a rhema word. Not every one led to such an impressive result, but all of them brought a sense of peace and confidence that I was being obedient to God and that He would work everything out. These were the words I needed at that moment! Often, when God gives a rhema word, it is from the Logos. He reminds me of a passage of Scripture: “Do not fear, for I am with you!” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="41" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 41:10</span>), “By His stripes we are healed” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="53" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 53:5</span>), “Do not grow weary in doing good!” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:9</span>), and so the many examples continue!

<!-- p48 -->
Receiving a rhema word not only strengthens our faith but also deepens our relationship with God, as we trust Him more fully in everyday decisions. We will continue our study of how to learn to hear God in the chapter on the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p49 -->
Our faith is also strengthened as we see God at work, but sometimes what we see in the natural world challenges what we hear from God. Reconciling the discrepancy between what we see and what we hear is an important ministry of the Holy Spirit, who promises to guide us into all truth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span>).

### Faith is more than what we can see

<!-- p51 -->
After His resurrection, Jesus appeared multiple times to many of His disciples. Thomas (one of the Twelve) was not with the other disciples, so he missed the appearance of the risen Savior. Thomas believed in Jesus and trusted his fellow disciples, but he did not believe that Jesus had risen in bodily form; he insisted he would not truly believe it until he experienced it personally.

<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="24-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

When Jesus appeared to His disciples and calmed them (I imagine how unsettling it is when someone appears among you, having passed through locked doors), He addressed Thomas. He did not criticize or accuse Thomas and did not class him with an evil and adulterous generation seeking a sign (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 16:4</span>), but revealed Himself to Thomas.

<!-- p60 -->
Jesus goes on to say: ‘*blessed are those who have not seen and yet have believe*d’. This is not Jesus endorsing ‘blind or ignorant faith’ and not a denial of the use of empirical evidence. Rather, He says that we are more blessed when we can believe without the necessity of empirical proof. Why? If our faith, our trust, were limited exclusively to what we had seen empirically demonstrated, we could never have confidence in a future work. We would have greater confidence in what has already been accomplished, but we would not walk in God’s peace regarding our tomorrow and our future.

<!-- p61 -->
To use a relational example, how difficult would my relationship with my wife be if my posture were constantly: ‘<u>I’ll believe it when I see it!</u>’? Instead, a trust-based relationship says: ‘I know that you are a trustworthy person.’

3. Our act of faith is expressed in obedience.

<!-- p91 -->
The word for "obeyed" is **hupakouō** (**hoop-ak-oo'-o** - G5219): **hupakouō** means *to hear under* (as *a subordinate*), that is, *to listen attentively*; figuratively—*to heed* or *to conform* to a command or authority: - to heed, be obedient, obey.

<!-- p92 -->
In a workplace setting, when a boss or supervisor gives instructions, they are not "talking just to talk," but giving directives they expect their employees to follow.

<!-- p93 -->
When we truly listen to God, it should lead us to action—to trust Him and obey. As Scripture says:

<!-- p94 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

The counselor will teach him the principles of financial freedom and help him create a budget. That counseling will produce (confidence / faith) in what they need to do to be free from debt. But confidence is only the first step; the person must obey the principles he has learned through disciplined practice in order to obtain a good outcome and the blessing of freedom from debt.

As we build our faith in God, it will naturally produce obedience in us. This obedience is for our good and brings a good harvest. Returning to my personal example from this chapter, I would not have experienced peace during that period and the blessing of a promotion if I had not obeyed God's word to me—to wait on Him!

### Practical steps to grow your faith

<!-- p153 -->
Walking with God is a relationship, but if you want a good relationship, there are skills you need to learn. In earthly relationships, the skills that lead to strong and lasting relationships are based on:

<!-- p154 -->
- Open and free dialogue and communication

<!-- p155 -->
- Healthy conflict resolution

<!-- p156 -->
- Commitment

<!-- p157 -->
- Consistency

<!-- p158 -->
If you want strong, vibrant relationships, you must show up regularly, reveal (communicate) yourself (our thoughts and emotions), receive the other person (listen), and walk together with them, if you want solid, vibrant relationships! All of these skills apply to our relationship with God.

<!-- p159 -->
Beyond the basic relationship skills, there are specific practices and disciplines that will strengthen your relationship with God:

<!-- p160 -->
- **Build and maintain a prayer life; listen to and communicate with God.** Regular, sincere prayer is essential for a close relationship with God. Through prayer you can share your concerns, joys, and gratitude, asking for His guidance and strength. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 4:6-7</span>*) Sensitivity to the voice of the Holy Spirit helps you discern God’s will, find wisdom in decisions, and grow in spiritual maturity. Make time for quiet reflection to hear His direction. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:14</span>*)

<!-- p161 -->
- **Spend time in worship.** Worship is a powerful way to draw near to God, expressing love and reverence. Through praise, singing, or quiet reflection, worship deepens your awareness of His presence. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="100" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 100:2-4</span>*)

<!-- p162 -->
- **Read, study, and meditate on Scripture.** God’s Word reveals His character, promises, and instructions for life. Devoting time to studying Scripture helps you grow in faith, understanding, and spiritual resilience. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>*)

<!-- p163 -->
- **Obey His commandments.** Obedience to God’s instruction demonstrates your love, respect, and trust in Him. Walking in obedience aligns your life with His purpose and brings blessings. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:15</span>*)

<!-- p164 -->
- **Actively attend a local church and serve.** Participation in a church community provides spiritual support, encouragement, and opportunities to serve others. Involvement helps you find a sense of belonging and strengthens your commitment to God’s mission. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:24-25</span>*)

<!-- p165 -->
- **Build relationships with other believers.** Fellowship with other Christians provides accountability, encouragement, and shared wisdom. These relationships enrich your faith journey and help you grow through mutual support. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="27" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 27:17</span>*)

<!-- p166 -->
Our ‘faith in God’ is not a one-time event for salvation, but an ongoing practice and relationship that we must cultivate throughout our lives. The benefits of trusting and fruitful relationships are incredibly numerous, but we must do the good work of developing that trust and growth in order to see them flourish.
