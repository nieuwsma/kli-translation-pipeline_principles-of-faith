# Chapter 9 - The Church &amp; our role in it

<!-- p1 -->
The Church is how God reveals Himself on the earth—through His people doing His work. We are called to be His body; we are His hands and feet to carry out His work and purpose. Without our efforts, the harvest will remain in the field and go unharvested.

<!-- p2 -->
## What is the Church?

<!-- p3 -->
The Church, ***Ekklesia** (eh-klee-SEE-ah - **G1577**),* comes from two Greek words:

<!-- p4 -->
- **"Ek"**: "Out of."

<!-- p5 -->
- **"Kaleo"**: "To call."

<!-- p6 -->
Together, **Ekklesia** literally means "the called-out" or "a called-out assembly." In ancient Greek city-states, this referred to a gathering of citizens convened for a specific purpose, such as legislative or judicial sessions. It was a secular term denoting an assembly of people.

<!-- p7 -->
In the New Testament, this word is primarily used to refer to the community of believers in Jesus Christ. It can mean:

<!-- p8 -->
- **Local Church**: A specific gathering of believers in a given place (e.g., the church in Corinth, Philippi, etc.).

<!-- p9 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p10 -->
> **<u>1 Corinthians 1:2</u>** (NKJV)
>
> To the church of God which is at Corinth, to those who are sanctified in Christ Jesus, called to be saints, with all who in every place call on the name of Jesus Christ our Lord, both theirs and ours:
>

<!-- p11 -->
</span>

<!-- p12 -->
- **Universal Church**: The collective body of all believers across all times and places.

<!-- p13 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p14 -->
> **<u>Matthew 16:18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And I also say to you that you are Peter, and on this rock I will build My Church, and the gates of Hades shall not prevail against it.
>

<!-- p15 -->
</span>

<!-- p16 -->
The Church is people, not a building or the place where they meet. **The called**: reflects the idea that Christians are called out of the world and set apart for God’s purposes (e.g., <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:9</span>) for a specific mission and purpose (the Great Commission). Technically, the church is any two or more believers gathering together (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="18" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 18:20</span>). Usually, a church is organized into regular meetings, possibly with a set place and time, but where two or more gather in the name of Jesus—there is the church!

<!-- p17 -->
The Church was established by Jesus and multiplied in the 1st century A.D. through the labor of the disciples who followed Him. The Church we have and experience today in all its local communities throughout the earth is the direct result of the initial work of Christ and His apostles. As believers, we should be committed to a local church for fellowship, provision, equipping for ministry, and the doing of good works!

<!-- p18 -->
To help us understand what the Church is, Scripture gives us powerful images showing what it is called to be.

<!-- p19 -->
## Understanding the Church

<!-- p20 -->
There are several primary metaphorical images used to describe the characteristics of the church:

<!-- p21 -->
1.  Body

<!-- p22 -->
2.  Bride

<!-- p23 -->
3.  Temple

<!-- p24 -->
4.  Family

<!-- p25 -->
5.  Flock

<!-- p26 -->
6.  Vine and branches

<!-- p27 -->
Each analogy emphasizes a unique aspect of the church, but together they reveal its identity as a unified and diverse community under the leadership of Christ.

<!-- p28 -->
### Body

<!-- p29 -->
The church is often described as the **Body of Christ**, emphasizing both its unity and diversity. With Christ as the Head, the church functions as a living organism where every believer plays a necessary and potentially varied role. Each member is uniquely gifted and contributes to the health and mission of the body according to their function. This image underscores the interdependence of believers, namely that the body must be connected in order to function, and that our function is cooperative rather than competitive. When each part acts together, the body grows and thrives in unity and love (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:12-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:15-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 1:18</span>).

<!-- p30 -->
### Bride

<!-- p31 -->
The church is described as the **Bride of Christ**, reflecting an intimate and loving relationship with Jesus. Just as a bride is cherished and prepared for her husband, the church is sanctified and cleansed by Christ’s sacrificial love. This metaphor conveys the devotion and faithfulness expected of the church, as well as Christ’s deep love for His people. This image also points to the ultimate union between Christ and the church, celebrated at the marriage supper of the Lamb, where the church appears in glory without spot or blemish (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="25-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:25-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 19:7-9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="21" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 21:2</span>).

<!-- p32 -->
### Temple

<!-- p33 -->
The church is described as the **Temple of God**, the dwelling place of His Spirit. Believers are described as living stones being built together on the foundation of the apostles and prophets, and Christ Himself—the cornerstone. This holy structure signifies God’s presence among His people and their role as a community of worship and prayer. The image of the temple emphasizes the importance of holiness and the church’s collective calling in glorifying God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 3:16-17</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:19-22</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:4-5</span>).

<!-- p34 -->
### Family

<!-- p35 -->
The church is described as the **Family of God**, emphasizing relationships of unity and care among its members. Through the Spirit of adoption, believers become God’s children and members of His household. This family image highlights the love, belonging, and shared inheritance that believers have in Christ. It also reflects the call to treat one another with compassion and humility as brothers and sisters in God’s family (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:19</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:15-17</span>). Additionally, through the image of the family it is shown how church members are to become mature, to be taught and nourished by spiritual mothers and fathers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:1-2</span>), so that we may move from infants drinking the pure milk of the Word to adults able to eat the solid food of the Word (Hebrews 5).

<!-- p36 -->
### Flock

<!-- p37 -->
The church is described as a **Flock**, sheep under the care of Jesus, the Good Shepherd. This image conveys the church’s dependence on Christ for guidance, nourishment, and protection. It also underscores the responsibility of church leaders to faithfully shepherd God’s flock as under-shepherds, imitating Christ’s example in love and care. The image of the flock speaks of trust, obedience, and close relationship between the Great Shepherd and His sheep (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:14-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:2</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>).

<!-- p38 -->
### Vine and branches

<!-- p39 -->
The church is described as the **Vine and branches**, illustrating the vital connection between believers and Christ. Jesus is the true vine, and believers are the branches who must abide in Him in order to bear fruit. This metaphor emphasizes dependence on Christ for spiritual vitality, growth, and fruitfulness. Without Him believers can do nothing, but by abiding in Him they glorify God and fulfill their purpose. Jesus will prune so that He may bring more fruit in our lives (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:1-5</span>).

<!-- p40 -->
## Roles of the Church

<!-- p41 -->
The church’s mission, like the role of the Holy Spirit, can be understood through three dimensions: upward, inward, and outward; each reflects a unique aspect of its calling in God’s plan. These dimensions guide the church in fulfilling its role as a worshiping, edifying, and missional community.

<!-- p42 -->
### Upward: Worship and the Glorification of God

<!-- p43 -->
The church’s first purpose is to worship and glorify God. Corporate worship, when believers gather together to exalt God, is a visible reflection of His glory and a response to His greatness. As the psalmist says: “*Worship the Lord in the beauty of holiness; tremble before Him, all the earth*” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="96" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 96:9</span>). Beyond corporate worship, the church is called to live in obedience and self-sacrifice, offering themselves as “living sacrifices” as an act of spiritual worship (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:1-2</span>). Such an upward focus keeps the church centered on God’s majesty and aligns its priorities with His will.

<!-- p44 -->
### Inward: Building Up the Body

<!-- p45 -->
The Great Commission commands us to ‘*teach them to observe*’; this command goes far beyond formal teaching methods, such as this book. Disciple-making (discussed more fully in the chapter on discipleship) is the formation of a follower in the pattern of discipline, much like a parent raises a child. Good parents do not hand a five-year-old an encyclopedia and tell them to figure it out! Instead, they model, demonstrate, correct, guide, nurture, love, care, and shepherd.

<!-- p46 -->
Parents sacrifice for their children because they love them, because they are part of the same body, because they share one blood. As members of the Body and family of Christ, we have one Body; we have one Blood. Communion reminds us through its symbolism that we are joined to one another through Christ’s sacrifice. The act of communion should be a point of unity, reminding us that we all need redemption and that salvation is only from God. In this unity we see that no part of the body is greater than another, except for our head—Christ.

<!-- p47 -->
### Outward: Reaching the World

<!-- p48 -->
The most well-known part of the church’s mission is outward—to reach the world with the Good News. Jesus’ Great Commission calls His followers to evangelize and make disciples of all nations (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19-20</span>). Empowered by the Holy Spirit, the church is called to be His witnesses to the ends of the earth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>).

<!-- p49 -->
Jesus is the light of the world (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 8:12</span>). Light proceeds from a source and shines in all directions. His light shines through His manifestations: the Word, His Spirit, and us, His Body. We are the light of the world; by our lives we must radiate His light into our darkened worlds and display good works that glorify God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 5:14-16</span>). Practically, this means the church is called to uphold justice and mercy—both spiritual and physical. As James says, our pure worship is to care for the vulnerable, orphans, and widows (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:27</span>).

<!-- p50 -->
## Church Leadership

<!-- p51 -->
Across the Christian world there is a great variety of denominations, meetings, gatherings, and assemblies. There are few universal rules for church leadership, but those we have are very significant:

<!-- p52 -->
1.  Jesus is the sole Head of the Church. All serve Him (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 1:18</span>).

<!-- p53 -->
2.  High standards are required of those who lead; they are worthy of double honor, for they will receive double judgment (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:17</span>).

<!-- p54 -->
2.1  They must be mature, not arrogant, responsible (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:1-13</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 1:5-9</span>).

<!-- p55 -->
2.2.  They must be servants of all (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42-45" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 10:42-45</span>).

<!-- p56 -->
2.3.  They must devote themselves to the Word and to prayer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:4</span>).

<!-- p57 -->
2.4.  They are responsible for equipping the Body for the work of ministry.

<!-- p58 -->
3.  There are no greater or lesser members in the Body. Scriptures that affirm this:

<!-- p59 -->
3.1.  There must be no favoritism between rich and poor (James 2).

<!-- p60 -->
3.2.  There is no distinction between Jew and Gentile, slave or free, male or female (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 3:27-28</span>).

<!-- p61 -->
The structure of the Church, apart from rules of governance, is important, but much of it depends on the situation and is a matter of tradition rather than something universally prescribed. “<u>We’ve always done it this way!</u>” — a popular assertion; if you look back far enough, there was usually a good reason why it was done that way. This is generally neither good nor bad, but a matter of taste, needs, and availability.

<!-- p62 -->
A helpful example of how tradition serves as a basis for structure is family time at dinner. The most important part of a family dinner is that everyone is fed and that the food is nourishing. The variations are endless: what they eat, how they eat, how they season the food, etc. Different combinations are generally acceptable and are largely a matter of taste and preference.

<!-- p63 -->
A church with 10,000 members has different needs and capacities than a church of 50. The pattern established in the book of Acts and through the canon of the New Testament was a model of **elders** and **deacons**. Although leadership structures may look different in various churches and denominations, this model ultimately represents the minimally viable leadership structure for a church. Let’s look at these leadership roles and how they are selected.

<!-- p64 -->
### Elders (overseers)

<!-- p65 -->
Role and responsibilities:

<!-- p66 -->
- **Spiritual leadership** - Provide oversight, instruction, and pastoral care for the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:2</span>).

<!-- p67 -->
- **Teaching and preaching** - Responsible for teaching sound doctrine and guarding against false teachings (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:17</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 1:9</span>).

<!-- p68 -->
- **Shepherding the flock** - Care for the spiritual needs of the congregation, exemplifying humility and service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:2-3</span>).

<!-- p69 -->
- **Guarding the church** - Serve as watchmen, protecting the church from false teachings and divisions (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29-31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:29-31</span>).

<!-- p70 -->
- **Equipping the saints** - Train and prepare believers for works of ministry and discipleship (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-12</span>).

<!-- p71 -->
Selection process:

<!-- p72 -->
- **Appointed in every church** - Elders were appointed by the apostles or their representatives, often through prayer and fasting (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 14:23</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 1:5</span>).

<!-- p73 -->
- **Confirmed by the Holy Spirit** - Their appointment was considered Spirit-led, recognizing God’s call and gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>).

<!-- p74 -->
- **Tested and approved** - Candidates had to demonstrate maturity and faithfulness before being entrusted with leadership (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:10</span>). They must not be recent converts! ((<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:6</span>)

<!-- p75 -->
### Deacons (servants)

<!-- p76 -->
Role and responsibilities:

<!-- p77 -->
- **Practical service** - Focused on meeting physical needs in the church, freeing the elders for prayer and teaching (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-4</span>).

<!-- p78 -->
- **Supportive service** - Assist with hospitality, care for the poor, and administrative tasks (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:3</span>).

<!-- p79 -->
- **Fostering unity** - Address issues of fairness and care to prevent division (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-7</span>).

<!-- p80 -->
- **Examples of faith** - Serve as models of humility, faith, and service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:13</span>).

<!-- p81 -->
The selection process (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-6</span>):

<!-- p82 -->
- **Recognized by the congregation** - Chosen based on their reputation and spiritual character.

<!-- p83 -->
- **Confirmed by the leaders** - The apostles examined and ordained the selected deacons through prayer and the laying on of hands.

<!-- p84 -->
- **Proven in service** - Deacons were first tested to prove their faithfulness before they were officially appointed (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:10</span>).

<!-- p85 -->
### Leadership requirements

<!-- p86 -->
#### Requirements for both elders and deacons:

<!-- p87 -->
- Moral character - Both must be blameless, dignified, and show self-control (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:2</span>, 8).

<!-- p88 -->
- Family life - Both must be faithful in marriage (“husband of one wife”) and manage their households well (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:4-5</span>, 12).

<!-- p89 -->
- Spiritual maturity - Both must be tested and prove themselves before appointment (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:6</span>, 10).

<!-- p90 -->
- Reputation - Both must have a good reputation both inside and outside the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:7</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
- Faith and integrity - Both must hold the faith with a pure conscience and live in accordance with their convictions (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:9</span>).

<!-- p92 -->
### Examples of structures

<!-- p93 -->
Using what we have learned thus far about the rules and structures of leadership, let’s look at two examples of church organization found in the New Testament, Jerusalem and Antioch.

<!-- p94 -->
### The church in Jerusalem

<!-- p95 -->
The church in Jerusalem was primarily led by the **apostles**, who focused on teaching, prayer, and spiritual care (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:42</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:4</span>). As the congregation grew, the need for an administrative structure became apparent, especially to address practical matters such as caring for widows and distributing resources. This led to the appointment of **deacons** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-7</span>). These individuals, chosen for their spiritual maturity and good reputation, handled practical ministries so that the apostles could focus on their spiritual duties. Over time, **elders** also became leaders in the Jerusalem church. For example, during the Jerusalem Council (Acts 15) both apostles and elders were involved in decision-making, demonstrating a shared leadership structure.

<!-- p96 -->
### The church in Antioch

<!-- p97 -->
The church in Antioch had a more decentralized and diverse leadership structure compared to Jerusalem. It was led by a group of **prophets and teachers**, including Barnabas, Saul (Paul), Simeon, Lucius, and Manaen (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 13:1</span>). The church in Antioch was also much more oriented toward reaching the Gentiles, whereas the church in Jerusalem more strongly reflected its Jewish roots. Although deacons are not explicitly mentioned in the New Testament in Antioch, practical roles likely existed, given the growing scale of the church and its involvement in sending aid to Jerusalem (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29-30" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 11:29-30</span>). The church in Antioch became a model for ordaining missionaries: the leaders prayed, fasted, and laid hands on Paul and Barnabas to send them out (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 13:2-3</span>). This demonstrates a structure that combined spiritual care (prophets and teachers) with missionary ministry and practical support.

<!-- p98 -->
Unlike Paul’s corrections to the Corinthians and Galatians, Scripture does not criticize the leadership structures of Jerusalem or Antioch. This suggests that these churches, under the guidance of the Holy Spirit, were free to adapt their gatherings to their unique needs and circumstances within the framework of sound doctrine and apostolic instruction (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:42</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>).

<!-- p99 -->
Throughout these passages, some terms are used that we have not yet defined or studied: *apostle, prophet, evangelist*. These gifts are part of the fivefold ministry that Jesus gave to the church.

<!-- p100 -->
### The Gifts of the Fivefold Ministry

<!-- p101 -->
Jesus established five distinct ministry gifts, often called the fivefold ministry gifts, to equip believers for ministry and build up the Body of Christ.

<!-- p102 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p103 -->
> **<u>Ephesians 4:11-13</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And He Himself gave some to be apostles, some prophets, some evangelists, and some pastors and teachers,
>
> for the equipping of the saints for the work of ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ,
>
> till we all come to the unity of the faith and of the knowledge of the Son of God, to a perfect man, to the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ;
>

<!-- p104 -->
</span>

<!-- p105 -->
The five gifts given to equip the saints for the work of ministry are:

<!-- p106 -->
1.  **Apostles** - Pioneers and visionaries who lay foundations, plant churches, and advance the Kingdom of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:20</span>). Apostles model boldness and faith, demonstrating how to break new ground and take spiritual initiative.

<!-- p107 -->
2.  **Prophets** - Messengers and guides who proclaim God’s truth, bringing encouragement, edification, and direction (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>). Prophets inspire faith, call others to align with the Word of God, and model dependence on hearing God’s voice.

<!-- p108 -->
3.  **Evangelists** - Heralds of the Gospel who focus on reaching the lost and prompt others to action in proclaiming Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:5-8</span>). Evangelists train disciples to share their faith effectively, model boldness in witness, and inspire compassion for the perishing.

<!-- p109 -->
4.  **Pastors (Shepherds)** - Caregivers and mentors who protect, guide, and exercise spiritual oversight for the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>). Pastors teach disciples the importance of community, caring for others, and accountability within the Body of Christ.

<!-- p110 -->
5.  **Teachers** - Instructors and coaches who clarify doctrine and help believers grow in understanding and applying God’s Word (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 2:15</span>). Teachers equip disciples to study Scripture deeply, apply it to everyday life, and teach others effectively.

<!-- p111 -->
## Leadership and Gifts

<!-- p112 -->
One of the often misunderstood aspects of leadership in the church is the distinction between the authority and responsibility of leadership and specific gifts.

<!-- p113 -->
This confusion is tied to how we use words like teacher, pastor, prophet, apostle, evangelist. These words denote gifts. In the New Testament church there is no office of 'apostle'. There are people who do the work of an apostle, who have the gift or calling of an apostle. Having the gift of an apostle grants them neither authority nor responsibility.

<!-- p114 -->
I am a trained musician; I have played the saxophone for over 20 years. I played in school and college and performed semi-professionally for several summers, working with an evangelistic team. Even though I have the skill, gift, and ability of a saxophonist, I do not have the right to come into your church and get on stage with the worship team.

<!-- p115 -->
Likewise, there are people gifted in pastoral (shepherding) ministry, but that does not make them the 'SENIOR PASTOR' of a local church, nor does it make them 'your' pastor. It is not the fivefold ministry gifts that lead the church, but leaders (elders and deacons). Often leaders have specific fivefold ministry gifts, but that gift enhances their leadership rather than creates it. In the same way, it is not uncommon for people with fivefold giftings, still in the process of being formed, to serve and aspire to leadership; but God is primarily concerned with character, and then — gifts. We all must become mature, able to discern the Word of God, full of the Spirit, and doing good works.

<!-- p116 -->
Every leader and every gift must first submit to being a disciple, being part of the body.

<!-- p117 -->
Jesus gave specific ministry gifts to equip the saints for the work of ministry. They are distinct from motivational gifts (discussed later in this chapter) or manifestation gifts (discussed in the chapter on baptisms). As disciples grow, the fivefold ministry gifts equip them for service, while elders and deacons provide oversight to ensure sound teaching and practice.

<!-- p118 -->
## Your Role in the Church

<!-- p119 -->
So far in this chapter we have looked at church leadership and the primary metaphors for the church. Let’s develop the specific metaphor of the body and consider a marathoner.

<!-- p120 -->
### You are part of the whole

<!-- p121 -->
The church, as the Body of Christ, depends on each member working together to fulfill its mission. Just as a body cannot properly function if one part is lacking, the church needs every believer actively participating, growing, and serving. Each member of the body has a very important role. To be part of the body means to remain connected. The body can survive with reduced ability without a limb, but the limb itself cannot live when separated from the body. We must be an active part of the church and not become separated. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:24</span> says that we should gather and not forsake assembling.

<!-- p122 -->
To truly work and function together, you need fellowship! You need to know others, love others, and ideally, be liked by others! Build strong relationships and friendships and restore them when they are strained or injured. When a joint is injured or inflamed, you need to try to give it rest and/or set it back in place; so that a joint that is dislocated does not become broken! A limb that is broken and not healing will eventually be separated, and you cannot be part of the body, alone.

<!-- p123 -->
We do not all have the same function. Some of us may share the Gospel from a stage, others around the dinner table. But we are ALL called to be ministers of reconciliation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 5:18-19</span>). We are all accepted into the priesthood of believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:9</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 1:6</span>). This means that we all should be able to do the work of ministry by the power of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p124 -->
Fingers work with feet, feet with ankles, and so on-each part contributes to the overall mission. Marathoners must train if they want to be able to finish the race. You need to be ready-not only by learning, but by actually practicing what you have learned. The church, like a marathoner, thrives when each member fulfills their role in unison.

<!-- p125 -->
There are a large number of people in your life whom perhaps only you can reach. The pastor cannot be the only one trying to shepherd them, and the Evangelist cannot be the only one trying to reach them. God wants to use you to reach unbelievers by the power of His Holy Spirit. We all do the work of ministry; the Holy Spirit helps us.

<!-- p126 -->
### You need training

<!-- p127 -->
Believers need to train and be equipped for their unique roles in the church, as a runner strengthens certain muscles for a race. You will not succeed if you rush into a marathon and have not trained, and not all preparation is equivalent; you need to be properly ready for the race. Jesus gave specific ministry gifts to equip believers, not to do all the work themselves, but to empower each member to serve effectively in their unique role.

<!-- p128 -->
Preparation for a marathon will lead to transformation. You need strength, and you need to be transformed. Our responsibility is to submit to God and allow Him to transform us in renewal (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:1-2</span>). Transformation should lead you to maturity, enabling you to carry your own burdens and support others. You should be able to bear MUCH fruit. This will be discussed in detail in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p129 -->
Training is hard, sometimes it is uncomfortable. Sometimes the transformation you need to go through you would prefer to skip. But if you want to reach the goal, you need to stay on the narrow path. At times you may need warning, comfort, or support—all of which are offered with patience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Thessalonians 5:14</span>).

<!-- p130 -->
Every great marathoner has a coach who trains him; you will need a mentor to help you. Of course, you have the Holy Spirit, but He often relies on His coaches, mature men and women of God, in the Body of Christ, alongside whom you are called to labor.

<!-- p131 -->
As individuals we must first submit to God, and second, in this context, to the body of believers. Submission in the church means honoring godly leadership (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 13:17</span>) and mutual submission in humility (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:21</span>).

<!-- p132 -->
Submission can be difficult, especially when you have previously experienced undermined trust or failed leadership. This does not imply blind obedience or control, but reflects a spirit of reverence and cooperation. Worldly submission is imposed by those in power and can be demeaning and lead to resentment. Biblical submission, rooted in humility and discernment, strengthens the church and fosters accountability. Be ready to listen to counsel and constructive feedback.

<!-- p133 -->
Now that we have laid a solid foundation—you are a significant part of the body, but you need training—let's move on to finding where you fit.

<!-- p134 -->
### Finding your place

<!-- p135 -->
Finding your role in the church involves seeking God's guidance, understanding your spiritual gifts, and actively participating in your church community. It is a journey of prayer, exploration, and growth, where you discover how God has uniquely equipped you to contribute to His Kingdom. It is very important to note that your role in the church will likely change over time and is unlikely to remain the same throughout your entire life.

<!-- p136 -->
Here is a practical approach to help you find your place; they are presented as a series of steps for clarity, but you will likely repeat the same step multiple times throughout your journey with God.

<!-- p137 -->
#### Step 1 - Seek God's guidance through prayer and the Word

<!-- p138 -->
Begin with **seeking God's guidance through prayer**. God is the ultimate source of your purpose and calling (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="29" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 29:11</span>). Pray regularly for clarity in how you can serve in the church, asking Him to reveal your spiritual gifts and desires. Trust that He will direct your steps as you submit your plans to His will (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 3:5-6</span>).

<!-- p139 -->
**Study God's Word** to deepen your understanding of the church's mission and your role in it. Key passages such as 1 Corinthians 12 and <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-12</span> emphasize spiritual gifts and the diversity of roles in the Body of Christ. Reflect on examples of people in Scripture who discovered and fulfilled their roles, such as Paul, Timothy, and Lydia.

<!-- p140 -->
#### Step 2 - Identify your gifts, passions, and skills

<!-- p141 -->
Identifying your **spiritual gifts** is an important step. It may be helpful to take a spiritual gifts assessment. These gifts are given by God for the edification of the church. Several classes of gifts are listed in the New Testament:

<!-- p142 -->
- **Motivational gifts** are given by God: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:6-8</span>

<!-- p143 -->
- Discussed after this list.

<!-- p144 -->
- The fivefold ministry gifts are given by Jesus: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-13</span>

<!-- p145 -->
- Discussed earlier in this chapter.

<!-- p146 -->
- Manifestation gifts are given by the Holy Spirit: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span>

<!-- p147 -->
- Discussed in the chapter on baptism.

<!-- p148 -->
**Motivational gifts** reflect how a believer tends to think, feel, and act. They prompt believers to serve in particular ways that edify the church. Each gift complements the others, ensuring that the church functions as one body with diverse roles (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:4-5</span>). They operate as grace-given abilities (Greek: ***charis** - khar'-ece - **G5463 -** unmerited gift*), which means that they are given by God, not earned or learned. Although the gifts come from God, the person who has a gift can become more skillful in its use.

<!-- p149 -->
Motivational gifts:

<!-- p150 -->
1.  **Prophecy** - A God-given inclination to boldly proclaim truth, often with an emphasis on correction and righteousness, guiding others to align with God’s Word.\*

<!-- p151 -->
2.  **Serving (helps)** - Meets practical needs through acts of service and assistance to support others.

<!-- p152 -->
3.  **Teaching** - Explains and clarifies truth, helping others understand and apply God’s Word.

<!-- p153 -->
4.  **Exhortation (encouragement)** - Motivates, encourages, and comforts, inspiring growth and perseverance.

<!-- p154 -->
5.  **Giving** - Generously provides resources to meet needs and advance God’s work, not seeking recognition.

<!-- p155 -->
6.  **Leadership (administration)** - Organizes and directs others to achieve goals with vision and effectiveness.

<!-- p156 -->
7.  **Mercy** - Shows compassion and care to comfort and support those who are suffering or in need.

<!-- p157 -->
\* Prophecy as used in the context of Romans is understood differently from prophecy as a manifestation gift of the Holy Spirit (1 Corinthians). The same Greek word is used in both passages of Scripture, but the context differs. For a more in-depth study of the differences, I recommend reading:

<!-- p158 -->
- *The Gift of Prophecy* by Kenneth E. Hagin ([www.rhema.org](http://www.rhema.org))

<!-- p159 -->
<!-- -->

<!-- p160 -->
- *Gifts and Ministries of the Holy Spirit* by Lester Sumrall

<!-- p161 -->
When identifying your gifts, consider your **passions and skills**. God often calls us to serve in areas that we are passionate about or in which we are skilled (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 31:2-6</span>). Reflect on what inspires you in serving God and people, and how your professional skills, hobbies, and life experience can serve the work of ministry.

<!-- p162 -->
Seek guidance from **leaders and mentors** who can give wisdom and insight (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 15:22</span>). Meet with your pastor or ministry leader to discuss your interests and ask for honest feedback about where you can best participate. Pray over the feedback you receive. Consider joining a discipleship group to receive training and ongoing support.

<!-- p163 -->
#### Step 3 - Be faithful now

<!-- p164 -->
The Parable of the Talents (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-30" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 25:14-30</span>) reminds us that we must be faithful in little so that we can be entrusted with much (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 16:10</span>).

<!-- p165 -->
**Get involved and serve** in various ministries to discern what God is calling you to. Start small, volunteering in areas of need in your church and in its supported ministries. For example:

<!-- p166 -->
- Join the hospitality team, children’s ministry, or outreach programs. Consider leading a discipleship-focused group for new believers using this book, *Principles of Faith,* employing the group discussion questions and the end-of-chapter assignments to facilitate dialogue.

<!-- p167 -->
- Be willing to step out of your comfort zone to discover new opportunities.

<!-- p168 -->
- Observe where God is working through you and where you feel most effective.

<!-- p169 -->
Practice giving. We can give to the church in many important ways, and we are commanded to do this for the fulfillment of the mission.

<!-- p170 -->
- Time - we can give our time and serve as volunteers. By giving time, we invest our effort and talents. It is also a great way to learn new skills and develop the ones you have.

<!-- p171 -->
- Resources - we can share our resources. Today this most often means giving to the church and ministries (not only to the “institution,” but also to those in need within the church). Money is helpful because the church can acquire needed resources with it, but people have also given material things besides money, such as extra homes, clothing, cars, food, building materials, tools, etc. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="32-35" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 4:32-35</span> we see an example of early believers freely giving, sharing the proceeds from selling extra lands and houses, and giving in such a way that 'no one was in need'.

<!-- p172 -->
Working together with others in ministry can reveal your strengths and deepen your understanding of your role. Participate in small groups or church classes to strengthen connections. Listen to the burdens and needs of those around you and consider how you can support them. Building relationships within the church helps you draw closer to others and understand the needs of the community.

<!-- p173 -->
#### Step 4 - Ask for help!

<!-- p174 -->
Jesus said that in life we will experience troubles! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:33</span>) We are called not to carry the weight of our burdens alone, but to cast them on God, who cares for us! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:7</span>)

<!-- p175 -->
Sometimes along the way we can be injured. Marathon runners, even when doing 'everything right,' can push their bodies to injury, or they can stumble and fall.

<!-- p176 -->
Not every ‘fall’ is related to sin, though some is; not every weakness is ‘our fault’—sometimes others sin against us. Regardless of the cause of our weakness or wound, we must be restored and healed.

<!-- p177 -->
There is great freedom in seeking inner healing and restoration. We are called to cover the vulnerable parts of the body (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:23</span>) and to restore those caught in transgressions (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:1</span>).

<!-- p178 -->
There is much more that could be said about this, and we cover the topics of healing in overview in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p179 -->
#### Step 5 - Be patient

<!-- p180 -->
Pay attention to confirmation from God and others. Often God confirms your calling through fruit, joy, and affirmation from the people around you. Reflect on where your efforts have borne fruit, and listen to feedback about the impact of ministry. This can help you recognize where you are most effective in serving the Body of Christ.

<!-- p181 -->
Be patient and trust God’s timing. Discovering your role can take time and involve trial and error (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 1:6</span>). Trust that God will guide you as you remain faithful and open to His leading. In this season, devote yourself to growth and preparation by attending seminars, studying the Bible, and using other resources that equip you for ministry. Do this together with the church; get involved in the Body of Christ to discover your gifts.

<!-- p182 -->
Remember, the church — is not a building or an institution; it is a living, breathing body made up of people like you. As you step into your role, you contribute to its mission and glorify Christ as its head.
