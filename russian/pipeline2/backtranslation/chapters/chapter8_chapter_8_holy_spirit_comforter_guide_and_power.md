# Chapter 8 — The Holy Spirit — Comforter, Counselor, and Power

<!-- p1 -->
The Holy Spirit is not a new spirit; He is from God and was with God from the very beginning. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 1:2</span> the Spirit of God was hovering over the waters during creation. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="NEH" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Nehemiah 9:20</span> Scripture says that it was the Spirit who instructed the people in the wilderness. By that same Holy Spirit God anointed kings, judges, and prophets in the Old Testament. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JOL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Joel 2:28–29</span> it is promised that the Spirit will be poured out on all flesh. By that same Holy Spirit God raised Jesus from the dead, and now He dwells in us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:11</span>). There is only one Holy Spirit, and He is always the same. The way He works with people has looked different at different times, but His heart has never changed.

<!-- p2 -->
Jesus gave His disciples—and all of us—the great promise of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p3 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p4 -->
> **<u>John 14:16–18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And I will pray the Father, and He will give you another Helper, that He may abide with you forever —
>
> the Spirit of truth, whom the world cannot receive, because it neither sees Him nor knows

<!-- p20 -->
The disciples were to wait for the promised outpouring of the Spirit, and this was to endow them with power to be witnesses to the ends of the earth. The Holy Spirit is an integral part of God’s plan for humanity and is necessary for us as believers — to complete our mission — and for the world.

<!-- p21 -->
## Getting to Know the Holy Spirit

<!-- p22 -->
First and foremost, the Holy Spirit is from God and of God. Scripture calls the Spirit the Spirit of God, the Spirit of the Lord, or the Spirit of Christ; although these expressions emphasize different aspects, they refer to the same One. The Holy Spirit, being fully God, is also eternal and holy (blameless, innocent, pure, unstained). In some translations the Holy Spirit is called the Holy Ghost.

<!-- p23 -->
The word for spirit in Greek is **Pneuma** (**NYOO-mah** - **G4151**), it means breath or wind. The Hebrew word for «spirit» means the same thing. Breath is important because we need it constantly for life. God’s breath He breathed into Adam to bring him to life; likewise Jesus breathed His last before His death on the cross. Without breath there is no life. Breath, like the wind, can be felt and experienced, but it is not as easy to observe as other «elements» — water, fire, earth.

<!-- p24 -->
There are also several other key adjectives that describe the Holy Spirit. Each is used in combination with ‘Spirit ...’, meaning these qualities are essential to the definition of the Spirit. For example, *Spirit of Truth* or *Spirit of Life*.

<!-- p25 -->
Spirit:

<!-- p26 -->
- **of Truth** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:17</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span> Refers to the Spirit’s role in instructing believers into all truth, revealing God’s will and the reality of Christ.

<!-- p27 -->
- **of Life** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:2</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:8</span> Emphasizes the Spirit’s role in granting believers spiritual and eternal life, freeing them from the law of sin and death.

<!-- p28 -->
- **of Grace** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:29</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ZEC" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Zechariah 12:10</span> Reflects the Spirit’s action in granting people God’s favor, forgiveness, and unmerited mercy.

<!-- p29 -->
- **of Glory** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 4:14</span> Indicates the Spirit’s role in unveiling and manifesting God’s glory, especially in times of suffering and persecution.

<!-- p30 -->
- **of Adoption** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:15</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 4:5</span> Refers to the work of the Spirit that makes believers God’s children, granting them the status of sons and daughters.

<!-- p31 -->
- **of Holiness** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 1:4</span> Points to the sanctifying work of the Spirit, setting believers apart for God’s purposes.

<!-- p32 -->
- **of Power** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 1:7</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span> Describes the Spirit’s endowment of believers with power for boldness, service, and the ability to live out their faith effectively.

<!-- p33 -->
In all these attributes the Holy Spirit is also rightly recognized as Helper or Comforter. Jesus calls the Holy Spirit the coming helper; He uses the Greek word ***Paraklētos** (*pa-RA-kley-tos - **G3875**), which means advocate, helper, one called to be alongside.

<!-- p34 -->
An advocate speaks on behalf of another, like an attorney. He intercedes and helps the person for whom he is responsible.

<!-- p35 -->
A helper helps. He helps empower the work, but his task first of all — is to assist the work. The helper does not bear sole responsibility for accomplishing the work, but comes alongside to help bring it to completion.

<!-- p36 -->
The Holy Spirit has several distinct purposes and roles in His ministry. His work is directed inward (into the hearts and lives of believers), upward (in service and acknowledgment of God), and outward (to our mission field, the world).

<!-- p37 -->
## Roles of the Holy Spirit-Inward, Upward, Outward

<!-- p38 -->
### Inward-The Holy Spirit connects us with Jesus

<!-- p39 -->
Before Jesus performed any miracles or began His public ministry, He was baptized by John the Baptist, and the Spirit of God descended upon Him, and a voice from heaven proclaimed: ‘This is My Beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:17</span>).

<!-- p40 -->
When we receive Jesus by faith, we receive salvation and the seal of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p41 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p42 -->
> **<u>Ephesians 1:13-14</u>** (NKJV)
>
> In Him you also, having heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation, and having believed in Him, were sealed with the Holy Spirit of promise,
>
> who is the guarantee of our inheritance until the redemption of the purchased possession, to the praise of His glory.
>

<!-- p43 -->
</span>

<!-- p44 -->
This seal is not metaphorical but literal. It serves as a sign of our redemption and a pledge of the full inheritance we receive as children of God. Romans 8 says that through the new birth we receive that very same Spirit of God who raised Christ from the dead into our mortal bodies, and we become a literal temple of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 6:16</span>).

<!-- p45 -->
This receiving of God’s Spirit transforms us and causes rivers of living water to flow from us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 7:38</span>). This renewal is instantaneous and complete, yet it continues throughout our lives. The river of the Holy Spirit should never cease to flow in our lives!

<!-- p46 -->
Thanks to salvation and the indwelling of the Holy Spirit within us we can abide in Christ, and He — in us. The Spirit of God no longer remains outside, but can dwell deeply with us. We can walk in fellowship and harmony with God from now and into eternal life.

<!-- p47 -->
Much more could be said about the inward work of the Holy Spirit and His role in our sanctification, but this will be addressed in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p48 -->
### Up-The Holy Spirit testifies of Jesus

<!-- p49 -->
The Holy Spirit is from God and is God’s. As Jesus said that He does nothing unless the Father shows Him (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 5:19</span>), so the Holy Spirit does nothing unless it is directed by the Father. Since the Holy Spirit — the Comforter, He does not act on His own, but acts in perfect harmony with the Father.

<!-- p50 -->
The Spirit testifies of Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:26</span>), glorifies Jesus, and declares and announces what Jesus has for us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:14</span>).

<!-- p51 -->
The Holy Spirit will testify of Jesus, glorify Him, teach and remind us of the teaching of Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:26</span>) and guide us into all truth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span>). Jesus told His disciples that He still had much to say to them, but they could not bear it yet, and that the Holy Spirit would lead them into all truth. This is another subtle theological point. These teachings that Jesus wanted His disciples to know were delivered in several ways. Jesus taught them personally both before His crucifixion and after His resurrection and before His ascension.

<!-- p52 -->
Jesus also promised that the Spirit of Truth, poured out on them on the Day of Pentecost, would lead them into all truth through divine revelation. Is there divine revelation today? Absolutely yes! The gifts of the Holy Spirit, including prophecy and words of knowledge and wisdom, have not yet ceased! However, no prophecy or word from a spirit is of private interpretation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2PE" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Peter 1:20</span>). This is extremely important. Some will mistakenly claim that they are listening to the Holy Spirit, but their deeds and words do not bear witness to Jesus. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 4:3</span> warns against any spirit that does not testify of Jesus, saying that such a spirit is of the antichrist. This bears repeating: if any spirit deviates from the teaching of Christ, then it is not the Holy Spirit but an unclean spirit!

<!-- p53 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p54 -->
> **<u>1 John 4:1</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Beloved! Do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits, whether they are of God, because many false prophets have gone out into the world.
>

<!-- p55 -->
</span>

<!-- p56 -->
Throughout Scripture we see examples of erroneous claims, often coming from false prophets or well-meaning people who spoke contrary to God’s will. For example, Jeremiah warned against prophets who spoke visions of their own heart, promising peace when destruction was looming (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="23" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 23:16-17</span>). Similarly, in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 16:16-18</span> the words of a certain slave girl about Paul and Silas were accurate but inspired by a demonic spirit rather than the Holy Spirit. These examples remind us that even words that seem true can come from ungodly sources.

<!-- p57 -->
Every claim 'God told me!' or a 'new' teaching must be measured against the biblical tests of truth. To discern rightly, we must ask:

<!-- p58 -->
- Does it align with Scripture?

<!-- p59 -->
- Does it exalt Jesus Christ?

<!-- p60 -->
- Does it bear godly fruit?

<!-- p61 -->
By applying these tests, we can distinguish the true guidance of the Holy Spirit from error or deception.

<!-- p62 -->
God is accustomed to giving us generous &amp; abundant wisdom and guidance (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:5</span> &amp; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="33" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 33:3</span>). God’s divine wisdom will not contradict Scripture, but will go beyond Scripture. His *entolē* *(*commandments*)* are immediate prescriptions to carry out; these are *rhema* words (as discussed in the chapter on faith).

<!-- p63 -->
This wisdom, understanding, and guidance are unlikely to be stated verbatim in Scripture. For example, Scripture gives excellent general guidelines on what to look for when choosing a spouse, but it does not give specific prescriptions regarding a particular person. God does promise to direct our path, to lead us into all truth, and we can take comfort in knowing that this wisdom and the leading of the Holy Spirit will never violate any teaching of Scripture.

<!-- p65 -->
Jesus gave us His mission, the Great Commission:

<!-- p66 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

>

>
> teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you; and behold, I am with you always, even to the end of the age." Amen.
>

<!-- p68 -->
</span>

<!-- p69 -->
The Holy Spirit has an integral role in the mission, convicting the world of sin, righteousness, and judgment:

<!-- p70 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="8-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

>
> of righteousness, because I go to My Father and you will see Me no more;
>
> of judgment, because the ruler of this world is judged.
>

<!-- p72 -->
</span>

<!-- p73 -->
Digging deeper, the Spirit brings conviction in these three truths:

<!-- p74 -->
1.  **Conviction of sin**: revealing the reality of human rebellion against God, drawing people’s attention to the depravity of their own lives.

<!-- p75 -->
2.  **Conviction of righteousness**: showing Jesus’ perfect fulfillment of the Father’s will and that Jesus’ sacrifice has provided the once-for-all redemption necessary for the forgiveness of sins.

<!-- p76 -->
3.  **Conviction of judgment**: affirming Satan’s defeat, the inevitability of God’s justice, and that all will stand for judgment for the work of their lives and deeds.

<!-- p77 -->
The Holy Spirit also empowers the mission. The Holy Spirit gives believers the strength to be bold witnesses in the face of persecution, to share the Gospel. Boldness does not mean loudness. It means having courage in spite of fear. Power is might, strength, and ability. The Holy Spirit gives the power and ability to be a witness of the Gospel despite threats and fear of punishment.

<!-- p78 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p79 -->
> **<u>Acts 1:8</u>** (NKJV)
>
> But you shall receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you; and you shall be witnesses to Me in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and even to the end of the earth."

<!-- p80 -->
</span>

<!-- p81 -->
The entire book of Acts is full of stories and descriptions in which the following general cycle is repeated:

<!-- p82 -->
1.  **Prayer**: Believers ask for boldness and God’s intervention.

<!-- p83 -->
2.  **Empowerment**: The Holy Spirit fills them with boldness, power, and guidance.

<!-- p84 -->
3.  **Proclamation**: They boldly proclaim the Gospel, often with signs, wonders, and miracles.

<!-- p85 -->
4.  **Opposition**: Resistance arises, leading to persecution or hardship. Sometimes believers suffered martyrdom; at other times they were imprisoned and/or beaten.

<!-- p86 -->
5.  **Renewal**: The Church returns to prayer, seeking fresh empowerment, and the cycle continues.

<!-- p87 -->
The New Testament Church grew to a great extent in the face of determined opposition. All over the world the Church suffers violence at the hands of oppressors and wicked rulers intent on suppressing the knowledge of the truth about Jesus.

<!-- p88 -->
God knew this opposition would occur; Jesus prophesied about this:

<!-- p89 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p90 -->

>

>
> For the Holy Spirit will teach you in that very hour what you ought to say."
>

<!-- p91 -->
</span>

<!-- p92 -->
Do not be afraid! Jesus promised us that:

<!-- p93 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

>
> And I also say to you that you are Peter, and on this rock I will build My church, and the gates of Hades shall not prevail against it.
>

<!-- p95 -->
</span>

<!-- p96 -->
This is an amazing and encouraging promise. Gates are stationary defensive structures. When Jesus says that the *gates of Hades will not prevail*, He paints the picture of a conquering army that knocks down the gates and takes the city!

<!-- p97 -->
The Holy Spirit brings supernatural boldness to proclaim the testimony of the Gospel in the face of persecution. The result of such empowerment can be described, but it is best experienced. It is a supernatural (beyond human) boldness that compels believers to share. This boldness in preaching is often accompanied by supernatural acts of the manifestation of the Spirit and power (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 2:1-5</span>). People are healed physically and emotionally, the dead are raised, those tormented by evil spirits are set free, people with hardened hearts turn and repent.

Jesus said that we will do these works and greater! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:12</span>). These collective works are supernatural, divine, and desperately needed by a world torn apart, miserable, full of pain, waiting for the mature sons of God to be revealed! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:22-23</span>)

<!-- p99 -->
Jesus’ chosen instrument of change on the earth is His Church, empowered, encouraged, and led by the Holy Spirit.

## Building a Relationship with the Holy Spirit

<!-- p101 -->
Any relationship requires time and experience to develop deeply. The Holy Spirit is our Advocate, our Comforter, our Helper, and our Teacher. He empowers us and sanctifies us.

<!-- p102 -->
Begin to think of the Holy Spirit as a person. In our relationships we can both work to strengthen them and do things that weaken them. He can be ignored, He can be quenched, blasphemed, or He can be heeded, honored, and obeyed.

<!-- p103 -->
Do not ignore the voice of the Holy Spirit. He is always speaking to us and to the church. If you do not yet know how to discern the voice of the Holy Spirit, it takes time, willingness, and effort (as do any healthy and beneficial relationships). But do not worry: you already have everything you need to begin building a personal relationship with the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p104 -->
The fastest and most reliable ways to build an effective relationship with the Holy Spirit

<!-- p105 -->
1.  Call upon Him individually in prayer, pause, and wait for Him to speak.

<!-- p106 -->
2.  Call upon Him together with a group of people who know Him (the church), pause, and wait for Him to speak.

<!-- p107 -->
I use the expression ‘call upon’ in the literal sense—with your mouth or in whatever way you speak (with your hands, if you cannot speak aloud with your mouth). Why speak? Several reasons:

<!-- p108 -->
1.  We are speaking spirits, made in God’s image. God speaks, and so do we.

<!-- p109 -->
2.  We use speech as our primary means of communication. You can also write down what you want to say (journaling) and speak it aloud.

<!-- p110 -->
3.  Speech is an intentional act and a sign to the one you are addressing that you wish to enter into conversation.

<!-- p111 -->
#### What should you say?

<!-- p112 -->
If you have never spoken to God, try introducing yourself. Of course, it may feel a little strange, but that discomfort is normal and temporary. After introducing yourself, tell God that you want to speak with Him and that you want Him to speak back to you.

<!-- p113 -->
Consider asking Him a question. It does not have to be a deep theological question; you can start with something simple. Could You show me Your love for me? Why am I feeling a certain way? What would You like to say to me?

<!-- p114 -->
#### What next?

<!-- p115 -->
Well, as in all effective relationships, you speak a little, then pause and listen. God will speak.

<!-- p116 -->
God speaks in many ways, but He desires to do so personally and internally. It will most likely not be an audible voice (as if your best friend spoke your name out loud), but an inner voice addressing your heart and mind. Elijah calls it the ‘still small voice’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1KI" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Kings 19:11-13</span>).

<!-- p117 -->
The most common ways I have personally seen God speak to people—and I have been privileged to help dozens of people learn to speak with God directly—are some combination of the following:

<!-- p118 -->
- Insight - the person speaking with God receives an instantaneous understanding that helps them.

<!-- p119 -->
- Image - also known in Scripture as a vision or a dream (a vision - when you are awake, a dream - when you are sleeping), God will give you an image in your mind. Perhaps it is a memory, or a place where you have been or that you know? Explore the image, what do you see? What do you feel as a result? What is happening?

<!-- p120 -->
- The inner voice - like an audible voice, but one that only you hear, and addressed to your heart and mind.

<!-- p121 -->
#### What will God say?

<!-- p122 -->
Not sure! But that's the interesting part. I can say that He is NOT a spirit of fear, but of perfect love, and He will never violate the teaching of Scripture. For example, God will never tell you to enter into an extramarital affair.

<!-- p123 -->
What now?

<!-- p124 -->
Keep the dialogue going! It really is that simple.

### Why build a relationship?

<!-- p129 -->
The Holy Spirit — the Helper. A Helper helps us! Things go much more smoothly if we have a shared language and relationship with Him. Can you imagine trying to work with someone at work if you have no relationship, you don’t speak the same language, and you can’t communicate?!

<!-- p130 -->
We serve a God who wants to communicate with us. Jesus said that His sheep know His voice (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:27</span>), we already have all the natural abilities we need built in, but we may lack practiced skill.

<!-- p131 -->
Speaking from personal experience, a personal and active relationship with the Holy Spirit is one of the best aspects of the Christian life. His presence brings us peace, comfort, and direction, but only if you know how to seek Him, listen, and obey Him.

<!-- p132 -->
Develop sensitivity to the Holy Spirit. Like many spouses over time, you can communicate with your spouse in a unique way and sometimes — with just a glance. One glance alone tells you everything you need to know about what just happened and what will happen next. Build that same sensitivity and closeness with the Holy Spirit. Include Him in your whole life, this is a beautiful part of your relationship with Him that will add so much depth and richness to your journey of faith.

<!-- p133 -->
The Holy Spirit came to lead and guide us, to empower us to be God’s people and to see the radical transformation of the people of the Earth, being delivered from the kingdom of darkness into the kingdom of light. Getting to know Him, building trust in Him — is vital for everyday life and for carrying out the work that Jesus entrusted to us.

<!-- p134 -->
Our discussion of the work of the Holy Spirit does not end in this chapter. His ministry is examined in detail in the remaining chapters of this book, especially in the chapters on sanctification and baptism.

## Questions for Personal Reflection

<!-- p136 -->
1.  How have you experienced the Holy Spirit as your Comforter, Teacher, or source of power? Which role resonates most in your current season of life?

<!-- p137 -->
2.  Reflect on the difference between the Holy Spirit being “within” (internal work) and “upon” (external work). How does this distinction affect your understanding of His work in your life?

<!-- p138 -->
3.  What steps can you take to cultivate a deeper relationship with the Holy Spirit and to recognize His voice more clearly?

<!-- p139 -->
4.  We must rely on the power of the Holy Spirit to act boldly and fulfill the mission God has given. What specific mission and direction has God given you?

<!-- p140 -->
5.  The Holy Spirit leads us into truth and discernment. Are there areas in your life where you need clarity or wisdom? How can you invite Him to guide you?

## Questions for Group Discussion (20-30 minutes)

<!-- p142 -->
1.  **Icebreaker Question:** “When you think of the Holy Spirit, what is the first word or image that comes to mind? Why?”

<!-- p143 -->
2.  **Scripture Study:** Read <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:26</span>. How does the Holy Spirit’s role as Teacher and Helper encourage you in your faith journey?

<!-- p144 -->
3.  **The Holy Spirit «within» vs. «on»:** Discuss the difference between when the Holy Spirit dwells «within» for transformation and when He is «on» for empowerment. Why are both aspects necessary for a believer?

<!-- p145 -->
4.  **Practical Guidance:** In what practical ways can we attune ourselves to hear the voice of the Holy Spirit and follow His guidance in daily decisions?

<!-- p146 -->
5.  **Reflection**: Recall a time when you sensed the Holy Spirit empowered you to act. How did that experience affect your faith and the people around you?
