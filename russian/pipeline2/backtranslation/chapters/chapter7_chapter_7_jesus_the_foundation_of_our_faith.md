# Chapter 7 - Jesus - the foundation of our faith

<!-- p1 -->
Building on our study of Jesus as the Messiah, in this chapter we continue to consider the life, person, and work of Jesus. We will see that Jesus is the Mediator of a new and better covenant - and that He perfectly fulfills the roles of Prophet, Priest, and King. It is in this New Covenant that we, as believers, have a good relationship with God and move from a covenant based on works to a covenant based on trust (faith). Finally, we will read and study the *Apostles' Creed* as a way to conclude our exploration about Jesus.

<!-- p2 -->
## Mediator of the New Covenant

<!-- p3 -->
The concept of a mediator implies someone who serves as a link between two parties, facilitating a relationship or agreement. In the narrative of Exodus, Moses is the original mediator of the covenant that God established with His people. Moses received the Law, offered sacrifices (priest), called God’s people to obedience (prophet), and judged them (judge/king). In the rest of the Old Testament after Moses, the mediatorial role was carried out through the ministry of the prophet, the Levitical priesthood, as well as the judges and later the kings of the people.

<!-- p4 -->
Old Testament roles of mediators:

<!-- p5 -->
1.  **Prophet:** Prophets were God’s messengers, called to speak His heart to the people and to bring them back when they strayed. Moses, for example, served as a prophet who mediated in the Old Testament by receiving the Law from God and teaching it to Israel. The prophets also foretold the coming of the Messiah and the final redemption of humanity. Other prominent prophets: Isaiah, Jeremiah, Elijah, Elisha, Samuel.

<!-- p6 -->
2.  **Levitical priesthood:** The priests acted as mediators by offering sacrifices for the atonement of sins and interceding for the people before God. The High Priest had a special role on the Day of Atonement, entering the Holy of Holies to sprinkle blood on the mercy seat, symbolizing reconciliation with God. The ministry of the priest is to intercede for the people.

<!-- p7 -->
3.  **Judge/King:** Judges, and later kings, were called to rule God’s people, enforce His laws, judge between people, deliver them from enemies, and lead them in righteousness. The Book of Judges tells the stories of these leaders—such as Deborah, Samson, and Gideon—whom God raised up to save and guide Israel. Later Israel had a succession of kings—many evil, some good (see 1 and 2 Kings, 1 and 2 Samuel, and 1<sup>st</sup> and 2<sup>nd</sup> Chronicles). Some notable examples are Saul, David, and Solomon.

<!-- p8 -->
It is worth noting that all Old Testament mediators were fully human and not divine. As humans, they could sin and often fell into sin. Some of these mediators were sinful and led the people away from God into ungodly worship: false prophets, lying and corrupt priests, and wicked kings.

<!-- p9 -->
### Christ as the Mediator of the New Covenant

<!-- p10 -->
The death, resurrection, and ascension of Jesus fulfill the Old Covenant and establish the New Covenant.

<!-- p11 -->
The New Covenant is a transformative relationship between God and His people, established through the life, death, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. Unlike the Old Covenant, which was centered on external laws, the New Covenant emphasizes inward transformation and grace. It was instituted by Jesus at the Last Supper, where He proclaimed that His blood would establish this covenant, replacing the repeated animal sacrifices of the Old Covenant with His once-for-all accomplished redemption (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 22:20</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:10</span>). Through this sacrifice, the New Covenant provides complete forgiveness and cleansing from sin, satisfying the requirement for perfect and lasting reconciliation with God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 31:34</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:12-18</span>).

<!-- p12 -->
One of the central features of the New Covenant is its internal nature: God writes His law on the hearts of His people, forming a heartfelt relationship with Him through the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 31:33</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 3:3</span>). This shift enables believers to fulfill God’s will not merely by observing external commandments, but through empowerment by the Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:1-4</span>). The New Covenant also provides direct access to God, removing the need for an earthly priesthood. Jesus serves as the eternal High Priest, and believers are called a royal priesthood and can approach God with boldness through Christ’s sacrifice (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 7:24-25</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:19-20</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:9</span>).

<!-- p13 -->
Unlike the Old Covenant, which was intended exclusively for Israel, the New Covenant is universal, extending to all who believe in Christ, regardless of ethnicity or background (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 3:28</span>). This covenant is entirely based on grace, offering salvation and forgiveness freely through faith in Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>). It is sustained not by human effort but by the empowering work of the Holy Spirit, who transforms and enables believers to live in obedience and reflect God’s character (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EZK" data-chapter="36" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ezekiel 36:26-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:9-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-23</span>). Moreover, the New Covenant is everlasting, surpassing the temporary and conditional nature of the Old Covenant (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="32" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 32:40</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:13</span>). It represents the ultimate fulfillment of God’s redemptive plan, reconciling humanity to Himself and enabling believers to live as His people under His rule for eternity.

<!-- p14 -->
Jesus is explicitly called the Mediator of the New Covenant in

<!-- p15 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p16 -->
> **<u>Hebrews 9:15</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And for this reason He is the Mediator of the new covenant, by means of death, for the redemption of the transgressions committed under the first covenant, that those who are called may receive the promise of the eternal inheritance.
>

<!-- p17 -->
</span>

<!-- p18 -->
By His atoning death and resurrection, Christ inaugurates this new covenant, replacing the old system of sacrifices and external laws with a covenant written on the hearts of believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 31:31-34</span>). He stands between God and humanity, not only interceding for His people but also providing the means of reconciliation by His own blood (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:12</span>). Unlike the mediators of the Old Covenant, Jesus is sinless, eternal, and fully able to satisfy the divine demands of justice and mercy, securing a lasting relationship between God and believers.

<!-- p19 -->
Jesus fulfills and perfects these roles

<!-- p20 -->
1.  **Prophet:** Jesus — the perfect Prophet, not merely a messenger of God’s word, but the very Word made flesh (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:14</span>). He fully and perfectly reveals God’s character, will, and plan of salvation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 1:1-2</span>). His teachings, miracles, and prophetic statements fulfill the prophecies of the Old Testament and lay the foundation for the New Testament.

<!-- p21 -->
2.  **Priest:** Jesus — the perfect High Priest, who offered Himself as a sacrifice for sin once and for all (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 7:27</span>). Unlike the priests of the Old Testament, who had to offer sacrifices again and again for their sins and the sins of the people, Jesus, being sinless, offered His own blood to secure eternal redemption (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:14</span>).

<!-- p22 -->
3.  **King &amp; Judge:** Jesus — the eternal King, reigning in righteousness and justice. As the Son of David, He fulfills the promise of an everlasting kingdom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2SA" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Samuel 7:16</span>). His reign is not only earthly but cosmic: He rules over all creation and establishes peace by His victory over sin and death (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 2:9-11</span>). Jesus will return as king and judge and will judge «the living and the dead» (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 10:42</span>).

<!-- p23 -->
In Christ the roles of Prophet, Priest, and King converge and are perfectly fulfilled. As the Mediator of the New Covenant, He bridges the gap between the holy God and sinful humanity, giving believers boldness to approach God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 4:16</span>) and providing the eternal inheritance promised in Him.

## The Apostles' Creed

<!-- p25 -->
*The Apostles' Creed* is not found in Scripture; it was created and revised on the basis of the earliest teachings of the church. A creed is an official confession of beliefs; you can think of it as an oath or pledge combined with a statement of faith. Creeds are important because they are used to unite and distinguish those who believe a certain way from those who do not. In the United States at civic events, such as city council or legislative sessions, participants often recite the Pledge of Allegiance. This pledge is widely used in the United States but is not employed in other countries because their citizens are not required to pledge allegiance to the United States. The pledge does not make a person «American», but it expresses an important essential posture for those who are citizens.

<!-- p26 -->
The first recognizable versions of the creed were written in the 4th century A.D. Over the centuries the creed changed slightly and has small variations depending on the denomination. Not every Christian denomination uses this creed, but to a large extent it is widely accepted in one form or another throughout Christianity.

<!-- p27 -->
The Apostles' Creed has been used over the centuries as a teaching and catechetical tool and sets forth the most fundamental truths of the Christian faith — the basic beliefs that must be acknowledged and confessed in order to be a Christian. In some denominations there is a formal process of catechesis through which a new believer joins that particular denomination and absorbs its teaching.

<!-- p28 -->
A person does not need to fully understand these beliefs to come to God and receive salvation; salvation is by faith, not by the results of a doctrinal examination! However, as we continue to live out this faith, we all must come to an understanding of these foundational truths.

## Brief Explanation of the Apostles' Creed

<!-- p31 -->
There are many foundational truths in the Apostles' Creed, and they are quite deep; a full explanation would take many weighty volumes. Although this chapter focuses on Jesus, many topics also touch on the other Persons of the Divine Trinity. What follows is a shortened explanation of the creed, focused only on the most central themes. Several of these topics are treated in more detail in other chapters of this book.

<!-- p32 -->
#### *I believe in God, the Father Almighty, Creator of heaven and earth.*

<!-- p33 -->
This affirms faith in God as the Almighty, Sovereign Creator and Father. The title «Father» underscores God’s loving relationship with His creation, especially with His children by faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:12</span>). As Creator, He brought the universe into being by His word, bringing order out of chaos (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 1:1-3</span>). His omnipotence is displayed in the vastness of creation, and His fatherly care in His ongoing sustaining and providence. This foundational statement establishes God as the source and Lord of all life, worthy of worship and trust.

<!-- p34 -->
#### *I believe in Jesus Christ, His only Son, our Lord.*

<!-- p35 -->
Jesus Christ is the unique Son of God, eternally begotten, not made, and sharing in the divine nature (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:1-2</span>, 14). Jesus is the «Word» from the very beginning, and He became flesh and dwelt among us. As the «Christ», He is the anointed Messiah, fulfilling the Old Testament promises of a Savior. The title «Lord» acknowledges His authority over all creation and His rightful status as Master and King (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 2:10-11</span>). He is not only Lord, but the Lord who saves (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 16:31</span>).

<!-- p36 -->
Jesus is not **merely** sent by God; He **is** part of God. Jesus abides in the Father, and the Father abides in Jesus. The Gospel of John repeatedly affirms that Jesus and the Father are one (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="30" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:30</span>), that Jesus did not act on His own (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 5:19</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="49-50" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 12:49-50</span>), and that everything He did was for the glory of His Father (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="17" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 17:4</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:13</span>), and was accomplished by the authority and power of His Father (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 8:28-29</span>).

<!-- p37 -->
#### *He was conceived by the power of the Holy Spirit and born of the Virgin Mary.*

<!-- p38 -->
This affirms the miraculous conception of Jesus by the Holy Spirit, emphasizing His divine origin. Born of the Virgin Mary, Jesus fulfills the prophecy from <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 7:14</span>, which foretold the virgin birth of the Messiah. This event marks the incarnation, when the eternal Word of God took on human flesh (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:14</span>). Being fully God and fully man, Jesus bridges the gap between God and humanity, enabling Him to be the perfect Mediator and Savior.

<!-- p39 -->
Jesus was conceived from the seed of the Holy Spirit and did not inherit the sinful nature that we, born of human seed, have. Jesus was tempted and tested in all things, but He was perfect and without sin. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 4:15</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 5:21</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:22</span>.

<!-- p40 -->
#### *He suffered under Pontius Pilate, was crucified, died, and was buried.*

<!-- p41 -->
The suffering and death of Jesus were real historical events that occurred under the Roman governor Pontius Pilate, which affirms the Christian faith in verifiable historical facts. His crucifixion fulfilled Old Testament prophecies, for example, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="53" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 53:5</span>, which speaks of the Suffering Servant who would bear the sins of many. The death of Jesus was sacrificial: He took upon Himself the punishment for sin for the sake of all humanity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:24</span>). His burial confirms the reality of His death and prepares for the significance of the resurrection.

<!-- p42 -->
#### *He descended to the dead.*

<!-- p43 -->
After His death Jesus descended into the realm of the dead, which Scripture often calls *hell* or *Sheol*. This act demonstrates His complete identification with humanity, even in death, and marks His victory over sin, death, and the devil (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 3:18-20</span>). In descending, He proclaimed His triumph and the completion of His redemptive work.

<!-- p44 -->
#### *On the third day He rose again.*

<!-- p45 -->
The resurrection of Jesus is the cornerstone of our Christian faith, confirming His divinity and the truth of His teaching. Rising from the dead, He conquered sin and death, giving believers assurance of eternal life (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 15:17-22</span>). The resurrection also fulfills Scripture, including Jesus’ own predictions (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 16:21</span>). This miraculous event turned the disciples’ despair into bold faith, becoming the foundation of the early Church’s witness.

<!-- p46 -->
#### *He ascended into heaven and is seated at the right hand of the Father.*

<!-- p47 -->
The ascension of Jesus marked His return into the Father’s presence in glory. Seated at the Father’s right hand (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:20</span>), He assumes the role of our eternal High Priest, interceding for believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 7:25</span>). His position of authority also signifies His exaltation as King, reigning over all creation and directing the Church’s mission (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:22</span>) to do greater works (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:12</span>) and to fulfill the Great Commission He gave us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:18-20</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 16:15-18</span>). The ascension assures believers of His continual presence and power through the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p48 -->
#### *He will come again to judge the living and the dead.*

<!-- p49 -->
This points to the future return of Christ, when He will bring final judgment and restoration. Jesus will judge all people, rewarding the faithful and executing just judgment upon the unrepentant (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 4:1</span>). His return will accomplish God’s ultimate purpose for creation, ushering in His eternal Kingdom of righteousness and peace. This hope urges believers to live faithfully and expectantly, knowing that their labor in the Lord is not in vain (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="58" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 15:58</span>).

<!-- p50 -->
#### *I believe in the Holy Spirit.*

<!-- p51 -->
The Holy Spirit, the third Person of the Trinity, is the presence and power of God at work in the world today. He convicts of sin, regenerates hearts, and empowers believers to live holy lives (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:8-13</span>). The Spirit unites Christians into one body, the Church, and endows them with spiritual gifts for ministry (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:4-11</span>). He is the Comforter, Advocate, and Teacher, dwelling in believers as the seal of their eternal inheritance (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>).

<!-- p52 -->
#### *The holy catholic Church, the communion of saints.*

<!-- p53 -->
The phrase "holy catholic Church" refers to the universal Body of Christ, encompassing all believers in all times and all places. The Church is set apart for God’s purposes, called to reflect His holiness and to bear the good news. "The communion of saints" underscores the unity of all Christians, living and departed, as members of the Body of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:4-6</span>). This communion transcends earthly divisions, demonstrating the shared hope and mission of believers.

<!-- p54 -->
Jesus is the head of the Church. The Church is called both the Bride of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="25-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:25-27</span>), and the Body of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 1:18</span>).

<!-- p55 -->
#### *The forgiveness of sins.*

<!-- p56 -->
This underscores the heart of the gospel: through the sacrificial death of Jesus, God offers full forgiveness of sins to all who repent and believe (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:7</span>). This forgiveness restores the broken relationship between humanity and God, removing guilt and shame. It also calls believers to forgive others, reflecting the grace they have received (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 6:14-15</span>).

<!-- p57 -->
#### *The resurrection of the body.*

<!-- p58 -->
Like Jesus, we will experience bodily resurrection at the end of the age. This resurrection will transform mortal, perishable bodies into glorified, imperishable ones, fit for eternal life (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42-44" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 15:42-44</span>). It underscores the Christian hope of renewal—not only of individuals, but of all creation—when God makes all things new (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="21" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 21:4-5</span>).

<!-- p59 -->
#### *And the life everlasting. Amen.*

<!-- p60 -->
Eternal life with God is the highest promise of the Christian faith. This life begins even now through a relationship with Christ and will be fully realized in the new heaven and the new earth, where believers will dwell with God forever (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 22:1-5</span>). This concluding statement reflects the joy and assurance of salvation, echoing the trust and hope expressed throughout the Creed.

<!-- p61 -->
## Conclusion

<!-- p62 -->
In the foundational truths expressed in the Apostles’ Creed, we see the life and work of Jesus as central to God’s plan for humanity. Each statement of the Creed reflects key aspects of our faith in God and of who Jesus is—His divinity, humanity, mission, and ultimate victory. These truths point to His unique role as Mediator of the New Covenant. As Mediator He invites us to draw near to God with confidence, trusting His perfect sacrifice and continual intercession. This calls us to worship Him as the rightful King and to follow His teaching as the supreme Prophet. Ultimately, studying Jesus leads to a deeper relationship with Him, transforming us into people who live in the light of His grace and truth.

<!-- p63 -->
Understanding who Jesus is and what He accomplished is the foundation of the Christian faith. The many names, titles, and roles of Jesus shed light on various facets of His character and mission, giving a fuller picture of His love and authority.

<!-- p64 -->
As with our study of God, Scripture is full of various names referring to Jesus. Each name represents a particular aspect of His whole character, ministry, and role. These names are given to us to reveal an aspect of Jesus.
