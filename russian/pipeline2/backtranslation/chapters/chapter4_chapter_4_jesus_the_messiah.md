# Chapter 4 - Jesus-Messiah

<!-- p1 -->
Building on our study of sin, we have concluded that sin exacts a serious price: from those against whom it is committed and who require restoration, and from those who commit the sin and must make restitution. The price for sin must be paid. In the Old Testament God established a set of sacrifices, carried out by priests, individuals, and communities, to deal with sin, resolve it, and adjudicate it justly.

<!-- p2 -->
Even with all the sin offerings, they still were not enough—not because God failed, but because people continued to choose sin. Although animal sacrifice could provide limited atonement, no amount of animal offerings could change the sinful nature of people.

<!-- p3 -->
God had a beautiful plan—the <u>Messiah</u> (in Hebrew ***Mashiach,*** pronounced *Ma-shi-akh -* **H4899**), also known as the <u>Christ</u> (in Greek ***Christos,*** pronounced *Khris-tos* - **G5547**), which means “the Anointed One”; a person consecrated for a special role (for example, a king, a priest, or a deliverer).

<!-- p4 -->
To understand who Jesus is, it is helpful to look at how the people of His time expected the Messiah. These expectations shaped how they perceived Jesus and, in many cases, why it was difficult for them to recognize in Him the fulfillment of God’s purpose.

<!-- p5 -->
## Historical Context

<!-- p6 -->
The Jewish people long awaited the coming of the Messiah. Jewish ideas about the Messiah in the period just before Jesus were formed on the basis of Old Testament prophecies, whose interpretations varied and had no universal consensus. Most Jews expected that the Messiah would be a political and military leader who would deliver Israel from foreign oppression (primarily Roman rule) and restore the Davidic kingdom. Here is an overview of common messianic expectations and how they did (or did not) align with biblical prophecy:

<!-- p7 -->
### Common Jewish Expectations of the Messiah before Jesus

<!-- p8 -->
### **1. A political and military liberator**

<!-- p9 -->
- **Belief:** The Messiah will be a king like David, leading Israel in a victorious uprising against foreign powers and restoring Israel’s independence.

<!-- p10 -->
- **Scriptures cited:** <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 9:6-7</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="23" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 23:5-6</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ZEC" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Zechariah 9:9-10</span>.

<!-- p11 -->
- **Historical context:** Jews under Roman rule were oppressed and heavily taxed, which gave rise to hopes for a Messiah who would overthrow Rome and establish a powerful earthly kingdom.

<!-- p12 -->
- **Truth:** Jesus is King, but His kingdom is not of this world (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="18" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="36" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 18:36</span>). He established a spiritual kingdom at His first coming, but will return as the victorious King to judge and reign in righteousness, as described in the book of Revelation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 19:11-16</span>).

<!-- p13 -->
### **2. Restorer of the Davidic kingdom**

<!-- p14 -->
- **Belief:** The Messiah will restore the dynasty of David and bring a golden age of prosperity, justice, and peace.

<!-- p15 -->
- **Scriptures cited:** <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2SA" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Samuel 7:12-16</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="AMO" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Amos 9:11</span>.

<!-- p16 -->
- **Interpretation:** The emphasis was often on national restoration rather than spiritual renewal.

<!-- p17 -->
- **Truth:** Jesus, as a descendant of David, fulfills this promise by establishing an eternal kingdom. Although this kingdom is presently spiritual in nature, it will culminate in a physical reign at His second coming (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="32-33" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 1:32-33</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 20:4-6</span>).

<!-- p18 -->
### **3. A prophet like Moses**

<!-- p19 -->
- **Belief:** Some Jews believed that the Messiah would be a prophet like Moses, who would speak directly with God and on His behalf.

<!-- p20 -->
- **Scripture references:** <span class="x-scripture" data-book="DEU" data-chapter="18" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Deuteronomy 18:15-19</span>.

<!-- p21 -->
- **Expectation:** Although many expected a prophetic figure, they often did not consider this role central to the Messiah, but merely an additional aspect.

<!-- p22 -->
- **Truth:** Jesus fulfilled this prophecy as the supreme Prophet, proclaiming the Word of God and directly revealing the Father (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:18</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:10</span>). His prophetic role surpasses Moses, since He is the mediator of a new and better covenant (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:6-7</span>).

<!-- p23 -->
### **4. The Ruler who will bring justice**

<!-- p24 -->
- **Conviction:** The Messiah will judge the wicked, uphold the righteous, and bring justice to the oppressed.

<!-- p25 -->
- **Scripture references:** <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 11:3-5</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="72" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 72:1-4</span>.

<!-- p26 -->
- **Interpretation:** Justice was expected to be primarily political and social rather than spiritual.

<!-- p27 -->
- **Truth:** Jesus brings final justice. At His first coming He granted spiritual redemption and justice through the cross (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:13-15</span>). At the second coming He will execute judgment on the wicked and establish perfect justice for all eternity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 20:11-15</span>).

<!-- p28 -->
### Various Messianic beliefs

<!-- p29 -->
Just as today the Christian world is divided into separate sects, denominations, or systems of doctrine, so too were the Jews of Jesus’ era divided. In the time of Jesus, four major Jewish sects shaped the religious and political landscape of Israel, each with distinctive beliefs and expectations regarding the Messiah. Understanding some background about these different groups can help provide meaningful context for studying the New Testament Scriptures.

<!-- p30 -->
**Pharisees** were the most influential among the common people, known for strict observance of the Law (the Law of the Torah) and oral traditions. They believed in the resurrection, the afterlife, and the existence of angels, emphasizing personal piety and obedience to God’s commandments. Their Messianic expectations were focused on a figure who would uphold the Law, bring justice, and restore Israel both politically and religiously.

<!-- p31 -->
**Sadducees**, by contrast, were a wealthy and politically influential group who controlled the Temple and the priesthood. They rejected oral traditions and recognized only the Torah as authoritative Scripture. The Sadducees denied the resurrection, the afterlife, and the existence of angels, focusing on maintaining their power and cooperating with Rome for political stability. As a result, they paid little attention to the question of the Messiah, since their primary concern was preserving the status quo.

<!-- p32 -->
**Essenes** were a separatist group who withdrew from mainstream Jewish society, living in isolated communities such as Qumran. They were deeply focused on ritual purity, apocalyptic expectations, and a disciplined way of life. The Essenes believed in two Messiahs: a priestly Messiah who would restore the holiness of the Temple, and a royal Messiah who would lead Israel in righteousness. This concept of a dual Messiah reflected their hope for spiritual and political renewal. The Essenes are not mentioned directly in Scripture.

<!-- p33 -->
Finally, **Zealots** were militant nationalists who sought to overthrow Roman rule through armed revolt. They rejected Roman authority and were deeply committed to restoring the sovereignty of Israel. Their Messianic expectation was tied to a warrior-Messiah, like David, who would lead them into battle against their oppressors and establish a powerful earthly kingdom.

<!-- p34 -->
Although these groups were focused on earthly expectations, Jesus’ mission revealed a far greater plan—one that addressed not only political oppression but also humanity’s spiritual bondage to sin.

<!-- p35 -->
## The Truth about the Messiah

<!-- p36 -->
### The Anointing of Jesus

<!-- p37 -->
The Pharisees’ and Zealots’ concept of the Messiah reflects a recurring pattern in human history—the pursuit of salvation by earthly means while simultaneously rejecting God’s greater plan. As we see throughout Scripture, people often turn away from God—even though He is always faithful. Even when God is faithful and receives us, He is often forgotten and rejected, leading to cycles of disobedience and decline that plagued earthly kingdoms.

<!-- p38 -->
Although many expected a political Messiah, Jesus revealed a much higher purpose of His anointing—one that met spiritual and eternal needs. If Jesus had come as a conquering military leader, as the Jews hoped and expected, it would only have been a matter of time before the kingdom fell into decline due to human disobedience.

<!-- p39 -->
Instead, Jesus established a kingdom that cannot be shaken. His mission was not founded on military power or political domination, but on a divine plan. As the Anointed One, Jesus explained what He was anointed for and how He would accomplish it:

<!-- p40 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="16-21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p41 -->
> **<u>Luke 4:16-21</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And He came to Nazareth, where He had been brought up. And, as His custom was, He went into the synagogue on the Sabbath day and stood up to read.
>
> And He was handed the book of the prophet Isaiah. And when He had opened the book, He found the place where it was written:
>
> "THE SPIRIT OF THE LORD IS UPON ME, BECAUSE HE HAS ANOINTED ME TO PREACH THE GOSPEL TO THE POOR; HE HAS SENT ME TO HEAL THE BROKENHEARTED, TO PROCLAIM LIBERTY TO THE CAPTIVES AND RECOVERY OF SIGHT TO THE BLIND, TO SET AT LIBERTY THOSE WHO ARE OPPRESSED;
>
> TO PROCLAIM THE ACCEPTABLE YEAR OF THE LORD."
>

>
> And He began to say to them, "Today this Scripture is fulfilled in your hearing."
>

<!-- p42 -->
</span>

<!-- p43 -->
Jesus was anointed — set apart by God — and sent with a divine mission, full of purpose and power.

<!-- p44 -->
Jesus proclaimed:

<!-- p45 -->
- The Good News (Gospel) of the Kingdom.

<!-- p46 -->
- Deliverance — forgiveness, remission of sins, freedom.

<!-- p47 -->
- The favorable year (season) of the Lord.

<!-- p48 -->
The people to whom He came were:

<!-- p49 -->
- Brokenhearted — emotionally and spiritually shattered.

<!-- p50 -->
- Blind — spiritually, and sometimes physically.

<!-- p51 -->
- Poor — deprived, in poverty (spiritual and/or physical).

<!-- p52 -->
- Captives — that is, ‘taken by the spear,’ this is an image of those who are held in bondage against their will.

<!-- p53 -->
Jesus transformed these broken people in the following ways:

<!-- p54 -->
- Restoration of sight

<!-- p55 -->
- Granting freedom

<!-- p56 -->
- Binding up, healing, restoration

<!-- p57 -->
The purpose of ministry and of Jesus’ ministry was both individual and global, immediate and long-term. His purpose does not have a single focus, but is part of God’s divine plan to reconcile humanity to God. After sin entered the world, it caused a separation between God and humanity. God had to drive man out of the garden and out of His presence. Throughout the Old Testament, man could be with God, but only partially. The Spirit of God was given to people extremely rarely, and for the most part people lived in isolation from God. He found a people in the Jews, but He wanted a permanent, reliable, and complete way to restore His relationship with humanity.

<!-- p58 -->
The presence of God on earth in the time of Jesus was mainly limited to the innermost part of the temple, the Holy of Holies. Here, behind the thick veil that separated this inner room from the Holy Place, God’s presence dwelt at the ark of the covenant. Man was isolated from God, and God was separated from His creation.

<!-- p59 -->
God sent Jesus to fix, restore, renew, and reconcile humanity to Himself. (2 Cor 5:17-21)

<!-- p60 -->
### The Necessary Work of Jesus

<!-- p61 -->
To fully reconcile humanity to God, the mission of the Messiah required not only preaching and healing, but also the supreme act of atonement. Jesus had to become the perfect and complete sacrifice through His death on the cross.

<!-- p62 -->
The Epistle to the Hebrews explains in detail throughout all 13 chapters how Jesus became the perfect sacrifice and was both the sin offering and the High Priest who offered it (Hebrews 3 & 4). Jesus’ priesthood is not of the Levitical line (from the tribe of Levi), but according to the order of Melchizedek, who is said to have neither beginning nor end, that is, it is not limited by human ordinances and customs (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 7:3</span>). The limitation of the Levitical priesthood is that it depends on the blood of bulls and goats (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:12-13</span>), whose sacrifice is insufficient for the constant and lasting remission of sins. From our study of sin and sacrifice we know that the punishment for the people’s sins could be postponed for a year (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:3</span>) on the Day of Atonement, but this sacrifice had to be continually renewed.

<!-- p63 -->
Jesus, dying on the cross (accounts of this in Matthew 27, Mark 15, John 19 and Luke 23), shed His blood and paid for the sins of all humanity — past, present, and future. Isaiah 53 reveals Jesus as the Suffering Servant who took upon Himself the full weight of punishment and sorrow. His once-for-all death became the final, perfect sacrifice. Jesus is the Lamb of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:29</span>), without spot or blemish (of sin); since He was perfect, whole, and holy, the shedding of His innocent blood made redemption possible. **No sacrifices are required or accepted anymore. The former were only temporary — but the sacrifice of Jesus was perfect and final.**

<!-- p64 -->
Through death and resurrection we have a new and better covenant (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:6</span>), established by the blood of the Son of God Himself; Jesus is the mediator of this covenant; He is the mediator between the parties. Through the sacrifice of Christ we receive salvation, and He is the author *‘of eternal salvation for all who obey Him*’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 5:9</span>).

<!-- p65 -->
The word «salvation» in Greek is — **Sōtēria** (*so-tay-REE-ah - **G4991)**.* Primarily it means **salvation, deliverance, or preservation**. It encompasses the act of salvation both in the spiritual sense (eternal salvation through Christ) and in physical contexts (deliverance from danger or harm). The term conveys the idea of safety, wholeness, and restoration.

<!-- p66 -->
**Salvation or deliverance:**

<!-- p67 -->
- Refers to God’s deliverance of humanity from sin and its consequences through Jesus Christ.

<!-- p68 -->
- Used in the context of eternal salvation (justification, sanctification, and glorification).

<!-- p69 -->
**Preservation:**

<!-- p70 -->
- Implies remaining in safety, protection, or being kept from danger.

<!-- p71 -->
**Wholeness or healing:**

<!-- p72 -->
- Includes restoration to a state of fullness or soundness.

<!-- p73 -->
The sacrifice of Jesus paid the price for our forgiveness, opened for us the way to a relationship with God, and paved the road to abiding with Him, dependent only on our obedience, not on our impeccability or our ability to refrain from sin.

<!-- p74 -->
When we receive Jesus by faith (discussed later), we receive His salvation. This salvation connects us with God and writes our name in the ***Lamb’s Book of Life*** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 20:11-15</span>). The Gospel of John says:

<!-- p75 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p76 -->
> **<u>John 3:16</u>** (NKJV)
>
> For God so loved the world that He gave His only begotten Son, that whoever believes in Him should not perish but have everlasting life.
>

<!-- p77 -->
</span>

<!-- p78 -->
To perish means to be ruined - like fruit that spoils before it can be enjoyed. It is a life that does not attain what it was created for. Jesus came to give us unceasing, or eternal, life and to keep us from perishing. Eternal life is not life that begins after death; it is life that begins the moment we receive Jesus as our salvation, when we are born again.

### Born Again

<!-- p80 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p81 -->
> **<u>2 Corinthians 5:17</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation; the old has passed away; behold, everything has become new.
>

<!-- p82 -->
</span>

<!-- p83 -->
When we receive Jesus, we become ‘born again’. Jesus uses this phrase to describe the moment of transition when we are born of the Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:1-15</span>). This happens through faith — choosing to trust Him completely, like entering a marriage covenant, both with the heart and with words.

<!-- p84 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="9-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p85 -->
> **<u>Romans 10:9-10</u>** (NKJV)
>
> for if you confess with your mouth Jesus as Lord and believe in your heart that God raised Him from the dead, you will be saved.
>
> For with the heart one believes unto righteousness, and with the mouth confession is made unto salvation.
>

<!-- p86 -->
</span>

<!-- p87 -->
When we were born on earth (born of flesh and water), much happened: we took our first breaths and were recognized as alive, our parents received us, we were given a name, we became heirs, we received nourishment and protection, and we continued to grow.

<!-- p88 -->
When we place our trust in Jesus and receive Him, we are born again — not of the flesh, but of the Spirit. We come alive spiritually! As with physical birth, spiritual birth is accompanied by many things:

<!-- p89 -->
1.  When we received Jesus, God wiped away our sins and declared us forgiven (**justified**). He gave us a fresh start. The dividing wall between Him and us has been torn down. (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>)

<!-- p90 -->
2.  We were spiritually **reborn** and received the indwelling of the Holy Spirit within us. (1 Corinthians 6:19).

<!-- p91 -->
3.  We were **adopted** into God’s family and became heirs. (Ephesians 1:5)

<!-- p92 -->
4.  We receive a source of spiritual nourishment and eternal protection. (1 Peter 2:2)
