# Chapter 10 - Sanctified-prepared for God's purpose

<!-- p1 -->
In the previous two chapters we discussed several important topics. We explored the role and ministry of the Holy Spirit, as well as the role and ministry of the church.

<!-- p2 -->
We have our own role in the church, and, although we know that we need training, we also need cleansing. To truly be prepared for God's purposes, we must be sanctified.

<!-- p3 -->
The definition of **sanctification** — to cleanse something for a special purpose. This is closely related to the term **dedication**, which means to set something apart for a special purpose.

<!-- p4 -->
Imagine a master sculptor preparing to work with a large block of marble. The master envisions what this raw and rough marble can be turned into through his careful craftsmanship. He has a very specific design in mind, he orders his assistants to set aside this marble block for a special design (**dedication**). As he refines and works the marble, giving it texture, depth, and shape, he cleanses it (**sanctifies**) for this special design. The act of dedicating the marble to his special project was quick, whereas the process of bringing the marble to its final appearance requires considerable patience.

<!-- p5 -->
We were set apart, dedicated to God at salvation. Through the cleansing of our souls by the Spirit we are transformed. This process of cleansing is what sanctifies us.

<!-- p6 -->
When we accepted Jesus through salvation, we were born again. We were justified, our sins forgiven. Our spirit was reborn, we came alive in Christ. We were adopted into the family of God and became the literal temple of the Holy Spirit. Such an amazing transformation, that Scripture says: “*joy before the angels of God over one sinner who repents.” (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 15:10</span>).

<!-- p7 -->
Acceptance of Jesus through salvation (**justification**) saves our spirit, cleanses us and makes us acceptable to God, able to receive His Holy Spirit. However, despite such great salvation, **our souls** (our mind, will, and emotions) still **need work**!

<!-- p8 -->
Was the work of salvation unfinished? No! The work of salvation is effective, but must continue in the life of the believer through the renewal accomplished by the Holy Spirit. This process of renewal and transformation is the work of **sanctification.**

<!-- p9 -->
We know that after conversion there remains work, because our painful memories have not been resolved, many of our beliefs remained the same, and many of our habits or rituals have not disappeared. Moreover, conversion did not separate our mind from its fleshly (carnal) nature.

<!-- p10 -->
The word «carnal» comes from a Latin root meaning “flesh” or “meat”. The fleshly nature is present in each of us from birth; like an animal's instincts, it exists to help us survive. This fleshly nature operates, being self-focused, self-oriented, egocentric. Children are natural examples of the fleshly nature. Despite their innocence, their nature is primarily focused on their own desires, drives, needs; and «watch out!», if you do not satisfy its every expectation.

<!-- p11 -->
Part of growing up is maturing, moving beyond our childish ways, but, when we are pressed, we very easily return to a self-oriented pattern of thinking and living. As James says:

<!-- p12 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p13 -->
> **<u>James 4:1</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Where do wars and quarrels come from among you? Is it not from this — from your pleasures, warring in your members?
>

<!-- p14 -->
</span>

<!-- p15 -->
Paul explains this carnal nature in more detail in his epistle to the Romans (chapters 6 - 8). He says that this carnal nature holds him in what he hates and impels him to do evil. He describes this inner conflict as a war between his man, shaped by man, and his God-created spirit.

<!-- p16 -->
This conflict between the flesh and the spirit exists in each of us, and it is our task — to do something about it. If we do not resist our carnal nature, our souls will remain entangled in patterns of spiritual decay, even if our spirits are redeemed.

<!-- p17 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p18 -->
> **<u>Romans 8:6-8</u>** (NKJV)
>
> For carnal thoughts — are death, and spiritual thoughts — life and peace.
>
> Because the carnal mind is enmity against God; for it does not submit to the law of God, nor indeed can.
>
> So then those who live according to the flesh cannot please God.
>

<!-- p19 -->
</span>

<!-- p20 -->
These words of Paul are both a warning and a promise to us. Although we are born again, we still have a fleshly mind that needs to be brought into obedience to the knowledge of Christ. When we do this, we walk in God’s life and peace.

<!-- p21 -->
Take heart, we have hope! We must walk in the Spirit and be led by the Spirit of God. By doing so, we will not walk according to the flesh.

<!-- p22 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="1,13,14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p23 -->
> **<u>Romans 8:1,13,14</u>** (NKJV)
>
> There is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus, who do not walk according to the flesh, but according to the Spirit.
>
> For if you live according to the flesh you will die; but if by the Spirit you put to death the deeds of the body, you will live.
>
> For as many as are led by the Spirit of God, these are sons of God.
>

<!-- p24 -->
</span>

<!-- p25 -->
How do we learn to walk in the Spirit? Is it just a conscious choice? We learn to walk in the Spirit the same way we learn to walk in the natural. We are prompted to move and grow, so we struggle, strive, crawl, and wobble until we can stand on our own feet. Our earthly parents cannot walk for us, but they can support, encourage, help, and facilitate our walking.

<!-- p26 -->
In the same way, God cannot live our faith for us. He will teach us, but we need to put in the effort. As Paul said to the Philippians:

<!-- p27 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="12-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p28 -->
> **<u>Philippians 2:12-13</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Therefore, my beloved, as you have always obeyed, not as in my presence only, but now much more in my absence, work out your own salvation with fear and trembling;
>
> for it is God who works in you both to will and to do for His good pleasure.
>

<!-- p29 -->
</span>

<!-- p30 -->
We must work out our salvation. This does not mean that we will climb onto a wooden cross and be physically crucified, but we do need to crucify our fleshly nature (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:24</span>). God promises that He will work in us and help us, but we must make the decision to walk this path and hold to it.

<!-- p31 -->
It is true to say that ‘we’ deal with our fleshly nature, but it is equally true to say that ‘God’ deals with it. The work of the cross (justification) was hard for Jesus—He had to give His life for ours—but easy for us; it is a free gift. The work of the Spirit (sanctification) is easy for God—He walks in perfect truth and desires to dwell with us—but hard for us, because we must crucify our old nature, and our nature does not want to be crucified at all.

<!-- p32 -->
This process is collaborative. God helps, assists, guides, leads, and corrects. We listen, obey, follow, and respond. The result is growth and transformation. This is the pruning that Jesus said God would accomplish in us. Jesus is the vine, His Father is the vinedresser, and we are the branches:

<!-- p33 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p34 -->
> **<u>John 15:1-4</u>** (NKJV)
>
> "I am the true vine, and My Father is the vinedresser.
>
> Every branch in Me that does not bear fruit He takes away; and every branch that bears fruit He prunes, that it may bear more fruit.
>
> You are already clean because of the word which I have spoken to you.
>
> Abide in Me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself, unless it abides in the vine, neither can you, unless you abide in Me.
>

<!-- p35 -->
</span>

<!-- p36 -->
Pruning is extremely beneficial for a plant. Recalling our chapter on repentance, we are called to turn away from dead works (those that no longer bear fruit) and turn to God. As a result of this turning away from the dead places in our lives and turning to God, the great gardener of our lives is able to prune the branches, improve growth, and produce greater spiritual fruit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:2</span>).

<!-- p37 -->
Although pruning can feel painful, it reflects God’s loving care as He shapes us as vessels for His purposes (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 12:11</span>). Some may experience a sense of loss, even fear, as they go through the pruning process. However, God is gentle, and a bruised reed He will not break (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="42" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 42:3</span>). Grapevines are pruned during dormancy to minimize stress on the plant. A gardener who cares about growth cuts away the old, woody growth that no longer bears fruit as it once did; this allows the vine to direct its energy to those branches with the greatest potential for good fruit.

<!-- p38 -->
Like a gardener who prunes branches to direct growth, God may allow seasons of correction and discipline to purify our faith and character, but His work is patient and kind. God prunes those who abide in Him. Our focus should be more on abiding in Christ and less on the pruning work itself.

<!-- p39 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 12:11</span> reminds us that pruning "*yields the peaceable fruit of righteousness*" to those who have been trained by it.

<!-- p40 -->
How do we learn righteousness? By being transformed through the renewing of our mind.

<!-- p41 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p42 -->
> **<u>Romans 12:1-2</u>** (NKJV)
>
> I beseech you therefore, brothers, by the mercies of God, to present your bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable to God, which is your reasonable service.
>
> And do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind, that you may discern what is the good, acceptable, and perfect will of God.
>

<!-- p43 -->
</span>

<!-- p44 -->
Paul sets out two simple requirements for transformation:

<!-- p45 -->
<u>1. Present your bodies to God as a living sacrifice</u>. A living sacrifice does not physically die at the time of the offering, but since a living sacrifice cannot die, you must continually bring it to the altar!

<!-- p46 -->
<u>2. Renew your mind!</u> Renewal, from the Greek word: **Anakainōsis** (*An-ak-ah'-ee-no-sis -* **G342**), means renewal or restoration, with an emphasis on an ongoing process of inner change and growth.

<!-- p47 -->
This renewal leads to transformation. Transformation is based on the Greek word: **Metamorphoō** (*met-am-or-fo'-o -* **G3339**), which means to change into another form; to transfigure or transform, emphasizing a complete change. The English word metamorphosis comes from Metamorphoō, describing the process caterpillars go through to become butterflies.

<!-- p48 -->
God wants to radically transform our mind and our way of thinking. His desire is that we not conform to the ways of the world, but that we know what the good (beneficial), acceptable (pleasing), and perfect (complete & lacking nothing) will of God is.

<!-- p49 -->
The requirements for transformation really are that simple, **though it does require discipline**!

<!-- p50 -->
Our battle against the flesh will not be won by our flesh, but by God's armament for the battle for the mind. His words (the sword of truth) are powerful to correct every imagination, every proud thought, and to take into custody (captive) every thought that resists obedience to God.

<!-- p51 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="3-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p52 -->
> **<u>2 Corinthians 10:3-6</u>** (NKJV)
>
> For though we live in the flesh, we do not war according to the flesh,
>
> for the weapons of our warfare are not carnal but mighty in God for pulling down strongholds,
>
> casting down arguments and every high thing that exalts itself against the knowledge of God, and bringing every thought into captivity to the obedience of Christ,
>
> and being ready to punish all disobedience when your obedience is fulfilled.
>

<!-- p53 -->
</span>

<!-- p54 -->
When the authorities arrest someone, they put shackles on them and place them in a jail cell. If that person escapes custody, they don't turn a blind eye; they mobilize incredible resources and will not rest until that person is brought back under control. We must act with the same zeal in the battle for our mind!

<!-- p55 -->
We are renewed through obedience and the experience of the truth. Jesus said that if we abide in His words, we will be His disciples, we will experience (<u>know</u>) the truth, and it will make us free (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-32" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 8:31-32</span>)!

<!-- p56 -->
Experiencing the truth is different from knowing the truth. "Experience" is the Greek word **Ginōskō** (Ghin-oce'-ko **- G1097**); it means to know through personal experience or intimate understanding; it implies progressive learning.

<!-- p57 -->
It is not our academic knowledge of the truth that transforms us and renews our mind, but our personal experience of the truth. We are sanctified by His truth! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="17" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 17:17</span>).

## How to renew your mind

<!-- p59 -->
Renewing our mind is not a one-size-fits-all process - it depends on which part of the mind we are talking about! God created our mind with incredible complexity, including several different memory systems, each serving a specific purpose. Some operate automatically in the background, while others require focused, intentional thought. These systems are God's gifts to help us survive, grow, and thrive, but they can also lead us astray if misused or left unrenewed. Understanding how these systems work gives us insight into how to cooperate with God's process of transformation.

<!-- p60 -->
In the next section, I will describe each of the memory systems, their purpose and function, some practical steps for renewing this aspect of your mind, as well as resources for continued practice and study.

<!-- p61 -->
### **Memory Systems and How to Renew Them**

<!-- p62 -->
#### **1. Explicit Memory (Declarative Memory)**

<!-- p63 -->
**Purpose:** Stores facts, events, and experiences that we can consciously recall. It is divided into two types-**semantic** and **episodic** memory.

<!-- p64 -->
**Semantic Memory - Facts and knowledge.** Semantic memory is the place where we store facts, definitions, and general knowledge—things like historical dates, Bible verses, and practical information. It helps us predict outcomes, make decisions, and avoid danger (e.g., recognizing poisonous plants). While it gives us stability and order, it can also lead to rigidity- an overreliance on rules instead of adapting to unexpected situations.

<!-- p65 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p66 -->
- **Study Scripture** to shape our worldview (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="119" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="105" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 119:105</span>).

<!-- p67 -->
- **Memorize truths** and doctrines to stand firm in the faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 3:16</span>).

<!-- p68 -->
- **Read Christian books** such as *"Renewing the Mind" by Casey Treat* or *"Battlefield of the Mind" by Joyce Meyer* to develop godly patterns of thinking.

<!-- p69 -->
**Episodic Memory - Personal experience.** Episodic memory stores our personal experience, tying it to specific times and places- like a wedding day or a traumatic event. These memories provide context, helping us learn from the past and build relationships. However, episodic memory is prone to emotional bias and distortion. Past trauma can create frightening associations, making it hard to move forward.

<!-- p70 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p71 -->
- **Reflect and confess** to bring hidden pain into God's light (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 1:9</span>).

<!-- p72 -->
- **Reframe past experiences** through the lens of God's grace (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:28</span>).

<!-- p73 -->
- **Seek inner healing** through prayer and counseling (*“Your Pain Is Showing” by Larry F. Low, "The Bondage Breaker" by Neil T. Anderson*, *"Emotionally Healthy Spirituality" by Peter Scazzero*).

<!-- p74 -->
#### **2. Implicit Memory (Nondeclarative Memory)**

<!-- p75 -->
**Purpose:** Unconscious memory used for habits, skills, and emotional associations. It operates automatically, helping us respond quickly to situations. It is divided into **procedural memory** and **emotional conditioning**.

<!-- p76 -->
**Procedural Memory - Skills and habits.** Procedural memory allows us to perform tasks without thinking- like driving, typing, or playing an instrument. It’s efficient, saving our mental energy and time. However, harmful habits and negative patterns can also become deeply ingrained and be hard to break.

<!-- p77 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p78 -->
- **Practice spiritual disciplines** such as prayer and fasting to develop godly habits (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 5:14</span>).

<!-- p79 -->
- **Train yourself in godliness** through repetition and obedience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 4:7</span>).

<!-- p80 -->
- **Read books such as** *"Atomic Habits" by James Clear* (practical) and *"Spiritual Disciplines for the Christian Life" by Donald Whitney* (biblical), to develop habits that glorify God.

<!-- p81 -->
**Emotional conditioning — emotional associations.** Emotional conditioning links sensory cues (e.g., smells or sounds) with feelings, triggering responses even before we consciously process them. This system protects us but can also cause irrational fears or emotional reactions when past experiences are negative.

<!-- p82 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p83 -->
- **Pray for healing** from past wounds (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="41" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 41:10</span>).

<!-- p84 -->
- **Practice forgiveness** and release bitterness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-32" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:31-32</span>).

<!-- p85 -->
- **Replace fear with God’s promises** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 1:7</span>).

<!-- p86 -->
- **Seek guidance from books such as** *“Your Pain Is Seen” by Larry F. Low, "The Bondage Breaker" by Neil T. Anderson*, “*Soul Care" by Rob Reimer* to overcome emotional triggers.

<!-- p87 -->
#### **3. Working Memory (Short-Term Memory)**

<!-- p88 -->
**Purpose:** Provides temporary storage for problem-solving and decision-making. It helps us focus, briefly hold information, and process tasks—for example, remembering instructions or making quick decisions. Though it is powerful, it is also limited, prone to distractions, and easily overloaded, especially under stress.

<!-- p89 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p90 -->
- **Focus on and meditate** on God’s Word daily (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 1:2</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
- **Renew your thoughts** through intentional thinking (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:2</span>).

<!-- p92 -->
- **Practice mindfulness** and simplify mental noise (*"Habits of Grace" by David Mathis*).

<!-- p93 -->
- **Read** *"Celebration of Discipline" by Richard Foster* to hone focus through spiritual disciplines.

<!-- p94 -->
## Conclusion

<!-- p95 -->
Renewing the mind is not a quick fix but a gradual, lifelong transformation. Each memory system plays a role in shaping how we think, feel, and act. By intentionally partnering with the Holy Spirit, applying Scripture, and developing godly habits, we can train our minds to align with God’s truth. Whether working through past wounds, mastering new habits, or maintaining focus in the present moment, God has provided both **spiritual tools** and **practical wisdom** to help us walk in renewal and freedom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 10:5</span>).

<!-- p96 -->
Our world is full of troubles, sorrows, and trials. Through disciplined mind renewal, our souls are transformed, and we can live a life of freedom for God, filled with the fruit of His Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-25</span>).
