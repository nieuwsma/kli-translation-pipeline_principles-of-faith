# Chapter 11 - Baptisms-water and Spirit

<!-- p1 -->
Baptism in our modern time, separated from Jewish roots, is usually understood only as water baptism and baptism with the Holy Spirit (Pentecost).

<!-- p2 -->
The Epistle to the Hebrews was written to a Jewish audience. The vast majority of Christians in our time do not have Jewish roots or heritage. Without Jewish roots and culture, many of us lack the cultural context that first-century Messianic Jews shared (those Jewish believers who confess Jesus). Since we did not grow up in the Jewish tradition, we usually know little about the feasts, fasts, prayers, washings, rites, and rituals that were familiar to Jewish believers.

<!-- p3 -->
The word «baptisms» or «washings» in Greek: **Baptismos** (bap-tis-mos’ - **G909**) - the act of immersion or washing. Refers to ceremonial washings or ritual purification. It can denote immersion, rites of purification, or washings practiced in Jewish and Christian contexts.

Baptisms—one of the foundational teachings mentioned in the Epistle to the Hebrews:

## Old Testament Washings - historical context

<!-- p9 -->
In the Old Testament, ceremonial washings played a central role in maintaining ritual purity and symbolized holiness before God. These washings were prescribed by the Law of Moses and applied both to the priests who served in the Tabernacle and to the people of Israel. For example, the priests had to wash their hands and feet at the bronze laver before entering the Tabernacle or approaching the altar, which symbolized their readiness to serve in the presence of a holy God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="30" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 30:18-21</span>). Similarly, the rites for cleansing people from defilement—for example, when touching a dead body or in cases of skin diseases—involved washing with water as a sign of the restoration of purity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LEV" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Leviticus 14:8</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="NUM" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Numbers 19:17-19</span>). These actions served as an outward expression of an inward desire for holiness, emphasizing the importance of being set apart for God.

<!-- p10 -->
Ceremonial washings in the Old Testament required regular repetition to maintain ritual purity. Unlike permanent solutions, these rites provided only temporary external cleansing, leaving the deeper problem of sin unresolved. The author of Hebrews emphasizes this limitation, explaining that these practices were “symbolic for the present time” and could not cleanse the conscience of the worshiper (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:9-10</span>). These washings served as constant reminders of human imperfection and the need for something greater—an ultimate cleansing that could purify not only the body but also the heart and soul.

<!-- p11 -->
The ceremonial washings of the Old Testament find their fulfillment in Christ, who provides complete and eternal cleansing through His sacrifice. Unlike the temporary rites of the law, the blood of Christ purifies fully, cleansing not only the flesh but also the conscience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:13-14</span>). Through His death and resurrection, Jesus accomplished what the Old Testament rites only foreshadowed—the true reconciliation with God. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:22</span> calls believers “to draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, having our hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience and our bodies washed with pure water.” This language ties the work of Christ to the imagery of Old Testament washings while showing their incomparably greater effectiveness. His sacrifice does not merely cover sin but completely removes it (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 1:7</span>). Thus, the ceremonial washings served as a shadow, pointing to the deeper cleansing that only Christ could give, meeting humanity’s need for holiness through divine intervention.

<!-- p12 -->
To underscore this, Christians are not obligated to perform any ceremonial washings. Washings did not bring salvation under the old covenant and do not bring salvation under the new covenant. A ritual washing will not elevate our standing before God or bring us favor. Paul sharply condemns the Judaizers in Galatia (see Galatians), who sought to mix obligatory requirements of the Jewish context as necessary for salvation (for example, circumcision). Salvation is by faith in Jesus alone!

<!-- p13 -->
## John’s Baptism

<!-- p14 -->
The New Testament canon mentions several baptisms. We have already discussed the baptisms and washings of the Jewish tradition.

<!-- p15 -->
The ministry of John the Baptist was focused on preparing the way for the coming of Jesus Christ. His baptism, described as a baptism of repentance for the forgiveness of sins (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 1:4</span>), called people to confess their sins, turn their hearts to God, and prepare for the coming of the Messiah. It served as an outward sign of inward repentance, symbolizing a desire for spiritual cleansing and readiness for the Kingdom of God. However, John’s baptism was temporary and preparatory—it pointed forward to the greater work that Christ would accomplish. Although John’s baptism focused on repentance, it did not provide full forgiveness or spiritual regeneration, since it lacked the indwelling and empowering of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:11-12</span>). Instead, John openly proclaimed that One was coming who was greater than he, and who would baptize not only with water but with the Holy Spirit and fire (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 3:16</span>).

<!-- p16 -->
John’s baptism was fulfilled and surpassed through the death, burial, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. Christian baptism, instituted by Jesus and practiced by the apostles, signifies a deeper reality—union with Christ, death to sin, and resurrection to new life (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 6:3-4</span>). Unlike John’s baptism, Christian baptism is directly linked to faith in Jesus’ atoning sacrifice and to the indwelling of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:38</span>). The coming of the Holy Spirit at Pentecost marked the new covenant and the beginning of life in the power of the Spirit, which John’s baptism could not provide. For this reason, John’s baptism is no longer practiced, as it was replaced by the baptism commanded by Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19</span>), which includes repentance, faith, and the sealing of believers with the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>).

<!-- p17 -->
## The Baptism of Christ

<!-- p18 -->
The baptism of Christ became a powerful example for His followers and laid the foundation for the Christian practice of water baptism. Although Jesus was without sin, He received baptism from John in order “to fulfill all righteousness” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:15</span>). In this way He identified Himself with humanity’s need for repentance and pointed ahead to His own death, burial, and resurrection. During His baptism the Holy Spirit descended like a dove, and the Father proclaimed: «This is My Beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased» (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:16-17</span>). This moment marked the beginning of Jesus’ public ministry and revealed the unity of the Father, the Son, and the Spirit, foreshadowing the spiritual transformation that believers would experience through faith in Christ.

<!-- p19 -->
Later Jesus commanded His followers to be baptized, commissioning them: “Go and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19</span>). Water baptism, instituted by Christ, became an **act of obedience** and a **public confession of faith** for believers. It represents their identification with the death, burial, and resurrection of Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 6:3-4</span>). Immersion in water symbolizes dying to sin, and coming up from the water illustrates rising to new life in Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:12</span>). However, it is important to clarify that this outward act is not the source of salvation, but a **visible sign** of the **inward work of grace** already accomplished by the blood of Jesus.

<!-- p20 -->
Water baptism is a **symbol** of the cleansing power of Christ’s blood and renewal by the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:26</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>). It signifies repentance, faith, and a commitment to follow Jesus. It is an outward testimony of an inner transformation-a way for believers to publicly declare their allegiance to Christ and their participation in the Body of Christ, the Church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:41</span>). Though it is commanded by Jesus and shown in the early Church, it does not **save** a person. Salvation is granted by grace through faith alone (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>), and true cleansing occurs spiritually through the blood of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 1:5</span>). Water baptism is a response to this salvation, an act of obedience, not a requirement for it.

<!-- p21 -->
In the New Testament water baptism is invariably connected with **repentance and faith** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:38</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 16:16</span>), and both require a personal decision to follow Christ-which infants are not capable of making. Baptism symbolizes the believer’s conscious identification with the death, burial, and resurrection of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 6:3-4</span>), as well as his commitment to live in obedience to Him. Some denominations (for example, Catholics, Lutherans) practice infant baptism as a covenant sign similar to circumcision; however, since infants cannot express personal faith or repentance, the practice of **infant baptism** does not align with the biblical model of **believers’ baptism**. Instead many churches hold **dedication ceremonies** for infants, following the example of Hannah, who dedicated Samuel to the Lord (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1SA" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Samuel 1:27-28</span>), and Mary and Joseph, who presented Jesus in the temple (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 2:22</span>). Dedication is not a replacement for baptism, but represents the parents’ commitment to raise the child in the faith, teaching him the Word of God and modeling a godly life until the child is old enough to make his own decision to follow Christ and be baptized.

<!-- p22 -->
Unlike the **ritual washings** of the Old Testament, which required repeated rituals for cleansing, Christian baptism is performed once and reflects the completed action of grace. Old Testament washings symbolized external cleansing and foreshadowed a deeper spiritual cleansing that comes through Christ. By contrast, New Testament baptism symbolizes the inner renewal and regeneration accomplished by the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>). It points to the believer’s participation in the new covenant, where cleansing from sin is continual and based on the finished work of Christ.

<!-- p23 -->
Water baptism is closely connected with the **promise of the Holy Spirit**. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:38</span> Peter calls people to repent, be baptized, and receive the gift of the Holy Spirit. While water baptism publicly testifies to the believer’s repentance and faith, the true baptism happens within, when the Holy Spirit washes, regenerates, and seals the believer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:13</span>). This spiritual washing makes us members of the Body of Christ and empowers us to live a holy life. The **role of the Holy Spirit** in baptism reminds us that salvation is accomplished not through rituals, but through the transforming power of God.

<!-- p24 -->
It is important to distinguish between the **symbolic act of water baptism** and the spiritual reality of salvation through the blood of Christ. The Bible clearly teaches that salvation is based on faith in Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 10:9-10</span>), not on works, including baptism (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>). However, water baptism is an **act of obedience** that publicly identifies the believer with Christ and the Church. It demonstrates an outward commitment to live as a disciple of Jesus, but does not add to the work of salvation and does not complete it.

<!-- p25 -->
The water baptism of Christ became an example for believers, showing humility, obedience, and preparation for ministry. For Christians today, water baptism serves as a public confession of faith, symbolizing death to sin and resurrection to new life. Though it is a command of Jesus and an important step of obedience, it is not required for salvation. True cleansing occurs through faith in the blood of Jesus, and the power for the Christian life comes through the work of the Holy Spirit in the believer. Baptism unites us with Christ and His Church, reminding us of the inner transformation and renewal that God has already accomplished.

<!-- p26 -->
Having established water baptism as an outward sign of faith, we now turn to baptism with the Holy Spirit, which empowers believers for ministry and work.

<!-- p27 -->
## Baptism with the Holy Spirit

<!-- p28 -->
Baptism in the Holy Spirit is a profound and transforming experience, promised by God for empowering believers for ministry, work, and holy living. Every believer receives the Holy Spirit when he is born again-that is how we become spiritually alive. But later we also must receive baptism in the Holy Spirit, which endows us with boldness, spiritual gifts, and effectiveness in ministry.

<!-- p29 -->
Water baptism is a public confession of faith and symbolizes cleansing from sin through the blood of Christ, Spirit baptism represents the empowering presence of God in the life of the believer. Together these two kinds of baptism form complementary sides of the Christian journey-one as an outward sign of obedience, and the other as an inner source of power and renewal. Baptism with the Holy Spirit — this is the Spirit upon us, not within. At salvation all believers receive the Spirit within themselves, but when the Spirit of God comes upon them, they receive power and boldness for ministry.

<!-- p30 -->
John the Baptist foretold the baptism in the Holy Spirit, who proclaimed,

> **<u>Matthew 3:11</u>** (NKJV)
>
> I baptize you with water unto repentance, but He who is coming after me is mightier than I, and I am not worthy to carry His sandals. He will baptize you with the Holy Spirit and fire.

<!-- p33 -->
</span>

<!-- p34 -->
This promise pointed to the greater work that Jesus would accomplish after His resurrection-send the Holy Spirit to dwell within and empower His followers. Jesus confirmed this when He instructed His disciples to wait for the gift of the Spirit, explaining that they would receive power to be His witnesses (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>). The fulfillment of this promise occurred on the Day of Pentecost, when the disciples were filled with the Holy Spirit, which was manifested in speaking in tongues, prophesying, and boldness to proclaim the Gospel (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:1-4</span>).

<!-- p35 -->
This outpouring marked the beginning of the **Church age** and showed that the power of the Spirit is now available to all believers. This promise was not limited to the apostles, but extends to all who will believe, as Peter proclaimed: “The promise is for you and for your children and for all who are far off-for all whom the Lord our God will call” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="39" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:39</span>).

### The Spirit within versus the Spirit upon us

<!-- p37 -->
When someone places his faith in Jesus, the Holy Spirit gives him new life within. This is what it means to be born again (**born again**). At the moment of faith the Holy Spirit dwells **INSIDE** the believer, making him spiritually alive and adopting him into God’s family (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:5-6</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>). This is the new birth, or being born again, as Jesus described in John 3.

<!-- p38 -->
Biblical foundation:

<!-- p39 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>* - “He saved us by the washing of regeneration and renewing of the Holy Spirit.”

<!-- p40 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>* - The Holy Spirit seals believers as a **guarantee** of their inheritance.

<!-- p41 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:9</span>* - “Who does not have the Spirit of Christ is not His.”

<!-- p42 -->
This work of regeneration is universal for all believers at the moment of salvation and establishes the permanent indwelling of the Spirit. It does not depend on any external actions, such as water baptism, and guarantees salvation by grace through faith.

<!-- p43 -->
Conversely, baptism in the Holy Spirit is a separate and subsequent experience that empowers believers for ministry and witness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>). While regeneration concerns new life and identity, Spirit baptism is focused on empowerment coming UPON, and the release of spiritual gifts. This baptism is often accompanied by signs and manifestations of the gifts of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p44 -->
Biblical basis:

<!-- p45 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p46 -->

>
> But you will receive power when the Holy Spirit comes upon you; and you will be My witnesses in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and even to the ends of the earth."

<!-- p47 -->
</span>

<!-- p48 -->

<!-- p49 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 19:6</span>* - The disciples in Ephesus, already believers, received the Holy Spirit when Paul laid his hands on them.

<!-- p50 -->
Baptism with the Holy Spirit does not replace regeneration, but builds on it, equipping the believer for spiritual ministry and boldness in service. Baptism with the Holy Spirit is a free gift to the saved, not a requirement for salvation. It is not mandatory that someone manifest a <u>specific</u> gift of the Holy Spirit to prove their ‘baptism’ with the Holy Spirit, however one should expect that baptism with the Holy Spirit is manifested today just as in the book of Acts, with signs and boldness! Some mistakenly think that if a person does not speak in tongues, then he is not saved or that he does not have baptism with the Holy Spirit. Not everyone will receive every gift or manifestation of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29-31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:29-31</span>).

## How to Receive the Baptism with the Holy Spirit

<!-- p52 -->
Baptism with the Holy Spirit is described in Scripture as a gift promised to believers and received by faith. It is separate from salvation and intended primarily for empowerment and witness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>). Baptism with the Holy Spirit is a gift of empowerment for believers, promised by Jesus and fulfilled on the Day of Pentecost. It is received through repentance, faith, and asking God. While the laying on of hands is often used as a method, ultimately it is God who baptizes with the Spirit.

<!-- p53 -->
Scripture presents speaking in tongues and prophecy as a common sign, but the purpose of this baptism is much broader - the provision of boldness, power, and spiritual gifts for ministry. Every believer is called to seek this experience as part of their spiritual journey, trusting that God will equip them for His work.

<!-- p54 -->
The Bible sets out several key principles of how this baptism is received:

<!-- p55 -->
### **1. Baptism with the Holy Spirit — is a free gift from God**

<!-- p56 -->
Baptism with the Holy Spirit is not something believers can earn - it is a free gift given by God. God wants to give good gifts to His children and is eager to give the Holy Spirit to those who ask Him with faith and a desire to receive empowerment.

###  **2. Ask, seek, and receive by faith**

<!-- p64 -->
Scripture emphasizes that the baptism in the Holy Spirit is received through asking in prayer and faith-filled expectation. Faith, combined with a readiness to ask and receive, — is the key to experiencing baptism in the Spirit. If you are not sure whether God wants to give you the baptism in the Holy Spirit, ask Him about it, He — a good Father! When He prompts you, you may feel a tangible presence of God in your body that you have never experienced before. It is okay if you do not feel this! God — a great God, and He does not need to be boxed into a single perfect formula. If you feel that God is touching your heart, yield to Him!

<!-- p66 -->

## Gifts of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p79 -->
When the Holy Spirit was poured out, and people were baptized in the Holy Spirit, this was often accompanied by manifestations of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p80 -->
In the New Testament there are several lists of gifts:

<!-- p81 -->
- Motivational gifts given by God: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:6-8</span>

<!-- p82 -->
- Discussed in the chapter on the Church.

<!-- p83 -->
- Fivefold ministry gifts given by Jesus: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-13</span>

<!-- p84 -->
- Discussed in the chapter on the church.

<!-- p85 -->
- Manifestation gifts given by the Holy Spirit: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span>

<!-- p86 -->
- Discussed here.

<!-- p87 -->
Each of these sets of gifts is given for different purposes and operates differently, but together they point to the **diversity** and **unity** of the gifts in the **Body of Christ**. Each gift has a unique role in fulfilling God’s purposes.

<!-- p88 -->
### Purposes & Operations of the Gifts

<!-- p89 -->
The primary purpose of the gifts of the Holy Spirit is to build up the Body of Christ and glorify God:

<!-- p90 -->
- **Edification** - strengthening and encouraging believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
- **Unity** - fostering harmony and cooperation in the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:12-13</span>).

<!-- p92 -->
- **Evangelism** - demonstrating God’s power as a witness to unbelievers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="43" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:43</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:6-7</span>).

<!-- p93 -->
- **Service** - meeting the needs of others through acts of mercy and generosity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:6-8</span>).

<!-- p94 -->
These gifts are given not for personal glory but for the common good (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:7</span>). They function best when exercised in love and humility, reflecting the character of Christ (1 Corinthians 13).

<!-- p95 -->
Paul emphasized that spiritual gifts should be exercised decently and in order (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:40</span>). This ensures that the use of the gifts promotes unity, peace, and understanding, rather than confusion or division.

<!-- p96 -->
- Prophecies should be weighed (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:29</span>).

<!-- p97 -->
- When a message in tongues is given for the Body, pray that it may be interpreted (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:27-28</span>).

<!-- p98 -->
- The gifts should always bring **edification** to the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:12</span>).

<!-- p99 -->
Such order does not quench the Spirit but ensures that the gifts operate in a way that **honors God** and **benefits others**.

<!-- p100 -->
The operation of spiritual gifts must always be based on love. Paul devotes an entire chapter (1 Corinthians 13) to explaining that without love even the greatest spiritual gifts are meaningless. Love provides the motivation and the framework for using the gifts in a way that builds others up rather than destroying them.

<!-- p101 -->
Furthermore, the gifts operate as the Spirit wills (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:11</span>). They cannot be artificially produced or controlled by human efforts-they are sovereignly granted and established by the Holy Spirit, often in moments of need or ministry. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 13:8</span> says that ultimately prophecies and tongues will cease. In theology, people who believe that the gifts of the Holy Spirit have ceased are known as ‘Cessationists’. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 13:10</span> says: “when the prefect \[Jesus\] comes, then the desire which is partial will be the cause of cessation.” Since Jesus has not yet returned for His church, the gifts are still at work on earth.

<!-- p102 -->
It is important to understand that the gifts of the Spirit should not be separated from the fruit of the Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-23</span>). If the gifts demonstrate power, the fruit reveals character-love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control.

<!-- p103 -->
- **Gifts without fruit**: A believer may operate in gifts but lack maturity or character, which leads to pride, abuse, or harm.

<!-- p104 -->
- **Fruit without gifts**: A believer may have a godly character but may lack the ability for effective ministry.

<!-- p105 -->
Both are necessary. Gifts equip believers for ministry. Fruit is the sign of their spiritual health (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 7:16</span>) and gives a ‘pleasant taste’ to those who partake of it.

<!-- p106 -->
### Explanation of the 9 manifestation gifts of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p107 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span> lists the 9 manifestation gifts of the Holy Spirit:

<!-- p108 -->
1.  **Word of wisdom** - Supernatural insight into God’s purposes or direction for a situation. It applies divine wisdom to complex problems or decisions (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:10</span>).

<!-- p109 -->
2.  **Word of knowledge** - Supernatural revelation of information about a person, event, or circumstance that could not have been known by natural means (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 4:17-18</span>).

<!-- p110 -->
3.  **Faith** - A supernatural ability to trust God for the miraculous, even in impossible situations. This gift inspires boldness and confidence (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 3:6-7</span>).

<!-- p111 -->
4.  **Gifts of healings** - Supernatural power to heal physical, emotional, or spiritual diseases and restore wholeness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 3:6-8</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 9:17-18</span>).

<!-- p112 -->
5.  **Working of miracles** - The ability to perform supernatural acts that contradict natural laws, such as the multiplication of food or the raising of the dead (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40-41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 9:40-41</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 2:9-11</span>).

<!-- p113 -->
6.  **Prophecy** - Proclaiming God’s truth or will under the inspiration of the Holy Spirit. It should encourage, edify, and comfort (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>).

<!-- p114 -->
7.  **Discernment (recognition) of spirits** - The ability to discern whether a spiritual influence is from God, from the enemy, or of human origin (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 16:16-18</span>).

<!-- p115 -->
8.  **Various kinds of tongues** - Speaking in tongues, known to the speaker, often as an act of worship, prayer, or as a sign for unbelievers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:4</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:22</span>).

<!-- p116 -->
9.  **Interpretation of tongues** - The supernatural ability to interpret a message spoken in tongues so that the church may be edified (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:27</span>).

<!-- p117 -->
Paul makes it clear that all the gifts are valuable and should work together for the common good of the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:7</span>). However, he also urges believers to desire the greater gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:31</span>). The greater gifts are often understood as those that most directly edify the church, such as prophecy (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:1-5</span>).

<!-- p118 -->
- Prophecy is especially emphasized because it strengthens, encourages, and comforts others (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>). Paul instructs believers to earnestly desire this gift, as it promotes clarity and understanding in the church.

<!-- p119 -->
- Gifts such as tongues are highly valued for personal edification and prayer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:4</span>), but Paul teaches that when giving a message in tongues for the Church, one should pray for interpretation to ensure the edification of the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:27-28</span>).

<!-- p120 -->
Although the Holy Spirit gives gifts, we must yield and submit to the Holy Spirit in order to use them. Like muscles and talents, as we practice yielding to the Holy Spirit and allowing Him to use His gifts, we become more confident and reliable in their use.
