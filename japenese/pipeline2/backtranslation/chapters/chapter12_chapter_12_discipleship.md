# Chapter 12 - Discipleship

<!-- p1 -->
Discipleship means following Jesus, learning from Jesus, and becoming increasingly like Jesus in the way we live and love. It includes a lifelong walk of obedience, transformation, and service, led by the Holy Spirit and rooted in God’s Word.

<!-- p2 -->
Discipleship is the central command in the Great Commission. Jesus defined this mission as follows:

<!-- p3 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p4 -->
> **<u>Matthew 28:18-20</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And Jesus came and spoke to them, saying, “All authority has been given to Me in heaven and on earth.
>
> Go therefore and make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit,
>
> teaching them to observe all things that I have commanded you; and lo, I am with you always, even to the end of the age.” Amen.
>

<!-- p5 -->
</span>

<!-- p6 -->
We, the Lord’s disciples, are commanded to “make disciples of all nations.” Discipleship is both personal growth and the multiplication of that growth by teaching others to follow Christ. Discipleship is the vehicle for building God’s kingdom, and Jesus’ teachings are its message.

<!-- p7 -->
The call to be a disciple is more than attending church or memorizing Bible verses. It is an invitation to live like Jesus and to partner with Jesus to advance God’s kingdom. True discipleship helps believers mature spiritually and become effective in ministry (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:13</span>). It shapes both the heart and the skills needed to serve others and share the gospel. As Paul taught Timothy in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 2:2</span>, discipleship follows a pattern of spiritual multiplication: trained disciples continue to raise up new followers who then lead others to Christ.

<!-- p8 -->
Discipleship is both a personal calling and a global mission. On a personal level, it includes growing in faith, cultivating an active spiritual life, and having relationships with others &amp; with God. At the same time, it fuels the church’s mission to bring the gospel to every nation, ensuring that Christ’s message spreads across generations and continues to transform lives.

<!-- p9 -->
## Why Discipleship Matters

<!-- p10 -->
The impact of discipleship goes beyond personal transformation—it brings Kingdom impact. Paul reminded Timothy in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 2:2</span> to entrust what he had learned to faithful people who would be able to teach others also. This generational pattern highlights that discipleship is the engine driving the church’s growth and sustainability. Discipleship matters because it was the method Jesus chose to spread the gospel and build the Lord’s church.

<!-- p11 -->
Instead of focusing on large crowds, Jesus intentionally invested in a small number of disciples, teaching them, modeling faithfulness, and equipping them to carry on His mission. This personal and relational approach ensured that Jesus’ message would multiply as each disciple became equipped to make more disciples.

<!-- p12 -->
In essence, discipleship is central to fulfilling the Great Commission (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19-20</span>). Jesus has called us not merely to produce converts, but to produce disciples—followers who are rooted in God’s Word, empowered by the Holy Spirit, and equipped for ministry. Through discipleship, believers deepen their biblical knowledge, cultivate spiritual disciplines, and learn to walk in obedience to Christ. This equips the church to impact the world, advance the gospel, and reflect God’s glory among all nations.

<!-- p13 -->
### Nature vs. Nurture

<!-- p14 -->
Studies in biology, psychology, and anthropology often consider the influence of genetics and environment. A common question is: Are people shaped as a result of their innate, inborn genetic heritage? Or as a result of their environment and upbringing? In reality, every person is a blend of both. The two interact strongly, weaving together the growth of the human soul.

<!-- p15 -->
When you accepted Jesus, by the miracle of salvation you were born anew from incorruptible seed. You received a nature from God! Your spiritual DNA was rewritten! This divine nature awakens in our hearts the desire to draw near to our heavenly Father, to know Him, and to experience Him.

<!-- p16 -->
Just as a newborn with a wonderful, beautiful, spotless nature must be formed and guided by loving parents in order to grow into a healthy and mature individual, so we too must be formed, nurtured, and grown.

<!-- p17 -->
Discipleship is the process of spiritual development that builds on our divine nature and brings us to spiritual maturity. By investing in discipleship, we ensure that the faith is passed on, leaders are raised up, and the church remains healthy and vibrant—both locally and globally.

<!-- p18 -->
### Lecture Model vs. Apprenticeship Model

<!-- p19 -->
In modern Western education, the student’s primary relationship is with the material. Student learning is focused on content, materials, books, videos, and assignments. Transformation in education happens through a curriculum. This pedagogy is known as the *lecture model*.

<!-- p20 -->
The paradigm of the instructional model has not always been the norm. The model and methods used in Jesus’ day were the *apprenticeship model.* This learning model emphasizes that knowledge is 'caught more than taught' and focuses more on the relationship between student and teacher than on that between student and curriculum.

<!-- p21 -->
Over roughly a thousand years, the instructional model gradually became the default approach—through the first medieval universities in Europe in the 11<sup>th</sup> - 13<sup>th</sup> centuries, the invention of the printing press in the 15<sup>th</sup> century, the Enlightenment in the 17<sup>th</sup> &amp; 18<sup>th</sup> centuries, and the impact of the Industrial Revolution on information and education in the 19<sup>th</sup> &amp; 20<sup>th</sup> centuries.

<!-- p22 -->
An advantage of the instructional model is that it can scale more efficiently than the apprenticeship model. Massive Open Online Courses (MOOCs) can accommodate thousands of students at once, whereas even a highly skilled master can handle at most a few dozen apprentices at a time.

<!-- p23 -->
The result of the instructional model is that students master the content, but they do not become like the teacher. The teacher is a facilitator of the curriculum and imparts knowledge.

<!-- p24 -->
In the apprenticeship model, the master imparts more than knowledge—namely, wisdom. The result of the apprenticeship model is that the disciple is fully transformed to be like the master, able to reproduce the master’s work and take on disciples of their own.

<!-- p25 -->
The instructional model also has its use and place in the Kingdom of God, good examples being this book and other Christian educational materials. Jesus, a Rabbi skilled and trained in rabbinic methods, practiced the apprenticeship model as the primary model for his disciples.

<!-- p26 -->
Jesus explains the apprenticeship model as follows:

<!-- p27 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p28 -->
> **<u>Luke 6:40</u>** (NKJV)
>
> A disciple is not above his teacher, but everyone who is perfectly trained will be like his teacher.
>

<!-- p29 -->
</span>

<!-- p30 -->
In the instructional model, students become bearers of knowledge; in the apprenticeship model, students become like the teacher. Let’s learn the key terms in that passage in Luke.

<!-- p32 -->
In summary, the apprenticeship model used in discipleship has two purposes and goals:

<!-- p33 -->
**First, the disciple is fully trained**, like the teacher. This does not mean the disciple imitates everything the teacher does. We are commanded not to make clones, but to raise up mature men and women who can carry Jesus’ message to the ends of the earth.

<!-- p34 -->
**Second, the disciple now becomes a teacher for others.** The disciple must pass on what they have learned to someone else; otherwise, the message of the Kingdom stops with them. This does not necessarily mean they have exactly the same gifts as their teacher or enter full-time ministry. But it does mean they must reproduce for their training to be complete.

<!-- p35 -->
Any living thing that does not reproduce is infertile, and its line is cut off. An animal that cannot reproduce is not healthy, and likewise a church that cannot reproduce is unhealthy.

## Jesus’ Discipleship Model and Process

<!-- p37 -->
With his chosen disciples, Jesus modeled in the Gospels a mature and complete approach to discipleship. We should use these steps in broad outline as the foundation for our own journey of being and making disciples.

<!-- p38 -->
There are two roles in the apprenticeship model: master and student. Every master was once a student, and, hopefully, every student will become a master. In this book, we use the term *disciple* to refer to the student, and *disciple-maker* to refer to the master/teacher.

<!-- p39 -->
Every discipleship relationship follows a progression of eight stages. These stages (steps) are not purely isolated from one another; relationships often move back and forth between stages. Each stage has emphases regarding the disciple and emphases regarding the disciple-maker. We will study the qualities of a disciple-maker in more detail later in this chapter.

<!-- p40 -->
The eight stages of discipleship training are as follows:

<!-- p41 -->
1.  Invitation/Calling

<!-- p42 -->
2.  Teaching

<!-- p43 -->
3.  Modeling

<!-- p44 -->
4.  Participation/Attempt

<!-- p45 -->
5.  Correction

<!-- p46 -->
6.  Empowerment/Authorization

<!-- p47 -->
7.  Multiplication

<!-- p48 -->
8.  Staying Connected

<!-- p49 -->
### Invitation/Calling

<!-- p50 -->
Discipleship begins with a personal invitation from Jesus to “Follow Me.” In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 4:19</span>, Jesus called the first disciples: *“Follow Me. I will make you fishers of men.”* This invitation was relational and also transformational——it required leaving the old way of life and stepping into a new purpose centered on God’s mission. Jesus’ call was not an invitation to spectate, but an invitation to participate in His work and the work of the Kingdom.

<!-- p51 -->
Modern disciple-makers must invite people into genuine relationships that foster spiritual growth and obedience. The invitation to discipleship is deeply personal. Jesus meets people where they are, attending to each person’s unique needs and circumstances, while also calling them deeper—to grow, serve, and become more like Jesus. This pattern reflects the relational nature of discipleship, where the focus is not mere knowledge but a life-transforming relationship with Christ.

<!-- p52 -->
### Teaching

<!-- p53 -->
Teaching is at the heart of discipleship. Jesus spent much of His ministry teaching the principles of the Kingdom of God, especially in the Sermon on the Mount (Matthew 5–7). Jesus’ teaching provided the foundation for living rightly, loving others, and walking in obedience to God. Teaching from God’s Word remains central to discipleship today; the Scriptures are the primary instrument for spiritual growth, correction, and training in righteousness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>).

<!-- p54 -->
Disciple-makers must be devoted to faithfully teaching Scripture and helping others understand and apply it to their lives. Teaching is more than transmitting knowledge; it includes modeling a Christlike way of life so that disciples can see faith in practice. Jesus embodied the truths He taught, showing that effective teaching is not only spoken but visible. Likewise, disciple-makers should strive to live what they teach, offering practical examples of obedience and integrity.

<!-- p55 -->
### Modeling

<!-- p56 -->
Jesus did not merely teach His disciples—He showed them how to live the truths He spoke. He demonstrated, in concrete ways, what it means to love, serve, and minister. Examples in the Gospels include washing the disciples’ feet as an act of humility and service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 13:12-15</span>), healing the sick, and feeding the hungry. Through His actions, Jesus modeled compassion, faith, and boldness, leaving a clear example for the disciples to imitate.

<!-- p57 -->
Discipleship requires not only telling but showing. Modern disciple-makers should openly share their own walk of faith—including struggles and victories—so that others can see how faith works in real life. When rooted in practical demonstrations such as praying with those in need, sharing testimonies, and serving the community, discipleship thrives. Actions often speak louder than words, and showing becomes a powerful means of training others in the faith.

<!-- p58 -->
### Participation/Attempt

<!-- p59 -->
Jesus did not leave His disciples as bystanders—He actively involved them in ministry experiences. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 9:1-6</span> and <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 10:5-15</span>, Jesus sent the disciples out to proclaim the gospel, heal the sick, and cast out demons. Through these hands-on opportunities, the disciples learned by doing, developing confidence and their gifts under Jesus’ guidance.

<!-- p60 -->
Modern discipleship should also include practical involvement. Those who make disciples must invite disciples to participate in ministry through leading prayer groups, sharing testimonies, and doing outreach service. Providing a safe place to practice helps them grow in boldness while being supported and encouraged. Learning is most firmly acquired when it is applied, and participation is the key that turns theory into action。

<!-- p61 -->
### Correction

<!-- p62 -->
Growth often requires correction, and Jesus lovingly corrected His disciples when they misunderstood His teaching or failed in their ministry. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33-37" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 9:33-37</span>, He addressed their pride by teaching about servant leadership. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-32" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 22:31-32</span>, He prayed for Peter and prepared him to recover from failure and strengthen others。

<!-- p63 -->
Correction in discipleship is full of grace, constructive - rooted in Scripture, and aimed at restoration（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 4:2</span>）。It is not condemnation, but helping disciples grow and avoid pitfalls. Those who make disciples should provide accountability, use the Word of God to guide and encourage growth, and model humility in receiving correction themselves。

<!-- p64 -->
### Empowerment／Authorization

<!-- p65 -->
Jesus not only taught and corrected - He empowered His disciples to carry out His own mission. He gave them authority to proclaim the gospel, heal the sick, and cast out demons（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:18-20</span>）。After the resurrection, Jesus promised the Holy Spirit as the source of power for their ministry（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>）。

<!-- p66 -->
Modern discipleship should include empowering people to exercise their spiritual gifts and minister effectively. This includes discerning gifts（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-13</span>）, encouraging their use, and sending disciples out to serve. Empowerment also emphasizes relying on the Holy Spirit for power, boldness, and wisdom, ensuring that disciples are spiritually equipped to undertake the work God has entrusted to them。

<!-- p67 -->
### Multiplication

<!-- p68 -->
The goal of discipleship is not merely personal growth - but multiplication. Jesus demonstrated this by training His disciples to make more disciples（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19-20</span>）。After the resurrection, the apostles continued this mission of multiplication, spreading the gospel and planting churches throughout the known world of their time. Paul’s instruction in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 2:2</span> underscores this principle, urging Timothy to teach people who will in turn teach others。

<!-- p69 -->
Discipleship is equipping disciples so that they can make others into disciples, creating a chain reaction of faith that expands the kingdom of God. This process requires intentionality - teaching disciples how to mentor, teach, and train, while also sending them out with confidence and purpose（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 10:15</span>）。

<!-- p70 -->
### Maintaining Connection

<!-- p71 -->
Discipleship does not end when someone is sent. Jesus continued to encourage and teach His disciples even after the resurrection, demonstrating ongoing connection（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="21" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 21:15-19</span>）。The early church also maintained this pattern, gathering regularly for teaching, fellowship, and prayer（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42-47" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:42-47</span>）。

<!-- p72 -->
Modern discipleship should likewise nurture ongoing relationships, providing accountability, encouragement, and support as disciples grow and lead. Maintaining unity in the body of Christ ensures that disciples are not isolated but continue to be empowered and encouraged in their walk of faith。

<!-- p73 -->
## Who makes disciples?

<!-- p74 -->
Everyone who belongs to the body of Christ has the responsibility first to become a disciple and then to contribute to the process of making disciples. Because gifts and roles are diverse, our contributions may look different, but the responsibility belongs to everyone. While the leaders（**elders and deacons - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 14:23</span>**）bear the greatest ultimate responsibility for the state and health of discipleship in the local church, everyone has a role。

<!-- p75 -->
Discipleship is a living, growing process - similar to raising children to maturity. Healthy discipleship brings strength and life to people. Just as parents strengthen and nurture their children, and as a result the children mature healthily, those who make disciples help disciples to be nourished, strengthened, and to mature. The ultimate goal of discipleship is not the transfer of knowledge but transformation。

<!-- p76 -->
We all must move toward perfection（maturity） and make disciples. Even though gifts are diverse, there are characteristics common to all who make disciples。

<!-- p77 -->
### Characteristics of those who make disciples

<!-- p78 -->
A person who makes disciples is someone who not only follows Christ but is devoted to helping others grow in faith. This role requires specific qualities that reflect both spiritual maturity and practical devotion. Those who make disciples must always rely on God’s power, wisdom, and grace to overcome the challenges that come with guiding people’s spiritual journeys。

<!-- p79 -->
#### Rooted in Christ

<!-- p80 -->
The foundation of the life of a disciple maker must be deeply rooted in Christ. Jesus said, *“I am the vine; you are the branches. If someone remains in Me, and I in him, he will bear much fruit. Apart from Me you can do nothing”* (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:5</span>). Effective disciple makers know that the power to grow and to help others comes from remaining close to Jesus each day. However, resistance to personal growth and spiritual change can hinder this dependence. Disciple makers must continually cultivate their personal relationship with Jesus through prayer, Bible study, and obedience, and show the people they are leading what it means to abide in Christ.

<!-- p81 -->
#### Commitment to the Mission

<!-- p82 -->
Disciple makers must remain continually devoted to the disciple-making mission that Jesus commanded in the Great Commission (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19-20</span>). This mission requires intentional focus and prioritization, and it calls for understanding that disciple making is not an optional task but the central purpose of the church. However, challenges such as time constraints, busyness, and personal distractions can make it difficult to stay focused. Disciple makers must intentionally schedule time for mentoring, teaching, and relationship building, and view disciple making as a lifestyle rather than an occasional activity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 19:10</span>).

<!-- p83 -->
#### Led by the Holy Spirit

<!-- p84 -->
Disciple making cannot be accomplished by human strength alone; it requires the guidance and empowerment of the Holy Spirit. Jesus promised His disciples that they would receive power when the Holy Spirit came upon them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>). Through this, they were equipped as witnesses of the gospel and as servants. Disciple makers must be sensitive to the Spirit’s leading and rely on His wisdom to navigate complex situations, cultural barriers, and difficult conversations. This dependence on the Holy Spirit enables disciple makers to work with love, boldness, and spiritual discernment, ensuring that their work is Spirit-led rather than self-driven.

<!-- p85 -->
#### Humble and Teachable

<!-- p86 -->
Humility is a mark of an effective disciple maker. As Paul taught, *“Do nothing out of selfish ambition or vain conceit, but rather, in humility, regard others as more important than yourselves”* (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 2:3</span>). Disciple makers recognize that they too are lifelong learners who must maintain a teachable posture and accountability. They accept correction and feedback and model humility to those they mentor. Pride can become a major obstacle to growth for both disciple makers and disciples. Embracing accountability and keeping a servant’s heart foster trust and create an environment for growth and transparency.

<!-- p87 -->
#### Patient and Persistent

<!-- p88 -->
Discipleship is rarely immediate - it is a long-term process that requires patience and persistence. Paul reminds believers, *“Let us not grow weary in doing good, for in due season we will reap if we do not give up”* (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:9</span>). Growth in faith often involves setbacks, doubts, and failures. Yet disciple makers are called to walk alongside others, offering encouragement and support at every step. When progress seems slow, it is easy to be discouraged, but disciple makers trust that God is at work even when results are not immediately visible.

<!-- p89 -->
Being a disciple maker requires a combination of spiritual dependence, devotion, and humility. It is a calling that demands patience and persistence while being led by the Holy Spirit and maintaining a learning posture. Perfection is not required of disciple makers, but faithfulness is - to remain in Christ, invest in others, and trust that God will bring growth and transformation in His timing. These qualities reflect the love of Christ and help others become mature disciples who will, in time, make disciples themselves.

## Practical Tools for Discipleship

<!-- p91 -->
Effective discipleship requires intentional tools and practices that foster spiritual growth, accountability, and community. These tools help disciples build a strong foundation of faith, deepen intimacy with God, and be equipped to make other disciples. Practical methods such as Bible study, prayer, small groups, and mentoring provide both the framework and the support necessary for ongoing growth.

<!-- p92 -->
### Bible Study

<!-- p93 -->
Engaging deeply with Scripture is the foundation of discipleship. Jesus emphasized the importance of God’s word in spiritual growth and declared, *“Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceeds from the mouth of God”* (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 4:4</span>). Regular Bible study and memorization enable disciples to internalize truth, confront falsehood, and apply God’s wisdom to daily life.

<!-- p94 -->
Disciples need to be encouraged to immerse themselves in Scripture not merely as an academic exercise, but to know God and be transformed in heart (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="119" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="105" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 119:105</span>). Practical approaches such as reading plans, study guides, and group discussions provide opportunities to explore doctrine and discuss application within community. By building regular study habits, disciples lay a solid foundation for spiritual maturity and fruitful service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>).

<!-- p95 -->
### Prayer

<!-- p96 -->
Prayer is the lifeline of discipleship, connecting believers to God’s presence, power, and guidance. Jesus taught His disciples how to pray in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 11:1-4</span>, emphasizing both dependence on God and spiritual alignment with His will. Through prayer, disciples learn to seek God’s wisdom, intercede for others, and receive strength for ministry.

<!-- p97 -->
Teaching disciples how to pray - both individually and corporately - cultivates intimacy with God and equips them to discern His guidance (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 4:6-7</span>). Incorporating prayer journals, prayer groups, and the practice of fasting nurtures spiritual discipline and empowerment, ensuring that disciples are led by the Spirit and remain focused on the mission.

<!-- p98 -->
### Small Groups and Community

<!-- p99 -->
Discipleship thrives best within community. The early church modeled relational discipleship through fellowship, prayer, and the breaking of bread together (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42-47" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:42-47</span>). Small groups and accountability partnerships provide a safe place for disciples to grow together, share struggles, and rejoice in victories.

<!-- p100 -->
Local churches function as central hubs for disciple-making relationships, providing opportunities for group Bible study, prayer meetings, and fellowship events. These gatherings enable disciples to practice love, bear one another’s burdens (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:2</span>), and encourage one another toward spiritual maturity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:24-25</span>). Small groups also provide a framework for multiplying leaders, as mature disciples train others and are sent out to lead groups and mentor new believers.

<!-- p101 -->
### Mentoring

<!-- p102 -->
Mentoring offers a personal and intentional approach to disciple-making. Jesus modeled this by deeply engaging with the twelve disciples, walking with them through teaching, correction, and encouragement. Likewise, one-on-one relationships allow disciple-makers to tailor guidance to individual needs and help disciples grow in faith, character, and leadership.

<!-- p103 -->
Mentors function as accountability partners, offering support, prayer, and wisdom during times of growth and struggle. Practical tools such as spiritual assessments, goal setting, and regular check-ins help ensure that disciples remain focused and motivated. Peer relationships within the mentoring framework also foster mutual growth as both mentor and disciple learn from shared experiences (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="27" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 27:17</span>).

## **Personal Reflection Questions**

<!-- p106 -->

<!-- p107 -->

<!-- p108 -->

<!-- p109 -->
5.  Consider the people in your life. Whom might God be calling you to disciple? What first step can you take to begin building that relationship?
