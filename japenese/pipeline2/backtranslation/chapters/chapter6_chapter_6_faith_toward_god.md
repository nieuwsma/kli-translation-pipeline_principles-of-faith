Faith is a foundational concept in Christianity. Because the word faith is used so very frequently in Christianity, at times its meaning gets replaced by subtle misunderstandings or mistaken interpretations. Yet it is not something you can take in your hand - it is something that is formed in your heart as you come to trust God. Conviction is believing.

### Definition of Faith

<!-- p10 -->
Faith is an aspect of Christianity that is often misunderstood and misrepresented. As the proverb says, "There is a ditch on both sides of the road"! In modern Christianity, the word faith is used so commonly without adequate explanation that it can come to carry mistaken definitions amplified by misunderstandings. Faith is often portrayed either as a sweeping statement of abstracted belief or as an almost mystical power, that is, "magic," something one possesses only in a certain measured, limited amount.

<!-- p11 -->
#### What Faith Is Not

<!-- p12 -->
When faith is misrepresented in an <u>abstract</u> way, there is no concrete conviction or persuasion. This becomes clear when you probe a little, because such expressions of faith cannot be made concrete. When asked "What do you believe?" or "Why do you believe?", those who hold this understanding often cannot clarify beyond "I believe." <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 3:15</span> says that we should be ready to give a defense for the hope that is in us.

<!-- p13 -->
At times people think of faith as a magical power - but that is not what the Bible teaches. Faith involves a real relationship built on trust. In Scripture, faith is far more dynamic and relational - it is rooted in assurance, trust, and confidence in God's character and promises. It is not an impersonal force, but a volitional response to God that shapes thoughts, feelings, and actions.

<!-- p14 -->
Why is believing related to conviction?

<!-- p15 -->
Our beliefs shape how we see and engage with the world around us. Beliefs shape perception and trigger our actions. This is because our minds are wired that way - we live based on what we believe is possible.

<!-- p16 -->
We act based on what we perceive, understand, and believe to be possible. When walking down a hallway, the brain detects the boundary between the floor and the walls and automatically tells your feet where you can step and what to avoid. This truth applies beyond the physical navigation of the world around us.

<!-- p17 -->
If you believe that financial independence or a healthy, active lifestyle is impossible for you, then your actions will follow that belief, and, true to that belief, you will not do what you believe is impossible and will probably not even attempt it.

<!-- p18 -->
If you believe that God has a character that is merciless and punitive, that perception of Him will hinder you from walking with God as a son or daughter.

<!-- p19 -->
#### Faith Is Conviction

<!-- p20 -->
Then, what is faith? Assurance! The Greek ***pistis*** means assurance, or the result obtained by being persuaded. We are asked to have assurance toward God. In English, we might express faith (assurance) as ‘**in** God’. If you are married, hopefully you trust your spouse. That is, you believe your spouse ‘**in’**. You believe your spouse will do what they said, and that the person is faithful and genuine. This trust should extend beyond their words to the whole person.

<!-- p21 -->
It takes time and experience to cultivate faith, assurance, and trust in relationships. Hopefully, you have learned how to build trust with others in your life. The principles you have learned to increase your trust in someone you know also help to increase your faith in God. Faith in a relationship is increased through time and experience, consistency, and through actions—from small to large—that build confidence in the other’s character and nature.

<!-- p22 -->
‘Faith toward God’ is relational. Relationships are built by both parties, not just one. The good news about our faith in God is that God takes responsibility for His side of the relationship (made possible by Jesus’ atoning death and by being filled with the Spirit at new birth). And God also helps our side (through the work of the Comforter - the Holy Spirit -). God is doing almost all the work. Our role (as we will discuss later in this chapter) is to walk *WITH* God in obedience.

### Jesus matures our faith

<!-- p24 -->
Jesus is the author and finisher of our faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 12:2</span>)。 Our faith is intended to constantly grow and expand。 We do not reach a single point and obtain “enough ‘faith,’” but rather faith keeps increasing。 Think now of a deep, personal relationship in your life。 In any relationship, you do not arrive at a point where you have “come to ‘know’ that person.” We continue to know that person, deepen our understanding of them, and that refines, shapes, and grows the relationship。

<!-- p25 -->
How do we grow in faith toward God? By continuing to experience God across His many revelations, manifestations, and attributes。 Once again, think of a deep, personal relationship in your life。 The depth of the relationship increases by engaging in various ways, formats, media, and settings。 You talk, send messages, and sometimes write letters。 You meet one-on-one and in gatherings。 You speak about what you value, and perhaps even challenge one another。 The number of ways goes on endlessly。 Through these many expressions, you come to know the person more deeply。

<!-- p26 -->
We should all come to know God as Savior through Jesus。 Remember the names of God (*see the appendix),* Knowing God as Savior does not necessarily mean you have yet experienced God as Deliverer, Healer, Counselor, Lord。

<!-- p27 -->
If you have been persuaded to know God as Savior (have faith) and are at that point, building this relationship will give you assurance of His nature so that you can know God in His other ways as well。

<!-- p28 -->
I feel blessed that my life has been given several very deep relationships。 One of them is my relationship with my wife。 In our relationship, because of the degree of trust (faith) we have in each other, my wife can tell me what she is going to do (something I have not yet seen in reality), and I can have the assurance to believe it because of her proven character。

<!-- p29 -->
This example shows how a deep relationship with God can be experienced with trust and assurance！ We may not yet have fully experienced God (in fact, we will never fully experience Him in this lifetime)。 However, as the relationship matures, we can trust God beyond the bounds of what we personally know and the events we have already seen。

<!-- p30 -->
Jesus increases our faith through experiences with Himself。 One important way we can and should experience Him is through the Word。

*『Then assurance comes by hearing, and hearing comes by the word of God』*。 This truth is naturally self-evident。 The primary way a person comes to have assurance is by being persuaded (convinced)。 And when someone gives a ‘demonstration’—or a logical, clear explanation—we are convinced。

<!-- p38 -->
In the Bible, there are two Greek words for 「word」—**logos** and **rhema**。 By understanding this difference, we can see how faith grows。

<!-- p39 -->
**Logos**: Generally refers to the concept, thought, reason, or broader expression of the word of God。 It encompasses universal and foundational truths or messages and is often used to represent God’s eternal wisdom and Christ Himself, the living Word。

<!-- p40 -->
**Rhema（G4487）**：emphasizes a **specific, spoken word or utterance**. Typically, it refers to a special word or message given at a particular moment, especially a spoken or revealed word that applies directly to a person's situation.

<!-- p41 -->
*Logos* is like the message of an entire book, and *Rhema* is like a specific passage or sentence that speaks directly to a person at that time. While *Logos* is God’s comprehensive truth, *Rhema* is that a portion of that truth is spoken to an individual in a personal and applicable way. Both show the fullness of God’s communication; that is, *Logos* is the broad, foundational truth of God’s nature and will, and *Rhema* is the concrete, applied word that makes that truth come alive within a person’s life and circumstances.

<!-- p42 -->
### An example of how a Rhema word works

<!-- p43 -->
God promises revelation and wisdom to all who ask. When God speaks to us, that utterance is a *Rhema* word. A Rhema word is from God; it confirms, guides, and directs us. It is not a replacement for the *Logos* word, nor does it carry the authority of Scripture. A *Rhema* word should be tested by the same standards as prophecy and the interpretation of tongues（see the chapter on the Holy Spirit for a detailed study of this subject）.

<!-- p44 -->
Several times in my career, I prayed and sought God’s guidance. A few years ago, when I was wondering whether I should start looking for a new opportunity, I heard God say to me in prayer, 「‘*Just wait on me, I have an opportunity for you.’*」. I was dissatisfied with and frustrated by my role at the time, so I had started looking for a new job. Through God’s word of direction, 「‘*wait on me’*」, I was able to have peace and confidence even in a restless season.

<!-- p45 -->
After three or four months passed, I continued to work hard and faithfully, maintaining a positive attitude. God often reminded me, 「*‘wait on me’*」, and eventually that promise was fulfilled. I received a promotion opportunity and a generous pay increase.

<!-- p46 -->
I could have continued job hunting, quit, or started somewhere new. Without a doubt, someone else would have gotten that promotion! However, because I sought the Lord’s counsel and gained confidence in that **Rhema word**, I obeyed and benefited. God’s word is **living and personal**, and He promises to give us much, abundant wisdom（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:5</span>）。

<!-- p47 -->
Dozens of times, indeed, hundreds, I have prayed and asked God what I should do, and God has given me a Rhema word. Not all of them produced equally remarkable results, but each one brought me peace and confidence that I was following God and that He would bring things to pass. It was the word I needed in that moment! Often, when God brings a Rhema word, it is from the Logos. God brings a verse of Scripture to my remembrance: 「“*Do not fear, I am with you!*”」（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="41" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 41:10</span>）, 「“*By His stripes we are healed*”」（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="53" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 53:5</span>）, 「“*Do not grow weary in doing good!*”」（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:9</span>）, and many more examples follow!

<!-- p48 -->
Receiving a Rhema word not only strengthens our faith but also deepens our relationship with God as we trust Him more fully in our daily decisions. We will continue our study of learning to hear God’s voice in the chapter on the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p49 -->
Our faith is also strengthened by seeing what God does, but at times what is seen in this natural world can challenge what we hear from God. Mediating the discrepancy between what is seen and what is heard is an important role of the Holy Spirit, and the Holy Spirit promises to lead us into all truth（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span>）。

When Jesus appeared to the disciples and calmed them（I imagine one would be quite surprised if someone appeared in the middle after coming through locked doors）, Jesus addressed Thomas. Jesus did not criticize or blame Thomas, nor accuse him of being part of an evil and perverse generation that seeks a sign（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 16:4</span>）, but revealed Himself to Thomas.

<!-- p60 -->
Jesus further says, 「‘*blessed are they who have not seen and have believe*d’」. This is not Jesus encouraging 「‘blind and ignorant faith’」 or rejecting the use of empirical evidence. Rather, He is saying that we are more blessed when we can believe without requiring empirical proof. Why is that? If our faith—that is, our trust—were purely limited to only what has been empirically shown, we would have no assurance regarding future works. We might have great confidence in what has been accomplished in the past, but we would not be able to walk in God’s peace concerning tomorrow and the future.

<!-- p61 -->
Using a relational example, if my attitude toward my wife were consistently 「‘<u>I’ll believe it when I see it!</u>’」, how difficult would our relationship become? Instead, a trust -based relationship says, 「‘I know you are a trustworthy person.’」

### Practical Steps for Growing Faith

<!-- p153 -->
Walking with God is about relationship, and if you want a good relationship there are skills you should acquire. In earthly human relationships, the skills that produce strong and secure relationships are built on the following foundations.

<!-- p154 -->
- Open, free dialogue and communication

<!-- p155 -->
- Healthy conflict resolution

<!-- p156 -->
- Commitment

<!-- p157 -->
- Consistency

<!-- p158 -->
If you want a strong and vibrant relationship, you need to engage consistently, reveal yourself（communication＝expressing our thoughts and feelings）, receive the other（listening）, and walk with the other! All of these skills apply to our relationship with God as well.

<!-- p159 -->
In addition to core relational skills, there are specific practices and training that strengthen your relationship with God.

<!-- p160 -->
- **Build and maintain a life of prayer, listening to God and conversing with Him.** Regular, heartfelt prayer is indispensable to an intimate relationship with God. Through prayer, you can share your anxieties, joys, and gratitude, and seek God's guidance and strength. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 4:6-7</span>*) Being sensitive to the voice of the Holy Spirit helps you discern God's will, gain wisdom in decisions, and grow toward spiritual maturity. Take time to quietly reflect and listen for God's guidance. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:14</span>*)

<!-- p161 -->
- **Have times of worship.** Worship is a powerful way to draw near to God while expressing love and reverence. Through praise, song, and quiet contemplation, worship deepens your awareness of God's presence. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="100" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 100:2-4</span>*)

<!-- p162 -->
- **Read, study, and meditate on the Bible.** God's word reveals His character, promises, and guidance for life. Devoting time to study the Scriptures helps grow faith, understanding, and spiritual resilience. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>*)

<!-- p163 -->
- **Obey God's commandments.** Obeying God's instructions shows love, reverence, and trust for God. Walking in obedience aligns your life with God's purposes and brings blessing. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:15</span>*)

<!-- p164 -->
- **Gather with and serve actively in a local church.** Involvement in a church community provides spiritual support and encouragement and offers opportunities to serve others. By engaging, a sense of belonging is nurtured and your commitment to God's mission is strengthened. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:24-25</span>*)

<!-- p165 -->
- **Build relationships with other believers.** Fellowship with other Christians brings accountability, encouragement, and shared wisdom. These relationships enrich your journey of faith and help you grow through mutual support. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="27" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 27:17</span>*)

<!-- p166 -->
Our ‘faith in God’ is not a one-time event for salvation, but an ongoing practice and relationship to be cultivated throughout life. The benefits of a trusting, fruitful relationship are truly countless, but to see that relationship thrive powerfully, we must do the good work of nurturing that trust and growth.

### Faith produces obedience and good works

<!-- p80 -->
Our trust-filled relationship with God is strengthened through obedience, and as a result it brings forth good works! These works do not save us or win God’s love—they are fruit that naturally arises from a life rooted in God.

<!-- p81 -->
The Bible shows the natural progression of faith. When we know God and experience His presence, our trust in God deepens. This trust prompts us to act in keeping with faith and leads us to obey God’s commands. Our obedience produces good works, which in turn bring about fruitful and beneficial results.
