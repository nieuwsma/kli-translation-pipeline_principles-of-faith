# Chapter 4 - Jesus - The Messiah

<!-- p1 -->
Based on our study of sin, we have concluded that sin demands a serious cost: restoration is required for the one sinned against, and atonement is required for the one who sinned. The price of sin must be paid. In the Old Testament, God established a system of sacrifices carried out by priests, individuals, and the community to address, resolve, and adjudicate sin.

<!-- p2 -->
Even with many sacrifices for sin, it still was not enough - not because God failed, but because people continued to choose sin. Animal sacrifices could provide limited atonement, but no matter how many animals were offered, they could not change the sinful nature of humans.

<!-- p3 -->
God had a wonderful plan. It was the <u>Messiah</u> (in Hebrew, ***Mashiach,*** pronounced *Mah-shee-ahkh -* **H4899**), also known as <u>Christ</u> (in Greek, ***Christos,*** pronounced *Khris-tos* - **G5547**), meaning “anointed one”; one set apart for a special role (e.g., king, priest, or savior).

<!-- p4 -->
To understand who Jesus is, it helps to see what people at the time expected of the Messiah. These expectations shaped how people saw Jesus and, in many cases, why they struggled to recognize Jesus as the fulfillment of God’s plan.

<!-- p5 -->
## Historical Background

<!-- p6 -->
The Jewish people had long awaited the coming Messiah. Views of the Messiah in the period just before Jesus were shaped by Old Testament prophecy, but interpretations were varied and not necessarily uniform. Many Jews expected the Messiah to be a political and military leader who would free Israel from foreign oppression (primarily Roman rule) and restore the Davidic kingdom. Below is an overview of common messianic expectations and how they aligned (or did not align) with biblical prophecy:

<!-- p7 -->
### Common Jewish Expectations about the Messiah before Jesus

<!-- p8 -->
### **1. A Political and Military Liberator**

<!-- p9 -->
- **Belief:** The Messiah was thought to be a king like David who would lead a victorious revolt against foreign powers and restore Israel’s independence.

<!-- p10 -->
- **Reference verses:** <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 9:6-7</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="23" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 23:5-6</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ZEC" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Zechariah 9:9-10</span>.

<!-- p11 -->
- **Historical context:** Under Roman rule, the Jews were oppressed and heavily taxed, which heightened expectations for a Messiah who would overthrow Rome and establish a powerful earthly kingdom.

<!-- p12 -->
- **Truth:** Jesus is a king, but his kingdom is not of this world (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="18" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="36" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 18:36</span>). Through his first coming, Jesus established a spiritual kingdom, but he will one day return as the victorious King to judge and reign in righteousness. This is recorded in Revelation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 19:11-16</span>).

<!-- p13 -->
### **2. Restorer of the Davidic Kingdom**

<!-- p14 -->
- **Belief:** It was thought that the Messiah would rebuild the Davidic dynasty and bring about a golden age marked by prosperity, justice, and peace.

<!-- p15 -->
- **Reference verses:** <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2SA" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Samuel 7:12-16</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="AMO" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Amos 9:11</span>.

<!-- p16 -->
- **Interpretation:** The focus was often placed on national restoration rather than spiritual renewal.

<!-- p17 -->
- **Truth:** Jesus, a descendant of David, fulfills this promise by establishing an eternal kingdom. At present, this kingdom is spiritual in nature, but at his return it will culminate in an earthly rule (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="32-33" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 1:32-33</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 20:4-6</span>).

<!-- p18 -->
### **3. A Prophet like Moses**

<!-- p19 -->
- **Belief:** Some Jews believed that the Messiah would be a prophet like Moses, who would speak directly with God and speak on God’s behalf.

<!-- p20 -->
- **Reference Scripture:** <span class="x-scripture" data-book="DEU" data-chapter="18" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Deuteronomy 18:15-19</span>.

<!-- p21 -->
- **Expectation:** Many people longed for a prophetic figure, but they did not see this role as central to the Messiah and often considered it an ancillary aspect。

<!-- p22 -->
- **Truth:** Jesus, as the ultimate Prophet, spoke the word of God and fulfilled this prophecy by directly revealing the Father（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:18</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:10</span>）。His prophetic role surpasses Moses, and He is the mediator of a newer and better covenant（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:6-7</span>）。

<!-- p23 -->
### **4. A Leader Who Brings Justice**

<!-- p24 -->
- **Belief:** The Messiah was said to judge the wicked, uphold the righteous, and bring justice to the oppressed。

<!-- p25 -->
- **Reference Scripture:** <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 11:3-5</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="72" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 72:1-4</span>.

<!-- p26 -->
- **Interpretation:** Justice was expected to be primarily political and social rather than spiritual。

<!-- p27 -->
- **Truth:** Jesus brings ultimate justice。At His first coming, He granted spiritual redemption and justice through the cross（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:13-15</span>）。At His second coming, He will judge the wicked and establish perfect justice for all eternity（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 20:11-15</span>）。

<!-- p28 -->
### Diverse Messianic Views

<!-- p29 -->
Just as today’s Christian world is divided into various groups, denominations, or systems of belief, the Jews of Jesus’ time were also divided。In Jesus’ day, there were four major Jewish sects that shaped Israel’s religious and political landscape, each holding its own beliefs and expectations regarding the Messiah。Understanding the background of these different groups helps provide meaningful context for the study of the New Testament。

<!-- p30 -->
**Pharisees** were the most influential among the people and were known for strictly observing the Law (Torah) and the oral traditions。They believed in resurrection, the afterlife, and the existence of angels, and they emphasized personal piety and obedience to God’s commands。Their messianic expectations focused on a figure who would uphold the Law firmly, bring justice, and restore Israel both politically and religiously。

<!-- p31 -->
By contrast, the **Sadducees** were a wealthy and politically powerful group who controlled the temple and the priesthood。They rejected the oral traditions and accepted only the Law as authoritative Scripture。The Sadducees denied the resurrection, the afterlife, and the existence of angels, and they emphasized maintaining power and securing political stability through cooperation with Rome。As a result, they placed little emphasis on the Messiah; their primary concern was preserving the status quo。

<!-- p32 -->
The **Essenes** were a separatist group who withdrew from mainstream Jewish society and lived in isolated communities such as Qumran。They focused deeply on ritual purity, apocalyptic expectation, and disciplined living。The Essenes believed in two Messiahs: a priestly Messiah who would restore the sanctity of the temple and a royal Messiah who would lead Israel in righteousness。This dual messianic view reflected their hope for both spiritual and political renewal。The Essenes are not directly mentioned in Scripture。

<!-- p33 -->
Finally, the **Zealots** were militant nationalists who sought to overthrow Roman rule through armed revolt。They rejected Roman authority and were strongly devoted to restoring Israel’s sovereignty。Their messianic expectation was of a warrior-king Messiah, like David, who would lead the fight against oppressors and establish a powerful earthly kingdom。

<!-- p34 -->
While these sects focused on earthly expectations, Jesus’ mission revealed a far greater plan - one that addressed not only political oppression but also humanity’s spiritual bondage to sin -。

## The Truth about the Messiah

<!-- p36 -->
### The Anointing of Jesus

<!-- p37 -->
The messianic image held by the Pharisees and the Zealots reflects a pattern that recurs throughout human history - rejecting God’s greater plan and seeking salvation by earthly means。As we have seen throughout Scripture, people often turn away from God - even though God is always faithful。Even when God is faithful and has accepted us, He is often forgotten and rejected, resulting in a cycle of disobedience and desolation that consumed earthly kingdoms。

<!-- p38 -->
Many expected a political Messiah, but Jesus revealed the far greater purpose of His anointing - a purpose that meets spiritual and eternal needs。If Jesus had come as a conquering military leader, as the Jews desired and expected, it would only have been a matter of time before that kingdom fell into desolation because of human disobedience。

<!-- p39 -->
Rather, Jesus established a kingdom that cannot be shaken。His mission was not rooted in military might or political dominion, but in divine purpose。As the Anointed One, Jesus explained what He was anointed for and how He would accomplish it:

<!-- p40 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="16-21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p41 -->
> **<u>Luke 4:16-21</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Then He came to Nazareth, where He had been brought up. And, as was His custom, He went into the synagogue on the Sabbath and stood up to read.
>
> And the book of the prophet Isaiah was handed to Him. When He opened the book, He found the place where it was written.
>
> "The Spirit of the Lord is upon Me, because He has anointed Me to preach the gospel to the poor. He has sent Me to heal the brokenhearted, to proclaim liberty to the captives, and recovery of sight to the blind, to set at liberty those who are oppressed;
>

>
> He began to say to them, "Today this Scripture has been fulfilled in your hearing."
>

<!-- p42 -->
</span>

<!-- p43 -->
Jesus was anointed - consecrated by God -, and was sent bearing God’s mission full of purpose and power.

<!-- p44 -->
Jesus declared:

<!-- p45 -->
- The good news (gospel) of the Kingdom.

<!-- p46 -->
- Release - pardon, remission of sins, freedom.

<!-- p47 -->
- The year (season) acceptable to the Lord.

<!-- p48 -->
These are the kinds of people He came for:

<!-- p49 -->
- The brokenhearted - those shattered emotionally and spiritually.

<!-- p50 -->
- The blind - spiritually, and at times physically.

<!-- p51 -->
- The poor - those in need and in poverty (spiritually or physically).

<!-- p52 -->
- Captives - meaning ‘taken with a spear’, referring to those bound against their own will.

<!-- p53 -->
Jesus transformed these broken people as follows:

<!-- p54 -->
- Restoring sight

<!-- p55 -->
- Placing them in a state of freedom

<!-- p56 -->
- Binding up, healing, and restoring wounds

<!-- p57 -->
Jesus’ mission and ministry were personal yet global, immediate yet long-term. His purpose did not have a single focus, but was part of God’s divine plan to reconcile humanity to God. After sin entered the world, a division arose between God and man. God had no choice but to banish humanity from the garden and from His presence. Throughout the Old Testament, people could be with God, but only in part. It was exceedingly rare for the Spirit of God to be given to people, and most people lived in a state of separation from God. God found His people among the Jews, but He desired a permanent, certain, and complete way to restore relationship with humankind.

<!-- p58 -->
In the time of Jesus, God’s presence on earth was chiefly confined to the innermost part of the temple, that is, the Holy of Holies. There, behind the thick veil that separated the innermost area from the Holy Place, God’s presence remained with the Ark of the Covenant. People were separated from God, and God was apart from His creation.

<!-- p59 -->
God sent Jesus to repair, rebuild, restore, and reconcile humanity to Himself. (2 Cor 5:17-21)

<!-- p60 -->
### The Indispensable Work of Jesus

<!-- p61 -->
In order to fully reconcile humanity to God, the mission of the Messiah required not only preaching and healing but also the ultimate work of redemption. Through His death on the cross, Jesus had to become the perfect and complete sacrifice.

<!-- p62 -->
The Epistle to the Hebrews, over all 13 chapters, explains in detail how Jesus became the perfect sacrifice and, at the same time, was the High Priest who offered it (Hebrews 3 & 4). Jesus’s priesthood did not belong to the Levitical line (the tribe of Levi) but was rather according to the order of Melchizedek, of whom it is said that he has neither beginning nor end, which means it is not bound by human patterns or customs (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 7:3</span>). The limitation of the Levitical priesthood was that it relied on the blood of bulls and goats (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:12-13</span>), and those sacrifices were insufficient for a permanent and lasting forgiveness of sins. From our study of sin and sacrifice, we know that the consequences of the people’s sins could be deferred for a year on the Day of Atonement (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:3</span>), but these sacrifices had to be offered continually.

<!-- p63 -->
Jesus died on the cross (accounts are found in Matthew 27, Mark 15, John 19, and Luke 23), shed His blood, and paid the price for the sins of all humanity—past, present, and future. Isaiah 53 presents Jesus as the Suffering Servant, depicting Him as the One who bore upon Himself the full weight of punishment and anguish. His once-for-all death became the definitive and ultimate sacrifice. Jesus is the Lamb of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:29</span>), without spot or wrinkle (sin); because He was perfectly and wholly holy, the shedding of His innocent blood made redemption possible. **No further sacrifice is needed or acceptable. The former were temporary - but Jesus’s was perfect and final.**

<!-- p64 -->
Through His death and resurrection, we have come to have a new and better covenant (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:6</span>), guaranteed by the blood of God’s own Son, and Jesus is the mediator, the One who stands between, of that covenant. Through Christ’s sacrifice we receive salvation, and He is the author of *‘eternal salvation to all who obey Him*’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 5:9</span>).

<!-- p65 -->
In Greek, the word for salvation is **Sōtēria** (*so-tay-REE-ah - **G4991)**.* It primarily means **salvation, deliverance, or preservation**. It encompasses the act of being saved in both a spiritual sense (eternal salvation through Christ) and a physical context (rescue from danger or harm). The term conveys the ideas of safety, wholeness, and restoration.

<!-- p66 -->
**Salvation or deliverance:**

<!-- p67 -->
- Refers to God’s deliverance of humanity from sin and its consequences through Jesus Christ.

<!-- p68 -->
- Used in the context of eternal salvation (justification, sanctification, glorification).

<!-- p69 -->
**Preservation:**

<!-- p70 -->
- Implies being kept safe, protected, and preserved from danger.

<!-- p71 -->
**Wholeness or healing:**

<!-- p72 -->
- Includes restoration to a state of completeness or soundness.

<!-- p73 -->
Jesus’s sacrifice paid the price for our forgiveness, opened the way for us to have a relationship with God, and provided a path for remaining with God that depends not on our perfection or our ability to avoid sin, but on our obedience alone.

<!-- p74 -->
When we, by faith, receive Jesus (more on this later), we receive His salvation. This salvation joins us to God and records our names in the ***Lamb’s Book of Life*** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 20:11-15</span>). The Gospel of John says:

<!-- p75 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p76 -->
> **<u>John 3:16</u>** (NKJV)
>
> For God so loved the world that He gave His only begotten Son, that whoever believes in Him should not perish but have everlasting life.
>

<!-- p77 -->
</span>

<!-- p78 -->
To perish means to be ruined, like fruit that rots before it can be enjoyed. This is a life that loses the purpose for which it was made. Jesus came to give us an enduring—that is, eternal—life so that we would not perish. Eternal life is not a life that begins after we die; it begins the moment we accept Jesus as our salvation and are born again.

### New Birth

<!-- p80 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p81 -->
> **<u>2 Corinthians 5:17</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, that person is a new creation. The old things have passed away; behold, everything has become new.
>

<!-- p82 -->
</span>

<!-- p83 -->
When we receive Jesus, we become people who are “born anew (born again).” This term was used by Jesus to represent the turning point of being born by the Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:1-15</span>). It happens by faith—by choosing, both in heart and in words, to trust Jesus fully, as when entering into a marriage covenant.

<!-- p84 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="9-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p85 -->
> **<u>Romans 10:9-10</u>** (NKJV)
>
> If you confess with your mouth the Lord Jesus and believe in your heart that God raised Jesus from the dead, you will be saved.
>
> One believes with the heart and is justified, and confesses with the mouth and is saved.
>

<!-- p86 -->
</span>

<!-- p87 -->
When we were born on earth (born of flesh and water), many things happened. We took our first breath, were recognized as alive, were received by our parents, were given a name, became heirs, received nourishment and protection, and then continued to grow.

<!-- p88 -->
When we trust Jesus and receive him, we are born anew, not by the flesh but by the Spirit. We become spiritually alive! Just as with physical birth, spiritual birth also entails many things.

<!-- p89 -->
1.  When we received Jesus, God wiped away our sins and forgave us (**justification**). He gave us a fresh start. The dividing wall between God and us was removed. (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>)

<!-- p90 -->
2.  We were spiritually **regenerated** and received the indwelling of the Holy Spirit. (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 6:19</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
3.  We were welcomed into God’s family as those who were **adopted**, and became heirs. (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:5</span>)

<!-- p92 -->
4.  We receive a source of spiritual nourishment and eternal protection. (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:2</span>)
