# Chapter 13 - Spiritual Authority

<!-- p1 -->
Jesus is the ultimate and perfect source of spiritual authority. As the victorious Christ, He holds the final authority over all things in heaven and on earth.

<!-- p2 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p3 -->
> **<u>Matthew 28:18-20</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Then Jesus came and spoke to them, saying, “All authority has been given to Me in heaven and on earth.
>
> Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit,
>
> teaching them to observe all things that I have commanded you; and behold, I am with you always, to the end of the age.” Amen.
>

<!-- p4 -->
</span>

<!-- p5 -->
In this chapter, we will learn about Christ’s authority and power, the authority and power entrusted to Him (us, the Church), and how to operate in the authority of the name of Jesus. In addition, we will take up angelic beings and demonic beings, and examine their authority, power, and nature.

<!-- p6 -->
Authority means having the right to act. Power is the ability to carry it out. Both are essential to spiritual life. Authority is different from power. Look at the following table comparing Authority &amp; Power, and consider a few examples.

<!-- p7 -->
[See Table 7](../tables/table7.md)

<!-- p8 -->
The following are a few examples to help you understand the difference between power and authority.

<!-- p9 -->
Example 1: In the workplace, an employee may have the talent or skill (power) to sell company assets. This power is legitimate when the corresponding permission (authority) has also been given by the owner of the company. Without this authority, selling the company’s assets is theft.

<!-- p10 -->
Example 2: A police officer receives special training in weapons and hand-to-hand combat (power) to learn how to arrest and physically subdue offenders. Police officers are given a legally backed professional responsibility (authority) to enforce criminal laws.

## The source of Jesus' authority

<!-- p74 -->
Since we have looked closely at what authority is, we need to know who the source of that authority is. Jesus' authority is divine, and also delegated. That authority originates from God the Father, is confirmed by His own death and resurrection, and operates in perfect harmony with the Father's will. Though absolute, that authority respects human free will, and displays God's love rather than coercive rule.

<!-- p75 -->
Jesus' authority is rooted in His divine existence as the Son of God, and is bestowed by the Father. The Father has entrusted to Christ all authority, including rule and judgment in heaven and on earth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Gospel of Matthew 11:27</span>). Jesus' authority reflects the unity and love within the Trinity, and the Son perfectly accomplishes the Father's plan (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="35" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Gospel of John 3:35</span>).

<!-- p76 -->
Though Jesus, as the Son of God, possessed inherent authority, His victory over sin, death, and Hades was fully established through His own death and resurrection. By the obedience of offering Himself He was exalted (seated at the Father's right hand), and His authority over all creation was made certain. By conquering death, Christ obtained absolute dominion over life and eternity.

<!-- p115 -->
## Laying on of Hands

<!-- p116 -->
The laying on of hands is an important biblical practice used throughout both the Old and New Testaments, symbolizing blessing, appointment, healing, and the impartation of spiritual authority and gifts. It is more than a mere physical act - it represents spiritual connection and delegation under God's authority.

<!-- p117 -->
In the Old Testament, the laying on of hands was often associated with blessing and consecration. For example, Jacob laid his hands on his grandsons Ephraim and Manasseh and blessed them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="48" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 48:14</span>). Likewise, Moses laid his hands on Joshua to appoint him as his successor, imparting authority and wisdom for leadership (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="NUM" data-chapter="27" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Numbers 27:18-23</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="DEU" data-chapter="34" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Deuteronomy 34:9</span>). This act publicly indicated God's choosing and empowering for the ministry ahead.

<!-- p118 -->
In the New Testament, the laying on of hands is closely connected with the ministry of Jesus and the early church. Jesus regularly laid His hands on the sick and healed them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Gospel of Mark 6:5</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Gospel of Luke 4:40</span>), demonstrating both compassion and authority over sickness and disease. The apostles continued this practice, using it to impart the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:17</span>), to impart spiritual gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 4:14</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 1:6</span>), and to bless believers. It also played a role in appointing leaders and missionaries for service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 13:3</span>).

<!-- p119 -->
The laying on of hands symbolizes an act of faith and obedience to God's word. It is not a mechanical ritual, but a spiritual practice rooted in dependence on the Holy Spirit. For example, in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:6</span>, the apostles laid hands on those chosen to serve as deacons, committing them to God's service and publicly recognizing their calling. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 19:6</span>, when Paul laid his hands on the believers, they received the Holy Spirit, showing its role in spiritual empowerment.

<!-- p120 -->
However, Scripture also gives a warning concerning this practice：

<!-- p121 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p122 -->
> **<u>1 Timothy 5:22</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Do not lay hands on anyone hastily, nor share in other people's sins；keep yourself pure。
>

<!-- p123 -->
</span>

<!-- p124 -->
This emphasizes that discernment and spiritual maturity are essential when appointing individuals to positions of leadership。 As we learned in the chapter on the church、unless leaders are examined and proven、they cannot avoid promoting unqualified or immature believers who may harm the body of Christ。

<!-- p125 -->
A few additional guidelines regarding the laying on of hands：

<!-- p126 -->
1.  Be contextually appropriate - The person receiving the laying on of hands should be a willing participant。

<!-- p127 -->
2.  Be physically appropriate - The laying on of hands does not involve sexual・sensual contact。 In practice、placing a hand on the shoulder、hand、or forehead（with permission）is a manner of touch generally accepted in the West。

<!-- p128 -->
3.  Be culturally appropriate - In some cultures、it is not appropriate for men and women who are not related by blood or marriage to touch each other。

<!-- p129 -->
The laying on of hands continues to be a powerful expression of faith and obedience in the church today。 It is used as a means of blessing、healing、ordination、and commissioning、always pointing to the authority of Christ and the work of the Holy Spirit。 Whether praying for the sick、sending missionaries、or appointing leaders、it reminds believers that they act not in their own power but under the authority of Jesus Christ、in accordance with His will。

<!-- p130 -->
Since we have already studied the authority of Jesus and the authority of believers、we will next discuss other beings in the spiritual realm、namely angels and demons。

<!-- p131 -->
## Angels and Demons

<!-- p132 -->
### Angels

<!-- p133 -->
Angels are an important part of God's creation、serving as God's messengers and as agents who accomplish His will。 Scripture reveals the spiritual realm in which these beings、powerful、intelligent、and obedient to God's commands、dwell。 Though angels are often unseen、their presence and actions impact human history and spiritual warfare。 Understanding their roles、categories、and activities gives us deeper insight into God's plan and the unseen realities that surround us。

<!-- p134 -->
The term "angel" itself means "messenger," highlighting their primary function。 Angels work in accordance with God's will in delivering messages、providing protection、executing judgment、and engaging in spiritual warfare。 They are not objects of worship but models of obedience and reverence、always directing attention to the glory of God。 This chapter explores the nature、classifications、and roles of angels、shedding light on their significance in Scripture and their relevance for our lives today。

<!-- p135 -->
In Scripture、angels are seen as **messengers**、**servants**、and **warriors**。 Angels often serve as messengers、delivering important announcements、warnings、and encouragement。 They reveal God's plans and provide guidance when needed (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26-38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 1:26-38</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 2:13</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 1:1</span>)。 Angels are also actively involved in protecting、strengthening、and delivering believers。 They minister to God's people in times of trouble and provide supernatural help (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="91" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 91:11</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 12:7-11</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 4:11</span>)。 Angels engage in spiritual warfare、contending with demonic forces to advance God's kingdom and protect believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="DAN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Daniel 10:13</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:7-9</span>)。

<!-- p136 -->
#### The Nature of Angels

<!-- p137 -->
Angels are created beings、called into existence by God before the foundations of the world were laid。 They are distinct from humans and possess unique characteristics suited to their service in heaven。 Scripture teaches that angels are spiritual beings not bound by physical limitations、yet they can take on visible form when interacting with people。

<!-- p138 -->
Angels are immortal and are recorded as neither dying nor aging。 They are innumerable、and their vast hosts fill the heavenly realm。 Though they are often referred to with masculine pronouns、Scripture makes clear that angels are sexless and neither marry nor reproduce。 These characteristics underscore their purpose as God's servants、wholly devoted to His will and commands。 Angels possess notable traits that reflect their divine purpose。 They are obedient、reverent、wise、and powerful。 These qualities equip them to carry out God's purposes effectively and faithfully。

<!-- p139 -->
[See Table 8](../tables/table8.md)

<!-- p140 -->
The Bible highlights several roles and titles concerning angels, but it does not present a comprehensive classification system. However, some angels are identified by specific roles or functions:

<!-- p141 -->
- **The Angel of the LORD** - often regarded as a pre-incarnate appearance of Christ, appearing with divine authority. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="23" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 23:20-23</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JDG" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Judges 6:11-24</span>*)

<!-- p142 -->
- **Archangels** - high-ranking angels such as Michael, who leads armies, and Gabriel, who delivers God’s messages. (*Jude 9; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:7</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 1:19-26</span>*)

<!-- p143 -->
- **Cherubim** - guardians of God’s glory and presence, seen in Eden and at the Ark of the Covenant. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 3:24</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 25:22</span>*)

<!-- p144 -->
- **Seraphim** - those who worship God and continually proclaim His holiness. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 6:1-6</span>*)

<!-- p145 -->
- **The elect angels** - faithful angels who resisted Satan’s rebellion and remain loyal to God. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:21</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 25:41</span>*)

<!-- p146 -->
These categories highlight the diversity and order within the angelic realm, yet their primary focus is always to fulfill God’s purposes.

<!-- p147 -->
#### Human Interaction with Angels

<!-- p148 -->
In Scripture, humans often encounter angels, but we are never instructed to command them or pray to them. Rather, angels act according to God’s directives. They serve believers as **God’s servants** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 1:14</span>)。Their interactions include protection, guidance, and deliverance, consistently directing us back to God’s power and sovereignty.

<!-- p149 -->
- **Warning against angel worship**: Scripture forbids worshiping angels or seeking encounters with them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:18</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 22:8-9</span>)。

<!-- p150 -->
- **Pray to God, not to angels**: Believers should pray to God, and He sends angels as He wills (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="91" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 91:11</span>)。

<!-- p151 -->
- **Encouragement and trust**: Angels remind us of God’s care, but our faith should rest wholly in God, not in angels.

<!-- p152 -->
Angels are awe-inspiring beings created to serve God and minister to God’s people. Though their work is often unseen, Scripture reveals glimpses of their activity, showing their faithfulness, power, and reverence. Angels remind us of God’s provision and protection, yet always direct us to God’s glory. As believers, we are called to trust God’s care, knowing that angels are working as His servants to accomplish His purposes.

<!-- p153 -->
### Demons

<!-- p154 -->
Just as the study of angels is necessary to understand the spiritual realm, the study of demons is also necessary. Scripture clearly records their activity, influence, and defeat by Jesus Christ. Recognizing their role in spiritual warfare helps us stand firm in faith without fear.

<!-- p155 -->
#### The Origin and Nature of Demons

<!-- p156 -->
Demons are fallen angels who followed Satan’s rebellion against God. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:7-9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 25:41</span>).* Satan was a highly exalted angel once known as Lucifer（“light-bearer”）, who sought to exalt himself above God and was cast down from heaven along with the other rebellious angels. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 14:12-15</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EZK" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ezekiel 28:12-17</span>).* 

<!-- p157 -->
Demons are spirits that do not have physical bodies, but they can oppress and afflict humans and animals. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 5:11-13</span>).* They deceive, tempt, oppress, and seek to destroy. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 11:14</span>). Unlike God,* they are limited and are not omnipresent（being everywhere）, omniscient（knowing everything）, or omnipotent（having all power）*.* Their ultimate defeat is certain, and they face eternal punishment. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 20:10</span>)*

<!-- p158 -->
Scripture records the work and activity of the devil as follows:

<!-- p159 -->
- Opposing the work of God *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Thessalonians 2:18</span>)*

<!-- p160 -->
- Deceives the world *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 4:4</span>)*

<!-- p161 -->
- Tempts and accuses believers *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 22:31</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:10</span>)*

<!-- p162 -->
- Afflicts and harms *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JOB" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Job 1:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 13:16</span>)*

<!-- p163 -->
The devil has many names and descriptions, each of which represents different aspects of his work.

<!-- p164 -->
[See Table 9](../tables/table9.md)

<!-- p165 -->
The Gospels and Epistles further reveal the work of the enemy. Evil spirits, as disembodied beings, must primarily work through open vessels who willingly receive them. Just as God works through people, so the devil also works through people. This oppression is often characterized by torment, self-harm, and destructive behavior *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 8:27-29</span>).* Their attacks cause fear, temptation, and discouragement *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 12:7</span>).* The most common source through which their power (not authority) operates in a person’s life is through the influence of lies and emotional strongholds *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 10:3-5</span>).*

<!-- p166 -->
It should be noted that not every lie a person believes, every bodily illness, or every stronghold in life is necessarily the direct result of demonic influence; however, such wounded areas can become places the enemy can use to keep us in frustration and oppression.

<!-- p167 -->
#### Jesus’ Authority over the Works of the Enemy

<!-- p168 -->
Do not be afraid; Jesus has complete and absolute authority over the works of the enemy. During His ministry, Jesus demonstrated power over evil spirits *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 1:34</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 5:1-20</span>).* Both the works of the enemy and Satan himself were broken through Jesus’ death and resurrection *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:15</span>).*

<!-- p169 -->
As believers, we have been given authority delegated by Jesus to cast out evil spirits in the name of Jesus.

<!-- p170 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p171 -->
> **<u>Mark 16:17</u>** (NKJV)
>
> These signs will accompany those who believe: In My name they will cast out demons; they will speak in new tongues;
>

<!-- p172 -->
</span>

<!-- p173 -->
Furthermore, as heirs of salvation we have the authority to submit to God and resist the devil, and he will flee from us! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 4:7</span>).

<!-- p174 -->
When dealing with evil or unclean spirits, the pattern shown in Scripture is to take authority over it in the name of Jesus and command it to go out. If we ask, the Holy Spirit promises to give wisdom and discernment! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:5</span>). We do not need to make a spectacle of it, dramatize it like a movie, or talk with them. Such things are the devil’s schemes to distract us. Satan’s power is built and maintained only by the lies he can make us believe.

<!-- p175 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="18" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p176 -->
> **<u>Matthew 18:18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> "Truly, I tell you, whatever you bind on earth will be bound in heaven, and whatever you loose on earth will be loosed in heaven.
>

<!-- p177 -->
</span>

<!-- p178 -->
We speak to them in the name of Jesus and command them to leave.

<!-- p179 -->
When dealing with the demonic, there are two important cautions in Scripture.

<!-- p180 -->
1.  **Only those who have a relationship with Jesus have authority over evil spirits**. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 19:11-20</span> speaks about the sons of a Jewish chief priest named Sceva. They tried to intervene in the life of a man oppressed and tormented by a demon, but because they had no relationship with Jesus, the evil spirit refused to leave. The oppressed man leaped on them, stripped them, and beat them. Just as you must not impersonate a police officer, **do not pretend to be someone who has a relationship with Jesus. Rather, have a relationship with Jesus yourself**!

<!-- p181 -->
2.  **When an evil spirit departs, it may leave for a time, but it will try to return**. If it finds the place where it once lived left empty, it will return and make things worse than before. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="43-45" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 12:43-45</span>. When we minister to someone oppressed by the devil, that person needs to fill the vacuum in their life that the evil spirit occupied. That is, at the very least, to establish a personal relationship with Jesus and strengthen it through obedience to the Holy Spirit. Often, evil or unclean spirits gain power to operate in a person’s life through choices made on the basis of that person’s beliefs. Everyone who has been delivered from the devil needs to replace that old belief system and be transformed by the renewing of the mind（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:1-2</span>）。

<!-- p182 -->
We serve an awesome and powerful God, and we are ‘more than conquerors’ over a defeated enemy（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="37" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:37</span>）。 We do not need to fear a defeated enemy, because Jesus has granted us His authority and given us the power to crush his head! The devil’s time is short, and he will ultimately be judged along with every earthly rule and authority that refuses Christ’s reign.
