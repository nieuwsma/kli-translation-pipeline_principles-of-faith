# Chapter 8 - The Holy Spirit—Comforter, Guide, and Power

<!-- p1 -->
The Holy Spirit is not a new spirit but is of God, and was with God from the beginning. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 1:2</span>, at creation the Spirit of God was moving over the waters. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="NEH" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Nehemiah 9:20</span>, the Bible says it was the Spirit who taught and led the people in the wilderness. In the Old Testament, God anointed kings, judges, and prophets with this same Holy Spirit. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JOL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Joel 2:28-29</span>, it is promised that the Spirit will be poured out on all people. By this same Holy Spirit God raised Jesus from the dead, and He now dwells in us（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:11</span>）. The Holy Spirit is one and always the same. The way He engages with people may appear different from age to age, but His will never changes.

<!-- p2 -->
Jesus gave His disciples—and all of us—a powerful promise about the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p3 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p4 -->
> **<u>John 14:16-18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> I will ask the Father, and He will give you another Helper, that He may be with you forever—
>
> the Spirit of truth. The world cannot receive Him, because it neither sees Him nor knows Him; but you know Him. He dwells with you and will be in you.
>
> I will not leave you as orphans. I will come to you.
>

<!-- p5 -->
</span>

<!-- p6 -->
While Jesus walked the earth in a physical body, He had limitations just like we do. His body needed the same things ours need—rest, water, food, sleep, etc. These limitations affected not only Jesus Himself but also His disciples, because Jesus could be in only one place at a time. When Jesus traveled, large crowds often pressed in, calling out to Him or trying to touch Him. Jesus often needed time with the heavenly Father to be renewed in strength and to pray.

<!-- p7 -->
For the Father's works to increase and spread, Jesus needed to raise up disciples so they could carry on this work. Jesus knew that a crucial aspect of His earthly ministry depended on being connected to and abiding in God the Father. In fact, Jesus said that He could do nothing unless the Father showed Him what to do and did His works through Him (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 5:19</span>). In His wisdom, God sent the Holy Spirit to dwell within us, to lead and direct us, and to empower us to do the Father's works.

<!-- p8 -->
## When was the Holy Spirit sent to all believers?

<!-- p9 -->
Jesus said:

<!-- p10 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p11 -->
> **<u>John 16:7</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Nevertheless, I tell you the truth: it is to your advantage that I go away; for if I do not go away, the Helper will not come to you. But if I go, I will send Him to you.
>

<!-- p12 -->
</span>

<!-- p13 -->
After Jesus was crucified, His death made it possible for the Spirit of God to dwell within us. Until then, God's visible presence was kept primarily behind the thick veil of the temple. At times, specific individuals chosen by God were anointed (consecrated and set apart for special service) and received the Holy Spirit upon them. The anointing and Spirit of God upon them was special, temporary, and conditional. After his sin with Bathsheba, David prayed, “Do not take Your Holy Spirit from me!”（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="51" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 51:11</span>）. David had seen the Spirit depart from King Saul because of his disobedience.

<!-- p14 -->
We (humanity) could not receive the Holy Spirit within because of our sinful nature. But when Jesus dealt with the problem of sin by His own sacrifice, the way was opened for us to become God's children and heirs by faith（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:17</span>）.

<!-- p15 -->
In John chapter 20, after the resurrection, Jesus appeared to His disciples in the middle of a locked room, breathed on them, and said, “Receive the Holy Spirit” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 20:22</span>). There is theological debate about exactly what happened at that moment, but many Pentecostal interpretations view this as the event in which the disciples received the Spirit of God within, signifying regeneration—being born again. However, this was not the event in which the Spirit of God came upon them to empower them with boldness for ministry. This was when they received the Holy Spirit within, enabling them to abide in God and have fellowship with Him. We will address this in more detail later in this chapter and in the chapters on Sanctification and Baptism.

<!-- p16 -->
After that, at the end of the forty days from the resurrection to the ascension (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:3</span>), Jesus gave the promise of the Holy Spirit:

<!-- p17 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="4-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p18 -->
> **<u>Acts 1:4-9</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And being assembled together with them, Jesus commanded them, “Do not depart from Jerusalem, but wait for the promise of the Father, ‘which,’ He said, ‘you heard from Me.
>
> For John truly baptized with water, but you will be baptized with the Holy Spirit not many days from now.’”
>
> Therefore, when they had come together, they asked Him, “Lord, is it at this time You are restoring the kingdom to Israel?”
>
> He said to them, “It is not for you to know the times or seasons which the Father has set by His own authority.
>
> But you will receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you; and you will be My witnesses in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the end of the earth.”
>
> Now when He had spoken these things, while they watched, He was taken up, and a cloud received Him out of their sight.
>

<!-- p19 -->
</span>

<!-- p20 -->
The disciples had to wait for the promise of the Holy Spirit to be poured out, by which they were to receive power to be witnesses to the ends of the earth。 The Holy Spirit is essential to God’s plan for humanity, and for us believers, in order to fulfill our mission, and also for the world, an indispensable presenceです。

<!-- p21 -->
## Knowing the Holy Spirit

<!-- p22 -->
Above all, the Holy Spirit is the one who comes from God and belongs to God。 The Bible calls the Holy Spirit the Spirit of God, the Spirit of the Lord, or the Spirit of Christ。 These emphasize different aspects, but they all refer to the same person。 The Holy Spirit is fully God, and also eternal, holy（blameless, innocent, pure, not profane）です。 In some translations, the Holy Spirit is called the Holy Ghost。

<!-- p23 -->
In Greek, the word for “spirit” is **Pneuma**（**NYOO-mah** - **G4151**）, meaning breath, wind。 The Hebrew word for spirit has the same meaning。 Breath is important because we constantly need it to live。 It was God’s breath that He breathed into Adam to give him life, and likewise, it was that breath that Jesus finally yielded before He died on the cross。 Without breath there is no life。 Breath can be felt and experienced like the wind, but it is not as easily observed as the other “elements” such as water, fire, and earth。

<!-- p24 -->
There are several other core descriptors that represent the Holy Spirit。 These descriptors are used in connection with the phrase “the Spirit of,” indicating that these qualities are essential to defining the Holy Spirit。 For example, the Spirit of truth and the Spirit of life。

<!-- p25 -->
The Spirit of:

<!-- p26 -->
- **Truth** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:17</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span> refers to the Spirit’s role in guiding believers into all truth and making known God’s will and the reality of Christ。

<!-- p27 -->
- **Life** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:2</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:8</span> emphasizes the Spirit’s work of imparting spiritual and eternal life to believers and setting them free from the law of sin and death。

<!-- p28 -->
- **Grace** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:29</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ZEC" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Zechariah 12:10</span> expresses the Spirit’s work of distributing God’s favor, forgiveness, and unmerited kindness to humanity。

<!-- p29 -->
- **Glory** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 4:14</span> indicates the Spirit’s role in manifesting and revealing God’s glory, especially in times of suffering and persecution。

<!-- p30 -->
- **Adoption** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:15</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 4:5</span> refers to the Spirit’s work of making believers children of God and granting them the status of sons and daughters。

<!-- p31 -->
- **Holiness** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 1:4</span> points to the Spirit’s sanctifying work, that is, setting believers apart for God’s purposes。

<!-- p32 -->
- **Power** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 1:7</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span> expresses the Spirit’s work of empowering believers for boldness, service, and the ability to live out the faith effectively。

<!-- p33 -->
In all these attributes, the Holy Spirit is also rightly recognized as the Helper or Comforter。 Jesus refers to the Holy Spirit as the coming Helper, using the Greek word ***Paraklētos**（*pah-RAH-klay-tos - **G3875**）. This means an advocate, a helper, one called alongside。

<!-- p34 -->
An advocate speaks on behalf of another, like a defense attorney。 They intercede and assist the one entrusted to them。

<!-- p35 -->
A helper helps。 They empower and aid the work, but their duty is primarily to assist the work。 A helper is not responsible to carry out the work alone, but comes alongside to help accomplish it。

<!-- p36 -->
The Holy Spirit has several purposes and roles that are distinctly distinguished in His ministry。 His work extends inward（within the hearts and lives of believers）, upward（in service to and the recognition of God）, and outward（to the world, which is the field of our mission）。

<!-- p37 -->
## The Roles of the Holy Spirit - Inside, Upward, Outward

<!-- p38 -->
### Inside - The Holy Spirit unites us to Jesus

<!-- p39 -->
Before Jesus performed any miracles or began His public ministry, He was baptized by John the Baptist, the Spirit of God descended upon Him, and a voice from heaven said, ‘This is my beloved Son, I delight in Him’（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:17</span>）。

<!-- p40 -->
When we receive Jesus by faith, we receive salvation and the seal of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p41 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p42 -->
> **<u>Ephesians 1:13-14</u>** (NKJV)
>
> In Him you also, having heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation, and having believed, were sealed with the Holy Spirit of promise,
>
> who is the guarantee of our inheritance until the redemption of the purchased possession, to the praise of His glory.
>

<!-- p43 -->
</span>

<!-- p44 -->
This seal is not a metaphor but literal. It is the sign of our redemption and a down payment toward the full inheritance we receive as children of God. Romans 8 states that, through regeneration, we receive within our mortal bodies the very Spirit of God who raised Christ from the dead, and that we become a literal temple of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 6:16</span>).

<!-- p45 -->
Receiving the Spirit of God in this way changes us and causes rivers of living water to flow from within us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 7:38</span>). This renewing work is both instantaneous and complete, and yet continues throughout our lives. We must never allow the river of the Holy Spirit to stop flowing in our lives!

<!-- p46 -->
Through salvation and the indwelling of the Holy Spirit, we can abide in Christ and Christ can abide in us. The Spirit of God no longer remains outside, but dwells intimately with us. From now into eternal life, we can walk in fellowship and harmony with God.

<!-- p47 -->
There is much more to say about the inner work of the Holy Spirit and His role in our sanctification, but that will be addressed in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p48 -->
### Upward—The Holy Spirit testifies of Jesus

<!-- p49 -->
The Holy Spirit is from God and belongs to God. Just as Jesus said He does nothing except what the Father shows Him (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 5:19</span>), so the Holy Spirit does nothing without the Father’s direction. Because the Holy Spirit is the Helper, He does not work according to His own will, but in perfect harmony with the Father.

<!-- p50 -->
The Spirit bears witness about Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:26</span>), exalts Jesus, and declares what Jesus has prepared for us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:14</span>).

<!-- p51 -->
The Holy Spirit testifies of Jesus, exalts Jesus, teaches, brings to remembrance Jesus’ teachings (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:26</span>), and also guides us into all truth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span>). Jesus told His disciples that He had many more things to teach them, but they could not bear them then; however, the Holy Spirit would lead them into all truth. This is another subtle theological point. The teachings Jesus wanted His disciples to know were delivered in several ways. Jesus taught them directly both before the cross and after the resurrection, prior to His ascension.

<!-- p52 -->
Jesus also promised that the Spirit of truth poured out upon them on the Day of Pentecost would, by God’s revelation, lead them into all truth. Is there still revelation from God today? Certainly, there is! The gifts of the Holy Spirit, including prophecy, word of knowledge, and word of wisdom, have not ceased! However, no prophecy or word from the Spirit is of private interpretation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2PE" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Peter 1:20</span>). This is extremely important. There are people who falsely claim to be listening to the Holy Spirit, yet their actions and words do not testify of Jesus. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 4:3</span> warns that any spirit that does not testify of Jesus is the spirit of antichrist. Again, if any spirit strays from the teachings of Christ, it is not the Holy Spirit but an unclean spirit!

<!-- p53 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p54 -->
> **<u>1 John 4:1</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Beloved, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits, whether they are of God; because many false prophets have gone out into the world.
>

<!-- p55 -->
</span>

<!-- p56 -->
Throughout Scripture we find examples of false claims. Often they come from false prophets, or from people who, even if well-intentioned, spoke against the will of God. For example, Jeremiah warned about prophets who spoke the visions of their own hearts and cried “peace” when destruction was imminent (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="23" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 23:16-17</span>). Similarly, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 16:16-18</span> shows that the words a slave girl spoke about Paul and Silas were accurate, but they were inspired not by the Holy Spirit, but by an evil spirit. These examples remind us that words which seem plausible and true can still come from ungodly sources.

<!-- p57 -->
Every claim of “God spoke to me!” or of a “new” teaching should be measured against the biblical standard of truth. To discern rightly, we should ask:

<!-- p58 -->
- Does this align with Scripture?

<!-- p59 -->
- Does this exalt Jesus Christ?

<!-- p60 -->
- Does this bear fruit that pleases God?

<!-- p61 -->
By applying these tests, you can discern true guidance by the Holy Spirit from error and deception.

<!-- p62 -->
God customarily gives us sufficient &amp; abundant wisdom and guidance (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:5</span> &amp; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="33" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 33:3</span>). God’s divine wisdom will not contradict Scripture, though it may be revealed beyond the scope of Scripture. His *entolē* *(*commandment*)* is a prescription to be obeyed immediately, and it is a *rhema* word (as stated in the chapter on faith).

<!-- p63 -->
This wisdom, understanding, and guidance are not necessarily recorded verbatim in Scripture. For example, the Bible gives excellent general guidelines for what to look for in a spouse, but it does not give a specific prescription about a particular person. God has promised to direct our paths and to lead us into all truth. And we can take comfort in knowing that this wisdom and Spirit-led guidance will never contradict any teaching of Scripture.

<!-- p64 -->
### Outward - The Holy Spirit empowers the mission

<!-- p65 -->
Jesus has entrusted to us His own mission, namely the Great Commission:

<!-- p66 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p67 -->
> **<u>Matthew 28:18-20</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Then Jesus came and spoke to them, saying, "All authority has been given to Me in heaven and on earth.
>
> Go therefore and make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit,
>
> teaching them to observe all things that I have commanded you; and behold, I am with you always, to the end of the age." Amen.
>

<!-- p68 -->
</span>

<!-- p69 -->
The Holy Spirit plays an indispensable role in this mission, bringing conviction to the world regarding sin, righteousness, and judgment:

<!-- p70 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="8-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p71 -->
> **<u>John 16:8-11</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And when He comes, He will convict the world concerning sin, and righteousness, and judgment.
>
> Concerning sin, because they do not believe in Me,
>
> Concerning righteousness, because I go to the Father and you see Me no more,
>
> Concerning judgment, because the ruler of this world has been judged.
>

<!-- p72 -->
</span>

<!-- p73 -->
Going further, the Holy Spirit brings conviction regarding the following three truths:

<!-- p74 -->
1.  **Conviction of sin**: making clear the reality that humans are in rebellion against God, and directing people’s attention to the corruption of their own lives.

<!-- p75 -->
2.  **Conviction of righteousness**: showing that Jesus perfectly fulfilled the Father’s will, and that Jesus’ sacrifice provided the once-for-all atonement required for the forgiveness of sins.

<!-- p76 -->
3.  **Conviction of judgment**: affirming Satan’s defeat, the certainty of God’s justice, and that all will be judged for the works and deeds of their lives.

<!-- p77 -->
The Holy Spirit also empowers this mission. The Holy Spirit gives believers the power to be bold witnesses who share the gospel even in the face of persecution. Boldness does not mean being loud; it means having courage despite fear. Power means might, strength, and ability. The Holy Spirit gives us the strength and ability to be witnesses to the gospel despite threats and fear of punishment.

<!-- p78 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p79 -->
> **<u>Acts 1:8</u>** (NKJV)
>
> "But you shall receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you; and you shall be My witnesses in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the ends of the earth."

<!-- p80 -->
</span>

<!-- p81 -->
The Book of Acts as a whole is filled with stories and accounts that follow this general cycle:

<!-- p82 -->
1.  **Prayer**: The believers seek boldness and God's intervention.

<!-- p83 -->
2.  **Empowerment**: The Holy Spirit fills them with courage, power, and guidance.

<!-- p84 -->
3.  **Proclamation**: They often boldly proclaim the gospel, accompanied by signs, wonders, and miracles.

<!-- p85 -->
4.  **Opposition**: Resistance arises, leading to persecution and trials. At times believers are martyred, and at other times they are imprisoned or beaten.

<!-- p86 -->
5.  **Renewal**: The church returns to prayer, seeks fresh empowerment, and the cycle continues.

<!-- p87 -->
The church of the New Testament grew greatly while facing resolute opposition. Around the world, the church suffers violence at the hands of oppressors and evil rulers who seek to suppress the knowledge of the truth of Jesus.

<!-- p88 -->
God knew that this opposition would occur. Jesus prophesied about it:

<!-- p89 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p90 -->
> **<u>Luke 12:11-12</u>** (NKJV)
>
> "Now when they bring you to the synagogues and magistrates and authorities, do not worry about how or what you should answer, or what you should say.
>
> For the Holy Spirit will teach you in that very hour what you ought to say."
>

<!-- p91 -->
</span>

<!-- p92 -->
Do not fear! Jesus promised us this:

<!-- p93 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p94 -->
> **<u>Matthew 16:18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And I also say to you that you are Peter, and on this rock I will build My church, and the gates of Hades shall not prevail against it.
>

<!-- p95 -->
</span>

<!-- p96 -->
This is a remarkable, comforting promise. Gates are fixed defensive equipment. When Jesus says that the *gates of Hades will not prevail*, the Lord is painting the picture of a conquering army battering down the gates and taking the city!

<!-- p97 -->
The Holy Spirit brings a supernatural boldness to proclaim the testimony of the gospel even in the face of persecution. The results of this empowerment can be described, but the best thing is to experience it. It is a supernatural (beyond human) boldness that compels believers to share. This missional boldness is often accompanied by supernatural acts through manifestations of the Spirit and power (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 2:1-5</span>). People are healed physically and emotionally, the dead are raised, those tormented by evil spirits are set free, and those with stubborn hearts turn and repent.

<!-- p98 -->
Jesus said that we would do these works, and even greater! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:12</span>). These collective works are supernatural and divine, and are desperately needed in this world that is self-destructing, miserable, and full of pain, longing for the mature sons of God to be clearly revealed! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:22-23</span>)

<!-- p99 -->
The vessel Jesus has chosen to bring change on earth is the Lord's church, empowered, emboldened, and led by the Holy Spirit.

## Building a Relationship with the Holy Spirit

<!-- p101 -->
Every relationship needs time and experience to grow deep. The Holy Spirit is our advocate, comforter, helper, and guide. The Holy Spirit empowers us and sanctifies us.

<!-- p102 -->
Begin to think of the Holy Spirit as a person. In a relationship, we can build it together or do things that weaken it. The Holy Spirit can be ignored, quenched, or blasphemed, and he can also be listened to, honored, and obeyed.

<!-- p103 -->
Do not ignore the voice of the Holy Spirit. The Holy Spirit is always speaking to us and to the Church. If you don’t yet know how to discern the voice of the Holy Spirit, it takes time, willingness, and effort (just like any healthy, beneficial relationship). But don’t worry. Everything you need to begin building a personal relationship with the Holy Spirit is already within you.

<!-- p104 -->
The quickest and surest ways to build an effective relationship with the Holy Spirit are

<!-- p105 -->
1.  In prayer, call out to the Holy Spirit **personally**, pause, and wait for him to speak.

<!-- p106 -->
2.  As part of a **group** of people who know him (the Church), call out to the Holy Spirit, pause, and wait for him to speak.

<!-- p107 -->
I use the term ‘call out’ to mean literally speaking with your mouth, or communicating in whatever way you speak (with your hands if you cannot speak out loud). Why speak? There are several reasons:

<!-- p108 -->
1.  We are speaking spirits made in the image of God. God speaks, and we speak.

<!-- p109 -->
2.  We use speaking as our primary means of communication. You can also write down what you want to say (journaling) and read it aloud.

<!-- p110 -->
3.  Speaking is an intentional act and signals to the one you’re addressing that you want to have a conversation.

<!-- p111 -->
#### What should you say?

<!-- p112 -->
If you’ve never spoken to God, try introducing yourself. It will certainly feel a bit strange, but that awkwardness is okay and temporary. In addition to introducing yourself, tell God what you want to talk about, and that you would like him to speak back to you as well.

<!-- p113 -->
Consider asking God questions. They don’t need to be deep theological questions. You can start with simple ones. Could you show me your love for me? Why am I feeling a certain way? What do you want to say to me?

<!-- p114 -->
#### What next?

<!-- p115 -->
In an effective relationship, after speaking a little, you pause and listen. God speaks.

<!-- p116 -->
God speaks in many ways, but he desires to speak personally and internally. It will likely not be an audible voice (like your best friend calling your name out loud), but an inner voice that speaks to your heart and thoughts. Elijah calls this a ‘still small voice’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1KI" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Kings 19:11-13</span>).

<!-- p117 -->
The most common ways I have personally witnessed God speaking to people (I’ve been fortunate to help dozens learn how to talk with God directly) are some combination of the following:

<!-- p118 -->
- A sense of knowing - the person who is speaking to God receives an immediate, helpful understanding.

<!-- p119 -->
- Images - also called visions and dreams in the Bible (visions when you are awake, dreams when you are asleep). God gives images to your mind. It may be a memory, or a place you have been or know. Explore the image. What do you see? As a result, what do you feel? What is happening?

<!-- p120 -->
- Inner voice - It is like an actual audible voice, yet only you can hear it, and it speaks to your heart and mind.

<!-- p121 -->
#### What would God say?

<!-- p122 -->
We don't know for sure! But that's the interesting part. We can at least say this: He is not a spirit of fear but of perfect love, and what He speaks will never contradict the teaching of Scripture. For example, God will never tell you to commit adultery.

<!-- p123 -->
So, what do you do next?

<!-- p124 -->
Keep the conversation going! That's really all there is to it.

<!-- p125 -->

<!-- p126 -->

<!-- p127 -->
- *Good Morning Holy Spirit* by Benny Hinn ([<u>www.bennyhinn.org</u>](http://www.bennyhinn.org))

### Why build a relationship?

<!-- p129 -->
The Holy Spirit is the Helper. The Helper helps us! If we share a common language and have a relationship with the Holy Spirit, the work will go much more smoothly. Can you imagine working with someone at work with whom you have no relationship, don't speak the same language, and can't communicate?!

<!-- p130 -->
The God we serve desires to converse with us. Jesus said that His sheep know His voice (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:27</span>). We already possess the natural faculties we need, though we may lack the proficiency that comes with experience.

<!-- p131 -->
From personal experience, a personal and vibrant relationship with the Holy Spirit is one of the best gifts of being a Christian. His presence brings peace, comfort, and direction, but only when you know how to seek, hear, and obey His voice.

<!-- p132 -->
Cultivate sensitivity to the Holy Spirit. As happens with many couples over the years, you can communicate with your spouse in a unique way, sometimes with just a glance. From that single look, you know what has happened, what is about to happen, everything that is needed. Build that same sensitivity and intimacy with the Holy Spirit. Welcome Him into every part of your life. This is a wonderful aspect of the relationship that will add depth and richness to your walk of faith.

<!-- p133 -->
The Holy Spirit came to guide us, empower us to live as God's people, and to see people on earth fundamentally transformed and set free from the kingdom of darkness into the kingdom of light. Knowing the Holy Spirit and building trust with Him is essential both for daily life and for accomplishing the work Jesus has entrusted to us.
