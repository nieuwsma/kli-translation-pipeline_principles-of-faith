# Hearing the Voice of God

<!-- p1 -->
The following contributed appendix by Pastor Larry Low ([<u>www.LarryLow.com</u>](http://www.larrylow.com)) complements our exploration of the role of the Holy Spirit and provides practical insights into hearing the voice of God. The following guest contribution is used with permission.

<!-- p2 -->
During prayer one morning, I asked **God** the following question: “What is the most important thing I can teach Your people to prepare them for the age that is coming upon us?” The Lord answered very quickly, and I sensed a great urgency in His words. “**Teach them to hear My voice.**”

<!-- p3 -->
When the Lord gives such instruction, I always take a notepad and pencil and begin to write what little I know, or think I know, along with the Scripture passages that support it. I love to search the Word regarding the subjects the Lord is leading me to teach. I also love the anointing for preaching, but I truly love teaching the Word. It is an adventure to discover things that have been there all along yet overlooked.

<!-- p4 -->
David must also have longed to unveil God’s truth, for he prayed this in Psalm 119:18: “Open my eyes, that I may see wondrous things from Your law.”

<!-- p5 -->
That is what I want to see - wondrous things from the Word.

<!-- p6 -->
As I was studying and preparing the second message on discerning the voice of God, the Lord spoke to my heart as follows:

<!-- p7 -->
“*Teach them to hear My voice,  
Teach them to honor and obey.*

<!-- p8 -->
*Yes, teach them to hear My voice, and I will always lead them away:  
away from troubles that would destroy them,*

<!-- p9 -->
*away from the trials they now face,  
to a place of peace and love and joy that very few have ever entered.*

<!-- p10 -->
*to a place of renewal,  
to a place of divine ecstasy.*

<!-- p11 -->
*Oh yes, son, teach them to hear My voice,  
and these people will be free forever*.”

<!-- p12 -->
05/27/00 9:10 A.M.

<!-- p13 -->
The following 11 points should help you understand how the Lord may speak to you:

<!-- p14 -->
1.  <u>Hearing God’s voice is not an audible sound, but a conviction in the heart.</u>

<!-- p15 -->
There was a time when the Word came so clearly and powerfully that it seemed like an audible voice. It was so real that I responded out loud. However, since there was no one there but the Lord and me, I cannot say for certain whether it was actually spoken with a voice as we speak to one another.

<!-- p16 -->
When Jesus was baptized in the Jordan, the Father said: “This is My beloved Son; in Him I am well pleased,” yet some of those who were there said it sounded like thunder. So, while I think it is possible that the Lord spoke to me in an audible voice, for now, when I say “God spoke to me,” please understand that it is a conviction in the heart rather than a sound heard with the ears.

<!-- p17 -->
2.  <u>That conviction is not built by the logic in your mind; rather, it is a complete thought, or understanding.</u>

<!-- p18 -->
The Lord has never spoken anything to me that brought confusion. He has never spoken anything that made me tilt my head and ask, “Why?” It is always a conviction accompanied by complete understanding. God is not a God of confusion (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:33</span>).

<!-- p19 -->
3.  <u>When the Lord speaks, He speaks in complete thoughts.</u>

<!-- p20 -->
He never makes us fill in the blanks ourselves. In the blink of an eye, God is able to fill your heart with enough information to keep preaching to you for a lifetime. With a single touch from God, you are filled with a fire that will burn forever. He is a perfect communicator.

<!-- p21 -->
4.  <u>When God speaks to us, He never drives us. He leads.</u>

<!-- p22 -->
God is not a foreman but a shepherd. A drover drives the flock, but a shepherd leads. Jesus is the great Shepherd of the sheep, and we are the sheep of His pasture.

<!-- p23 -->
5.  <u>When God speaks to His children, He never condemns; He gives assurance.</u>

<!-- p24 -->
God’s children are those who have been born anew by confessing in faith the blood shed by **Jesus** and His work at Calvary (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:3</span>, 5). Who wants to listen to someone who is always harping on our failures? I think many of **God’s** children are afraid of **God** speaking to them because they have been taught that **God** berates them about this or that. Look at <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 3:19-20</span>. Who does it say is accusing and condemning us? It is our own spirit, that is, our heart, that accuses **God’s** child, but **God the Holy Spirit**, who knows all things, is greater than our hearts. The Holy Spirit is our Comforter. Read the following passages. You will see that the Holy Spirit condemns only sinners. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33-34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:33-34</span> teaches that nothing can come between **God’s** love and us. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:16</span>, 26 teaches that the Holy Spirit is our Comforter, not an accuser; our Counselor, not a chastiser; our Intercessor and Helper, not a hinderer. Read <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 9:31</span> and 15:28 as examples of how the Holy Spirit (**God’s** voice) helps, comforts, and strengthens us.

<!-- p25 -->
6.  <u>The voice of God can be tested. You can know that it is from God and not your own thoughts or the voice of a familiar spirit.</u>

<!-- p26 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 4:1-3</span> teaches us to “test the spirits,” so that by doing so we may verify whether they are from God. When you think God is speaking, you have the right and responsibility to question that voice (that is what it means to test or prove the spirits). The question to ask is: “Did Jesus Christ come in the flesh?” The one thing a familiar spirit will never acknowledge is the fact that Jesus came to earth in the flesh. Jesus was fully man and fully God. False spirits and religions do not acknowledge that Jesus came to this earth in the flesh and redeemed humanity forever from all sin. Test the voice you hear. Also know that God will never lead you outside His written Word, the Bible. If you are hearing something that cannot be supported by Scripture, it is not from God. Cast it away! Test the voice you hear to see whether it is from God.

<!-- p27 -->
7.  <u>The voice of God always brings glory to Jesus Christ.</u>

<!-- p28 -->
The Holy Spirit is the voice of heaven. It is the Holy Spirit who works with us on earth, and Jesus said that the Holy Spirit speaks only what He hears and always brings glory to Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13-14</span>). The Holy Spirit is not here to bring glory to your ministry, your achievements, or church programs. Those are good things and often necessary for what we are to do, but the Holy Spirit speaks and brings glory to the Person of Jesus. If the voice you are hearing results in you receiving glory, it is not the voice of God. There is a difference between receiving glory and being encouraged and strengthened. The Holy Spirit will always encourage and strengthen you every time He speaks, even when He is correcting you with the Word (in a leading way). But to “receive glory” means you believe that you are somebody. Without Jesus you are nothing—“the Somebody” is Jesus, not us.

<!-- p29 -->
8.  <u>**God’s** voice always reveals something to us.</u>

<!-- p30 -->
Look at <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:14</span>. The Holy Spirit always takes what is **Jesus’** and shows it to us. The Amplified, when considering the word “reveal,” adds the words declare, disclose, and transmit. “**God**” always discloses something to us after it has been declared, and then transmits it to us through your faith. Study <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 2:10</span> and 12 as well.

<!-- p31 -->
9.  <u>**God’s** voice always brings peace.</u>

<!-- p32 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:26</span> and 27, and <span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 3:14</span> and 15. The voice of God never agitates us. It is a voice that brings perfect peace. Inner peace comes from a “knowing” that brings confidence before God, and that leads to perfect peace. If the voice you are hearing is agitating you, it is not from God—cut it off. Jesus promised to give a peace unlike what the world gives, through the comforting words and constant presence of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:27</span>).

<!-- p33 -->
10. <u>**God’s** voice always brings truth.</u>

<!-- p34 -->
Read <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:17</span>, 26; 16:13. In fact, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span> calls the Holy Spirit “the Spirit of truth.” God cannot lie! God is truth, and therefore He speaks only truth. I have heard people say, “I just can’t believe what I’m hearing is from God. It’s too good to be true.” And it may indeed seem too good to be true; that’s precisely why you must always test the spirits against the Word of God to confirm that it is the truth. Some people say they can’t believe it because they are always hearing positive things. That is because they have been taught that God rebukes our mistakes. God speaks only truth, and it is always good news. The sad reality is that, because many people only receive bad news from the voices they are listening to and are not expecting to hear good news, when good news comes they suspect it might not be true. **Truth is always good news!** Since it is the **truth** that sets us free, how could the **truth** be anything but good news?

<!-- p35 -->
11. <u>**God’s** voice always leads us forward and upward.</u>

<!-- p36 -->
Read Deut. 28:1-2, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="30" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 30:21</span>, and <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span>

<!-- p37 -->
God will never bring you down! He will never belittle or demean you; He only builds you up. When God speaks to you, He speaks from the premise that everything the Word says about you has already been accomplished and has been accepted by you. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="33" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 33:3</span> says, “Call to Me, and I will answer you, and show you <b>great and mighty</b> things which you do not know.” Does that sound like He would belittle you or speak doom and gloom? There is no doom and gloom in God whatsoever. Therefore God cannot tell you something that sounds gloomy and dark. If you want to hear doom and gloom, you will have to tune in to the world’s news outlets.

<!-- p38 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="29" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p39 -->
> **<u>Jeremiah 29:11</u>** (NKJV)
>
> For I know the thoughts that I think toward you, says the LORD, thoughts of peace and not of evil, to give you a future and a hope.

<!-- p40 -->
</span>

<!-- p41 -->
Now then, if God has good thoughts toward us—peace, a future, and hope—why would He say anything different? The answer is this: He never would, and He doesn't! God, with great hope, simply leads us upward and forward to give us the future we hope for.

<!-- p42 -->
I hope this serves as an encouragement for you to learn how to "call on the Lord" and to **hear the Lord's voice**. God has many wonderful things to say to you, but for all those good things to benefit you, you must first listen.
