# Chapter 7 - Jesus-Our Faith’s Foundation

<!-- p1 -->
Building on our study of Jesus as the Messiah, this chapter continues to turn our attention to Jesus’s life, character, and work。We will see that Jesus is the mediator of a new and better covenant and that he perfectly fulfills the roles of prophet, priest, and king。It is in this new covenant that we believers are placed in a sound standing before God and move from a works-based covenant to a trust（faith）-based covenant。Finally, to conclude our study about Jesus, we will read and study the *Apostles’ Creed*。

<!-- p2 -->
## The Mediator of the New Covenant

<!-- p3 -->
The concept of a mediator refers to one who stands between two parties to bridge the gap and facilitate a relationship or agreement。In the Exodus story, Moses is the first mediator of the covenant God made with his people。Moses received the Law, offered sacrifices（priest）, called God’s people to obedience（prophet）, and judged them（judge/king）。In the Old Testament after Moses, these mediatory roles were carried out through the prophets, the Levitical priesthood, and the judges, and later the kings。

<!-- p4 -->
The roles of mediators in the Old Testament:

<!-- p5 -->
1.  **Prophet:** Prophets were God’s messengers, sent to speak God’s heart to the people and to call them back when they strayed。For example, Moses served as the prophet who mediated the old covenant by receiving the Law from God and teaching it to Israel。The prophets also foretold the coming of the Messiah and the ultimate redemption of humanity。Other notable prophets include Isaiah, Jeremiah, Elijah, Elisha, and Samuel。

<!-- p6 -->
2.  **Levitical priesthood:** The priests acted as mediators by offering sacrifices for the atonement of sin and interceding before God on behalf of the people。In particular, the high priest performed the crucial duty on the Day of Atonement of entering the Most Holy Place and sprinkling blood on the mercy seat, symbolizing reconciliation with God。The duty of the priests is to intercede for the people。

<!-- p7 -->
3.  **Judges/Kings:** The judges, and later the kings, bore the responsibility of governing God’s people, enforcing God’s Law, judging among the people, delivering them from enemies, and leading them in righteousness。The book of Judges relates the stories of these leaders, such as Deborah, Samson, and Gideon, whom God raised up to save and guide Israel。Israel then had a line of kings, many bad and some good（see 1<sup>st</sup> and 2<sup>nd</sup> Kings、1<sup>st</sup> and 2<sup>nd</sup> Samuel、1<sup>st</sup> and 2<sup>nd</sup> Chronicles）。Notable examples include Saul, David, and Solomon。

<!-- p8 -->
It is noteworthy that all the mediators of the old covenant were fully human and not God。Being human, they were capable of sin and often fell into it。Some were sinful, and as false prophets, priests full of falsehood and corruption, and wicked kings, they led people away from God into impious worship。

<!-- p9 -->
### Christ as the Mediator of the New Covenant

<!-- p10 -->
Jesus’s death, resurrection, and ascension fulfill the old covenant and establish the new covenant。

<!-- p11 -->
The new covenant is the transformative relationship between God and his people established through the life, death, and resurrection of Jesus Christ。Unlike the old covenant, which focused on external law, the new covenant emphasizes inner transformation and grace。It was instituted by Jesus at the Last Supper, where he declared that his blood establishes this covenant, replacing the repeated animal sacrifices of the old covenant with his once-for-all redemption（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 22:20</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:10</span>）。Through this sacrifice, the new covenant provides complete forgiveness and cleansing from sin and meets the conditions necessary for full and lasting reconciliation with God（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 31:34</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:12-18</span>）。

<!-- p12 -->
One of the central features of the new covenant is its internal character: God writes his law on the hearts of his people and fosters a heart-level relationship with God through the Holy Spirit（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 31:33</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 3:3</span>）。This shift enables believers to live out God’s will not by mere adherence to external commands but by empowerment from the Spirit（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:1-4</span>）。The new covenant also grants direct access to God and removes the need for an earthly priesthood。Jesus serves as the eternal High Priest, and believers are called a royal priesthood, able to approach God boldly through Christ’s sacrifice（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 7:24-25</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:19-20</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:9</span>）。

<!-- p13 -->
Unlike the old covenant, which was limited to Israel, the new covenant is universal and extends to all who believe in Christ, regardless of race or background（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 3:28</span>）。This covenant is entirely based on grace, freely granting salvation and forgiveness through faith in Jesus（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>）。It is sustained not by human effort but by the power of the Holy Spirit, who transforms believers and empowers them to live in obedience and reflect God’s character（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EZK" data-chapter="36" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ezekiel 36:26-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:9-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-23</span>）。Furthermore, the new covenant is everlasting, surpassing the temporary and conditional nature of the old covenant（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="32" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 32:40</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:13</span>）。It represents the ultimate fulfillment of God’s plan of salvation, reconciling humanity to God himself and enabling believers to live forever as God’s people under his rule。

<!-- p14 -->
That Jesus is the Mediator of the new covenant is made clear in the following passage

<!-- p15 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p16 -->
> **<u>Hebrews 9:15</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And for this reason He is the Mediator of the new covenant, that by means of death, for the redemption of the transgressions under the first covenant, those who are called may receive the promise of the eternal inheritance。
>

<!-- p17 -->
</span>

<!-- p18 -->
Through his atoning death and resurrection, Christ inaugurated this new covenant and, in place of the old system of sacrifices and external law, brought a covenant written on the hearts of believers（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 31:31-34</span>）。He stands between God and humanity, not only interceding for the people but also providing the means of reconciliation by his own blood（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:12</span>）。Unlike the mediators of the old covenant, Jesus is sinless and eternal, able to satisfy perfectly God’s demands of righteousness and mercy and to secure an enduring relationship between God and believers。

<!-- p19 -->
Jesus fulfills and completes these roles

<!-- p20 -->
1.  **Prophet:** Jesus is the ultimate Prophet, and not merely a messenger of God’s word, but the Word incarnate (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:14</span>). He fully and sufficiently reveals God’s nature, will, and plan of salvation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 1:1-2</span>). His teaching, miracles, and prophetic declarations fulfill Old Testament prophecy and provide the foundation of the New Covenant.

<!-- p21 -->
2.  **Priest:** Jesus is the perfect High Priest who offered Himself once for all as an eternal sacrifice for sin (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 7:27</span>). Unlike the priests of the Old Testament who had to offer sacrifices repeatedly for their own sins and for those of the people, the sinless Jesus offered His own blood to obtain eternal redemption (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:14</span>).

<!-- p22 -->
3.  **King &amp; Judge:** Jesus is the eternal King who reigns with righteousness and justice. As the Son of David, He fulfills the promise of an everlasting kingdom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2SA" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Samuel 7:16</span>). His kingship is not merely earthly but cosmic, ruling over all creation and establishing peace through His victory over sin and death (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 2:9-11</span>). Jesus will come again as King and Judge and will judge ‘both the living and the dead’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 10:42</span>).

<!-- p23 -->
In Christ, the offices of Prophet, Priest, and King are gathered into one and perfectly fulfilled. As Mediator of the New Covenant, He bridges the separation between the holy God and sinful humanity, enabling believers to approach God with confidence (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 4:16</span>), and He makes certain the eternal inheritance promised in Him.

## The Apostles' Creed

<!-- p25 -->
The *Apostles’ Creed* is not found in the Bible, but was composed and revised based on the earliest teachings of the early church. A creed is a formal confession of faith and can be thought of as a combination of an oath or pledge and a confession of faith. Creeds are important because they are used to unite and also to distinguish those who believe in a particular way from those who do not. In the United States, it is common in civic settings, such as city councils or legislative gatherings, for attendees to recite the Pledge of Allegiance together. That pledge is widely used in the United States, but not in other countries, because the citizens of those countries do not owe allegiance to the United States. This pledge does not make a person ‘American,’ and yet it expresses beliefs that are important and essential for those who are citizens.

<!-- p26 -->
The earliest recognized form of this creed was written in the fourth century AD. Over the centuries the creed was slightly modified, and there are minor differences among denominations. Not every Christian denomination uses this creed, but for the most part it is widely accepted in some form throughout Christianity.

<!-- p27 -->
For centuries, the Apostles’ Creed has been used as a tool for teaching and catechesis, conveying the most basic truths of the Christian faith—that is, the foundational beliefs that must be acknowledged and held in order to be a Christian. Some denominations have a formal process of catechesis by which new believers are added to that particular denomination and guided into its faith.

<!-- p28 -->
A person does not need to have a complete understanding of these beliefs in order to come to God and receive salvation. Salvation comes by faith, not by a doctrinal test! However, as we continue to walk in that faith, we should all come to understand these basic truths.

## A Brief Explanation of the Apostles' Creed

<!-- p31 -->
The fundamental truths in the Apostles’ Creed are numerous and very profound. A complete explanation would certainly require multiple volumes. This chapter speaks about Jesus, but many subjects also span the other Persons of the Godhead. Here we confine ourselves to a concise explanation of the Creed and focus only on the most central themes. Several of these subjects are studied more deeply in the chapters of this book.

<!-- p32 -->
#### *I believe in God, the Father Almighty, Creator of heaven and earth.*

<!-- p33 -->
This affirms faith in God as the omnipotent, sovereign Creator, and as the Father. The title "Father" emphasizes the loving relationship He has toward His creatures, especially those who have been made God’s children by faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:12</span>). As Creator, God called the universe into existence by speaking, bringing order out of chaos (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 1:1-3</span>). God’s almighty power is displayed in the vastness of creation, and His fatherly care is seen in His continual preservation and providence. This foundational declaration establishes that God is the source and ruler of all life and shows that He is worthy of worship and trust.

<!-- p34 -->
#### *I believe in Jesus Christ, God’s only Son, our Lord.*

<!-- p35 -->
Jesus Christ is the only-begotten Son of God, eternally begotten, not made, and shares in the divine nature (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:1-2</span>, 14). Jesus is the ‘Word’ from the beginning, who became flesh and dwelt among us. As the "Christ," He is the anointed Messiah who fulfills the Old Testament promises concerning the Savior. The title "Lord" acknowledges His authority over all creation and His rightful place as Ruler and King (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 2:10-11</span>). He is not merely Lord, but the Lord who saves (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 16:31</span>).

<!-- p36 -->
Jesus was **not only** sent by God, but is **a part of God**. Jesus is in the Father, and the Father is in Jesus. The Gospel of John repeatedly shows that Jesus and the Father are one (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="30" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:30</span>), that Jesus did not act on His own initiative (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 5:19</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="49-50" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 12:49-50</span>), that everything He did was for the Father’s glory (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="17" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 17:4</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:13</span>), and that these things were done by the Father’s authority and power (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 8:28-29</span>).

<!-- p37 -->
#### *He was conceived by the power of the Holy Spirit and born of the Virgin Mary.*

<!-- p38 -->
This affirms the miraculous conception of Jesus by the Holy Spirit and highlights His divine origin. Born of the Virgin Mary, Jesus fulfilled the prophecy of the Messiah’s virgin birth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 7:14</span>). This event shows the incarnation, meaning that the eternal Word of God took on human flesh (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:14</span>). Being truly God and truly man, Jesus bridges the gap between God and humanity, making it possible for Him to be the perfect Mediator and Savior.

<!-- p39 -->
Jesus was conceived by the seed of the Holy Spirit and did not inherit the sin nature that we, born from human seed, possess. Jesus was tempted and tested in every respect, yet was perfect and without sin. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 4:15</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 5:21</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:22</span>.

<!-- p40 -->
#### *He suffered under Pontius Pilate, was crucified, died, and was buried.*

<!-- p41 -->
The suffering and death of Jesus were real historical events that occurred under the Roman governor Pontius Pilate, grounding the Christian faith in verifiable history. His crucifixion was the fulfillment of Old Testament prophecy, as seen in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="53" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 53:5</span>, which speaks of the suffering servant who bears the sins of many. Jesus’ death was sacrificial, bearing the penalty of sin on behalf of humanity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:24</span>). His burial confirmed the reality of his death and prepared for the significance of the resurrection.

<!-- p42 -->
#### *He descended to the dead.*

<!-- p43 -->
After his death, Jesus descended to the realm of the dead, often called *Hades* or *Sheol* in Scripture. This act shows that he fully identified with humanity even unto death and signifies victory over sin, death, and the devil (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 3:18-20</span>). By descending, he proclaimed his triumph and the completion of his redemptive work.

<!-- p44 -->
#### *On the third day he rose again.*

<!-- p45 -->
The resurrection of Jesus is the foundation of our Christian faith and confirms his divinity and the truth of his teaching. By rising from the dead, he conquered sin and death and gave believers the assurance of eternal life (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 15:17-22</span>). The resurrection also fulfills Scripture, including Jesus’ own predictions (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 16:21</span>). This astonishing event transformed the disciples’ despair into bold faith and became the foundation of the early church’s witness.

<!-- p46 -->
#### *He ascended into heaven and is seated at the right hand of the Father.*

<!-- p47 -->
Jesus’ ascension indicates that he returned to the Father’s presence in glory. Seated at the Father’s right hand (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:20</span>), he carries out his office as the eternal High Priest, interceding for believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 7:25</span>). Moreover, his authoritative throne also means that he is exalted as King, rules over all creation, guides the mission of the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:22</span>), enables us to do greater works (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:12</span>), and brings to fulfillment the Great Commission given to us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:18-20</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 16:15-18</span>). The ascension assures believers of his continual presence and power at work through the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p48 -->
#### *He will come again to judge the living and the dead.*

<!-- p49 -->
This points to Christ’s eventual return to bring final justice and restoration. Jesus will judge all people, rewarding the faithful and bringing justice upon the unrepentant (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 4:1</span>). His return will fulfill God’s ultimate plan for creation and usher in his eternal kingdom of righteousness and peace. This hope encourages believers to live faithfully and expectantly, knowing that labor in the Lord is not in vain (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="58" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 15:58</span>).

<!-- p50 -->
#### *I believe in the Holy Spirit.*

<!-- p51 -->
The Holy Spirit, the third person of the Trinity, is the presence and power of God at work in the world today. He convicts of sin, regenerates hearts, and empowers believers to live holy lives (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:8-13</span>). He unites Christians into one body—the church—and equips them with spiritual gifts for service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:4-11</span>). The Holy Spirit is the Comforter, Helper, and Guide, who dwells within believers as the seal and guarantee of their eternal inheritance (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>).

<!-- p52 -->
#### *The holy catholic Church, the communion of saints.*

<!-- p53 -->
"The holy catholic Church" refers to the universal body of Christ, consisting of all believers across time and place. The church is set apart for God’s purposes, called to reflect God’s holiness and to share the gospel. "The communion of saints" emphasizes that all Christians, both living and dead, are one as members of the body of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:4-6</span>). This fellowship transcends earthly separations and points believers to a common hope and mission.

<!-- p54 -->
Jesus is the head of the church. The church is called the Bride of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="25-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:25-27</span>) and the Body of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 1:18</span>).

<!-- p55 -->
#### *The forgiveness of sins.*

<!-- p56 -->
This emphasizes the heart of the gospel: through Jesus’ sacrificial death, God grants full forgiveness of sins to all who repent and believe (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:7</span>). This forgiveness restores humanity’s broken relationship with God and removes guilt and shame. It also calls believers to forgive others, reflecting the grace they have received (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 6:14-15</span>).

<!-- p57 -->
#### *The resurrection of the body.*

<!-- p58 -->
Like Jesus, we will experience the resurrection of the body at the end of the age. Through this resurrection, our perishable, mortal bodies will be transformed into glorious, imperishable bodies suited for eternal life (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42-44" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 15:42-44</span>). This underscores the Christian hope for the restoration not only of individuals but of the whole creation when God makes all things new (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="21" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 21:4-5</span>).

<!-- p59 -->
#### *And the life everlasting. Amen.*

<!-- p60 -->
Eternal life with God is the ultimate promise of the Christian faith. This life begins here and now through a relationship with Christ and is fully realized in the new heaven and the new earth, when believers dwell with God forever (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 22:1-5</span>). This concluding declaration expresses the joy and assurance of salvation, echoing the trust and hope proclaimed throughout the creed.

<!-- p61 -->
## Conclusion

<!-- p62 -->
Within the fundamental truths expressed in the Apostles' Creed, we see that the life and work of Jesus are central to God's plan for humanity. Each declaration of the creed captures key aspects of our faith in God and of who Jesus is - his deity, humanity, mission, and ultimate victory. These truths point to Jesus' unique role as the mediator of the new covenant. As mediator, he invites us to approach God boldly, trusting in his perfect sacrifice and unceasing intercession. It calls us to worship him as the rightful King and to follow his teaching as the ultimate Prophet. Ultimately, studying Jesus deepens our relationship with him and transforms us into those who live in the light of his grace and truth.

<!-- p63 -->
Understanding who Jesus is and what he accomplished is the foundation of the Christian faith. Jesus' many names, titles, and roles illuminate various aspects of his nature and mission, presenting a more complete picture of his love and authority.

<!-- p64 -->
As with the study of God, Scripture is filled with various names referring to Jesus. Each name represents a specific aspect of his overall nature, ministry, and role. These names are given to us to reveal an aspect of Jesus.
