# Chapter 9 - The Church & Our Role Within It

<!-- p1 -->
The church is the way in which God reveals Himself on the earth-through His people carrying out His works-。 We are called to be His body、 the hands and feet of the Lord to accomplish His works and purposes。 Without our efforts、 the harvest will remain in the fields and will not be gathered in。

<!-- p2 -->
## Who is the Church？

<!-- p3 -->
The church、 ***Ekklesia** (eh-klee-SEE-ah - **G1577**),* derives from two Greek words:

<!-- p4 -->
- **"Ek"**: "from。"

<!-- p5 -->
- **"Kaleo"**: "to call。"

<!-- p6 -->
Taken together、 **Ekklesia** literally means "called out" or "called-out assembly"。 In the ancient Greek city-states, it referred to an assembly of citizens convened for specific purposes such as legislative and judicial meetings。 It was a secular term that referred to an assembly of people。

<!-- p7 -->
In the New Testament、 it is used primarily as a term referring to the community of those who believe in Jesus Christ。 This can mean the following:

<!-- p8 -->
- **Local church**: A specific gathering of believers in a particular place（e.g.: the churches in Corinth, Philippi, etc.）。

<!-- p9 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p10 -->
> **<u>1 Corinthians 1:2</u>** (NKJV)
>
> To the church of God which is in Corinth、 to those who are sanctified in Christ Jesus、 called to be saints、 and together with all who in every place call on the name of our Lord Jesus Christ—their Lord and ours—:
>

<!-- p11 -->
</span>

<!-- p12 -->
- **The universal Church**: The collective body of all believers that exists across time and space。

<!-- p13 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p14 -->
> **<u>Matthew 16:18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And I also say to you。 You are Peter。 On this rock I will build My church。 The gates of Hades shall not prevail against it。
>

<!-- p15 -->
</span>

<!-- p16 -->
The church is not a building or the place where they gather、 but the people themselves。 **The called-out ones**: This reflects the idea that Christians are called out of the world and set apart for God’s purposes（e.g.: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:9</span>）。 For a specific mission and purpose（the Great Commission）。 Strictly speaking、 the church is wherever two or more believers gather together（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="18" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 18:20</span>）。 Normally、 churches are organized to have regular gatherings and may have set places and times、 but wherever two or more are gathered in the name of Jesus、 there is the church！

<!-- p17 -->
The church was established by Jesus and expanded in the first century A.D. through the work of the disciples who followed Him。 Today、 the church we have and experience in every local congregation on earth is the direct result of the initial work of Christ and His apostles。 As believers、 we should devote ourselves to the local church for fellowship、 equipping、 training for service、 and to do good works！

<!-- p18 -->
To help us understand what the church is、 the Bible gives powerful images that show what it is intended to be。

<!-- p19 -->
## Understanding the Church

<!-- p20 -->
There are several central metaphorical images used to describe the characteristics of the church:

<!-- p21 -->
1.  Body

<!-- p22 -->
2.  Bride

<!-- p23 -->
3.  Temple

<!-- p24 -->
4.  Family

<!-- p25 -->
5.  Flock of sheep

<!-- p26 -->
6.  Vine and branches

<!-- p27 -->
Each metaphor highlights a unique aspect of the church, but taken together they reveal the church’s identity as a community, under Christ’s guidance, endowed with unity and diversity.

<!-- p28 -->
### Body

<!-- p29 -->
The church is often described as the **Body of Christ**, with both its unity and diversity emphasized. With Christ as the head, the church functions as a living organism, and every believer has roles that are indispensable and can be diverse. Each member is given unique gifts and contributes to the body’s health and mission according to their function. This image underscores believers’ mutual interdependence, showing especially that, for the body to function, it must remain connected, and that our work is cooperation, not competition. When all the parts work together, the body grows and thrives in unity and love (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:12-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:15-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 1:18</span>).

<!-- p30 -->
### Bride

<!-- p31 -->
The church is portrayed as the **Bride of Christ**, which represents an intimate and loving relationship with Jesus. Just as a bride is cherished and prepared for her husband, the church is sanctified and cleansed by Christ’s sacrificial love. This metaphor captures the devotion and faithfulness required of the church, as well as the profound affection Christ has for His people. This image also points to the final union of Christ and the church, celebrated at the marriage supper of the Lamb, where the church is presented in a glorious state, without spot or blemish (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="25-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:25-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 19:7-9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="21" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 21:2</span>).

<!-- p32 -->
### Temple

<!-- p33 -->
The church is depicted as the **temple of God**, the dwelling place of the Spirit. Believers, as living stones, are being built together on the foundation of the apostles and prophets, with Christ Himself as the cornerstone. This holy structure signifies God’s presence in the midst of His people and their role as a community of worship and prayer. The image of the temple underscores the importance of holiness and the church’s shared purpose of manifesting God’s glory (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 3:16-17</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:19-22</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:4-5</span>).

<!-- p34 -->
### Family

<!-- p35 -->
The church is portrayed as the **family of God**, emphasizing relational unity and care among its members. By the Spirit of adoption, believers become children of God and members of God’s household. This family image highlights the love, belonging, and shared inheritance of believers in Christ. It also reflects the call to treat one another with compassion and humility as brothers and sisters in God’s family (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:19</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:15-17</span>)。Furthermore, this family image depicts the church’s members moving toward maturity—that is, being taught and nurtured by spiritual fathers and mothers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:1-2</span>)、and our progress from infants who drink the pure milk of the word to adults who can eat the solid food of the word（Hebrews 5）。

<!-- p36 -->
### Flock of sheep

<!-- p37 -->
The church is depicted as a **flock**, sheep under the care of Jesus, the Good Shepherd. This image conveys the church’s dependence on Christ for guidance, nourishment, and protection. It also emphasizes that church leaders, as those under the Shepherd, have the responsibility to faithfully shepherd God’s flock, reflecting Christ’s model of love and care. The image of the flock speaks of trust, obedience, and close relationship between the great Shepherd and His sheep (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:14-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:2</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>).

<!-- p38 -->
### Vine and branches

<!-- p39 -->
The church is portrayed as the **vine and branches**, showing the indispensable union between believers and Christ. Jesus is the true vine, and believers are the branches that must abide in Him to bear fruit. This metaphor emphasizes dependence on Christ for spiritual life, growth, and fruitfulness. Apart from Him believers can do nothing, but by abiding in Him they manifest God’s glory and fulfill their purpose. Jesus prunes us so that more fruit may be borne in our lives (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:1-5</span>)。

<!-- p40 -->
## The Role of the Church

<!-- p41 -->
The mission of the church, like the role of the Holy Spirit, can be understood in three dimensions: upward, inward, and outward. Each of these reflects a distinct facet of the church’s calling within God’s plan. These dimensions serve as guidelines for the church to fulfill its role as a community that worships, builds up, and witnesses.

<!-- p42 -->
### Upward: Worship and Giving Glory to God

<!-- p43 -->
The church’s primary purpose is to worship God and give glory to Him. Congregational worship, in which believers gather together to exalt God, visibly reflects His glory and responds to His greatness. As the psalmist declares, “*Worship the Lord in the beauty of holiness; all the earth, tremble before Him*” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="96" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 96:9</span>). Beyond congregational worship, the church is called to a life of obedience and sacrifice, and offering ourselves as “living sacrifices” is said to be an act of spiritual worship (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:1-2</span>). This upward focus keeps the church centered on the majesty of God and continually aligns its priorities with His will.

<!-- p44 -->
### Inward: Building Up the Body

<!-- p45 -->
The Great Commission commands us to ‘*teach them to observe*’. This mandate extends far beyond formal methods of instruction like this book. Disciple-making (discussed further in the chapter on discipleship) is shaping followers into the likeness of the teaching, as a parent raises a child. A good parent does not hand a five-year-old an encyclopedia and say, “Figure it out yourself!” Rather, they model, demonstrate, correct, guide, nurture, love, care for, and shepherd.

<!-- p46 -->
Parents make sacrifices for their children because they love them, because they are part of the same body, and because they share the same blood. As members of Christ’s body and family, we share the same body and share the same blood. The Lord’s Supper, through its symbolism, reminds us that we are bound to one another by Christ’s sacrifice. The act of Communion becomes the keystone of unity, reminding us that we all need redemption and that salvation comes only from God. In this unity, we see that no part of the body is greater than another, except for Christ the head.

<!-- p47 -->
### Outward: Reaching the World

<!-- p48 -->
The most well-known part of the church’s mission is its outward expansion to reach the world with the gospel. Jesus’ Great Commission calls His followers to evangelize and make disciples of all nations (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19-20</span>). Empowered by the Holy Spirit, the church is to be the Lord’s witnesses to the ends of the earth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>).

<!-- p49 -->
Jesus is the light of the world (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 8:12</span>). Light shines outward in every direction from its source. The Lord’s light shines through its manifestations—His Word, His Spirit, and us, His body. We are the light of the world; in the way we live, we should shine that light in a world shrouded in darkness and demonstrate good works that reveal God’s glory (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 5:14-16</span>). Practically, this means the church is called to champion justice and mercy both spiritually and materially. As James says, our pure worship is to care for the vulnerable, the orphans, and the widows (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:27</span>).

<!-- p50 -->
## Church Leadership

<!-- p51 -->
There is a remarkable diversity of denominations, assemblies, gatherings, and meetings within the Christian world. There are very few universal rules regarding church leadership, but the ones we have are very important:

<!-- p52 -->
1.  Jesus is the sole head of the church. All serve Him (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 1:18</span>).

<!-- p53 -->
2.  Leaders are held to a high standard, they are worthy of double honor, because they will receive double judgment (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:17</span>).

<!-- p54 -->
2.1  They should be mature, not become proud, and be responsible (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:1-13</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 1:5-9</span>).

<!-- p55 -->
2.2.  They must be servants of all (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42-45" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 10:42-45</span>).

<!-- p56 -->
2.3.  They must devote themselves to the Word and to prayer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:4</span>).

<!-- p57 -->
2.4.  They are responsible for equipping the body for works of service.

<!-- p58 -->
3.  There are no greater or lesser persons within the body. The following passages support this:

<!-- p59 -->
3.1.  There must be no favoritism between the rich and the poor (James 2).

<!-- p60 -->
3.2. There is no distinction between Jews and Gentiles, slaves and free, male and female (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 3:27-28</span>).

<!-- p61 -->
Church structures beyond rules for leadership are important, but many of them are, rather than being universally prescribed, matters of context and tradition. “<u>We’ve always done it this way!</u>” is a common saying；if you go back far enough, there was usually a valid reason they did it that way. This is generally not a matter of right and wrong, but of preference, need, and availability.

<!-- p62 -->
A useful example of tradition serving as the foundation of structure is family mealtime. The most important thing about a family meal is that everyone gets food and that the meal is nourishing. The ways it can vary are endless: what to eat, how to eat it, how to season it, and so on. The various combinations are generally acceptable and are largely matters of taste and preference.

<!-- p63 -->
A church of 10,000 has different needs and capacities than a church of 50. The pattern shown in Acts and throughout the New Testament canon was the model of **elders** and **deacons**. The structure of leadership may look different from church to church and denomination to denomination, but ultimately this model represents the minimally viable structure for church leadership. Let’s look at these leadership offices and how they are selected.

<!-- p64 -->
### Elders（overseers）

<!-- p65 -->
Roles and responsibilities:

<!-- p66 -->
- **Spiritual leadership** - providing oversight, guidance, and pastoral care to the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:2</span>).

<!-- p67 -->
- **Teaching and preaching** - responsible to teach sound doctrine and guard against false teaching (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:17</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 1:9</span>).

<!-- p68 -->
- **Shepherding the flock** - caring for the spiritual needs of the congregation and being an example of humility and service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:2-3</span>).

<!-- p69 -->
- **Protecting the church** - serving as watchmen and protecting the church from false doctrine and division (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29-31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:29-31</span>).

<!-- p70 -->
- **Equipping the saints** - training and preparing believers for works of service and disciple-making (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-12</span>).

<!-- p71 -->
Selection process:

<!-- p72 -->
- **Appointment in each church** - elders were appointed by the apostles or their delegates, often through prayer and fasting (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 14:23</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 1:5</span>).

<!-- p73 -->
- **Confirmation by the Holy Spirit** - their appointment was regarded as Spirit-led, with God’s calling and gifting recognized (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>).

<!-- p74 -->
- **Examination and approval** - candidates had to prove maturity and faithfulness before being entrusted with leadership (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:10</span>). They must not be recent converts! ((<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:6</span>)

<!-- p75 -->
### Deacons（servants）

<!-- p76 -->
Roles and responsibilities:

<!-- p77 -->
- **Practical service** - focusing on meeting the material needs of the church so that the elders can devote themselves to prayer and teaching (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-4</span>).

<!-- p78 -->
- **A ministry of support** - assisting with hospitality, care for the poor, and administrative tasks (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:3</span>).

<!-- p79 -->
- **Promoting unity** - addressing issues of fairness and care to prevent division (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-7</span>).

<!-- p80 -->
- **Example of Faith** - A model in humility, faith, and service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:13</span>).

<!-- p81 -->
Selection process (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-6</span>):

<!-- p82 -->
- **Recognition by the congregation** - Chosen based on reputation and spiritual character.

<!-- p83 -->
- **Approval by leaders** - The apostles affirmed and appointed the chosen deacons through prayer and the laying on of hands.

<!-- p84 -->
- **Proven in service** - Before being formally appointed, deacons were first tested to prove their faithfulness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:10</span>).

<!-- p85 -->
### Qualifications for Leaders

<!-- p86 -->
#### Qualifications common to both elders and deacons:

<!-- p87 -->
- Moral character - Both must be above reproach, dignified, and display self-control (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:2</span>, 8).

<!-- p88 -->
- Family life - Both must be faithful in marriage（“husband of one wife”）and manage their households well (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:4-5</span>, 12).

<!-- p89 -->
- Spiritual maturity - Must be tested and proven before appointment (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:6</span>, 10).

<!-- p90 -->
- Reputation - A good reputation is required both inside and outside the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:7</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
- Faith and integrity - Expected to hold the faith with a clear conscience and live out their convictions (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:9</span>).

<!-- p92 -->
### Illustrative Structures

<!-- p93 -->
Using the rules and structures of leadership we have studied so far, let us look at two examples of church structure found in the New Testament: Jerusalem and Antioch.

<!-- p94 -->
### The Church in Jerusalem

<!-- p95 -->
The church in Jerusalem was led primarily by the **apostles**, who devoted themselves to teaching, prayer, and spiritual oversight (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:42</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:4</span>). As the community grew, the need for an administrative structure became evident to address practical issues such as care for widows and the distribution of resources. This led to the appointment of **deacons** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-7</span>). They were chosen for their spiritual maturity and good reputation, and took on practical service so that the apostles could focus on their spiritual responsibilities. Over time, **elders** also emerged as leaders in the church at Jerusalem. For example, at the Jerusalem Council（Acts 15）, both apostles and elders were involved in decision-making, demonstrating a collaborative leadership structure.

<!-- p96 -->
### The Church in Antioch

<!-- p97 -->
The church in Antioch had a more decentralized and diverse leadership structure compared to Jerusalem. It was led by a group of **prophets and teachers** that included Barnabas, Saul (Paul), Simeon, Lucius, and Manaen (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 13:1</span>). The church in Antioch had a strong missionary focus to the Gentiles, whereas the church in Jerusalem reflected more of its Jewish roots. Although the New Testament does not explicitly mention deacons in Antioch, given the church’s growth in size and its involvement in sending relief to Jerusalem, it is likely that practical roles existed (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29-30" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 11:29-30</span>). The church in Antioch served as a model in the appointment of missionaries: the leaders prayed, fasted, laid hands on Paul and Barnabas, and sent them out (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 13:2-3</span>). This shows a structure that balanced spiritual oversight（prophets and teachers）with missionary work and practical support.

<!-- p98 -->
Unlike the corrections Paul made to the Corinthians and Galatians, Scripture does not criticize the leadership structures of Jerusalem or Antioch. This suggests that these churches, under the guidance of the Holy Spirit and within the framework of sound doctrine and apostolic teaching, freely adapted their gatherings to their unique needs and circumstances (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:42</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>).

<!-- p99 -->
Throughout these passages, terms we have not yet defined and studied are used. *Apostles, prophets, evangelists*. These gifts are part of the fivefold ministry that Jesus gave to the church.

<!-- p100 -->
### The Gifts of the Fivefold Ministry

<!-- p101 -->
Jesus appointed five distinct ministry gifts, often called the **Fivefold Ministry Gifts**, to equip believers for service and to build up the body of Christ.

<!-- p102 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p103 -->
> **<u>Ephesians 4:11-13</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And He Himself gave some to be apostles, some prophets, some evangelists, and some pastors and teachers,
>
> for the equipping of the saints for the work of ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ,
>
> till we all come to the unity of the faith and of the knowledge of the Son of God, to a perfect man, to the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ.
>

<!-- p104 -->
</span>

<!-- p105 -->
The five gifts given to equip the saints for the work of ministry are as follows.

<!-- p106 -->
1.  **Apostles** - pioneers and visionaries who lay foundations, plant churches, and advance the kingdom of God（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:20</span>）. Apostles model boldness and faith, stepping into new territories and demonstrating spiritual initiative.

<!-- p107 -->
2.  **Prophets** - messengers and guides who declare God’s truth, bring encouragement, edification, and direction（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>）. Prophets stir faith, call people to align with God’s word, and model a life that relies on hearing God’s voice.

<!-- p108 -->
3.  **Evangelists** - proclaimers of the gospel who reach out to the lost and spur others into action to share Christ（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:5-8</span>）. Evangelists train disciples in how to share their faith effectively, model boldness in witness, and inspire compassion for the lost.

<!-- p109 -->
4.  **Pastors（shepherds）** - caretakers and nurturers who protect, guide, and provide spiritual oversight to the church（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>）. Pastors teach disciples the importance of community, mutual care, and accountable involvement within the body of Christ.

<!-- p110 -->
5.  **Teachers** - leaders and trainers who clarify doctrine and help believers grow in understanding and applying God’s word（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 2:15</span>）. Teachers equip disciples to study Scripture deeply, apply it to daily life, and teach others effectively.

<!-- p111 -->
## Leadership and Gifts

<!-- p112 -->
What is often misunderstood regarding church leadership is separating leadership authority and responsibility from specific gifts.

<!-- p113 -->
This confusion centers on the way terms like teacher, pastor, prophet, apostle, and evangelist are used. These terms refer to gifts. There is no **office** of 『apostle』 in the New Testament church. There are people who do the work of an apostle and who have an apostolic gift and calling. However, possessing an apostolic gift does not in itself confer authority or responsibility.

<!-- p114 -->
I am a skilled musician. I have played the saxophone for over 20 years. I played throughout school and college, and while working on an evangelism team, I also played semi-professionally for several summers. Even if I have the skill, gift, and ability as a saxophonist, that does not give me the right to come to your church and take the stage with the worship team.

<!-- p115 -->
Similarly, even if someone has a pastoral／shepherding gift, that does not make them the ‘pastor’ of a local church or your ‘pastor’. It is not the fivefold ministry gifts that lead the church, but the leaders (elders and deacons). While leaders often have particular fivefold gifts, those gifts enhance leadership but do not themselves produce leadership. Likewise, it is common for people with developing fivefold gifts to serve and aspire to leadership, but what God is concerned with first is character, and only then gifts. We all should become mature, discern the word of God, be filled with the Spirit, and devote ourselves to good works.

<!-- p116 -->
All leaders and all gifts must first submit to being disciples, that is, members of the body.

<!-- p117 -->
Jesus gave specific ministry gifts to equip the saints for the work of ministry. These differ from the motivational gifts（covered later in this chapter）and the manifestation gifts（covered in the chapter on baptism）. As disciples grow, the fivefold ministry gifts equip them for service, and the elders and deacons oversee to ensure sound doctrine and practice are maintained.

<!-- p118 -->
## Your Role in the Church

<!-- p119 -->
Up to this point in this chapter, we have dealt with church leadership and the primary metaphors for the church. From here, building particularly on the metaphor of the 「body」, let us consider a marathon runner.

<!-- p120 -->
### You Are Part of the Whole

<!-- p121 -->
As the body of Christ, the church depends on all its members working together to fulfill its mission. Just as a body does not function properly when part of it is missing, the church needs each believer to participate actively, grow, and serve. Each part of the body has a very important role. Being part of the body means remaining connected. A body can survive, though with diminished function, even if it lacks a limb. However, that limb itself cannot live if it is severed from the body. We must remain an active part of the church and not become detached. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:24</span> says that we should gather and not stop meeting.

<!-- p122 -->
To truly work and function together, fellowship is necessary！ We need to know, love, and ideally take delight in others！ Build strong relationships and friendships, and repair them when they become strained or hurt. When a joint is sore or inflamed, it needs rest or adjustment； so that a dislocated joint does not end up broken！ A limb that is broken and does not heal will eventually be removed, and in isolation it cannot remain part of the body.

<!-- p123 -->
We do not all have the same function. One may preach the gospel from a stage, another may share it around a table. But we are all called to the ministry of reconciliation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 5:18-19</span>). We are all received into the priesthood of believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:9</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 1:6</span>). This means that all of us should be able to carry out works of ministry by the power of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p124 -->
The toe to the foot, the foot to the ankle, and so on- each part contributes to the mission of the whole. A marathoner must train if they want to finish. Preparation is necessary- not only by learning, but by actually putting what is learned into practice. Like a marathoner, the church shows its strength when each member, in unity, fulfills their role.

<!-- p125 -->
In your life there are many people whom only you may be able to reach. It should not be only pastors who shepherd them, nor only evangelists who seek to reach them. God desires to use you, by the power of the Holy Spirit, to reach unbelievers. We all do the work of ministry； the Holy Spirit helps us.

<!-- p126 -->
### Training Is Necessary

<!-- p127 -->
Believers must train and be equipped for their particular role in the church, as a runner strengthens specific muscles for a race. You will not succeed by suddenly entering a marathon without training, and not all training is the same. You need to be adequately prepared for the race. Jesus gave particular gifts of ministry to equip believers. Not so that He Himself would do all the work, but so that each member would be empowered to serve effectively in their unique role.

<!-- p128 -->
Training for a marathon brings change. You need strength, and you also need to be transformed. It is our responsibility to present ourselves to God and allow Him to transform us in renewal (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:1-2</span>). Change leads you to maturity, enabling you to carry your own load and support others. You should be able to bear much fruit. This will be addressed in detail in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p129 -->
Training is rigorous and at times uncomfortable. Sometimes you may wish to avoid the changes you must go through. But if you want to reach the goal, you need to stay on the narrow path. At times there is admonition, comfort, or support- all offered with patience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Thessalonians 5:14</span>).

<!-- p130 -->
Every excellent marathoner has a coach who trains them. You also need trainers who can help you. Of course, you have the Holy Spirit. But the Holy Spirit often works through His coaches- mature men and women of God within the body of Christ with whom you are called to labor.

<!-- p131 -->
As an individual, you must first and foremost submit yourself to God, and then, in this context, submit to the gathering of believers. Obedience in the church means honoring godly leadership (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 13:17</span>) and humbly submitting to one another (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:21</span>).

<!-- p132 -->
Submitting can be difficult, especially if you have previously experienced a breach of trust or failed leadership. This does not mean blind obedience or domination； it expresses a spirit of reverence and cooperation. Worldly obedience is demanded by those in authority and can lead to servility or resentment. Biblical obedience, rooted in humility and discernment, strengthens the church and fosters accountability. Maintain a posture that listens to guidance and constructive feedback.

<!-- p133 -->
A solid foundation has been laid - you are a meaningful member of the body, but training is necessary - so now let’s find where you fit.

<!-- p134 -->
### Finding Your Place

<!-- p135 -->
Finding your role in the church involves seeking God’s guidance, understanding your spiritual gifts, and actively engaging in the church community. It is a journey of prayer, exploration, and growth- a process of discovering how God has uniquely equipped you to contribute to His kingdom. It is very important to note that your role in the church is likely to change over time and is unlikely to remain the same throughout your life.

<!-- p136 -->
Below are practical efforts to help you find your place. They are presented as a series of steps for clarity, but in your walk with God you will likely repeat the same steps many times.

<!-- p137 -->
#### Step 1 - Seek God’s Guidance through Prayer and the Word

<!-- p138 -->
Begin by **seeking God’s guidance through prayer**. God is the ultimate source of your purpose and calling (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="29" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 29:11</span>). Pray regularly for clarity about how you can serve in the church, and ask Him to reveal your spiritual gifts and passions. Trust that as you submit your plans to His will, the Lord will make your paths straight (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 3:5-6</span>).

<!-- p139 -->
To deepen your understanding of the church’s mission and your role within it, **study God’s Word**. Key passages such as 1 Corinthians 12 and <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-12</span> highlight spiritual gifts and the diversity of roles in the body of Christ. Reflect on examples of biblical figures who found and fulfilled their roles, such as Paul, Timothy, and Lydia.

<!-- p140 -->
#### Step 2 - Discern Your Gifts, Passions, and Skills

<!-- p141 -->
Discerning your spiritual gifts is an essential step。Taking a spiritual gifts assessment may be helpful。These gifts are given by God to build up the church。The New Testament lists several types of gifts：

<!-- p142 -->
- **Motivational gifts** given by God: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:6-8</span>

<!-- p143 -->
- Explained after this list。

<!-- p144 -->
- **Fivefold ministry gifts** given by Jesus: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-13</span>

<!-- p145 -->
- Explained in an earlier section of this chapter。

<!-- p146 -->
- **Manifestation gifts** given by the Holy Spirit: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span>

<!-- p147 -->
- Explained in the chapter on Baptism。

<!-- p148 -->
**Motivational gifts** reflect how believers are wired to think, feel, and act。They motivate believers to serve in specific ways that build up the church。Each gift complements the others and ensures that the church functions as one body with diverse roles（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:4-5</span>）。They operate as abilities given by grace（Greek: ***charis** - khar'-ece - **G5463 -** a gift not obtained by oneself*）。That is, they are not acquired or learned, but given by God。The gifts come from God, yet the person who has them can become more skilled in using them。

<!-- p149 -->
Motivational gifts are:

<!-- p150 -->
1.  **Prophecy** - A God-given inclination to boldly declare truth。Often focuses on conviction and righteousness, guiding others to align with God’s word。\*

<!-- p151 -->
2.  **Service (Helps)** - Meets practical needs and supports others through acts of service and help。

<!-- p152 -->
3.  **Teaching** - Explains and clarifies truth, helping others understand and apply God’s word。

<!-- p153 -->
4.  **Exhortation (Encouragement)** - Motivates, encourages, and comforts others to promote growth and perseverance。

<!-- p154 -->
5.  **Giving** - Generously provides resources to meet needs and advance God’s work without seeking recognition。

<!-- p155 -->
6.  **Leadership (Administration)** - Organizes and leads people, with vision and efficiency, to achieve goals。

<!-- p156 -->
7.  **Mercy** - Shows compassion and care to comfort and support the hurting and those in need。

<!-- p157 -->
\* Prophecy as used in the context of Romans is understood to differ from prophecy as a manifestation gift of the Holy Spirit（1 Corinthians）。The Greek word used in both passages is the same, but the contexts differ。To study the differences more deeply, we recommend reading:

<!-- p158 -->
- *The Gift of Prophecy* by Kenneth E. Hagin（[www.rhema.org](http://www.rhema.org)）

<!-- p159 -->
<!-- -->

<!-- p160 -->
- *Gifts and Ministries of the Holy Spirit* by Lester Sumrall

<!-- p161 -->
When discerning your gifts, consider your **passions and skills**. God often calls us to serve in areas where we are passionate or skilled (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 31:2-6</span>). Reflect on what excites your heart about serving God and people, and consider how your professional skills, hobbies, and life experiences might contribute to ministry.

<!-- p162 -->
Seek guidance from **leaders and mentors** who can offer wisdom and insight (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 15:22</span>). Meet with pastors and ministry leaders to discuss your interests, and ask for candid feedback about where you might fit best. Pray over the feedback you receive. Consider joining a discipleship group for training and ongoing support.

<!-- p163 -->
#### Step 3 - Be faithful now

<!-- p164 -->
The Parable of the Talents (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-30" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 25:14-30</span>) reminds us that we must be faithful in small things in order to be entrusted with greater ones (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 16:10</span>).

<!-- p165 -->
To discern where God is calling you, start by **getting involved and serving**. Begin small by volunteering in areas of need in your church and its support ministries. For example:

<!-- p166 -->
- Join a welcome team, children’s ministry, or outreach program. Consider leading a discipleship-oriented group for new believers based on this book, *Principles of Faith,* using the discussion questions and end-of-chapter activities to foster dialogue.

<!-- p167 -->
- Step out of your comfort zone and be open to discovering new opportunities.

<!-- p168 -->
- Observe where God works through you and where you feel most effective.

<!-- p169 -->
Practice giving. We can give to the church in various important ways, and we are taught to do so in order to fulfill the mission.

<!-- p170 -->
- Time - We can give our time and serve as volunteers. When we give our time, we use our efforts and gifts. This can also be a great way to learn new skills and develop existing ones.

<!-- p171 -->
- Resources - We can give our resources. The most common today is probably financial giving to the church or ministries (not just the “institution,” but to people in need within the church). Giving is beneficial. The church can purchase necessary supplies with it. However, people have given not only money but also tangible resources such as an extra house, clothing, cars, food, building materials, and tools. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="32-35" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 4:32-35</span> shows an example of the early believers giving generously, selling extra land or houses and sharing the proceeds, so that ‘no one was in need.’

<!-- p172 -->
Engaging in ministry with others reveals your strengths and deepens your understanding of your role. Join small groups and church activities to cultivate connections. Listen to the burdens and needs of those around you and consider how you can support them. Building relationships within the church helps you be connected to others and understand the needs of the community.

<!-- p173 -->
#### Step 4 - Get help!

<!-- p174 -->
Jesus said we would experience trouble in life! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:33</span>) We are encouraged not to keep carrying our burdens alone but to cast them on the God who cares for us! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:7</span>)

<!-- p175 -->
We may get hurt along the way. A marathon runner, even when doing “everything right,” can place stress on the body and end up injured, and may also stumble and fall.

<!-- p176 -->
Not every ‘fall’ is related to sin, though sometimes it is. Not every weakness is ‘our fault,’ and at times others sin against us. Whatever the cause of our weaknesses or wounds, we should be restored and healed.

<!-- p177 -->
There is great freedom in seeking inner healing and restoration. We are called to cover the vulnerable parts of the body (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:23</span>) and to restore those who have fallen into wrongdoing (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:1</span>).

<!-- p178 -->
There is much to be said on this subject, and we will explore the theme of healing in overview in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p179 -->
#### Step 5 - Persevere

<!-- p180 -->
Pay attention to confirmation from God and from others. Often, God confirms your calling through fruitfulness, joy, and affirmation from the people around you. Look back at where your efforts have borne fruit, and listen to feedback about the impact of your ministry. This will help you discern where you are most effective in serving the body of Christ.

<!-- p181 -->
Be patient and trust God’s timing. It takes time to find your role, and it may involve trial and error (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 1:6</span>). Believe that God will lead you if you remain faithful and open to His guidance. During this season, commit to growth and training by participating in workshops, Bible studies, and other resources that prepare you for service. Do this with your church, and discover your gifts as you engage with the body of Christ.

<!-- p182 -->
Remember, the church is not a building or an institution but a living body made up of people like you. As you step into your role, you contribute to its mission and bring glory to Christ, the head.
