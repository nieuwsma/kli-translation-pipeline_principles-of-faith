# Chapter 11 - Baptism - Water and the Spirit

<!-- p1 -->
In the modern era, baptism is commonly discussed apart from its Jewish roots, and only in terms of water baptism and baptism in the Holy Spirit (Pentecost).

<!-- p2 -->
The Letter to the Hebrews was written to a Jewish readership. The overwhelming majority of modern Christians do not have Jewish roots or heritage. Lacking Jewish roots and culture, many of us do not share the cultural context that first-century Messianic Jews (Jewish believers who confessed Jesus) shared. Not having grown up within Jewish tradition, we typically know little about the feasts, fasts, prayers, washings, rites, and liturgies that Jewish believers know.

<!-- p3 -->
The Greek word for baptism, or washing, is: **Baptismos** (bap-tis-mos’ - **G909**) - the act of immersing or washing. Refers to ritual washings and liturgical purifications. It can denote immersions, purification rites, and washings carried out in Jewish or Christian contexts.

<!-- p4 -->
Baptism is one of the foundational doctrines listed in Hebrews:

<!-- p5 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p6 -->
> **<u>Hebrews 6:1-2</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Therefore, leaving the discussion of the elementary teachings about Christ, let us go on to maturity, not laying again the foundation of repentance from dead works and of faith toward God,
>
> of the doctrine of baptisms, of laying on of hands, of resurrection of the dead, and of eternal judgment.
>

<!-- p8 -->
## Washings in the Old Covenant - Historical Background

<!-- p9 -->
In the Old Covenant, ritual washings played a central role in maintaining purity in worship and symbolizing holiness before God. These washings were prescribed in the Law of Moses and applied both to the priests who served in the tabernacle and to the people of Israel. For example, the priests were required to wash their hands and feet at the bronze basin before entering the tabernacle or approaching the altar. This symbolized that they were prepared to minister before the Holy God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="30" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 30:18-21</span>). Likewise, purification rites from uncleanness associated with touching a corpse or with skin diseases included washing with water, which signified the restoration of purity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LEV" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Leviticus 14:8</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="NUM" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Numbers 19:17-19</span>). These acts outwardly expressed the desire for inward holiness and underscored the importance of being set apart for God.

<!-- p10 -->
The ritual washings in the Old Testament had to be performed repeatedly to maintain ceremonial purity in worship. Unlike a permanent solution, these rites provided only temporary, external cleansing and left the deeper issue of sin unresolved. The author of Hebrews highlights this limitation, stating that these practices were “symbols for the present time” and could not cleanse the worshipers’ conscience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:9-10</span>). These washings continually reminded people of human imperfection and of the need for something greater—the ultimate purification—namely, something that could cleanse not only the body but also the heart and soul.

<!-- p11 -->
The ritual washings of the Old Covenant are fulfilled in Christ. Through His own sacrifice, Christ grants complete and eternal cleansing. Unlike the temporary rites of the law, the blood of Christ cleanses fully, purifying not only the body but also the conscience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:13-14</span>). Through His death and resurrection, Jesus accomplished what the Old Testament rites foreshadowed as a shadow—true reconciliation with God. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:22</span> exhorts us to draw near to God with the full assurance of faith, as those who have had our hearts sprinkled to cleanse an evil conscience and our bodies washed with pure water. This wording connects Christ’s work to the imagery of Old Testament washings while showing its vastly superior efficacy. Christ’s sacrifice does not merely cover sin; it completely removes it (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 1:7</span>). Thus, the ritual washings served as shadows pointing to the deeper cleansing that only Christ can give, through which God’s intervention meets humanity’s need for holiness.

<!-- p12 -->
To reiterate this point, Christians are under no obligation to perform any ritual washings. Washings did not bring salvation under the Old Covenant, nor do they under the New. Ritual washings do not enhance our standing before God or gain us favor. Paul strongly rebukes the Judaizers in Galatia who tried to introduce Jewish requirements (e.g., circumcision) as necessary for salvation (read Galatians). Salvation is by faith in Jesus alone!

<!-- p13 -->
## John’s Baptism

<!-- p14 -->
The New Testament canon mentions several baptisms. We have already addressed baptisms and washings in Jewish tradition.

<!-- p15 -->
John the Baptist’s ministry centered on preparing for the coming of Jesus Christ. His baptism is described as “a baptism of repentance for the forgiveness of sins” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 1:4</span>), calling people to confess their sins, turn their hearts back to God, and prepare for the coming of the Messiah. It was an outward sign of inward repentance, symbolizing spiritual cleansing and readiness for the kingdom of God. However, John’s baptism was temporary and preparatory, pointing to the greater work that Christ would accomplish. Though John’s baptism focused on repentance, it did not grant full forgiveness or spiritual regeneration because it lacked the indwelling and empowering of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:11-12</span>). Rather, John openly declared that One greater than he would come, who would baptize not only with water but with the Holy Spirit and fire (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 3:16</span>).

<!-- p16 -->
John’s baptism was fulfilled and surpassed by the death, burial, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. Christian baptism, instituted by Jesus and practiced by the apostles, represents a deeper reality—union with Christ, death to sin, and resurrection to new life (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 6:3-4</span>). Unlike John’s baptism, Christian baptism is directly connected to faith in Jesus’ redemptive work and the indwelling of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:38</span>). The coming of the Holy Spirit at Pentecost marked the New Covenant and began the Spirit-empowered walk that John’s baptism did not provide. For this reason, John’s baptism is no longer practiced; it has been replaced by the baptism commanded by Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19</span>), which encompasses repentance, faith, and the Spirit’s sealing of the believer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>).

<!-- p17 -->
## Christ’s Baptism

<!-- p18 -->
Christ’s baptism served as a powerful example to the disciples and laid the foundation for water baptism in Christianity. Though sinless, Jesus submitted to be baptized by John “to fulfill all righteousness” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:15</span>). In doing so, Jesus identified with humanity’s need for repentance and foreshadowed His own death, burial, and resurrection. At His baptism, the Holy Spirit descended like a dove, and the Father declared, “This is my beloved Son, with whom I am well pleased” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:16-17</span>). This event marked the beginning of Jesus’ public ministry, revealed the unity of the Father, the Son, and the Spirit, and foreshadowed the spiritual transformation believers experience through faith in Christ.

<!-- p19 -->
Jesus later commanded the disciples to baptize, entrusting them to “Therefore, go and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19</span>). Water baptism, instituted by Christ, is an **act of obedience** for the believer and a **public declaration of faith**. It expresses identification with Jesus’ death, burial, and resurrection (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 6:3-4</span>). Going down into the water symbolizes dying to sin, and coming up out of the water signifies resurrection to new life in Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:12</span>). However, importantly, we must make clear that this outward act itself is not the source of salvation. It is the **visible sign** of an **inner work of grace** already accomplished by the blood of Jesus.

<!-- p20 -->
Water baptism is a **symbol** of the cleansing power of Christ’s blood and the regeneration by the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:26</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>)。It expresses repentance, faith, and the resolve to follow Jesus。This is an outward testimony of an inner change—and a way for the believer to publicly declare loyalty to Christ and participation in the church, the body of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:41</span>)。It was commanded by Jesus and modeled in the early church, but it does not **save** anyone。Salvation is by grace alone, through faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>)。And true cleansing happens spiritually through the blood of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 1:5</span>)。Water baptism is a response to that salvation, an act of obedience rather than a requirement。

<!-- p21 -->
In the New Testament, water baptism is consistently tied to **repentance and faith** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:38</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 16:16</span>)、both of which require a personal decision to follow Christ—something infants cannot do。Baptism symbolizes the believer’s intentional identification with Christ’s death, burial, and resurrection (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 6:3-4</span>) and the resolve to live obediently before Him。Some denominations（e.g.： Catholic, Lutheran）practice **infant baptism** as a covenant sign akin to circumcision。しかし、because infants cannot profess personal faith or repentance, the practice of **infant baptism** does not align with the biblical pattern of **believers’ baptism**。Instead, many churches hold **child dedication** services, following the example of Hannah dedicating Samuel to the Lord (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1SA" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Samuel 1:27-28</span>) and of Mary and Joseph presenting Jesus at the temple (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 2:22</span>)。Child dedication is not a substitute for baptism; rather, it is a parental vow to nurture the child in faith, teach the word of God, and model a godly life until the child is old enough to decide to follow Christ and be baptized。

<!-- p22 -->
Unlike the Old Testament **ceremonial washings**, which required repeated rites for cleansing, Christian baptism is a one-time event reflecting the finished work of grace。The OT washings symbolized external cleansing and foreshadowed the deeper spiritual cleansing brought by Christ。By contrast, New Testament baptism symbolizes the internal renewal and regeneration accomplished by the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>)。This indicates that cleansing from sin is enduring and points to the believer’s participation in the new covenant grounded in Christ’s finished work。

<!-- p23 -->
Water baptism is deeply connected to the **promise of the Holy Spirit**。In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:38</span>, Peter called the people to repent, be baptized, and receive the gift of the Holy Spirit。While water baptism publicly displays a believer’s repentance and faith, the true baptism happens inwardly when the Holy Spirit washes, regenerates, and seals the believer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:13</span>)。Through this spiritual washing, we are made members of the body of Christ and empowered to live holy lives。The **role of the Holy Spirit** in baptism reminds us that salvation is accomplished not by ritual but by God’s transforming power。

<!-- p24 -->
It is important to distinguish **the symbolic act of water baptism** from the spiritual reality of salvation through the blood of Christ。Scripture clearly teaches that salvation is based on faith in Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 10:9-10</span>) rather than on works, including baptism (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>)。That said, water baptism is an **act of obedience** that publicly identifies the believer with Christ and the church。It demonstrates an outward commitment to live as a disciple of Jesus, but it neither adds to nor completes the work of salvation。

<!-- p25 -->
Christ’s water baptism demonstrated humility, obedience, and readiness for service, and it became a model for believers to follow。For Christians today, water baptism is a public declaration of faith, symbolizing death to sin and resurrection to new life。It is commanded by Jesus and is an important step of obedience, but it is not essential for salvation。True cleansing happens through faith in the blood of Jesus, and the power to live as a Christian is given through the working of the Holy Spirit within the believer。Baptism joins us to Christ and His church and reminds us of the inner change and renewal God has already accomplished。

<!-- p26 -->
Having affirmed that water baptism is the outward sign of faith, we now turn to the baptism of the Holy Spirit, which empowers believers for service and ministry。

<!-- p27 -->
## Baptism of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p28 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit is a deeply transformative experience promised by God to empower believers for service, ministry, and holy living。All believers receive the Holy Spirit when they are born again—that is what makes us spiritually alive。しかしその後、we should also receive the baptism of the Holy Spirit, by which we receive boldness, spiritual gifts, and power for effective ministry。

<!-- p29 -->
Water baptism is the public declaration of faith and symbolizes the cleansing from sin by the blood of Christ, while baptism by the Spirit represents God’s powerful presence in a believer’s life。These two modes of baptism shape complementary aspects of the Christian walk—one is an outward sign of obedience, the other an inward source of power and renewal。The baptism of the Holy Spirit is the Spirit coming upon us, not within。At salvation, all believers receive the Spirit within, but when God’s Spirit comes upon them, they receive power and boldness for service。

<!-- p30 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit was foretold by John the Baptist, who declared,

<!-- p31 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p32 -->
> **<u>Matthew 3:11</u>** (NKJV)
>
> I indeed baptize you with water for repentance, but He who comes after me is mightier than I, and I am not worthy to carry His sandals. He will baptize you with the Holy Spirit and fire.
>

<!-- p33 -->
</span>

<!-- p34 -->
This promise pointed to the still greater work Jesus would accomplish after His resurrection—the sending of the Holy Spirit to indwell His disciples and empower them。Jesus confirmed this by commanding the disciples to wait for the gift of the Spirit, explaining that they would receive power to be His witnesses (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>)。The fulfillment of this promise occurred on the day of Pentecost, when the disciples were filled with the Holy Spirit, evidenced by tongues, prophecy, and bold proclamation of the gospel (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:1-4</span>)。

<!-- p35 -->
This outpouring marked the beginning of the **church age**, showing that the power of the Holy Spirit is now given to all believers。This promise was not limited to the apostles; as Peter declared, “This promise is for you and your children and for all who are far off—for all whom the Lord our God will call” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="39" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:39</span>)。

<!-- p36 -->
### The Spirit within vs. the Spirit upon

<!-- p37 -->
When someone places trust in Jesus, the Holy Spirit gives new life within that person。This is (regeneration)—being born again。At that moment of faith, the Holy Spirit dwells on the inside **WITHIN** the believer, makes that person spiritually alive, and adopts them into the family of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:5-6</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>)。This is what Jesus spoke of in John 3—regeneration, that is, being born again。

<!-- p38 -->
Biblical basis:

<!-- p39 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>* - “Through the washing of regeneration and renewal by the Holy Spirit, God saved us.”

<!-- p40 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>* - The Holy Spirit seals believers as the **guarantee** of their inheritance.

<!-- p41 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:9</span>* - “Anyone who does not have the Spirit of Christ does not belong to Him.”

<!-- p42 -->
This work of regeneration occurs universally in all believers in salvation and establishes the Spirit’s permanent indwelling. It does not depend on external acts such as water baptism, and it guarantees salvation by grace through faith.

<!-- p43 -->
By contrast, the baptism in the Holy Spirit is a distinct, subsequent experience that equips believers with power for service and witness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>). While regeneration concerns new life and identity, the baptism in the Holy Spirit focuses on the **coming** of power and the release of spiritual gifts. This baptism is often accompanied by signs and manifestations of the Spirit’s gifts.

<!-- p44 -->
Biblical basis:

<!-- p45 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p46 -->
> **<u>Acts 1:8</u>** (NKJV)
>
> But when the Holy Spirit comes upon you, you will receive power; and you will be My witnesses in Jerusalem, in all Judea and Samaria, and to the ends of the earth."
>

<!-- p47 -->
</span>

<!-- p48 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:15-17</span>* - The Samaritans had believed and been baptized, but afterward received the Holy Spirit through prayer and the laying on of hands.

<!-- p49 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 19:6</span>* - The disciples at Ephesus, who were already believers, received the Holy Spirit when Paul laid hands on them.

<!-- p50 -->
The baptism in the Holy Spirit does not replace regeneration; rather, it builds upon it, equipping believers for spiritual service and boldness in ministry. The baptism in the Holy Spirit is a free gift to the saved and not a condition for being saved. It is not necessary to manifest a <u>specific</u> gift of the Spirit to prove that someone has received the baptism in the Holy Spirit, yet, as recorded in Acts, we should still expect signs and boldness to accompany the manifestation of the baptism in the Holy Spirit today! Some fall into the error of thinking that if a person does not speak in tongues, they are not saved or do not have the baptism in the Holy Spirit. Not everyone receives every gift or manifestation of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29-31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:29-31</span>).

<!-- p51 -->
## How to receive the baptism in the Holy Spirit

<!-- p52 -->
The baptism in the Holy Spirit is presented in Scripture as a gift promised to believers and received by faith. It is distinct from salvation and is primarily for endowment and witness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>). The baptism in the Holy Spirit is a gift of power to believers, promised by Jesus and fulfilled at Pentecost. It is received through repentance and faith, and by asking God. The laying on of hands is often used as a means, but ultimately it is God Himself who administers the baptism in the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p53 -->
Scripture presents tongues and prophecy as common signs, but the purpose of this baptism is more than that - to grant boldness, power, and spiritual gifts for ministry -. All believers are encouraged to seek this experience as part of their spiritual walk, trusting that God will equip them for His work.

<!-- p54 -->
Scripture sets forth several core principles regarding how this baptism is received:

<!-- p55 -->
### **1. The baptism in the Holy Spirit is a free gift from God**

<!-- p56 -->
The baptism in the Holy Spirit is not something believers earn by merit - it is a free gift given by God. God desires to give good gifts to His children and gladly gives the Holy Spirit to those who, in faith, ask to receive power.

<!-- p57 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p58 -->
> **<u>Luke 11:13</u>** (NKJV)
>
> If you then, being evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will the heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to those who ask Him!"
>

<!-- p59 -->
</span>

<!-- p60 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="38-39" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p61 -->
> **<u>Acts 2:38-39</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Then Peter said to them, "Repent, and let every one of you be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of sins; and you shall receive the gift of the Holy Spirit.
>
> For the promise is to you and to your children, and to all who are afar off, as many as the Lord our God will call."
>

<!-- p62 -->
</span>

<!-- p63 -->
###  **2. Ask, seek, and receive by faith**

<!-- p64 -->
Scripture emphasizes that the baptism in the Holy Spirit is given as it is sought in prayer and received with faith-filled expectation. The key to experiencing the baptism in the Holy Spirit is the connection of faith with the will to seek and receive. If you are not sure whether God desires to give you the baptism in the Holy Spirit, ask God—He is a good Father! When He prompts you, you may feel God’s tangible presence in your body in a way you have never experienced before. It’s okay if you don’t feel anything! God is great and does not need to be pressed into a single perfect mold. If you feel your heart being drawn to God, follow Him!

<!-- p65 -->

## Gifts of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p79 -->
When the Holy Spirit was poured out and people received the baptism in the Holy Spirit, manifestations of the Spirit often accompanied it.

<!-- p80 -->
The New Testament contains several lists of gifts:

<!-- p81 -->
- Motivational gifts given by God: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:6-8</span>

<!-- p82 -->
- Discussed in the chapter on the Church.

<!-- p83 -->
- The fivefold ministry gifts given by Jesus: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-13</span>

<!-- p84 -->
- Discussed in the chapter on the Church.

<!-- p85 -->
- The manifestation gifts given by the Holy Spirit: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span>

<!-- p86 -->
- Discussed here.

<!-- p87 -->
Each set of gifts is given for different purposes and operates in different ways, yet together they point to the reality that within **the body of Christ** the gifts display both **diversity** and **unity**. Each gift carries a unique role in accomplishing God’s purposes.

<!-- p88 -->
### Purpose and Function of the Gifts

<!-- p89 -->
The primary purposes of the gifts of the Holy Spirit are to build up the body of Christ and to give glory to God:

<!-- p90 -->
- **Edification** - to strengthen and encourage believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
- **Unity** - to promote harmony and cooperation within the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:12-13</span>).

<!-- p92 -->
- **Evangelism** - to demonstrate God’s power as a witness to unbelievers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="43" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:43</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:6-7</span>).

<!-- p93 -->
- **Service** - to meet the needs of others through acts of mercy and generosity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:6-8</span>).

<!-- p94 -->
These gifts are not for personal glory but for the common good (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:7</span>). They function best when used in love and humility that reflect the character of Christ（1 Corinthians 13）.

<!-- p95 -->
Paul emphasized that spiritual gifts must be used decently and in order (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:40</span>). In this way, the use of the gifts ensures unity, peace, and understanding rather than confusion or division.

<!-- p96 -->
- Prophecy should be weighed (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:29</span>).

<!-- p97 -->
- When a message in tongues is addressed to the church, pray that it may be interpreted (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:27-28</span>).

<!-- p98 -->
- Gifts must always bring edification to the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:12</span>).

<!-- p99 -->
This order does not quench the Spirit; rather, it ensures that the gifts work in a way that **honors God** and **benefits people**.

<!-- p100 -->
The operation of spiritual gifts must always be rooted in love。Paul devotes an entire chapter（1 Corinthians chapter 13）to explain that without love, even the greatest spiritual gifts are meaningless。Love provides the motivation and framework to build people up with the gifts rather than to harm them。

<!-- p101 -->
Furthermore, the gifts operate **as the Spirit wills**（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:11</span>）. They cannot be manufactured or controlled by human effort - they are often sovereignly given and empowered by the Holy Spirit at times of need and service。<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 13:8</span> states that prophecy and tongues will eventually cease。Theologically, those who believe the gifts of the Holy Spirit have ceased are known as ‘Cessationists’. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 13:10</span> says, 「When the perfect one \[Jesus\] comes, the partial will be done away with」。Since Jesus has not yet returned for His church, the gifts are still at work on earth。

<!-- p102 -->
It is critically important to recognize that the **gifts of the Spirit** must not be separated from the **fruit of the Spirit**（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-23</span>）。While the gifts display power, the fruit expresses character—love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control—。

<!-- p103 -->
- **Gifts without fruit**: Believers may exercise gifts yet lack maturity and character, which can lead to pride, misuse, or harm。

<!-- p104 -->
- **Fruit without gifts**: Believers may have godly character yet lack the power to minister effectively。

<!-- p105 -->
Both are necessary。The gifts equip believers for service。The fruit is a sign of their spiritual health（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 7:16</span>）and gives a ‘pleasant flavor’ to those who taste it。

<!-- p106 -->
### Explaining the nine manifestation gifts of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p107 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span> lists the nine manifestation gifts of the Holy Spirit:

<!-- p108 -->
1.  **Word of wisdom** - A supernatural insight into God’s plan and guidance for a situation。It applies God’s wisdom to complex problems and decisions（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:10</span>）。

<!-- p109 -->
2.  **Word of knowledge** - A supernatural revelation of information about people, events, or circumstances that could not be known naturally（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 4:17-18</span>）。

<!-- p110 -->
3.  **Faith** - The supernatural ability to trust God and expect miracles even in seemingly impossible situations。This gift calls forth boldness and confidence（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 3:6-7</span>）。

<!-- p111 -->
4.  **Gifts of healings** - Supernatural power to heal physical, emotional, and spiritual illnesses and restore wholeness（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 3:6-8</span>、<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 9:17-18</span>）。

<!-- p112 -->
5.  **Working of miracles** - The ability to perform supernatural acts that transcend natural laws, such as multiplying food or raising the dead（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40-41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 9:40-41</span>、<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 2:9-11</span>）。

<!-- p113 -->
6.  **Prophecy** - Declaring God’s truth and will under the inspiration of the Holy Spirit。This should encourage, edify, and comfort（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>）。

<!-- p114 -->
7.  **Discerning of spirits（discernment）** - The ability to distinguish whether a spiritual influence is from God, from the enemy, or human（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 16:16-18</span>）。

<!-- p115 -->
8.  **Various kinds of tongues** - Speaking in a language unknown to the speaker。Often used as worship or prayer, or as a sign to unbelievers（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:4</span>、<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:22</span>）。

<!-- p116 -->
9.  **Interpretation of tongues** - The supernatural ability to interpret a message spoken in tongues so that the church may be edified（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:27</span>）。

<!-- p117 -->
Paul makes it clear that all the gifts are precious and should work together for the common good of the church（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:7</span>）。However, he also urges believers to desire the greater gifts（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:31</span>）。The greater gifts are often understood to be those that most directly edify the church, such as prophecy（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:1-5</span>）。

<!-- p118 -->
- Prophecy is particularly emphasized because it strengthens, encourages, and comforts people（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>）。Paul teaches believers to earnestly seek this gift because it promotes clarity and understanding in the church。

<!-- p119 -->
- Gifts such as tongues are highly valued for personal edification and prayer（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:4</span>）。However, Paul teaches that when giving a message in tongues to the church, one should pray for interpretation so that the church may be built up（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:27-28</span>）。

<!-- p120 -->
The Holy Spirit gives gifts, but in order to use them, we need to yield ourselves to and obey the Holy Spirit. Just like muscles or talents, as we practice yielding to the Spirit and using those gifts, we become more confident in how to use them and are able to use them more consistently.
