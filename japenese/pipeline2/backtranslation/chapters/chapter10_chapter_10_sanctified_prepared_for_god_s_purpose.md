# Chapter 10 - Sanctification - Preparation for God's Purpose

<!-- p1 -->
In the past two chapters, we have addressed several important themes. We have explored the role and work of the Holy Spirit, as well as the role and work of the Church.

<!-- p2 -->
While we know we have a part to play in the Church and need training, we also need cleansing. To truly prepare for God's purpose, we must be sanctified.

<!-- p3 -->
The definition of **sanctification** is to cleanse something for a special purpose. It is closely related to the term **consecration**, which means setting something apart for a special purpose.

<!-- p4 -->
Imagine a master sculptor preparing to work on a large block of marble. The master envisions how this rough, shapeless marble can be transformed by his painstaking craftsmanship. He has a very specific design and instructs his assistants to set this block of marble apart for a special creation (**consecration**). As he refines the marble, giving it texture, depth, and form, he purifies it for that special design (**sanctification**). The act of dedicating the marble to a special project is momentary, but the process of shaping the marble into the final design requires considerable patience.

<!-- p5 -->
At salvation, we were set apart and consecrated to God. Through the work of cleansing our souls with the help of the Spirit, we are transformed. This process of cleansing is what sanctifies us.

<!-- p6 -->
When we received Jesus through salvation, we were born anew. We were justified, and our sins were forgiven. Our spirit was reborn and became alive in Christ. We were adopted into God's family and literally became the temple of the Holy Spirit. Concerning such a remarkable change, the Bible says, "*There is joy in the presence of the angels of God over one sinner who repents.*" (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 15:10</span>).

<!-- p7 -->
Receiving Jesus by salvation (**justification**) saves our spirit, cleanses us, enables us to stand before God, and allows us to receive God's Holy Spirit. Yet despite such a great salvation, our soul (mind, will, and emotions) still needs to be dealt with!

<!-- p8 -->
Was the work of salvation incomplete? No! The work of salvation is effective, but it must be continued throughout the believer's life by renewal brought about by the Holy Spirit. This process of renewal and transformation is the work of **sanctification**.

<!-- p9 -->
We see that dealings must continue even after conversion because our painful memories have not been resolved, many beliefs remain, and many habits and rituals have not disappeared. Moreover, conversion did not sever our mind from its carnal (of the flesh) nature.

<!-- p10 -->
The word "carnal" (fleshly) derives from a Latin root meaning "flesh" or "body." The carnal nature is within each of us from birth and, like animal instinct, exists to help us survive. The way the fleshly nature operates is self-directed, self-centered, and self-serving. A child is a natural example of the carnal nature. Despite that innocence, this nature is primarily oriented toward its own wants, desires, and needs, and—watch out!—if each and every one of those expectations is not met.

<!-- p11 -->
Part of becoming an adult is maturing out of childish ways, but when pressed, we can very easily revert to patterns of self-centered thinking and living. As James says.

<!-- p12 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p13 -->
> **<u>James 4:1</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Where do wars and fights come from among you? Do they not come from your desires for pleasure that war in your members?
>

<!-- p14 -->
</span>

<!-- p15 -->
Paul further explains this carnal nature in the Epistle to the Romans (chapters 6–8). He says that this fleshly nature makes him do what he hates and do evil. He describes this inner conflict as a battle between a human-derived nature and the spirit created by God.

<!-- p16 -->
This conflict between flesh and spirit exists within each of us, and it is our responsibility to deal with it. If we do not confront the nature of the flesh, even though our spirit has been redeemed, our soul will remain entangled in patterns of spiritual ruin.

<!-- p17 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p18 -->
> **<u>Romans 8:6-8</u>** (NKJV)
>
> For to be carnally minded is death, but to be spiritually minded is life and peace.
>
> Because the carnal mind is enmity against God; for it is not subject to the law of God, nor indeed can be.
>
> So then, those who are in the flesh cannot please God.
>

<!-- p19 -->
</span>

<!-- p20 -->
These words of Paul are both a warning and a promise to us. Even if we have been born anew, we still have a carnal mindset that must be brought into obedience to the knowledge of Christ. As we do so, we walk in God’s life and peace.

<!-- p21 -->
Take courage; we have hope! We must walk according to the Spirit and be led by the Spirit of God. If we do so, we will not walk according to the flesh.

<!-- p22 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="1,13,14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p23 -->
> **<u>Romans 8:1,13,14</u>** (NKJV)
>
> There is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus. They do not walk according to the flesh, but according to the Spirit.
>
> For if you live according to the flesh, you will die. But if by the Spirit you put to death the deeds of the body, you will live.
>
> For as many as are led by the Spirit of God, these are sons of God.
>

<!-- p24 -->
</span>

<!-- p25 -->
How do we learn to walk in the Spirit? Is it merely a matter of just consciously choosing? We learn to walk in the Spirit the same way we learn to walk in the natural realm. We are moved and motivated to grow; we struggle and wrestle; we crawl and stumble, and little by little we advance until we can stand on our own feet. Our earthly parents cannot walk in our place, but they can support, encourage, help, and strengthen us in our walking.

<!-- p26 -->
Likewise, God will not walk out our faith for us. God teaches us, but we must make the effort. As Paul said to the Philippians:

<!-- p27 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="12-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p28 -->
> **<u>Philippians 2:12-13</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Therefore, my beloved, as you have always obeyed, not as in my presence only, but now much more in my absence, work out your own salvation with fear and trembling;
>
> for it is God who works in you both to will and to do for His good pleasure.
>

<!-- p29 -->
</span>

<!-- p30 -->
We must work out our own salvation. This does not mean climbing onto a wooden cross and being physically crucified, but we must crucify our fleshly nature (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:24</span>). God promises to work in us and help us, but we must decide to walk this out and see it through.

<!-- p31 -->
It is true to say that we are dealing with our fleshly nature, and it is also true to say that God is dealing with it. The work of the cross (justification) was difficult for Jesus, because He had to give His life for us. But for us it is easy; it is a free gift. The work of the Spirit (sanctification) is easy for God, because He delights to walk in perfect truth and to dwell with us. But it is difficult for us, because we must crucify the old nature, and our nature is not very interested in being crucified.

<!-- p32 -->
This process is cooperative. God helps, aids, leads, directs, and corrects. We listen, obey, walk in obedience, and respond. The result is growth and change. This is the pruning that Jesus said God performs in us. Jesus is the vine, the Father is the vinedresser, and we are the branches:

<!-- p33 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p34 -->
> **<u>John 15:1-4</u>** (NKJV)
>
> "I am the true vine, and My Father is the vinedresser.
>
> Every branch in Me that does not bear fruit He takes away; and every branch that bears fruit He prunes, that it may bear more fruit.
>
> You are already clean because of the word which I have spoken to you.
>
> Abide in Me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself unless it abides in the vine, neither can you, unless you abide in Me.
>

<!-- p35 -->
</span>

<!-- p36 -->
Pruning is very healthy for plants. Recalling the chapter on repentance, we are called to turn away from dead works (works that no longer bear fruit) and turn toward God. As a result of this turning away from the dead areas of our lives and turning toward God, the great Gardener of our lives can prune the branches, improve growth, and bring forth even greater spiritual fruit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:2</span>).

<!-- p37 -->
Pruning can be painful, but it is an expression of loving care that shows God is shaping us into vessels for His purposes (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 12:11</span>). In the course of pruning, some may feel a sense of loss, or even fear. Yet God is gentle; He will not break a bruised reed (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="42" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 42:3</span>). Vines are pruned during dormancy so that stress on the plant is minimized. A gardener who tends growth cuts back the old, woody growth that no longer bears as it once did, thereby enabling the vine to direct its energy to the branches most likely to bear good fruit.

<!-- p38 -->
Like a gardener who shapes branches to direct growth, God may allow seasons of correction and training to refine our faith and character, yet His work is patient and kind. God prunes those who abide in Him. Our focus should be on abiding in Christ rather than on the pruning work itself.

<!-- p39 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 12:11</span> reminds us that pruning “*produces the peaceable fruit of righteousness*” in those who are trained by it.

<!-- p40 -->
How are we trained by righteousness? By being transformed through the renewing of our minds.

<!-- p41 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p42 -->
> **<u>Romans 12:1-2</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Therefore, brothers, I urge you by the mercies of God: present your bodies as a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable to God. This is your reasonable service of worship.
>
> Also, do not be conformed to this world. Rather, be transformed by the renewing of your mind, so that you may discern what is the will of God—that is, what is good, acceptable, and perfect.
>

<!-- p43 -->
</span>

<!-- p44 -->
Paul presents two concise requirements for transformation:

<!-- p45 -->
<u>1. Offer your body to God as a living sacrifice</u>. A **living** sacrifice does not physically die in the midst of the offering. However, because a living sacrifice cannot die, we must continually carry it to the altar!

<!-- p46 -->
<u>2. Renew your mind!</u> Renewal is a word based on the Greek **Anakainōsis** (*An-ak-ah'-ee-no-sis -* **G342**), meaning “renewal” or “renovation,” and it focuses on the ongoing process of inner change and growth.

<!-- p47 -->
This renewal brings about transformation. Transformation is a word based on the Greek **Metamorphoō** (*met-am-or-fo'-o -* **G3339**), meaning “to change into another form,” “to transfigure,” and it emphasizes complete change. Metamorphoō is the etymological root of the English metamorphosis（transformation）, which refers to the process by which a caterpillar becomes a butterfly.

<!-- p48 -->
God desires to fundamentally change our hearts and our patterns of thought. His desire is that we not assimilate to the ways of the world, but that we know what the will of God is—that is, what is good（beneficial）, pleasing（acceptable）, and perfect（complete & lacking nothing）.

<!-- p49 -->
The requirements for transformation really are that simple, **though training is required**!

<!-- p50 -->
Our battle against the flesh cannot be won by our flesh. Rather, we win with what God provides for the battle of the mind. The Word（the sword of truth）is powerful to correct every imagination and proud thought, and to place every thought under arrest（captivity）that opposes obedience to God.

<!-- p51 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="3-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p52 -->
> **<u>2 Corinthians 10:3-6</u>** (NKJV)
>
> For though we walk in the flesh, we do not wage war according to the flesh.
>
> The weapons of our warfare are not of the flesh, but are mighty in God for pulling down strongholds,
>
> casting down arguments and every lofty thing that exalts itself against the knowledge of God, and taking every thought captive to the obedience of Christ,
>
> and being ready to punish every disobedience when your obedience is fulfilled.
>

<!-- p53 -->
</span>

<!-- p54 -->
When authorities arrest someone, they put handcuffs and shackles on them and put them in jail. If that person escapes restraint, they do not turn a blind eye; they expend tremendous resources and do not rest until the person is back under control. We too should act with that kind of zeal in the contest over our thoughts!

<!-- p55 -->
We are renewed by obedience and by the experience of truth. Jesus said that if we abide in the Word, we will be His disciples, we will experience（<u>know</u>）the truth, and it will set us free（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-32" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 8:31-32</span>）!

<!-- p56 -->
The experience of truth is different from knowledge of the truth. Experience is the Greek Ginōskō (Ghin-oce'-ko **- G1097**), meaning to know through personal experience and intimate understanding, and it implies progressive learning.

<!-- p57 -->
It is not academic knowledge about the truth but personal experience of the truth that transforms us and renews our minds. We are sanctified by the Lord’s truth!（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="17" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 17:17</span>）。

<!-- p58 -->
## How to Renew the Mind

<!-- p59 -->
Renewing our minds is not a uniform, one-size-fits-all process-which part of the mind we are talking about depends! God designed our minds with astonishing complexity, including several different memory systems. Each system serves a unique purpose. Some operate automatically in the background, while others require focused, intentional thought. These systems are gifts God gave to help us survive, grow, and thrive, but when misused or left unrenewed, they can also mislead us. Understanding how these systems function gives us insight into how to cooperate with God’s process of transformation.

<!-- p60 -->
In the next section, we will describe each memory system, its purpose and function, practical steps to renew this aspect of the mind, and resources for ongoing practice and learning.

<!-- p61 -->
### **Memory Systems and How to Renew Them**

<!-- p62 -->
#### **1. Explicit Memory (Declarative Memory)**

<!-- p63 -->
**Purpose:** Stores facts, events, and experiences that can be consciously recalled. This is divided into two types-**semantic** and **episodic** memory.

<!-- p64 -->
**Semantic memory - facts and knowledge.** Semantic memory is where facts, definitions, and general knowledge-for example, historical dates, Bible verses, and practical information-are stored. This helps predict outcomes, make decisions, and avoid danger (e.g., recognizing poisonous plants). While it brings stability and order, it can also lead to rigidity-becoming overly reliant on rules rather than adapting to unexpected situations-.

<!-- p65 -->
**How to renew:**

<!-- p66 -->
- **Study the Bible** to shape your worldview (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="119" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="105" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 119:105</span>).

<!-- p67 -->
- **Memorize truth** and remember doctrine to stand firm in faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 3:16</span>).

<!-- p68 -->
- **Read Christian books.** For example, read *"Renewing the Mind" by Casey Treat* and *"Battlefield of the Mind" by Joyce Meyer* to cultivate God-pleasing thought patterns.

<!-- p69 -->
**Episodic memory - personal experiences.** Episodic memory stores our personal experiences and ties them to specific times and places-such as a wedding day or a traumatic event. These memories provide context, help us learn from the past, and build relationships. However, episodic memory is prone to emotional biases and distortions. Past trauma can create fear associations and make it difficult to move forward.

<!-- p70 -->
**How to renew:**

<!-- p71 -->
- **Reflect and confess** to bring hidden pain into God’s light (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 1:9</span>).

<!-- p72 -->
- **Reframe past experiences** through the lens of God’s grace (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:28</span>).

<!-- p73 -->
- **Seek inner healing** through prayer and counseling (*“Your Pain is Showing” by Larry F. Low, "The Bondage Breaker" by Neil T. Anderson*, *"Emotionally Healthy Spirituality" by Peter Scazzero*).

<!-- p74 -->
#### **2. Implicit Memory (Nondeclarative Memory)**

<!-- p75 -->
**Purpose:** Unconscious memory used for habits, skills, and emotional associations. It operates automatically and helps you respond quickly to situations. This is divided into **procedural memory** and **emotional conditioning**.

<!-- p76 -->
**Procedural memory - skills and habits.** Procedural memory enables you to perform tasks without thinking-driving, typing, playing an instrument, etc. It is efficient and saves mental energy and time. However, bad habits and negative patterns can also become deeply ingrained, making them difficult to break.

<!-- p77 -->
**How to renew:**

<!-- p78 -->
- **Practice spiritual disciplines** such as prayer and fasting to build godly habits (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 5:14</span>).

<!-- p79 -->
- **Train yourself for godliness** (through repetition and obedience) (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 4:7</span>).

<!-- p80 -->
- **Read books like** *"Atomic Habits" by James Clear*（practical）and *"Spiritual Disciplines for the Christian Life" by Donald Whitney*（biblical）, thereby building habits that honor God.

<!-- p81 -->
**Emotional Conditioning - Emotional Associations.** Emotional conditioning links sensory cues such as smells and sounds to emotions, triggering responses before we consciously process them. This system protects us, but when past experiences are negative, it can also trigger irrational fears and emotional reactions.

<!-- p82 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p83 -->
- **Pray for healing from past wounds**（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="41" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 41:10</span>）.

<!-- p84 -->
- **Practice forgiveness** and let go of bitterness（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-32" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:31-32</span>）.

<!-- p85 -->
- **Replace fear with God’s promises**（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 1:7</span>）.

<!-- p86 -->
- **Seek advice from books such as** *“Your Pain is Showing” by Larry F. Low, "The Bondage Breaker" by Neil T. Anderson*, “*Soul Care" by Rob Reimer* to help overcome emotional triggers.

<!-- p87 -->
#### **3. Working Memory (Short-term Memory)**

<!-- p88 -->
**Purpose:** Provides a temporary memory store for problem-solving and decision-making. It helps us focus, hold information briefly, and process tasks—such as remembering instructions or making quick decisions. While powerful, it is easily distracted and can be easily overloaded, especially under stress.

<!-- p89 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p90 -->
- **Focus on and meditate on God’s Word daily**（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 1:2</span>）.

<!-- p91 -->
- **Renew your mind through intentional thinking**（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:2</span>）.

<!-- p92 -->
- **Practice mindfulness** and simplify mental clutter（*“Habits of Grace” by David Mathis*）.

<!-- p93 -->
- **Read** *"The Celebration of Discipline" by Richard Foster*、to sharpen focus through spiritual disciplines.

<!-- p94 -->
## Conclusion

<!-- p95 -->
Renewing the mind is not a quick fix. It is a lifelong, incremental process of transformation. Each memory system plays a role in shaping our thoughts, emotions, and behaviors. By intentionally cooperating with the Holy Spirit, applying Scripture, and cultivating godly habits, we can train our minds to align with God’s truth. Whether addressing past wounds, learning new habits, or staying focused in the present moment, God has provided both spiritual tools and practical wisdom to help us walk in renewal and freedom（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 10:5</span>）.

<!-- p96 -->
This world is full of troubles, hardships, and trials. Through disciplined renewal of the mind, our souls can be transformed and live a life of freedom before God, filled with the fruit of the Spirit（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-25</span>）.
