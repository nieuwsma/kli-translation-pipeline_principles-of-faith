# Chapter 9 - The Church &amp; Our Role in It

<!-- p1 -->
The church is the way God reveals Himself on earth—through His people, accomplishing His work. We are called to be His body; we are His hands and feet, to accomplish His work and will. Without our efforts, the harvest would remain in the fields, unable to be gathered.

<!-- p2 -->
## Who Is the Church?

<!-- p3 -->
The Church, ***Ekklesia**（eh-klee-SEE-ah - **G1577**），* comes from two Greek words:

<!-- p4 -->
- **"Ek"**: "Out of."

<!-- p5 -->
- **"Kaleo"**: "To call."

<!-- p6 -->
Taken together, **Ekklesia** literally means "called out" or "an assembly of those who have been called out." In the ancient Greek city-states, it referred to citizens summoned to gather for a specific purpose, such as legislative or judicial meetings. It is a secular term referring to an assembly of people.

<!-- p7 -->
In the New Testament, it is used primarily to refer to the community of those who trust in Jesus Christ. It can refer to:

<!-- p8 -->
- **The local church**: the gathering of a group of believers in a specific location (for example: the churches in Corinth, Philippi, etc.).

<!-- p9 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p10 -->
> **<u>1 Corinthians 1:2</u>** (NKJV)
>
> To the church of God which is in Corinth, to those sanctified in Christ Jesus, called to be saints, together with all who in every place call on the name of our Lord Jesus Christ——He is their Lord and ours:
>

<!-- p11 -->
</span>

<!-- p12 -->
- **The universal Church**: the whole body of believers across time and space.

<!-- p13 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p14 -->
> **<u>Matthew 16:18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And I also say to you: You are Peter, and on this rock I will build My church; the gates of Hades shall not prevail against it.
>

<!-- p15 -->
</span>

<!-- p16 -->
The church is people, not the building or location where they meet. **The called-out ones**: this embodies the idea that Christians are called out from the world and set apart for God’s purposes (for example: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:9</span>), for a specific mission and purpose (the Great Commission). Strictly speaking, whenever two or more believers gather together (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="18" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 18:20</span>), that constitutes a church. Typically, churches organize regular gatherings, perhaps with a fixed place and time; but wherever two or three gather in Jesus’ name, that is the church!

<!-- p17 -->
The church was established by Jesus and grew in the first century through the work of His followers, the disciples. The various local churches we experience across the earth today are the direct result of the initial work of Christ and His apostles. As believers, we should commit ourselves to a local church for fellowship, provision, ministry equipping, and doing good works!

<!-- p18 -->
To help us understand what the church is, Scripture gives us powerful images that show what it is meant to be.

<!-- p19 -->
## Understanding the Church

<!-- p20 -->
There are several core metaphorical images used to portray the characteristics of the church：

<!-- p21 -->
1.  The Body

<!-- p22 -->
2.  The Bride

<!-- p23 -->
3.  The Temple

<!-- p24 -->
4.  The Family

<!-- p25 -->
5.  The Flock

<!-- p26 -->
6.  The Vine and the Branches

<!-- p27 -->
Each metaphor emphasizes a distinct aspect of the church, but together they reveal the church’s communal identity as both unified and diverse under Christ’s leadership。

<!-- p28 -->
### The Body

<!-- p29 -->
The church is often described as the **body of Christ**, emphasizing its unity and diversity。With Christ as the head, the church is like a living organism, and every believer plays an indispensable yet distinct role。Each member has unique gifts and contributes according to its function to the health and mission of the body。这一意象凸显信徒彼此的相互依存，特别是身体必须相连才能运作，并且我们的功用是合作而非竞争。当各部分同心配搭，身体就在合一与爱中成长兴旺（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:12-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:15-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 1:18</span>）。

<!-- p30 -->
### The Bride

<!-- p31 -->
The church is described as the **bride of Christ**, reflecting an intimate and loving relationship with Jesus。Just as a bride is cherished and prepared for her husband, the church is sanctified and cleansed by Christ’s self-giving love。This metaphor embodies the devotion and fidelity that the church ought to have, and it also reflects Christ’s deep love for His people。这一意象也指向基督与教会最终的联合，在羔羊的婚宴中庆贺，教会将以荣耀呈现，毫无斑点或瑕疵（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="25-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:25-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 19:7-9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="21" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 21:2</span>）。

<!-- p32 -->
### The Temple

<!-- p33 -->
The church is described as the **temple of God**, the place where His Spirit dwells。Believers are called living stones, joined together and built on the foundation of the apostles and prophets, with Christ Himself as the cornerstone。这座圣洁的结构表明神住在祂的子民中间，也表明他们作为敬拜与祷告群体的身份与角色。The temple imagery underscores the importance of holiness and the church’s common purpose of glorifying God（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 3:16-17</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:19-22</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:4-5</span>）。

<!-- p34 -->
### The Family

<!-- p35 -->
The church is described as the **household of God**, emphasizing relational unity and mutual care among its members。By the Spirit of adoption, believers become God’s children and are brought into His household。This family imagery highlights the love, belonging, and shared inheritance that believers have in Christ。It also reflects the call for us to treat one another with compassion and humility in God’s household, as brothers and sisters（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:19</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:15-17</span>）。Furthermore, through the family image it depicts how the members of the church are to become mature, that they would be taught and nourished by spiritual mothers and fathers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:1-2</span>), that we could go from infants drinking the pure milk of the Word, to full grown adults able to eat the strong meat of the Word (Hebrews 5).

<!-- p36 -->
### The Flock

<!-- p37 -->
The church is described as a **flock**, a flock under the care of Jesus, the Good Shepherd。This image expresses the church’s dependence on Christ for guidance, provision, and protection。It also emphasizes the responsibility of church leaders, as under-shepherds, to faithfully tend God’s flock, following Christ’s example of love and care。The flock imagery speaks of trust, obedience, and the intimate relationship between the Great Shepherd and His flock（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:14-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:2</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>）。

<!-- p38 -->
### The Vine and the Branches

<!-- p39 -->
The church is described as the **vine and the branches**, illustrating the vital connection between believers and Christ。Jesus is the true vine, and believers are the branches who must abide in Him to bear fruit。This metaphor emphasizes dependence on Christ for spiritual vitality, growth, and fruitfulness。Apart from Him, believers can do nothing, but if they remain in Him, they can glorify God and fulfill their purpose。Jesus prunes us so that we may bear more fruit in our lives（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:1-5</span>）。

<!-- p40 -->
## The Role of the Church

<!-- p41 -->
The mission of the church，like the role of the Holy Spirit，can be understood in three dimensions：upward、inward、and outward，each reflecting a unique facet of its calling in God's plan。这些维度引导教会成就其作为敬拜、造就、并宣教群体的角色。

<!-- p42 -->
### Upward: Worship and Glorify God

<!-- p43 -->
The church's primary purpose is to worship and glorify God。Corporate worship，that is, believers gathering together to exalt God，is a visible reflection of His glory，and a response to His greatness。正如诗人宣告：“*Worship the Lord in the beauty of holiness；let all the earth tremble before Him*”（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="96" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 96:9</span>）。In addition to corporate worship，the church is called to a life of obedience and surrender，offering itself as a “living sacrifice”，as an act of spiritual worship（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:1-2</span>）。This upward focus centers the church on God's majesty，and aligns its priorities with His will。

<!-- p44 -->
### Inward: Building Up the Body

<!-- p45 -->
The Great Commission commands us to ‘teach them to obey’，and this command goes far beyond formal teaching methods like those in a book such as this。Discipleship（discussed further in the chapter on discipleship training）is to shape followers into a form consistent with this discipline，just as parents raise children。Good parents do not hand an encyclopedia to a five-year-old and tell him to figure it out for himself！Instead，they model、demonstrate、correct、guide、nurture、cherish、care for、and shepherd。

<!-- p46 -->
Parents sacrifice for their children，because they love their children，because they belong to one body，because they share the same bloodline。As members of Christ's body and family，we share the same body，we share the same bloodline。The Lord's Supper reminds us through its symbolism that through Christ's sacrifice we are bound to one another。Receiving the Lord's Supper should be a focal point of unity，reminding us that we all need redemption，and that salvation comes from God alone。在这合一中我们看到，身体上没有哪一部分比另一部分更大，惟有作我们头的基督除外。

<!-- p47 -->
### Outward: Reaching the World

<!-- p48 -->
The most well-known part of the church's mission is to reach outward, touching the world with the gospel。Jesus' Great Commission calls His followers to proclaim the gospel and make disciples of all nations（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19-20</span>）。By the power of the Holy Spirit，the church is to be His witness to the ends of the earth（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>）。

<!-- p49 -->
Jesus is the light of the world（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 8:12</span>）。Light shines out in all directions from its source。His light shines through His self-revelation：the Word、His Spirit、and us，His body。We are the light of the world；in our world shrouded in darkness，our lives should radiate His light and display good works that glorify God（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 5:14-16</span>）。Practically，this means the church is called to speak for justice and mercy，both spiritual and physical。As James says，our pure worship is to look after the vulnerable、the orphans、and the widows（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:27</span>）。

<!-- p50 -->
## Church Leadership

<!-- p51 -->
Across the whole Christian world，there are all kinds of denominations、councils、gatherings、and assemblies。There are few universal rules about church leadership，but the ones we do have are highly significant：

<!-- p52 -->
1.  Jesus is the church's only head。Everyone serves Him（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 1:18</span>）。

<!-- p53 -->
2.  The standards for leaders are high，and they are worthy of double honor，because they will face double judgment（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:17</span>）。

<!-- p54 -->
2.1  They should be mature、not arrogant、and accountable（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:1-13</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 1:5-9</span>）。

<!-- p55 -->
2.2.  They must be servants of all（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42-45" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 10:42-45</span>）。

<!-- p56 -->
2.3.  They must devote themselves to the word and to prayer（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:4</span>）。

<!-- p57 -->
2.4.  They are responsible for equipping the body to carry out the work of ministry。

<!-- p58 -->
3.  There are no greater or lesser members in the body。Scriptures supporting this include：

<!-- p59 -->
3.1.  There should be no favoritism between rich and poor（James chapter 2）。

<!-- p60 -->
3.2.  There is no distinction between Jews and Gentiles, slave or free, male or female (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 3:27-28</span>).

<!-- p61 -->
The structure of the church, apart from the rules for leadership, is certainly important, but much of it depends on the specific context and is a matter of tradition rather than a universal regulation. “<u>This is how we have always done it!</u>” is a common saying；if you trace it back far enough, there were usually good reasons for doing it that way in the first place. Generally speaking, there is nothing absolutely good or bad about this, but it has to do with preferences, needs, and available resources.

<!-- p62 -->
A useful example of tradition as a structural foundation is family mealtime. What matters most at family mealtime is that everyone is fed and the meal is nutritious. The variations are endless：what they eat, how they eat, how it is seasoned, and so on. Various combinations are generally acceptable, depending primarily on tastes and preferences.

<!-- p63 -->
A church with 10,000 members and a church with only 50 people have different needs and capacities. The paradigm established in Acts and throughout the New Testament is the model of **elders** and **deacons**. Although the leadership structures of various churches and denominations may differ, in the end this model represents the minimum viable structure for church leadership. Let us take a look at these leadership offices and how they are selected.

<!-- p64 -->
### Elders (Overseers)

<!-- p65 -->
Roles and responsibilities:

<!-- p66 -->
- **Spiritual leadership** - Provide oversight, guidance, and shepherding for the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:2</span>).

<!-- p67 -->
- **Teaching and preaching** - Responsible for teaching sound doctrine and guarding against false teaching (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:17</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 1:9</span>).

<!-- p68 -->
- **Shepherding the flock** - Care for the congregation’s spiritual needs, setting an example in humility and service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:2-3</span>).

<!-- p69 -->
- **Guarding the church** - Serve as watchmen, protecting the church from the harm of false doctrine and division (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29-31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:29-31</span>).

<!-- p70 -->
- **Equipping the saints** - Train and equip believers to take on the work of ministry and discipleship (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-12</span>).

<!-- p71 -->
Selection process:

<!-- p72 -->
- **Appointed in each church** - Elders are appointed by the apostles or their delegates, often accompanied by prayer and fasting (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 14:23</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 1:5</span>).

<!-- p73 -->
- **Confirmed by the Holy Spirit** - Their appointment is seen as led by the Holy Spirit, recognizing God’s calling and gifting (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>).

<!-- p74 -->
- **Tested and approved** - Candidates must demonstrate maturity and faithfulness before being entrusted with leadership (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:10</span>). They must not be recent converts! ((<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:6</span>)

<!-- p75 -->
### Deacons (Servants)

<!-- p76 -->
Roles and responsibilities:

<!-- p77 -->
- **Practical service** - Focus on meeting practical needs in the church so that the elders can devote themselves to prayer and teaching (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-4</span>).

<!-- p78 -->
- **Supporting ministries** - Assist with hospitality, care for the poor, and administrative matters (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:3</span>).

<!-- p79 -->
- **Promoting unity** - Address issues of fairness and care to prevent division (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-7</span>).

<!-- p80 -->
- **An example of faith** - Set an example in humility, faith, and service（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:13</span>）。

<!-- p81 -->
Selection process（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-6</span>）：

<!-- p82 -->
- **Recognized by the congregation** - Chosen based on their reputation and spiritual character.

<!-- p83 -->
- **Recognized by the leaders** - The apostles examined and appointed the selected deacons through prayer and the laying on of hands.

<!-- p84 -->
- **Confirmed in service** - Deacons are first tested before formal appointment to prove their faithfulness（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:10</span>）。

<!-- p85 -->
### Qualifications for leadership

<!-- p86 -->
#### Qualifications common to elders and deacons:

<!-- p87 -->
- Moral character - Both must be above reproach, dignified, and self-controlled（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:2</span>, 8)。

<!-- p88 -->
- Family life - Both must be faithful in marriage (“husband of one wife”), and manage their own households well（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:4-5</span>, 12)。

<!-- p89 -->
- Spiritual maturity - Both must be tested and proven qualified before appointment（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:6</span>, 10)。

<!-- p90 -->
- Reputation - Both need to have a good reputation inside and outside the church（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:7</span>）。

<!-- p91 -->
- Faith and integrity - Both must hold the faith with a clear conscience and live out what they believe（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:9</span>）。

<!-- p92 -->
### Structural examples

<!-- p93 -->
Using what we have learned so far about leadership rules and structure, let’s look at two examples of church structure in the New Testament: Jerusalem and Antioch。

<!-- p94 -->
### The church in Jerusalem

<!-- p95 -->
The church in Jerusalem was primarily led by the **apostles**, who focused on teaching, prayer, and spiritual oversight（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:42</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:4</span>）。As the community grew, the need for administrative structure became evident, especially in practical matters like caring for widows and distributing resources. This led to the establishment of **deacons**（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-7</span>）。These individuals were chosen for their spiritual maturity and good reputation, taking on practical ministry so that the apostles could focus on their spiritual responsibilities. Over time, **elders** also emerged as leaders in the Jerusalem church. For example, during the Jerusalem Council（Acts 15）, the apostles and elders participated together in decision-making, showing a collaborative leadership structure.

<!-- p96 -->
### The church in Antioch

<!-- p97 -->
Compared with Jerusalem, the church in Antioch had a more decentralized and diverse leadership structure. The church was led by a group of **prophets and teachers**, including Barnabas, Saul (Paul), Simeon, Lucius, and Manaen（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 13:1</span>）。The Antioch church was also more oriented toward outreach to the Gentiles, whereas the Jerusalem church more reflected its Jewish roots. Although the New Testament does not explicitly mention deacons in Antioch, considering the growth in the church’s size and their participation in sending relief to Jerusalem（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29-30" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 11:29-30</span>），it is likely that corresponding practical service roles existed. The Antioch church became a model for sending missionaries; the leaders prayed, fasted, and laid hands on Paul and Barnabas, sending them out（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 13:2-3</span>）。This shows a structure that balances spiritual oversight (prophets and teachers) with mission expansion and practical support.

<!-- p98 -->
Unlike Paul’s corrections to the Corinthians and Galatians, the Bible does not criticize the leadership structure of either Jerusalem or Antioch. This indicates that under the guidance of the Holy Spirit, these churches could adjust their gatherings according to their unique needs and circumstances within the framework of sound doctrine and apostolic teaching（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:42</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>）。

<!-- p99 -->
Some terms we have not yet defined and studied are used in these passages: *apostles、prophets、evangelists*. These gifts are part of the fivefold ministry that Jesus gave to the church.

<!-- p100 -->
### The Fivefold Ministry Gifts

<!-- p101 -->
Jesus established five distinct ministry gifts, commonly called the **Fivefold Ministry Gifts**, to equip believers for service and to build up the body of Christ.

<!-- p102 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p103 -->
> **<u>Ephesians 4:11-13</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And He Himself gave some to be apostles, some prophets, some evangelists, and some pastors and teachers,
>
> for the equipping of the saints for the work of ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ,
>
> till we all come to the unity of the faith and of the knowledge of the Son of God, to a perfect man, to the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ;
>

<!-- p104 -->
</span>

<!-- p105 -->
The five gifts given to equip the saints for the work of ministry are:

<!-- p106 -->
1.  **Apostle** - Pioneers and visionaries who lay foundations, plant churches, and advance God’s kingdom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:20</span>). Apostles model courage and faith, showing how to step into new territory and take spiritual initiative.

<!-- p107 -->
2.  **Prophet** - Messengers and guides who proclaim God’s truth, bringing encouragement, edification, and direction (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>). Prophets stir faith, call people to align with God’s word, and demonstrate reliance on hearing God’s voice.

<!-- p108 -->
3.  **Evangelist** - Those who proclaim the gospel, focused on reaching the lost and inspiring others to act in sharing Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:5-8</span>). Evangelists train disciples to share their faith effectively, model boldness in witness, and stir compassion for the lost.

<!-- p109 -->
4.  **Pastor (shepherd)** - Caregivers and nurturers who protect, guide, and provide spiritual oversight for the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>). Pastors teach the importance of a community of disciples, mutual care, and mutual accountability within the body of Christ.

<!-- p110 -->
5.  **Teacher** - Mentors and trainers who explain doctrine and help believers grow in understanding and applying God’s word (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 2:15</span>). Teachers equip disciples to study Scripture deeply, apply it to daily life, and teach others effectively.

<!-- p111 -->
## The Difference Between Leadership and Gifts

<!-- p112 -->
One commonly misunderstood aspect of church leadership is distinguishing the authority and responsibility of leadership from specific gifts.

<!-- p113 -->
This confusion centers on how we use words like teacher, paster, prophet, apostle, evangelist. These words are gifts. The New Testament church did not have an ‘apostle’ **office**. Some do the work of an apostle and have the gift or calling of an apostle. Having an apostolic gift does not confer authority or responsibility.

<!-- p114 -->
I am a skilled musician; I have played saxophone for more than twenty years. I played all through middle school and college and performed semi-professionally for a few summers while working with an evangelistic team. Even though I have the skill, gift, and ability as a saxophonist, I do not have the right to walk into your church and step onto the worship team’s platform.

<!-- p115 -->
Likewise, some people have a pastoral gift, but that does not make them the ‘THE PASTOR’ of a local church, nor does it make them ‘your’ pastor. The church is led not by Fivefold ministry gifts but by leaders (elders and deacons). It is common for leaders to have certain Fivefold ministry gifts, but these gifts only enhance their leadership; they do not create leadership. Similarly, those who are still growing and who have Fivefold gifts will often serve and aspire to be leaders, but God values character first and gifts second. We all ought to be mature, able to discern God’s word, filled with the Holy Spirit, and do all kinds of good works.

<!-- p116 -->
Every leader, and every gift, must first submit to discipleship and be part of the body.

<!-- p117 -->
Jesus gave specific ministry gifts to equip the saints for the work of service. These are different from motivational gifts (discussed later in this chapter) or manifestation gifts (discussed in the chapter on baptism). As disciples grow, the Fivefold ministry gifts equip them to participate in ministry, while elders and deacons provide oversight to ensure sound doctrine and practice.

<!-- p118 -->
## Your Role in the Church

<!-- p119 -->
So far in this chapter, we have discussed church leadership and the core metaphors for the church. Let us continue with the specific metaphor of the *body* and consider a marathon runner.

<!-- p120 -->
### You are part of the whole

<!-- p121 -->
The church, as the body of Christ, relies on every member working together to accomplish its mission. Just as a body cannot function properly without a part, the church also needs every believer to participate actively, grow, and serve. Every part of the body has a very important role. Being part of the body means staying connected. Even if the body can survive with limited capacity when missing a limb, that limb cannot survive once separated from the body. We must actively be part of the church and not become disconnected from it. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:24</span> says we should meet together and not forsake assembling.

<!-- p122 -->
To truly work together and operate with one heart you need fellowship with one another! You need to know people, love people, and ideally also like people! Build solid relationships and friendships, and repair them when they are strained or hurt. When a joint is injured or inflamed, you need to work to rest or reset it; that way a dislocated joint won’t turn into a fracture! A limb that is broken and cannot heal will eventually be separated, and on your own you cannot be part of the body.

<!-- p123 -->
We do not all have the same function. Some may share the gospel from the pulpit, others at the table. But we are all called to be ministers of reconciliation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 5:18-19</span>). We have all been brought into the priesthood of believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:9</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 1:6</span>). This means we should all engage in the work of ministry by the power of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p124 -->
Toes work with the foot, the foot with the ankle, and so on- each part contributes to the overall mission. A marathon runner must train in order to finish the race. You need preparation- not only to learn, but to practice what you have learned. Like a marathon runner, the church thrives when every member plays their role in unity.

<!-- p125 -->
There are many people in your life whom only you may be able to reach. The pastor cannot be the only one shepherding them, nor can the evangelist be the only one reaching them. God wants to use you, by the power of His Holy Spirit, to reach unbelievers. We are all doing the work of ministry; the Holy Spirit helps us.

<!-- p126 -->
### You need training

<!-- p127 -->
Believers must train and be equipped for their unique role in the church, just as runners strengthen specific muscle groups for a race. If you rashly enter a marathon without training, you will not succeed, and training is not all the same, you need to be well prepared for the race. Jesus gave specific ministry gifts to equip believers, not to have them do all the ministry, but to enable each member to serve effectively in their unique role.

<!-- p128 -->
Training for a marathon brings change. You need strength, and you need to be transformed. We have a responsibility to submit ourselves to God and let Him transform us through renewal (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:1-2</span>). Change should lead you toward maturity, enabling you to carry your own load and help others. You should be able to bear much fruit. This will be explained in detail in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p129 -->
Training is hard, and sometimes uncomfortable. Sometimes you would rather skip the changes you must go through. But if you want to reach the goal, you need to keep to the narrow path. At times you may need warning, comfort, or help- all of this must be done with patience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Thessalonians 5:14</span>).

<!-- p130 -->
Every excellent marathon runner has a coach to train them, you also need a mentor to help you. Of course, you have the Holy Spirit, but He often helps you through His coaches, godly, mature men and women in the body of Christ, and you are also called to serve alongside them.

<!-- p131 -->
As individuals, we first submit to God, secondly, in this context, to a community of believers. Submission in the church means honoring godly leadership (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 13:17</span>) and, with humility, submitting to one another (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:21</span>).

<!-- p132 -->
Submission can be very difficult, especially when you have experienced broken trust or failed leadership in the past. This does not mean blind obedience or control, but reflects a heart of reverence and cooperation. Worldly submission is often demanded by those in power and can humiliate and breed resentment. Biblical submission, rooted in humility and discernment, strengthens the church and promotes mutual accountability. Be willing to listen to guidance and constructive feedback.

<!-- p133 -->
Since we have already laid a solid foundation - you are a meaningful part of the body, but you need training - now let’s begin to find the place that fits you.

<!-- p134 -->
### Find your place

<!-- p135 -->
Finding your role in the church involves seeking God’s guidance, understanding your spiritual gifts, and actively participating in your church community. It is a journey of prayer, exploration, and growth in which you discover how God has uniquely equipped you to contribute to His kingdom. It is very important to note that your role in the church will likely change over time and is unlikely to remain the same for your whole life.

<!-- p136 -->
Here is a practical approach to help you find your place, presented as a series of steps for clarity, but as you walk with God, you will likely repeat the same step many times.

<!-- p137 -->
#### Step 1 - Seek God’s guidance through prayer and God’s Word

<!-- p138 -->
First, **seek God’s guidance through prayer**. God is the ultimate source of your life’s purpose and calling (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="29" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 29:11</span>). Pray regularly, asking for clarity about how you can serve in the church, and for Him to reveal your spiritual gifts and passions. Surrender your plans to His will, and trust that He will direct your steps (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 3:5-6</span>).

<!-- p139 -->
**Study God’s Word** to deepen your understanding of the church’s mission and your role in it. Key passages such as 1 Corinthians 12 and <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-12</span> emphasize spiritual gifts and the diversity of roles in the body of Christ. Reflect on biblical examples of individuals who discovered and fulfilled their roles, such as Paul, Timothy, and Lydia.

<!-- p140 -->
#### Step 2 - Identify Your Gifts, Passions, and Skills

<!-- p141 -->
Identifying your **spiritual gifts** is a key step. Taking a spiritual gifts assessment may be helpful for you. These gifts are given by God to build up the church. The New Testament lists several categories of gifts:

<!-- p142 -->
- God-given **motivational gifts**: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:6-8</span>

<!-- p143 -->
- Discussed after this list.

<!-- p144 -->
- The fivefold ministry gifts given by Jesus: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-13</span>

<!-- p145 -->
- Discussed earlier in this chapter.

<!-- p146 -->
- Manifestation gifts given by the Holy Spirit: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span>

<!-- p147 -->
- Discussed in the chapter on baptism.

<!-- p148 -->
**Motivational gifts** reflect how believers are shaped to think, feel, and act. They motivate believers to serve in particular ways, thereby building up the church. Each gift complements the others, ensuring that the church functions as a unified body with diverse roles (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:4-5</span>). They operate as abilities given by grace (Greek: ***charis** - khar'-ece - **G5463 -** unmerited gift*), meaning they come from God and are not earned or learned. Although the gifts come from God, those who have a certain gift can become more skillful in its use.

<!-- p149 -->
Motivational gifts include:

<!-- p150 -->
1.  **Prophecy** - A God-given inclination to boldly proclaim truth, often focusing on conviction of sin and righteousness, guiding others into alignment with God's word.*

<!-- p151 -->
2.  **Serving (Helps)** - Meeting practical needs through serving and assisting, supporting others.

<!-- p152 -->
3.  **Teaching** - Explaining and clarifying truth, helping others understand and apply God's word.

<!-- p153 -->
4.  **Exhortation (Encouragement)** - Motivating, encouraging, and comforting others to foster growth and perseverance.

<!-- p154 -->
5.  **Giving** - Generously providing resources to meet needs and advance God's work, without seeking recognition.

<!-- p155 -->
6.  **Leadership (Administration)** - Leading others with vision and effective organization to achieve goals.

<!-- p156 -->
7.  **Mercy** - Comforting and helping the wounded or those in need with compassion and care.

<!-- p157 -->
* Prophecy in the context of Romans is understood as different from prophecy as a manifestation gift of the Holy Spirit (1 Corinthians). The same Greek word is used in both passages, but the context is different. For a deeper study of the distinction between the two, I recommend reading:

<!-- p158 -->
- The Gift of Prophecy by Kenneth E. Hagin ([www.rhema.org](http://www.rhema.org))

<!-- p159 -->
<!-- -->

<!-- p160 -->
- *Gifts and Ministries of the Holy Spirit* by Lester Sumrall

<!-- p161 -->
When identifying your gifts, consider your **passion and skills**. God often calls us to serve in areas we are passionate about or skilled in (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 31:2-6</span>). Reflect on what excites you in serving God and others, and consider how your professional skills, hobbies, and life experiences can contribute to ministry.

<!-- p162 -->
Seek guidance and wisdom from **leaders and mentors** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 15:22</span>). Meet with pastors or ministry leaders to discuss your interests, and ask them for honest feedback about where you fit best. Pray over the feedback you receive. Consider joining a discipleship group to receive training and ongoing support.

<!-- p163 -->
#### Step 3 - Be Faithful Now

<!-- p164 -->
The Parable of the Talents (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-30" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 25:14-30</span>) reminds us that being faithful in little leads to being entrusted with much (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 16:10</span>).

<!-- p165 -->
**Get involved and serve** in various ministries to explore where God is calling you. Start by volunteering in your church and the ministries it supports, beginning with small things. For example:

<!-- p166 -->
- Join the hospitality team, children’s ministry, or outreach ministry. Consider leading a discipleship-focused group for new believers, using the group discussion questions and the end-of-chapter activities from this book, *Principles of Faith,* to facilitate conversation.

<!-- p167 -->
- Be willing to step out of your comfort zone to discover new opportunities.

<!-- p168 -->
- Pay attention to where God is working through you and where you feel most effective.

<!-- p169 -->
Practice generosity. We can give to the church in many important ways, and we are taught to do so to accomplish the mission.

<!-- p170 -->
- Time - We can give our time and volunteer. When we give time, we are also putting in effort and using our talents. This can also be a good way to learn new skills and improve existing ones.

<!-- p171 -->
- Resources - We can give our resources. Today, the most common may be financial giving to the church and ministries (not only to “organizations,” but also to people in the church who are in need). Monetary giving is useful because the church can use it to purchase needed resources, but people also often give tangible resources besides money, such as extra houses, clothing, cars, food, building materials, tools, etc. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="32-35" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 4:32-35</span>, we see an example of the early believers’ generous giving: they shared the proceeds from selling extra land and houses and gave to others, so that “there was not a needy person among them.”

<!-- p172 -->
Serving in ministry with others can reveal your strengths and deepen your understanding of your role. Participate in groups or church activities to foster connection. Listen to the burdens and needs of those around you, and consider how you can support them. Building relationships within the church helps you connect with others and understand the needs of the community.

<!-- p173 -->
#### Step 4 - Seek Help!

<!-- p174 -->
Jesus said that in this life we will have tribulation! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:33</span>) We are encouraged not to carry our burdens alone, but to cast them on the One who cares for us! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:7</span>)

<!-- p175 -->
At times on the journey we may be injured. A marathon runner, even when “doing everything right,” may be hurt from bodily overexertion, or may stumble and fall.

<!-- p176 -->
Not all “falls” are related to sin, though some are, and not all weakness is “our fault”; sometimes others wrong us. Whatever the source of our weakness or injury, we should be restored and healed.

<!-- p177 -->
Seeking inner healing and restoration brings great freedom. We are called to cover the weaker members of the body (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:23</span>) and to restore those caught in transgression (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:1</span>).

<!-- p178 -->
There is much to say on this subject; we will explore the topic of healing in an overview in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p179 -->
#### Step 5 - Persevere

<!-- p180 -->
Pay attention to confirmation from God and others。God often affirms your calling through fruit、joy, as well as the affirmation of those around you。Recall where your efforts have borne fruit, and listen to feedback about the impact of your ministry。这能帮助你认出你在服事基督的身体时最有效的地方。

<!-- p181 -->
Be patient, and trust God’s timing。Discovering your role may take time and involve trial and error（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 1:6</span>）。Believe that as you remain faithful and open to His leading, God will guide you。在此期间，立志通过参加工作坊、查经班，以及其他能装备你服事的资源来成长和受训。Do this together with the church, stay connected to the body of Christ, and discover your gifts。

<!-- p182 -->
Remember, the church is not a building or an institution; it is a living, breathing body made up of people like you。As you step into your role, you contribute to its mission and honor Christ as the Head。
