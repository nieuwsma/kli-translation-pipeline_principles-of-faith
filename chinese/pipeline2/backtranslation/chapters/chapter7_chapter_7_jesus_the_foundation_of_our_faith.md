# Chapter 7 - Jesus - The Foundation of Our Faith

<!-- p1 -->
Building on our study of Jesus as the Messiah, in this chapter we will continue to examine Jesus’ life, person, and work. We will see that Jesus is the mediator of a new and better covenant - and that He perfectly assumes the offices of prophet, priest, and king. It is within this new covenant that we, as believers, have a right standing before God and move from a works-based covenant to a trust (faith)-based covenant. Finally, we will read and study the *Apostles’ Creed* to complete our study of Jesus.

<!-- p2 -->
## The Mediator of the New Covenant

<!-- p3 -->
The concept of “mediator” refers to one who stands between two parties as a bridge, facilitating relationship or agreement. In the story of Exodus, Moses was the initial mediator of the covenant God made with His people. Moses received the Law, offered sacrifices (priest), called God’s people to obedience (prophet), and judged them (judge/king). After Moses, throughout the rest of the Old Testament this mediatorial role was carried by the prophets, the Levitical priesthood, and the nation’s judges and later its kings.

<!-- p4 -->
The mediatorial offices in the Old Testament:

<!-- p5 -->
1.  **Prophets:** Prophets are God’s messengers, sent to speak His will to the people and to call them back when they stray. For example, Moses, as a prophet, mediated under the old covenant by receiving the Law from God and teaching Israel. The prophets also foretold the coming of the Messiah and humanity’s ultimate redemption. Other notable examples include: Isaiah, Jeremiah, Elijah, Elisha, Samuel.

<!-- p6 -->
2.  **The Levitical priesthood:** Priests served as mediators by offering atoning sacrifices and interceding before God on behalf of the people. Especially the high priest, who entered the Most Holy Place on the Day of Atonement and sprinkled blood on the mercy seat, symbolizing reconciliation with God. The ministry of the priests was to intercede for the people.

<!-- p7 -->
3.  **Judges/Kings:** The judges, and later the kings, were entrusted with governing God’s people, enforcing His Law, administering justice among the people, delivering them from enemies, and leading them in righteousness. 《Judges》 tells how these leaders—such as Deborah, Samson, and Gideon—were raised up by God to save and guide Israel. Israel later had a series of kings, most evil and a few good（see 1 and 2 Kings, 1 and 2 Samuel, as well as 1<sup>st</sup> and 2<sup>nd</sup> Chronicles）. Some notable examples are Saul, David, and Solomon.

<!-- p8 -->
It is worth noting that all the mediators in the Old Testament were entirely human rather than divine. As humans, they could sin, and often fell into sin. Some mediators were themselves wicked, turning people away from God toward profane worship: false prophets, lying and corrupt priests, and evil kings.

<!-- p9 -->
### Christ as the Mediator of the New Covenant

<!-- p10 -->
Jesus’ death, resurrection, and ascension fulfilled the old covenant and established the new covenant.

<!-- p11 -->
The new covenant is a transformative relationship between God and His people, established through the life, death, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. Unlike the old covenant, which focused on an external law, the new covenant emphasizes inner transformation and grace. Jesus inaugurated the new covenant at the Last Supper, declaring that His blood would establish it, replacing the repeated animal sacrifices of the old covenant with a once-for-all atonement（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 22:20</span>；<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:12</span>；<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:10</span>）. Through this sacrifice, the new covenant brings complete forgiveness and cleansing from sin, fulfilling the requirement for full and permanent reconciliation with God（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 31:34</span>；<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:12</span>；<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:12-18</span>）.

<!-- p12 -->
One of the core features of the new covenant is its inwardness: God writes His law on the hearts of His people, cultivating a heartfelt relationship with Him through the Holy Spirit（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 31:33</span>；<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:10</span>；<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 3:3</span>）. This transformation enables believers not merely to comply with external commands but to live out God’s will through the Spirit’s empowerment（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:1-4</span>）. The new covenant also grants direct access to God, removing the need for an earthly priestly system. With Jesus as the eternal High Priest, believers are called a royal priesthood and can come before God with boldness through Christ’s sacrifice（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 7:24-25</span>；<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:19-20</span>；<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:9</span>）.

<!-- p13 -->
Unlike the old covenant, which was limited to Israel, the new covenant is universal, extending to all who trust in Christ, regardless of ethnicity or background（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:16</span>；<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 3:28</span>）. This covenant is entirely by grace, granting salvation and forgiveness freely through faith in Jesus（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>）. It is not sustained by human effort but by the power of the Holy Spirit; the Spirit renews and empowers believers to live in obedience, reflecting God’s character（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EZK" data-chapter="36" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ezekiel 36:26-27</span>；<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:9-16</span>；<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-23</span>）. Furthermore, the new covenant is everlasting, surpassing the temporary and conditional nature of the old（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="32" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 32:40</span>；<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:13</span>）. It is the final fulfillment of God’s redemptive plan, reconciling humanity to Him and enabling believers to live forever under His rule as His people.

<!-- p15 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

</span>

<!-- p18 -->
Through His substitutionary death and resurrection, Christ inaugurated this new covenant, replacing the old sacrificial system and external law with a covenant written on the hearts of believers（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 31:31-34</span>）. He stands between God and humanity, not only interceding for His people but also providing, by His own blood, the way of reconciliation with God（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:12</span>）. Unlike the mediators of the old covenant, Jesus is sinless, eternal, and fully able to satisfy God’s requirements of righteousness and mercy, ensuring an enduring relationship between God and believers.

<!-- p19 -->
Jesus fulfills and perfects these offices

<!-- p20 -->
1.  **Prophet：** Jesus is the supreme Prophet, not merely a messenger of God’s word, but the Word made flesh (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:14</span>）。He fully and perfectly revealed God’s character, will, and plan of salvation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 1:1-2</span>）。His teaching, miracles, and prophetic proclamations fulfilled the prophecies of the Old Testament and laid the foundation for the New Testament.

<!-- p21 -->
2.  **Priest：** Jesus is the perfect High Priest who offered himself for sin as a once-for-all sacrifice (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 7:27</span>）。Unlike the priests of the Old Testament who had to repeatedly offer sacrifices for their own sins and for the sins of the people, Jesus is sinless; he offered his own blood to accomplish eternal redemption (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:14</span>）。

<!-- p22 -->
3.  **King &amp; Judge：** Jesus is the eternal King who rules with righteousness and justice. As the Son of David, he fulfills the promise concerning an everlasting kingdom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2SA" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Samuel 7:16</span>）。His kingship is not merely earthly, but cosmic, establishing peace through his victory over sin and death (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 2:9-11</span>）。Jesus will come again as King and Judge and will judge ‘the living and the dead’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 10:42</span>）。

<!-- p23 -->
In Christ, the offices of prophet, priest, and king converge and are fully fulfilled. As the mediator of the New Covenant, he bridges the gap between a holy God and sinful humanity, enabling believers to come boldly before God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 4:16</span>），and securing the eternal inheritance promised in him.

## The Apostles' Creed

<!-- p25 -->
“The Apostles’ Creed” does not come from the Bible; rather, it was created and continually revised in accordance with the earliest teachings of the church. A creed is a formal confession of faith—think of it as a combination of an oath or pledge and a declaration of belief. Creeds are important because they serve to unite and distinguish those who believe in a certain way from those who do not. In the United States, at civic occasions such as city council or legislative sessions, participants often recite the Pledge of Allegiance in unison. This pledge is widely used in the United States, but not in other countries, because citizens of other countries do not pledge allegiance to the United States. This pledge is not what makes someone an ‘American,’ but it does represent the important core convictions of citizens.

<!-- p26 -->
The earliest identifiable version of the creed was written in the fourth century AD. Over the centuries, this creed has undergone slight changes and shows minor differences among denominations. Not every Christian denomination uses the creed, but overall it has been widely received throughout Christianity in some form.

<!-- p27 -->
For centuries, the Apostles’ Creed has been used as a tool for teaching and catechesis, conveying the most basic truths of the Christian faith—the foundational beliefs that must be confessed and held in order to become a Christian. Some denominations have a formal catechism process by which new believers join the denomination and are instructed in its beliefs.

<!-- p28 -->
One does not need to fully understand these beliefs to come to God and be saved; salvation is by faith, not by passing a doctrine exam！However, on the ongoing journey of faith, we should all gradually understand these basic truths.

## A brief explanation of the Apostles' Creed

<!-- p31 -->
The basic truths in the Apostles’ Creed are many and profound; a complete exposition would undoubtedly require many volumes. Although this chapter focuses on Jesus, many themes also intersect with the other persons of the Godhead. Only a brief explanation is given here, focusing on the most central themes. Other chapters of this book will explore several of these topics in greater depth.

<!-- p32 -->
#### *I believe in God, the Father almighty, Creator of heaven and earth.*

<!-- p33 -->
This affirms faith in God: he is the all-powerful, sovereign Creator and Father. The title “Father” highlights God’s loving relationship with his creatures, especially with those who are called his children by faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:12</span>）。As Creator, he brought the universe into existence by his word, bringing order out of chaos (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 1:1-3</span>）。His omnipotence is displayed in the vastness of creation, and his fatherly care is shown in his ongoing preservation and providence. This foundational declaration establishes God as the source and Lord of all life, worthy of worship and trust.

<!-- p34 -->
#### *I believe in my Lord Jesus Christ, God’s only Son.*

<!-- p35 -->
Jesus Christ is the unique Son of God, eternally begotten and not made, of the same divine nature (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:1-2</span>, 14）。From the beginning Jesus is the “Word,” and he became flesh and dwelt among us. As the “Christ,” he is the anointed Messiah who fulfills the Old Testament promises concerning the Savior. The title “Lord” acknowledges his authority over the entire universe and his rightful rule and kingship (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 2:10-11</span>）。He is not only Lord, but the Lord who saves (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 16:31</span>）。

<!-- p36 -->
Jesus is not only sent from God; he is a part of God. Jesus is in the Father, and the Father is in Jesus. The Gospel of John repeatedly shows that Jesus and the Father are one (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="30" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:30</span>），and that Jesus did not act on his own (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 5:19</span>；<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="49-50" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 12:49-50</span>），but did everything to glorify the Father (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="17" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 17:4</span>；<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:13</span>），and accomplished it all by the Father’s authority and power (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:10</span>；<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 8:28-29</span>）。

<!-- p37 -->
#### *He was conceived by the power of the Holy Spirit, and born of the virgin Mary.*

<!-- p38 -->
This affirms that Jesus was miraculously conceived by the Holy Spirit, manifesting his divine origin. Born of the virgin Mary, Jesus fulfills the prophecy in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 7:14</span> concerning the Messiah’s virginal birth. This event marks the Incarnation——the eternal Word of God taking on human flesh (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:14</span>）。Being fully God and fully man, Jesus bridges the gap between God and humanity, making him the perfect mediator and Savior.

<!-- p39 -->
Jesus was conceived by the seed of the Holy Spirit and did not inherit a sin nature as we, who are born of human seed, do. Jesus was tempted and tried in every way, yet was completely without sin. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 4:15</span>，<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 5:21</span>，<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:22</span>。

<!-- p40 -->
#### *He suffered under Pontius Pilate, was crucified, died, and was buried。*

<!-- p41 -->
Jesus’s suffering and death were real, historical events，which took place under the Roman prefect Pontius Pilate，providing a verifiable historical foundation for the Christian faith。His crucifixion fulfilled Old Testament prophecy, such as <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="53" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 53:5</span>，which speaks of the suffering servant who would bear the iniquities of many。Jesus’s death was sacrificial，bearing the penalty of sin on behalf of humanity（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:24</span>）。His burial confirmed that he had truly died，and prepared the way for the significance of the resurrection。

<!-- p42 -->
#### *He descended to the dead。*

<!-- p43 -->
After his death，Jesus descended to the realm of the dead, often referred to in Scripture as *Hades* or *Sheol*。This act shows that he fully identified with humanity，even in death，and also demonstrates his victory over sin, death, and the devil（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 3:18-20</span>）。By descending there，he proclaimed his triumph and the completion of his work of redemption。

<!-- p44 -->
#### *On the third day he rose again。*

<!-- p45 -->
Jesus’s resurrection is the cornerstone of our Christian faith，confirming his divinity and the truth of his teaching。He rose from the dead，conquering sin and death，and providing believers with the assurance of eternal life（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 15:17-22</span>）。The resurrection also fulfilled Scripture, including Jesus’s own predictions（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 16:21</span>）。This miraculous event turned the disciples’ despair into bold faith，forming the basis of the early church’s witness。

<!-- p46 -->
#### *He ascended into heaven, and is seated at the right hand of the Father。*

<!-- p47 -->
Jesus’s ascension marks his return to the Father’s presence in glory。He sits at the Father’s right hand（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:20</span>），serving as our eternal High Priest and interceding for believers（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 7:25</span>）。His authority also shows that he is exalted as King，ruling over all creation，and guiding the church’s mission（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:22</span>），to do greater works（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:12</span>），and to accomplish the Great Commission he has given us（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:18-20</span>，<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 16:15-18</span>）。The ascension also assures believers of his ongoing presence and power through the Holy Spirit。

<!-- p48 -->
#### *He will come again to judge the living and the dead。*

<!-- p49 -->
This points to Christ’s future return，when he will bring final justice and restoration。Jesus will judge all people，reward the faithful，and execute justice upon the unrepentant（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 4:1</span>）。His return will accomplish God’s ultimate plan for creation，bringing his eternal kingdom of righteousness and peace。This hope encourages believers to live faithfully and eagerly，knowing that their labor in the Lord is not in vain（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="58" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 15:58</span>）。

<!-- p50 -->
#### *I believe in the Holy Spirit。*

<!-- p51 -->
The Holy Spirit, the third person of the Triune God，is the presence and power of God at work in the world today。He convicts of sin, regenerates, and empowers believers to live holy lives（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:8-13</span>）。The Holy Spirit unites Christians into one body, the church，and gives them spiritual gifts in order to serve（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:4-11</span>）。He is the Comforter, Advocate, and Guide，dwelling within believers as the seal of their eternal inheritance（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>）。

<!-- p52 -->
#### *The holy catholic Church, the communion of saints。*

<!-- p53 -->
The term "holy catholic Church" refers to the universal body of Christ，including all believers across time and space。The church is set apart for God’s purposes，called to reflect his holiness and share the gospel。"The communion of saints" emphasizes the unity of all Christians, both the living and the dead, as members of Christ’s body（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:4-6</span>）。This fellowship transcends earthly divisions，displaying believers’ shared hope and mission。

<!-- p54 -->
Jesus is the head of the church。The church is called the Bride of Christ（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="25-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:25-27</span>），and also the Body of Christ（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 1:18</span>）。

<!-- p55 -->
#### *the forgiveness of sins。*

<!-- p56 -->
This emphasizes the heart of the gospel：through Jesus’s sacrificial death，God grants full forgiveness of sins to all who repent and believe（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:7</span>）。This forgiveness restores the broken relationship between humanity and God，removing guilt and shame。It also calls believers to forgive others，reflecting the grace they have received（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 6:14-15</span>）。

<!-- p57 -->
#### *the resurrection of the body。*

<!-- p58 -->
Like Jesus，we will experience the resurrection of the body at the end of the age。This resurrection will transform mortal, perishable bodies into glorious, imperishable bodies suited for eternal life（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42-44" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 15:42-44</span>）。This highlights the Christian hope of restoration，a hope that extends not only to individuals but also to all creation，for God will make all things new（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="21" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 21:4-5</span>）。

<!-- p59 -->
#### *and the life everlasting。Amen。*

<!-- p60 -->
Eternal life with God is the ultimate promise of the Christian faith. This life begins now through relationship with Christ and will be fully realized in the new heavens and new earth, when believers will dwell with God forever（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:16</span>；<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 22:1-5</span>）。This final affirmation reflects the joy and confidence of salvation, echoing the trust and hope expressed throughout the Creed.

<!-- p61 -->
## Conclusion

<!-- p62 -->
Within the foundational truths expressed in the Apostles’ Creed, we see that the life and work of Jesus are the center of God’s plan for humanity. Each statement in the Creed encompasses the essentials of our faith in God, and who Jesus is-his deity, humanity, mission, and ultimate victory. These truths point to his unique role as the Mediator of the New Covenant. As Mediator, he invites us to come boldly before God, trusting in his perfect sacrifice and ongoing intercession. It calls us to worship him as the true King and to follow his teaching as the supreme Prophet. Ultimately, the study of Jesus leads us into a deeper relationship with him, transforms us, and enables us to live in the light of his grace and truth.

<!-- p63 -->
Understanding who Jesus is and what he has accomplished is the foundation of the Christian faith. Jesus’s many names、titles, and roles illuminate different facets of his character and mission, presenting a more complete picture of his love and authority.

<!-- p64 -->
Just as in our study of God, Scripture is filled with various names that refer to Jesus. Each name represents a specific aspect of his overall character, ministry, and role. These names are given to us to reveal a particular facet of Jesus.
