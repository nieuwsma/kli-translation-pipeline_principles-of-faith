# Chapter 8 - The Holy Spirit-Comforter、Guide与Power

<!-- p1 -->
The Holy Spirit is not a new spirit, but belongs to God and has been with God since the very beginning. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 1:2</span>, the Spirit of God was moving over the face of the waters at creation. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="NEH" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Nehemiah 9:20</span>, the Bible says it was the Holy Spirit who instructed the people in the wilderness. In the Old Testament, God also anointed kings, judges, and prophets through this same Holy Spirit. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JOL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Joel 2:28-29</span>, God promised to pour out the Holy Spirit on all flesh. It was also through this same Holy Spirit that God raised Jesus from the dead, and He now dwells in us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:11</span>). There is only one Holy Spirit; He has always been the same. The way He works together with people may look different at different times, but His heart has never changed.

<!-- p2 -->
Jesus gave His disciples, and all of us, a powerful promise concerning the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p3 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p4 -->
> **<u>John 14:16-18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> I will ask the Father, and He will give you another Helper, that He may be with you forever—
>
> the Spirit of truth, whom the world cannot receive, because it neither sees Him nor knows Him； but you know Him, because He abides with you and will be in you.
>
> I will not leave you as orphans； I will come to you.
>

<!-- p5 -->
</span>

<!-- p6 -->
When Jesus walked on earth in His flesh, He was limited just like us； His body had the same needs as ours: rest, water, food, sleep, etc. These limitations affected not only Jesus but also His followers, because He could be in only one place at a time. When Jesus traveled, He was often crowded by large multitudes who would try to cry out to Him and touch Him. Jesus also often needed to spend time with the Father to be renewed and to pray.

<!-- p7 -->
In order for the Father's work to be multiplied, Jesus needed to raise up disciples so that they could continue this work. Jesus knew that a crucial aspect of His earthly ministry depended on His connection with and presence of the Father. In fact, Jesus said that He could do nothing unless the Father did it through Him and showed Him what to do (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 5:19</span>). In His wisdom, God sent His Holy Spirit to live in us, to lead and guide us, and to empower us to do the Father's work.

<!-- p8 -->
## When was the Holy Spirit given to all believers?

<!-- p9 -->
Jesus said:

<!-- p10 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p11 -->
> **<u>John 16:7</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Nevertheless, I tell you the truth: it is to your advantage that I go away； for if I do not go away, the Helper will not come to you； but if I go, I will send Him to you.
>

<!-- p12 -->
</span>

<!-- p13 -->
After Jesus was crucified, His death made it possible for the Spirit of God to dwell in us. Before that, God's tangible presence was mostly kept behind the thick veil of the temple. At times, God would choose specific people and anoint them (set them apart for unique service), and they would receive the Holy Spirit coming upon them. God's anointing and the Holy Spirit coming upon them were special, temporary, and conditional. After sinning with Bathsheba, David prayed, ‘Do not take Your Holy Spirit from me！’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="51" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 51:11</span>), because David had seen the Holy Spirit depart from King Saul due to his disobedience.

<!-- p14 -->
We (humankind) could not have the Holy Spirit dwell in us because of our sinful nature, but when Jesus dealt with sin through His sacrifice, He opened a way for us by faith so that we could become God's children and heirs (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:17</span>).

<!-- p15 -->
In John chapter 20, after His resurrection, Jesus appeared to the disciples in a locked room and breathed on them, saying, ‘Receive the Holy Spirit’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 20:22</span>). There is still some theological debate about exactly what happened at that moment, but most Pentecostal interpretations hold that this was when the disciples received God's Spirit to dwell within them, marking new birth, that is, being born again. However, this was not when they received God's Spirit coming upon them to empower them with courage for ministry. This was when they received the Holy Spirit to dwell in them so that they could dwell with God and have fellowship with God. This will be discussed in greater depth later in this chapter and in the chapters on sanctification and baptism.

<!-- p16 -->
Later, when the forty days between the resurrection and the ascension were fulfilled (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:3</span>), Jesus gave the promise of the Holy Spirit:

<!-- p17 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="4-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p18 -->
> **<u>Acts 1:4-9</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And being assembled together with them, He commanded them not to depart from Jerusalem, but to wait for the promise of the Father, “which,” He said, “you heard from Me；
>
> for John truly baptized with water, but you shall be baptized with the Holy Spirit not many days from now.”
>
> So when they had come together, they asked Him, saying, “Lord, is it at this time that You will restore the kingdom to Israel？”
>
> He said to them, “It is not for you to know the times or seasons which the Father has put in His own authority.
>
> But you shall receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you； and you shall be My witnesses in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the end of the earth.”
>
> Now when He had spoken these things, while they watched, He was taken up, and a cloud received Him out of their sight.
>

<!-- p19 -->
</span>

<!-- p20 -->
The disciples were to wait for the promised Holy Spirit to be poured out, which would empower them to be witnesses to the ends of the earth. The Holy Spirit is an indispensable part of God's plan for humanity, and is crucial for us as believers, for fulfilling our mission, and for this world.

<!-- p21 -->
## Knowing the Holy Spirit

<!-- p22 -->
The Holy Spirit, first and foremost, is from God and belongs to God. Scripture calls the Holy Spirit the Spirit of God, the Spirit of the Lord, or the Spirit of Christ; though they emphasize different aspects, they are the same person. Since the Holy Spirit is fully God, he is eternal, and he is holy (blameless, innocent, pure, undefiled). Some translations refer to the Holy Spirit as the Holy Ghost.

<!-- p23 -->
In Greek, the word for “spirit” is **Pneuma** (**NYOO-mah** - **G4151**), meaning breath or wind. The Hebrew word for “spirit” has the same meaning. Breath is essential because we need a continual supply in order to live. It was God's breath breathed into Adam that made him alive; likewise, Jesus breathed his last breath on the cross before he died. Without breath there is no life. Breath, like wind, can be felt and experienced, but it is not as easily observed as other ‘elements’ such as water, fire, and earth.

<!-- p24 -->
There are also several core adjectives used to describe the Holy Spirit. Each adjective is used with ‘the Spirit of’, meaning these attributes are essential in defining the Holy Spirit. For example, *the Spirit of truth* or *the Spirit of life*.

<!-- p25 -->
The Spirit of:

<!-- p26 -->
- **Truth** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:17</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span> refers to the Holy Spirit's role in guiding believers into all truth and in revealing God's will and the reality of Christ.

<!-- p27 -->
- **Life** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:2</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:8</span> emphasizes that the Holy Spirit imparts spiritual and eternal life to believers, freeing them from the law of sin and death.

<!-- p28 -->
- **Grace** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:29</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ZEC" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Zechariah 12:10</span> reflects the Spirit's work of extending God's favor, forgiveness, and unmerited lovingkindness to humanity.

<!-- p29 -->
- **Glory** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 4:14</span> indicates the Spirit's role in revealing and manifesting God's glory, especially in times of suffering and persecution.

<!-- p30 -->
- **Adoption** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:15</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 4:5</span> refers to the Spirit's work of making believers children of God, granting them the status of children.

<!-- p31 -->
- **Holiness** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 1:4</span> points to the sanctifying work of the Spirit, setting believers apart to accomplish God's will.

<!-- p32 -->
- **Power** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 1:7</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span> describes the Holy Spirit empowering believers for boldness, service, and to live out their faith effectively.

<!-- p33 -->
Among all these attributes, the Holy Spirit is also rightly recognized as the Helper or Comforter. Jesus called the Holy Spirit the Helper who was to come, and he used the Greek term ***Paraklētos** (*pah-RAH-klay-tos - **G3875**)，meaning an advocate, a helper, one called alongside.

<!-- p34 -->
An advocate speaks on behalf of others, like a defense attorney. They intercede and provide help for the one entrusted to their care.

<!-- p35 -->
A helper helps. They assist in empowering the work, but their role is primarily supportive. A helper is not solely responsible for carrying out the task; rather, they come alongside to help accomplish it.

<!-- p36 -->
The Holy Spirit has several distinct purposes and roles in his ministry. His work is inward (in the hearts and lives of believers), upward (in service to and acknowledgment of God), and outward (toward our mission field, the world).

<!-- p37 -->
## The Roles of the Holy Spirit - Inward, Upward, Outward

<!-- p38 -->
### Inward - The Holy Spirit unites us with Jesus

<!-- p39 -->
Before Jesus worked any miracles or began his public ministry, he was baptized by John the Baptist; the Spirit of God descended upon him, and a voice from heaven said, ‘This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased’（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:17</span>）。

<!-- p40 -->
When we receive Jesus by faith, we obtain salvation and receive the seal of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p41 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p42 -->
> **<u>Ephesians 1:13-14</u>** (NKJV)
>
> You also, in Him, after you heard the word of truth—the gospel of your salvation—and believed in Him, were sealed with the promised Holy Spirit,
>
> who is the guarantee of our inheritance until the redemption of the purchased people, to the praise of His glory.
>

<!-- p43 -->
</span>

<!-- p44 -->
This seal is not figurative but real. It is the mark of our redemption and the down payment on the full inheritance we will receive as God’s children. Romans chapter 8 describes that, through new birth, we receive in our mortal bodies the very Spirit of God who raised Christ from the dead, and we become a true temple of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 6:16</span>).

<!-- p45 -->
This experience of receiving God’s Spirit transforms us and causes rivers of living water to flow from within us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 7:38</span>). This renewal is immediate and complete, and it also runs throughout our entire lives. The rivers of the Holy Spirit should not cease in our lives!

<!-- p46 -->
Through salvation and the indwelling of the Holy Spirit, we can abide in Christ, and He abides in us. God’s Spirit no longer remains outside but can dwell deeply with us. From now into eternal life, we can walk with God in fellowship and harmony.

<!-- p47 -->
There is much more to be said about the work of the Holy Spirit within us and His role in our sanctification, but that will be discussed in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p48 -->
### Upward - The Holy Spirit Bears Witness to Jesus

<!-- p49 -->
The Holy Spirit comes from God and belongs to God. Just as Jesus said He does nothing unless the Father shows Him (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 5:19</span>), so it is with the Holy Spirit: He does nothing apart from the Father’s leading. For the Holy Spirit is the Helper; He does not act on His own initiative but works in perfect harmony with the Father.

<!-- p50 -->
The Holy Spirit bears witness to Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:26</span>), glorifies Jesus, and declares what Jesus has prepared for us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:14</span>).

<!-- p51 -->
The Holy Spirit will bear witness to Jesus, glorify Him, teach us, and bring to our remembrance the teachings of Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:26</span>), and He will guide us into all truth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span>). Jesus told the disciples that He had many more things to teach them, but they could not bear them at that time; however, the Holy Spirit would guide them into all truth. This is another subtle theological point. The teachings Jesus wanted the disciples to know were communicated in various ways. Jesus personally taught them before His Passion, and also after the Resurrection and before the Ascension.

<!-- p52 -->
Jesus also promised that the Spirit of truth would be poured out on them at Pentecost and would lead them into all truth through God’s revelation. Is there still divine revelation today? **Absolutely yes!** The gifts of the Holy Spirit—including prophecy, a word of knowledge, and a word of wisdom—have not ceased! However, no prophecy or word from any spirit is a matter of one’s own interpretation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2PE" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Peter 1:20</span>). This is crucial. Some will falsely claim to be listening to the Holy Spirit, yet their actions and words do not bear witness to Jesus. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 4:3</span> warns that every spirit that does not confess Jesus is not from God; that spirit is of the antichrist. This bears repeating: if any spirit deviates from the teaching of Christ, it is not the Holy Spirit but an unclean spirit!

<!-- p53 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p54 -->
> **<u>1 John 4:1</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Beloved, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits to see whether they are from God, because many false prophets have gone out into the world.
>

<!-- p55 -->
</span>

<!-- p56 -->
Throughout Scripture we see many false claims, often from false prophets or from well-meaning people whose words run contrary to God’s will. For example, Jeremiah warned against prophets who spoke visions from their own minds—proclaiming peace when disaster was approaching (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="23" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 23:16-17</span>). Similarly, in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 16:16-18</span>, a slave girl’s statements about Paul and Silas, though accurate, were inspired by an unclean spirit rather than the Holy Spirit. These examples remind us that even words that seem true may spring from an ungodly source.

<!-- p57 -->
Every claim of “God told me!” or a “new” teaching should be tested by the truth of Scripture. To discern rightly, we must ask:

<!-- p58 -->
- Is this consistent with Scripture?

<!-- p59 -->
- Does this exalt Jesus Christ?

<!-- p60 -->
- Does this bear godly fruit?

<!-- p61 -->
By applying these tests, we can distinguish the Holy Spirit’s true guidance from error or deception.

<!-- p62 -->
God often grants us ample &amp; abundant wisdom and guidance (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:5</span> &amp; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="33" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 33:3</span>）。God’s heavenly wisdom will not contradict Scripture, but it will go beyond the scope of Scripture. His *entolē* *(*commandments*)* are directives that require immediate obedience; they are *rhema* words (as discussed in the chapter on faith).

<!-- p63 -->
This wisdom, understanding, and guidance are not likely written word for word in Scripture. For example, Scripture gives very good overall direction about what to value when seeking a spouse, but it does not give explicit instructions about a specific person. God does indeed promise to direct our paths and to lead us into all truth; we can be comforted, knowing that this wisdom and guidance from the Holy Spirit will never contradict any teaching of Scripture.

<!-- p64 -->
### Outward - The Holy Spirit Empowers for Mission

<!-- p65 -->
Jesus entrusted His mission to us, namely the Great Commission:

<!-- p66 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p67 -->
> **<u>Matthew 28:18-20</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And Jesus came and spoke to them, saying, "All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to Me.
>
> Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit,
>
> teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you; and behold, I am with you always, to the end of the age." Amen.
>

<!-- p68 -->
</span>

<!-- p69 -->
The Holy Spirit plays an indispensable role in this mission, bringing conviction to the world concerning sin, righteousness, and judgment:

<!-- p70 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="8-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p71 -->
> **<u>John 16:8-11</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And when He has come, He will convict the world of sin, and of righteousness, and of judgment:
>
> of sin, because they do not believe in Me;
>
> of righteousness, because I go to the Father and you see Me no more;
>
> of judgment, because the ruler of this world has been judged.
>

<!-- p72 -->
</span>

<!-- p73 -->
Furthermore, the Holy Spirit brings confirmation of the following three truths:

<!-- p74 -->
1.  **Conviction of sin**: Reveals the reality of humanity’s rebellion against God, drawing attention to the corruption in one’s own life.

<!-- p75 -->
2.  **Confirmation of righteousness**: Manifests that Jesus perfectly accomplished the Father’s will, and that Jesus’ sacrifice provides the once-for-all atonement needed for forgiveness.

<!-- p76 -->
3.  **Confirmation of judgment**: Affirms that Satan has been defeated, that God’s justice is beyond doubt, and that everyone must stand under judgment for the works and deeds of their life.

<!-- p77 -->
The Holy Spirit also empowers this mission. The Holy Spirit gives believers the capacity to bear bold witness and to proclaim the gospel in the face of persecution. Boldness does not mean a loud voice; it means courage in the midst of fear. Power is strength, authority, and ability. The Holy Spirit imparts strength and power so that even when facing threats and fear of punishment, one can testify to the gospel.

<!-- p78 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p79 -->
> **<u>Acts 1:8</u>** (NKJV)
>
> But you shall receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you; and you shall be My witnesses in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the end of the earth."

<!-- p80 -->
</span>

<!-- p81 -->
The entire book of Acts is filled with stories and accounts that follow this universal cycle：

<!-- p82 -->
1.  **Prayer**: Believers ask for boldness and God's intervention。

<!-- p83 -->
2.  **Empowerment**: The Holy Spirit fills them with courage、power, and guidance。

<!-- p84 -->
3.  **Proclamation**: They boldly proclaim the gospel，often accompanied by signs、wonders and miracles。

<!-- p85 -->
4.  **Opposition**: Resistance arises，leading to persecution or challenges。Sometimes believers are martyred，other times they are imprisoned and/or beaten。

<!-- p86 -->
5.  **Renewal**: The church returns to prayer，seeking fresh empowerment，and the cycle continues。

<!-- p87 -->
The New Testament church grew primarily in the face of resolute opposition。Churches around the world suffer violence at the hands of oppressors and evil rulers who intend to suppress the knowledge of the truth of Jesus。

<!-- p88 -->
God knew this opposition would occur，and Jesus foretold it：

<!-- p89 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p90 -->
> **<u>Luke 12:11-12</u>** (NKJV)
>
> "When they bring you to the synagogues、the magistrates and those in authority，do not worry about how you should answer、what you should say。
>
> For at that time，the Holy Spirit will teach you what you ought to say."
>

<!-- p91 -->
</span>

<!-- p92 -->
Do not be afraid！Jesus promises us：

<!-- p93 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p94 -->
> **<u>Matthew 16:18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And I also say to you：You are Peter，and on this rock I will build My church，and the gates of Hades shall not prevail against it。
>

<!-- p95 -->
</span>

<!-- p96 -->
This is an astonishing and comforting promise。City gates are fixed fortifications。When Jesus says *the gates of Hades shall not prevail*，He paints a picture of a besieging army breaching the gates、and taking the city！

<!-- p97 -->

## Building a Relationship with the Holy Spirit

<!-- p101 -->
Every relationship takes time and experience to deepen. The Holy Spirit is our advocate, comforter, helper, and guide. He empowers us and sanctifies us.

<!-- p102 -->
Begin to regard the Holy Spirit as a person. In our relationship, we can work together to build it, and we can also do things that weaken it. He can be ignored, he can be quenched, he can be blasphemed, or he can be listened to, honored, and obeyed.

<!-- p103 -->
Do not ignore the voice of the Holy Spirit. He is always speaking to us and to the church. If you do not yet know how to discern the Holy Spirit’s voice, it takes time, willingness, and effort (as with all healthy and beneficial relationships). But don’t worry—you already have within you everything necessary to begin building a personal relationship with the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p104 -->
The fastest and most reliable way to build an effective relationship with the Holy Spirit is

<!-- p105 -->
1.  Call on Him in prayer in a personal way, pause, and wait for Him to speak.

<!-- p106 -->
2.  As a member of a community (the church) of those who know Him, call on Him, pause, and wait for Him to speak.

<!-- p107 -->
By “call on,” I mean literally say it with your mouth, or speak in whatever way you can (if you cannot speak aloud, use your hands). Why speak it out? There are several reasons:

<!-- p108 -->
1.  We are speaking beings made in God’s image. God speaks, and we speak.

<!-- p109 -->
2.  We primarily use speech to communicate. You can also write down what you want to say (journal it) and read it out loud.

<!-- p110 -->
3.  Speaking is an intentional act that shows the one you are addressing that you are willing to engage in a conversation.

<!-- p111 -->
#### What should you say?

<!-- p112 -->
If you have never spoken to God, try introducing yourself. Of course, this will feel a bit awkward, but that discomfort is acceptable and temporary. Besides introducing yourself, tell God you want to talk with Him and that you hope He will speak to you as well.

<!-- p113 -->
Consider asking Him a question. It doesn’t have to be a profound theological question; you can start simple. Can you show me your love for me? Why do I feel a certain way? What do you want to say to me?

<!-- p114 -->
#### Then what?

<!-- p115 -->
In all effective relationships, you speak for a while and then pause to listen. God will speak.

<!-- p116 -->
God speaks in many ways, but He wants to speak in a personal and inward way. It most likely will not be an audible external voice (like your best friend saying your name), but an inner voice that speaks to your mind and heart. Elijah called it a ‘still small voice’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1KI" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Kings 19:11-13</span>).

<!-- p117 -->
From my personal experience of seeing God speak to people, and having had the privilege of helping dozens learn how to converse directly with God, the most common ways are usually one or a combination of the following:

<!-- p118 -->
- A knowing - The person who is talking with God suddenly receives an insight that helps them.

<!-- p119 -->
- A picture - Also called a vision or a dream in Scripture (a vision when awake, a dream when asleep), God gives you a picture in your mind. Perhaps it is a memory, or a place you’ve been or know? Explore the picture: What do you see? How does it make you feel? What is happening?

<!-- p120 -->
- An inner voice - like an audible voice, but only you can hear it, and it speaks to your heart and mind.

<!-- p121 -->
#### What will God say?

<!-- p122 -->
Not sure! But that’s part of the fun. I can say that He is not a spirit of fear, but of perfect love, and He will never contradict the teachings of the Bible. For example, God will never tell you to commit adultery.

<!-- p123 -->
What now?

<!-- p124 -->
Keep the conversation going! It really is that simple.

<!-- p127 -->
- *Good Morning, Holy Spirit* by Benny Hinn ([<u>www.bennyhinn.org</u>](http://www.bennyhinn.org))

### Why build a relationship?

<!-- p129 -->
The Holy Spirit is the Helper. The Helper helps us! If we share a common language with Him and build a relationship with Him, the work will go much more smoothly. Can you imagine, in your job, trying to work together when you have no relationship with the other party, are speaking different languages, and can’t communicate?!

<!-- p130 -->
The God we serve is willing to communicate with us. Jesus said His sheep recognize His voice（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:27</span>）, and the natural capacity we need is already built into us, but we may lack practiced skill.

<!-- p131 -->
From personal experience, a personal and active relationship with the Holy Spirit is one of the best things about being a Christian. His presence brings peace, comfort, and guidance, but only if you know how to seek, listen to, and obey Him.

<!-- p132 -->
Cultivate sensitivity to the Holy Spirit. Just as many married people, over time, can communicate with their spouse in unique ways—sometimes a single look is enough. That look can tell you everything about what just happened and what will happen next. Build that kind of sensitivity and familiarity with the Holy Spirit. Bring Him into your whole life; this is a beautiful part of your relationship with Him and will bring great depth and richness to your journey of faith.

<!-- p133 -->
The Holy Spirit has come to lead and guide us, to empower us to be God’s people, and to see people on earth thoroughly transformed, delivered from the kingdom of darkness into the kingdom of light. Knowing Him and building trust with Him is crucial for everyday life and for completing the work Jesus has given us to do.
