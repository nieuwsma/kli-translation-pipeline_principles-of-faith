# Chapter 2 - God-the Eternal Creator and Loving Father

<!-- p1 -->
It is crucial that we build a strong foundation in the knowledge of who God is。We can understand through study：

<!-- p2 -->
- His desires、intentions and works,

<!-- p3 -->
- His nature、character and attributes,

<!-- p4 -->
- What people say about Him、and what He says about Himself。

<!-- p5 -->
The Bible reveals God's attributes in a unique way，each facet deepens our understanding of this unchanging、eternal God。

<!-- p6 -->
Summarizing God with a single descriptor is challenging，even impossible，because He is too vast and multifaceted to be reduced。Although we can，也必将，strive to explain、describe and portray God，ultimately，as with art and music，He must be understood through experience。

<!-- p7 -->
When a person listens to music，they describe it by expressing how it moves them-how it resonates within，rather than merely the notes and rhythms themselves。A melody may carry warmth or tension，the rhythm may pulse like a heartbeat，or build with a crescendo to create a feeling of exhilaration or longing。Music can soothe like gentle waves，or electrify like a storm，lifting the listener's spirit、settling their thoughts，or carrying them entirely elsewhere。这种体验远不止是声音的序列; it is the soul of the music，lingering on，manifested in how it shapes the listener's emotions and personal journey。

<!-- p8 -->
Similarly，a person viewing a painting goes beyond colors or brushstrokes，exploring the meaning embodied by these elements。A landscape may evoke a tranquil escape，while an abstract piece can stir curiosity or introspection。Certain tones can bring warmth，and a composition might create calm、chaos、or beauty。The painting becomes a living experience，prompting a shift in perspective，or a deep emotional response that words alone cannot capture。Thus，music and art transcend their physical forms-they become encounters that touch an essence，changing the listener or viewer in a profound and personal way。

## Exploring God through the Old Testament

<!-- p10 -->
### Genesis：The God who dwells with His creation

<!-- p11 -->
Within the narrative about God，一个核心主题是祂渴望与祂的受造物同住。This is clear throughout the story of Genesis。Spanning its fifty chapters，and from Adam（the first human）to Joseph（the last significant figure in Genesis、the last important leader among God's people before Moses）in the genealogies，the time span is approximately ~2,400 years。Through these stories，we can see that even when humanity is evil，God's goodness remains constant。

<!-- p12 -->
In Genesis，God establishes relationships with several specific individuals。

<!-- p13 -->
1.  **Adam and Eve** - The first humans，created by God and placed in the Garden of Eden。God walked with them and spoke to them，giving them instructions and establishing a relationship of mutual love and obedience。However，this relationship was affected when they ate from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil（Genesis 1-3）。

<!-- p14 -->
2.  **Cain** - Although Cain ultimately rebelled by murdering his brother Abel，God still spoke directly to him，warning him about the danger of sin and giving guidance before the murder occurred。Even after administering punishment to Cain，God set a mark on him to protect him from harm（Genesis 4）。

<!-- p15 -->
3.  **Noah** - In an age of widespread corruption，God formed a unique relationship with Noah，a righteous man。God instructed Noah to build an ark to save his family and the animals from the flood。Afterward，God made a covenant with Noah，promising never again to destroy the earth with a flood，and gave the rainbow as the sign of this covenant（Genesis 6-9）。

<!-- p16 -->
4.  **Abraham (Abram)** - One of the most important relationships in Genesis，God called Abram to leave his homeland and promised to make him the father of a great nation。God made a covenant with Abram，promising land、descendants、and to bless all nations through him。Abram's faith and obedience became central themes，and as part of the covenant his name was changed to Abraham（Genesis 12, 15, 17, 22）。

<!-- p17 -->
5.  **Hagar** - Sarah's Egyptian maidservant，who met God in the wilderness after being mistreated。God comforted her，promised a future for her son Ishmael，and instructed her to return to Abraham and Sarah。Hagar called God “El Roi”（“the God who sees me”），acknowledging His care for her（Genesis 16）。

<!-- p18 -->
6.  **Isaac** - The son of Abraham and Sarah，Isaac inherited the promises of the covenant God had made with his father。God reaffirmed these promises directly to Isaac，including the promise of descendants and blessing。God also guided and protected Isaac during a famine，establishing a covenant relationship（Genesis 26）。

<!-- p19 -->
7.  **Rebekah** - Although her relationship with God was more indirect，God still answered Rebekah's prayer about her difficult pregnancy，revealing that her twins，Jacob and Esau，would become two nations with different destinies（Genesis 25）。

<!-- p20 -->
8.  **Jacob** - God established a personal relationship with Jacob, appeared to him in dreams, and confirmed the covenantal promises concerning land, offspring, and blessing. Jacob encountered God multiple times, including the famous wrestling match during which God changed his name to Israel, symbolizing his transformation and the establishment of Israel as a nation (Genesis 28, 32, 35).

<!-- p21 -->
9.  **Joseph** - Although Joseph’s relationship with God was not based on direct manifestations or covenant-making like that of Abraham or Jacob, God was still deeply involved in his life. Through dreams, divine favor, and providence, God guided Joseph from being sold into slavery to becoming a powerful leader in Egypt. Joseph’s faith in God’s sovereignty enabled him to forgive his brothers, recognizing that God used his suffering to accomplish good (Genesis 37, 39-50).

<!-- p22 -->
Throughout the narrative, the God who longs to dwell with His creatures had to dwell for a time with a few individuals, and ultimately established a people through the covenant made with Abraham (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="17" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 17:7</span>).

<!-- p23 -->
### Exodus: The God Who Dwells with His People

<!-- p24 -->
God’s desire to dwell with His people is also clearly revealed in the second book of the Bible, Exodus. In the book of Exodus, the Israelites, who had once lived in Egypt in relative peace and harmony for a time, were now oppressed by a Pharaoh who did not know Joseph and feared the Hebrews (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 1:6-8</span>). God’s people lived in Egypt for about ~400 years (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40-41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 12:40-41</span>), the latter half of which was spent in slavery (there is no universally agreed consensus regarding the exact length of Israel’s enslavement in Egypt).

<!-- p25 -->
In the powerful presence of the burning bush that was not consumed, God revealed Himself to Moses, a man born a Hebrew, raised in Egypt, a murderer in exile, who was tending sheep for his father-in-law in the wilderness.

<!-- p26 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p27 -->
> **<u>Exodus 3:14</u>** (NKJV)
>
> God said to Moses, "I AM WHO I AM." He also said, "You shall say this to the Israelites, 'I AM has sent me to you.' "
>

<!-- p28 -->
</span>

<!-- p29 -->
The phrase “I AM WHO I AM” (Ehyeh-Asher-Ehyeh) is one of the most profound self-revelations in Scripture. When Moses asked for God’s name, God answered with this statement, which can be translated as "I am who I am," "I will be what I will be," or simply "I am." This declaration may seem somewhat puzzling on the surface, yet it carries deep meaning. Can you imagine introducing yourself to someone newly met as ‘I am’? When God reveals Himself as ‘I AM WHO I AM’, He is bearing witness to His nature, His eternal presence, His sovereignty. ‘I am’ is limitless in meaning and purpose.

<!-- p30 -->
God used Moses to reveal Himself to Pharaoh:

<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

</span>

<!-- p34 -->
Moses told Pharaoh that God is ‘I AM’, and that God demanded the release of the Israelites so that they could worship Him in the wilderness.

<!-- p35 -->
Plagues and afflictions followed, but eventually Pharaoh relented, allowing the Israelites to leave.

<!-- p36 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p39 -->
In the wilderness, God miraculously delivered His people from the vengeful Egyptian army (Exodus 14), provided them shade under the blazing sun and warmth by a pillar of fire in the cool desert nights (Exodus 13), gave them food from heaven (Exodus 16), and gave them water to drink (Exodus 17).

<!-- p40 -->
God led His people to Mount Sinai, where He commanded Moses to consecrate the people, gave them the Law (including the Ten Commandments), and made a covenant with them (the Old Covenant) (Exodus 19、20、24). This covenant (the Mosaic covenant) was built upon the Abrahamic covenant and continued from Moses to Jesus.

<!-- p41 -->
Despite God making a covenant with His people, this relationship is one in which a loving and jealous God pursues and dwells with a very stubborn and ungrateful people (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="32" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 32:9</span>). They disregarded God’s wondrous interventions and continued to rebel throughout much of the narrative. God even brought them to the Promised Land and told them they could receive it as an inheritance (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="33" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 33:3</span>), but out of fear and disobedience, they ignored God’s supernatural deliverance and instead had to wander in the wilderness for forty years, until almost that entire generation had died (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="NUM" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33-34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Numbers 14:33-34</span>).

<!-- p42 -->
### The Rest of the Old Testament

<!-- p43 -->
Over the ~1,400 years between Moses’ death and Christ’s birth, the Israelites repeatedly experienced a cycle: consecration brought God’s favor and blessing, followed by rebellion, God’s judgment, then repentance, and finally God’s deliverance. This pattern is especially evident in 《**Judges、Kings、Chronicles**》, which record the people turning away from God and often worshiping other gods, resulting in periods of oppression or exile. In response to their cries, God repeatedly raised up judges, prophets, or leaders to deliver them.

<!-- p44 -->
During these years, despite the Israelites repeatedly failing and not keeping the covenant He made with them, God remained faithful, displaying both justice and mercy. He continually rescued and forgave His people, revealing His enduring commitment and love.

<!-- p45 -->
As we see in Scripture, God reveals Himself through actions, relationships, and even names that disclose His character. Each name represents a specific facet of His overall character. These names were given within the narrative, telling how God revealed Himself to His people. For your personal study, the appendix includes a reference chart: *Table 10 - Names of God in the Old Testament.* Please read through these names and examine the related passages. Each reference is a story that accurately portrays how God saves, delivers, and rescues His people.

<!-- p46 -->
The appendix also contains another set of further distilled attributes that describe His nature, works, and will: *Table 11 - The Attributes of God.* Though these attributes are more abstract than the names of God, they are just as real!

<!-- p47 -->
These names help us know God-the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit (**Trinity**)-working together as one. In our subsequent study, we will examine more deeply the names and attributes of Jesus and the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p48 -->
God’s character and works are fully expressed in the unity of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit. Scripture teaches that God is one in essence, yet exists as three persons: Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. Scripture affirms God’s oneness, saying, “There is no God besides Me” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="45" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 45:5</span>). However, the Godhead is understood as a compound unity-three persons cooperating with the same mind and purpose, united as one in the truest sense. Each person of the Trinity has a distinct role: the Father creates, the Son redeems, and the Holy Spirit sanctifies. The term “Trinity” does not appear in Scripture; it is a theological term developed to describe this concept, which is revealed through the Bible.

<!-- p49 -->
The Trinity can be illustrated by examples from nature, which help clarify the idea of unity in diversity. For example, water can be liquid, ice, or vapor, yet it is still H₂O; each person of the Trinity is fully God, yet distinct in person. Or imagine a triangle, with three sides and three angles forming a complete shape; if one side is removed, it is no longer a triangle. Likewise, **if any person were missing, God’s nature would be incomplete**, and the three work together in perfect unity.

<!-- p50 -->
- **God the Father**: He is the Creator and Sustainer of all things, often understood as the initiator within the Godhead. His love for humanity is central to the biblical story, as shown in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:16</span>. In our study, we will examine how God the Father, as the loving Father and righteous Judge, made a way for our redemption.

<!-- p51 -->
- **The Son**: Jesus, coexistent and coeternal with the Father, took on human nature, was conceived by the Holy Spirit, and born of the virgin Mary. He lived a sinless life, died to redeem humanity, and rose again, conquering sin and death. He now sits at the right hand of the Father, interceding for us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:1-3</span>；<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 1:23</span>；<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 2:5</span>). In our study, we will examine Jesus as the perfect atoning sacrifice, the risen Lord, and the head of the church.

<!-- p52 -->
- **The Holy Spirit**: The Holy Spirit is coexistent and coequal with the Father and the Son, sent by the Father through the Son to convict the world of sin and to prepare believers as the Bride of Christ. He gives power, comfort, and sanctifies those who are in Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:26</span>；15:26；16:8). In our study, we will examine the Holy Spirit as the Helper, whom God gives to equip, empower, sanctify, and be with us.

<!-- p53 -->
Through the persons of these three, God’s triune nature is manifested as perfect unity in purpose, will, and love; in the divine mission of creation, redemption, and sanctification, each plays a vital role. The Trinity invites believers to experience God fully: as the loving Father, the redeeming Savior, and the Spirit who transforms hearts.

## Conclusion

<!-- p55 -->
We can be uniquely known by names: husband, father, son, brother, friend. Each is a declaration of our identity in relationship to others. We can also be known by attributes: faithful, patient, kind, wise, humorous. Likewise, we can know God through His attributes and names- He is our Healer, Provider, Shepherd, and Redeemer. Yet titles and words are only a small part of us; for others to truly know us, we must be actually experienced. This is even more true of God. To truly and fully know Him, we must experience Him.

<!-- p56 -->
God’s names reveal His character, but personal encounters deepen our understanding of what those names mean. For example, “the God who heals me” takes on richer, deeper meaning when we understand that God has rescued us from the depths of sickness, despair, or helplessness. Knowing Him as our Provider is also transformed when we face real need and find Him faithful.

<!-- p57 -->
The Bible provides the context for these encounters, showing us God’s faithfulness to His people across time and circumstances, reinforcing the fact that His names and attributes are not merely titles but realities He brings to life and that are experienced in people’s lives. It is through the interplay of personal experience and the biblical narrative that we come to know God-not merely as a distant description, but as a relationship present with us and actively at work in every season of our lives.
