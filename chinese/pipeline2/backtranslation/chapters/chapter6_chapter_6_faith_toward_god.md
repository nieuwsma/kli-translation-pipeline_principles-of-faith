# Chapter 6—Faith Toward God

<!-- p1 -->
Faith is a cornerstone concept of the Christian faith. The word “faith” is used so frequently in Christianity that at times its meaning is replaced by subtle misunderstandings and misconceptions. But it is not something you can hold in your hand—it is something formed within you as you learn to trust God. Trustful obedience is faith.

<!-- p2 -->
The author of Hebrews teaches us to have “faith toward God.”

### Defining Faith

<!-- p10 -->
Faith is an aspect of the Christian faith that is often misunderstood and misrepresented. As the saying goes, “There are ditches on both sides of the road”! Because in contemporary Christianity the word “faith” is used frequently without proper explanation, it is easily misunderstood and then inflated with wrong definitions. People often portray faith either as a vague and abstract assertion of belief, or as a near-mystical ability or “magic,” as though it were something you possess with a particular measure and limit.

<!-- p11 -->
#### What Faith Is Not

<!-- p12 -->
When faith is misrepresented in an abstract way, it lacks concrete beliefs or obedient trust. A little probing reveals this, because such a portrayal of faith cannot be made specific. If you ask, “What do you believe?” or “Why do you believe?”, people who think this way often can say little beyond “I believe.” <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 3:15</span> says we should always be ready to give a defense for the hope that is in us.

<!-- p13 -->
Sometimes people treat faith as a magical ability—but that is not what the Bible teaches. Faith is about a real relationship built on trust. In Scripture, faith is more dynamic and relational—it is rooted in trustful obedience, reliance, and assurance regarding God’s character and promises. It is not an impersonal force; it is an active response to God that shapes one’s thoughts, affections, and actions.

<!-- p14 -->
Why are belief and obedient trust related?

<!-- p15 -->
Our beliefs shape how we view and interact with the world around us. Beliefs forge perception and give rise to our actions. This is how our minds are set—we live according to what we consider possible.

<!-- p16 -->
We act based on what we perceive, understand, and believe to be possible. When walking down a hallway, the brain recognizes the boundaries of the floor and walls and automatically tells our feet where to step and what to avoid. This principle goes beyond our physical navigation of the world around us.

<!-- p17 -->
If you believe you cannot achieve financial independence or live a healthy and active lifestyle, your actions will follow that belief; in line with your belief, you will not do—and will very likely not even attempt—the things you deem impossible.

<!-- p18 -->
If you think God lacks love and is characterized by punishing people, such a view will keep you from walking with Him as His child.

<!-- p19 -->
#### Faith Is Trustful Obedience

<!-- p20 -->
So, what is faith? Persuasion! The Greek word ***pistis*** means persuasion, or the result of being persuaded. We ought to have persuasion toward God. In English we might say faith (persuasion) ‘**in** God’. If you are married, hopefully you also have ‘**in’** faith in your spouse. You believe they will do what they say they will do, and you believe they are sincere and genuine. Such trust should go beyond what they say, extending to an overall trust in who they are as a person.

<!-- p21 -->
Cultivating faith, persuasion, and trust in a relationship takes time and experience. Hopefully, in life you have learned how to build a mutually trusting relationship with others. The same principles that make you trust the people you know more can also increase your faith in God. Faith in a relationship increases over time, through experiences, consistency, and those actions, big and small, that build your confidence in the other person’s character and nature.

<!-- p22 -->
‘Faith toward God’ is relational. A relationship is built by two parties, not one. There is good news regarding our faith toward God: God takes responsibility for His side of the relationship (made possible through Jesus’ atoning death and the indwelling of His Spirit at new birth), and He also helps us on our side (through the work of the Helper——the Holy Spirit). God has done almost all the work; our part (to be discussed later in this chapter) is to walk *WITH* Him in obedience.

<!-- p23 -->
### Jesus matures our faith

<!-- p24 -->
Jesus is the author and finisher of our faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 12:2</span>). Our faith is meant to continually grow and expand. We do not arrive at a single point at which we have enough ‘faith’, but rather our faith keeps increasing. Think of a deep and personal relationship in your life today. As with any relationship, we do not reach a particular milestone and thereby ‘know’ the person. We keep getting to know them, growing in our knowledge of them, and this refines, shapes, and grows our relationship.

<!-- p25 -->
How do we grow in faith toward God? By continually experiencing Him in His many revelations, manifestations, and attributes. Again, think of a deep personal relationship in your life. You deepen the relationship by interacting with the other person in different ways, different forms, different mediums, different contexts. You talk with them, send messages, and perhaps write letters. You meet them individually and also in groups. You talk about what you value, and perhaps challenge each other. The ways are countless and can extend to infinity. Through these varied expressions you come to know the person more deeply.

<!-- p26 -->
We all should know God as our Savior through Jesus. Remember the names of God (*see Appendix),* merely because we know God as Savior does not mean we have experienced Him as Deliverer, Healer, Counselor, Lord.

<!-- p27 -->
If you are at the point of having been persuaded (faith) to know God as your Savior, building this relationship will establish your confidence in His character, thereby enabling you to know Him in other ways.

<!-- p28 -->
I am blessed to have several very deep relationships in my life. One of them is with my wife. In our relationship, because of the level of trust (faith) between us, my wife can tell me some things she is going to do, and although I have not yet seen them accomplished, because of her proven character, I have faith to believe her.

<!-- p29 -->
This example is exactly a portrayal of how our deep relationship with God can be experienced—with trust and faith! We may not have fully experienced Him (in fact, in this life we never will fully experience Him), but as the relationship matures, we can trust Him beyond the boundaries of what I personally know and what I have already seen happen.

<!-- p30 -->
Jesus increases our faith through experiences with Him. One important way we can and should experience Him is through His word.

<!-- p36 -->
When we replace the word *faith* with *persuasion*, it reads:

<!-- p37 -->
*‘So then persuasion comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God’*. This truth is self-evident. The reason a person has persuasion, a primary way, is by being persuaded (being convinced), and when someone presents an ‘argument’-or provides a reasoned and clearly expressed explanation, we are persuaded.

<!-- p38 -->
There are two Greek words in the Bible for ‘Word’-**logos** and **rhema**. Understanding the difference between them helps us see how faith grows.

<!-- p39 -->
**Logos**: Usually refers to the **concept, idea, rationale, or broader expression** of God’s word. It encompasses a universal, foundational truth or message, and is often used to describe God’s eternal wisdom, as well as Christ Himself as the living Word.

<!-- p40 -->
**Rhema (G4487)**：emphasizes a specific, spoken word or utterance。It usually refers to a particular word or message given at a specific moment, especially a spoken or revelatory word that directly applies to an individual’s situation。

<!-- p41 -->
*Logos* is like the overall message of an entire book, whereas *rhema* is like the particular paragraph or sentence that speaks directly to someone at a given moment。*Logos* is God’s macro truth, while *rhema* is when a part of that truth is spoken to someone in a personal and applicable way。Together, the two display the fullness of God’s communication：*logos* is the broad, foundational truth about God’s nature and will, and *rhema* is the specific, applied word that makes that truth come alive in a person’s life and circumstances。

<!-- p42 -->
### Examples of Rhema Words

<!-- p43 -->
God promises to give revelation and wisdom to all who ask。When He speaks to us, such speech is a *rhema* word。Rhema words come from God to confirm, lead, and guide us。They are not a substitute for the *logos* word and do not carry the authority of Scripture。*Rhema* words should be discerned by the same standards as prophecy or the interpretation of tongues（see the chapter on the Holy Spirit for a deeper study of this topic）。

<!-- p44 -->
At several points in my career, I have prayed to seek divine guidance。A few years ago, I was considering starting to look for a new opportunity, and while praying I heard God say to me: ‘*Just wait for Me; I have prepared an opportunity for you.*’ I began looking for a new job because I was dissatisfied with my current role and felt discouraged。The guiding word God gave me, ‘*wait for Me’*, helped me gain peace and confidence in a season that would otherwise have been restless and anxious。

<!-- p45 -->
Three or four months passed, and I continued to work diligently and faithfully, maintaining a positive attitude。God would remind me, *‘wait for Me’*, and then His promise was fulfilled。I received a promotion opportunity and a substantial raise。

<!-- p46 -->
I could have continued job hunting; I could have left。I could have started over elsewhere。Without a doubt, someone else would have gotten that promotion! But because I sought the Lord’s guidance and was confirmed by His **rhema word**, I obeyed and benefited as a result。God’s word is **living and personal**, and He promises to give us multifaceted and abundant wisdom（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:5</span>）。

<!-- p47 -->
Dozens of times, if not hundreds, I have asked God in prayer what to do, and He has given me a rhema word。Not every time has had equally notable results, but they have all brought peace and confidence, assuring me that I was obeying God and that He would accomplish everything。These were the words I needed in the moment! Many times, when God brings a rhema word, it comes from the logos。He will bring to my mind a passage of Scripture, “*Do not fear, for I am with you!*”（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="41" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 41:10</span>）， “*By His stripes we are healed*”（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="53" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 53:5</span>）， “*Do not grow weary in doing good!*”（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:9</span>），and the examples are countless！

<!-- p48 -->
Receiving a rhema word not only strengthens our faith but also deepens our relationship with God, because we trust Him more fully in our daily decisions。We will continue to learn how to listen to God’s voice in the chapter on the Holy Spirit。

<!-- p49 -->
Our faith is also strengthened by what we see God do, but at times what we see in the natural will challenge what we have heard from God。Reconciling the inconsistency between what is seen and what is heard is an important work of the Holy Spirit; He promises to guide us into all truth（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span>）。

### Faith Beyond Sight

<!-- p51 -->
After the resurrection, Jesus appeared many times to many disciples。Thomas (one of the twelve) was not among the other disciples at the time, and thus missed the appearance of the risen Savior。Thomas believed in Jesus and trusted his fellow disciples, but he did not believe that Jesus had risen bodily; he insisted that unless he experienced it personally, he could not truly believe。

> Jesus said to him, "Thomas, because you have seen Me, you have believed。Blessed are those who have not seen and yet have believed。"

When Jesus appeared to the disciples and settled them down（I suppose it would indeed be quite alarming when someone appears among you after passing through a locked door）, He spoke to Thomas。He did not criticize or rebuke Thomas, nor did He identify him as part of the evil and adulterous generation that seeks a sign（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 16:4</span>），but rather revealed Himself to Thomas。

<!-- p60 -->
Jesus then said, ‘*Blessed are those who have not seen and yet have believed*d’。This is not Jesus encouraging ‘blind or ignorant faith’ or denying the use of empirical evidence。Rather, it means that we are more blessed when we can believe without needing empirical proof。Why? If our faith—our trust—is limited only to what we have seen empirically verified, we will never be able to have any assurance about the works yet to come。We will have great confidence in what has already been accomplished, but we will not be able to walk in God’s peace for tomorrow and the future。

<!-- p61 -->
Take relationships as an example：if my attitude toward my wife were always ‘<u>I will only believe after I see it！</u>’，how challenging would our relationship be？By contrast, a trust -based relationship would say ‘I know you are a trustworthy person’。

### Faith produces obedience and good works

<!-- p80 -->
Our trusting relationship with God is strengthened through obedience, resulting in good works! These good works do not save us, nor do they earn us God’s love-they are the natural result of a life rooted in Him.

<!-- p81 -->
The Bible presents the natural progression of faith. As we come to know God and experience His presence, our faith in Him grows. This belief compels us to act in ways consistent with our faith, causing us to keep His commands. Our obedience produces good works, bearing positive and beneficial fruit.

<!-- p82 -->

<!-- p92 -->
In a work environment, when a boss or supervisor is issuing instructions, they are not “speaking just to speak,” but giving guidance they want their employees to follow。

### Practical Steps to Cultivate Your Faith

<!-- p153 -->
Walking with God is about relationship, but if you want a healthy relationship, you need to learn some skills. In earthly human relationships, the skills that build strong and secure relationships rest on the following foundations:

<!-- p154 -->
- Open and free dialogue and communication

<!-- p155 -->
- Healthy conflict resolution

<!-- p156 -->
- Commitment

<!-- p157 -->
- Consistency

<!-- p158 -->
If you want a strong, vibrant relationship, you must show up consistently, reveal (communicate) yourself (our thoughts and emotions), receive the other (listen), and walk with them! All these skills apply to our relationship with God.

<!-- p159 -->
Beyond core relational skills, there are specific practices and disciplines that can strengthen your relationship with God:

<!-- p160 -->
- **Establish and maintain a prayer life, listening and communicating with God.** Frequent and sincere prayer is essential for intimacy with God. Through prayer, you can share your concerns, joys, and gratitude, seeking His guidance and strength. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 4:6-7</span>*) Sensitivity to the Holy Spirit’s voice enables you to discern God’s will, find wisdom in decisions, and grow in spiritual maturity. Set aside quiet, reflective time to listen for His guidance. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:14</span>*)

<!-- p161 -->
- **Spend time in worship.** Worship is a powerful way to draw near to God, expressing love and reverence. Through praise, singing, or quiet meditation, worship deepens your awareness of His presence. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="100" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 100:2-4</span>*)

<!-- p162 -->
- **Read, study, and meditate on Scripture.** God’s Word reveals His character, promises, and guidance for life. Investing time in studying the Bible helps you grow in faith, understanding, and spiritual resilience. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>*)

<!-- p163 -->
- **Keep His commandments.** Obedience to God’s instructions demonstrates your love, respect, and trust for Him. Walking in obedience aligns your life with His will and brings blessing. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:15</span>*)

<!-- p164 -->
- **Actively engage in a local church and serve.** Participating in a church community brings spiritual support, encouragement, and opportunities to serve others. Being invested helps build a sense of belonging and strengthens your commitment to God’s mission. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:24-25</span>*)

<!-- p165 -->
- **Build relationships with other believers.** Fellowship with other Christians provides mutual accountability, encouragement, and shared wisdom. These relationships enrich your faith journey and help you grow through mutual support. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="27" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 27:17</span>*)

<!-- p166 -->
Our ‘faith toward God’ is not a one-time salvation event, but an ongoing practice and relationship we should cultivate throughout our lives. The benefits of a trusting and fruitful relationship are many, but we must do the good work of nurturing that trust and growth in order to see it thrive.
