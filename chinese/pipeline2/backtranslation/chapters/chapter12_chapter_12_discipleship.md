# Chapter 12 - Discipleship

<!-- p1 -->
Discipleship means following Jesus, learning from Him, and becoming more like Him in our lives and love. It consists of a lifelong journey of obedience, transformation, and service, guided by the Holy Spirit and rooted in God’s Word.

<!-- p2 -->
In the Great Commission, discipleship is the primary command. Jesus defined this mission as follows:

<!-- p3 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p4 -->
> **<u>Matthew 28:18-20</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Then Jesus came and said to them, “All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to Me.
>
> Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit,
>
> teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you; behold, I am with you always, to the end of the age.” Amen.
>

<!-- p5 -->
</span>

<!-- p6 -->
As His followers, we are to *“go and make disciples of all nations.”* Discipleship is about personal growth and also about multiplying that growth by teaching others to follow Christ. Discipleship is the vehicle by which we build God’s kingdom; Jesus’ teaching is the message.

<!-- p7 -->
The call to be a disciple is not merely to attend church or memorize Bible verses-it is an invitation to live like Jesus and to partner with Him in advancing God’s kingdom. True discipleship helps believers mature spiritually and become more effective in ministry (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:13</span>). It shapes the mindset and skills needed to serve others and share the gospel. As Paul taught Timothy in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 2:2</span>, discipleship follows a pattern of spiritual multiplication, where trained disciples continue to raise up new followers and lead others to Christ.

<!-- p8 -->
Discipleship is both a personal calling and a global mission. On a personal level, it involves growth in faith, developing a vibrant spiritual life, and building relationships with others and with God. At the same time, it propels the church to reach the nations with the gospel, ensuring that Christ’s message continues to expand and transform lives across generations.

<!-- p9 -->
## Why Discipleship Matters

<!-- p10 -->
The impact of discipleship goes beyond personal change-it brings kingdom impact. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 2:2</span>, Paul reminds Timothy to entrust what he has learned to faithful people who will be able to teach others also. This generational pattern shows that discipleship is the engine driving the church’s growth and endurance. Discipleship matters because it is the method Jesus chose to spread the gospel and build His church.

<!-- p11 -->
Jesus did not focus on massive crowds, but intentionally cultivated a small group of followers, teaching them, modeling faithfulness for them, and preparing them to carry on His mission. This personal, relational approach ensured that His message would multiply, because each disciple was equipped to bring forth more disciples.

<!-- p12 -->
Essentially, discipleship is at the heart of fulfilling the Great Commission (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19-20</span>). Jesus calls us not merely to decisions of faith but to make disciples-these followers are rooted in God’s Word, empowered by the Holy Spirit, and equipped for ministry. Through discipleship, believers grow in their understanding of Scripture, establish spiritual disciplines, and learn to walk in obedience to Christ. Discipleship prepares the church to impact the world, advance the gospel, and reflect God’s glory among the nations.

<!-- p13 -->
### Nature and Nurture

<!-- p14 -->
In studies of biology, psychology, and anthropology, we often consider the influence of nature and nurture. People often ask whether a person is shaped more by inborn genetic inheritance, or more by environment and upbringing. The truth is that everyone is a fusion of nature and nurture. These two factors interact powerfully, together composing the growth of a person’s soul.

<!-- p15 -->
When you receive Jesus, the miracle of salvation causes you to be born again by that imperishable seed. You receive a godly nature! Your spiritual DNA is rewritten! This godly nature stirs our hearts to draw near to the Father, to know Him, and to experience Him.

<!-- p16 -->
Just as a newborn’s beautiful, lovely, innocent nature must be shaped by loving parents in order to grow into a healthy, mature person, we too must be formed and nourished in order to grow.

<!-- p17 -->
Discipleship is the spiritual growth process that builds upon our godly nature and brings us to spiritual maturity. By committing to discipleship, we ensure that the faith is passed on, leaders are raised up, and the church remains healthy and vibrant at both the local and global levels-whether locally or globally.

<!-- p18 -->
### Didactic Model vs Apprenticeship Model

<!-- p19 -->
In contemporary Western education, a student’s primary relationship is with the curriculum. Students’ learning focuses on content, materials, books, videos, assignments, and so on. Educational change is achieved through courses. This instructional approach is called the *didactic model*.

<!-- p20 -->
The lecture-based model has not always been the norm. The model and method used in Jesus’ time was *the apprenticeship model.* This learning model emphasizes that knowledge is more “caught than taught,” and it focuses more on the relationship between student and teacher than on the relationship between student and curriculum.

<!-- p21 -->
Over roughly a millennium, the lecture-based model gradually became the default method, from the earliest medieval universities in Europe in the 11<sup>th</sup> - 13<sup>th</sup> centuries, the invention of the printing press in the 15<sup>th</sup> century, the Enlightenment in the 17<sup>th</sup> &amp; 18<sup>th</sup> centuries, and the impact of the Industrial Revolution on information and education in the 19<sup>th</sup> &amp; 20<sup>th</sup> centuries.

<!-- p22 -->
The advantage of the lecture-based model is that it is more efficient at scale than the apprenticeship model. Massive Open Online Courses (MOOCs) can host tens of thousands of students at once, whereas even a very skilled master can perhaps take on only dozens of apprentices at a time.

<!-- p23 -->
The result of the lecture-based model is that students gain mastery of content; however, students do not become like the teacher. The teacher is the facilitator of the course. The teacher imparts knowledge.

<!-- p24 -->
In the apprenticeship model, the master shares not only knowledge but imparts wisdom. The result of the apprenticeship model is that students become so completely like the master that they can reproduce the master’s work and take on apprentices of their own.

<!-- p25 -->
The lecture-based model has its use and place in the kingdom of God; a good example is this book or any other Christian educational material. As a rabbi, Jesus was proficient in and trained in rabbinic methods, and He employed the apprenticeship model as the primary mode for His disciples.

<!-- p26 -->
Jesus described the apprenticeship model this way:

<!-- p27 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p28 -->
> **<u>Luke 6:40</u>** (NKJV)
>
> A disciple is not above his teacher, but everyone who is perfectly trained will be like his teacher.
>

<!-- p29 -->
</span>

<!-- p30 -->
In the lecture-based model, students become carriers of knowledge; in the apprenticeship model, students become like the teacher. Let’s study the key words in that passage in Luke.

<!-- p31 -->
[See Table 6](../tables/table6.md)

<!-- p32 -->
In sum, the purposes and goals of the apprenticeship model used in discipleship are twofold:

<!-- p33 -->
**First, disciples are fully trained**, like the teacher. This does not mean disciples must imitate everything the teacher does. We are not commanded to make clones, but to raise mature men and women who can carry Jesus’ message to the ends of the earth.

<!-- p34 -->
**Second, disciples now become teachers of others.** Disciples must pass on what they have learned to others, or the message of the kingdom stops with them. This does not mean they will necessarily have exactly the same gifts as the teacher or enter full-time ministry, but it does mean they must multiply for their training to be considered complete.

<!-- p35 -->
Any organism that cannot reproduce is sterile; its lineage is cut off. Animals that cannot reproduce are not healthy, and churches that cannot reproduce are likewise unhealthy.

## Jesus’ Discipleship Model and Process

<!-- p37 -->
In the Gospels, Jesus, together with the disciples He chose, demonstrated a mature and complete example of discipleship. We should broadly base our own path of being and making disciples on these steps.

<!-- p38 -->
The apprenticeship model has two roles: master and student. Every master was once a student, and hopefully every student will become a master. I will use the term *disciple* for the student and *disciple-maker* for the master/teacher.

<!-- p39 -->
Every discipleship relationship progresses through eight stages. These stages (or steps) are not entirely discrete, and relationships often flow between stages. In each stage, both the disciple and the disciple-maker have their respective emphases. We will explore the qualities of a disciple-maker in depth later in this chapter.

<!-- p40 -->
The eight stages of discipleship are:

<!-- p41 -->
1.  Invitation/Call

<!-- p42 -->
2.  Teaching

<!-- p43 -->
3.  Demonstration

<!-- p44 -->
4.  Participation/Trying

<!-- p45 -->
5.  Correction

<!-- p46 -->
6.  Empowerment/Authorization

<!-- p47 -->
7.  Multiplication

<!-- p48 -->
8.  Staying Connected

<!-- p49 -->
### Invitation/Call

<!-- p50 -->
Discipleship begins with Jesus issuing a personal invitation to people to follow Him. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 4:19</span>, Jesus called His first disciples, saying: *“Follow Me, and I will make you fishers of men.”* This invitation is both relational and transformative-it requires leaving behind an old way of life and entering a new purpose centered on God’s mission. Jesus’ call is not to be a spectator, but to participate in His ministry and Kingdom work.

<!-- p51 -->
Contemporary disciple-makers must invite others into genuine relationships that spark spiritual growth and obedience. The invitation to discipleship is deeply personal. Jesus meets people in their circumstances, addressing their unique needs and situations, but He also calls them further- to grow, serve, and become more like Him. This pattern reflects the relational nature of discipleship, where the focus is not merely knowledge, but a life-changing relationship with Christ.

<!-- p52 -->
### Teaching

<!-- p53 -->
Teaching is central to discipleship. Jesus devoted much of His ministry to teaching the principles of the Kingdom of God, most notably in the Sermon on the Mount (Matthew 5-7). His teaching provided a foundation for living righteously, loving others, and doing God’s will. In discipleship today, teaching God’s Word remains central, because Scripture is the primary tool for spiritual growth, correction, and training in righteousness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>).

<!-- p54 -->
Disciple-makers must be committed to faithfully teaching the Bible, helping others understand it and apply it to life. Teaching is not merely the transfer of knowledge-it requires modeling a Christlike life so that disciples can see faith in action. Jesus lived out the truths He taught, showing that effective teaching is both spoken and visible. Likewise, disciple-makers should strive to live what they teach, providing practical examples of obedience and faithfulness.

<!-- p55 -->
### Demonstration

<!-- p56 -->
Jesus did not only teach His disciples- He also demonstrated how to live out the truths He proclaimed. Jesus showed in concrete ways what love, service, and ministry look like. Examples in the Gospels include: Jesus washing His disciples’ feet as an act of humility and service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 13:12-15</span>), healing the sick, and feeding the hungry. Through His actions, He set an example of compassion, faith, and courage, leaving His followers with a clear pattern to imitate.

<!-- p57 -->
Discipleship requires showing, not just telling. Contemporary disciple-makers must be transparent about their journey of faith, including struggles and victories, so that others can see faith at work in real life. Whether praying with those in need, sharing testimonies, or serving the community, discipleship thrives when it is grounded in practical demonstration. Actions often speak louder than words, making demonstration a powerful tool for training others in the faith.

<!-- p58 -->
### Participation/Trying

<!-- p59 -->
Jesus did not leave His disciples on the sidelines- He actively involved them in ministry experiences. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 9:1-6</span> and <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 10:5-15</span>, Jesus sent the disciples out to preach the gospel, heal the sick, and cast out demons. These hands-on opportunities allowed the disciples to learn by doing under His guidance, gaining confidence and skill.

<!-- p60 -->
Modern discipleship should also include practical participation. Disciplers must invite disciples to engage in ministry, whether leading a prayer group, sharing testimonies, or serving in outreach. Provide a safe space to practice, so that disciples grow in courage while receiving support and encouragement. Learning works best when applied, and participation is the key to turning theory into action.

<!-- p61 -->
### Correction

<!-- p62 -->
Growth often requires correction, and when disciples misunderstood His teaching or failed in their tasks, Jesus corrected them with compassion. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33-37" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 9:33-37</span>, He addressed their pride by teaching servant leadership. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-32" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 22:31-32</span>, He prayed for Peter, preparing him to recover from failure and strengthen others.

<!-- p63 -->
Correction in discipleship should be full of grace and constructive-rooted in Scripture and aimed at restoration (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 4:2</span>). It is not condemnation, but helping disciples grow and avoid pitfalls. Disciplers should provide accountability, use God's Word to guide and encourage growth, while modeling humility in receiving correction themselves.

<!-- p64 -->
### Empowerment/Authorization

<!-- p65 -->
Jesus did not only teach and correct- He also empowered His disciples to carry out His mission. He gave them authority to proclaim the gospel, heal the sick, and cast out demons (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:18-20</span>). After His resurrection, He promised the Holy Spirit as the source of power for their ministry (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>).

<!-- p66 -->
Modern discipleship should include enabling others to operate in their spiritual gifts and serve effectively. This includes identifying gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-13</span>), encouraging their use, and releasing disciples to serve. Empowerment also emphasizes relying on the strength, boldness, and wisdom given by the Holy Spirit, ensuring disciples are spiritually equipped to accomplish the work God has called them to do.

<!-- p67 -->
### Multiplication

<!-- p68 -->
The goal of discipleship is not merely personal growth- but multiplication. Jesus modeled this by training disciples to make more disciples (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19-20</span>). After His resurrection, the apostles continued this multiplying mission, spreading the gospel and planting churches across the known world. Paul's instruction in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 2:2</span> reinforces this principle, urging Timothy to teach those who will teach others.

<!-- p69 -->
Discipleship involves equipping disciples to make others, forming a chain reaction of faith that expands God's kingdom. This process requires intentionality- teaching disciples how to mentor, teach, and train others, while sending them out with confidence and purpose (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 10:15</span>).

<!-- p70 -->
### Staying Connected

<!-- p71 -->
Discipleship does not end when someone is sent out. Jesus modeled ongoing connection with His disciples, continuing to encourage and teach them even after His resurrection (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="21" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 21:15-19</span>). The early church continued this pattern, frequently gathering for teaching, fellowship, and prayer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42-47" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:42-47</span>).

<!-- p72 -->
Modern discipleship should also cultivate ongoing relationships, providing accountability, encouragement, and support as disciples continue to grow and lead others. Maintaining unity within the body of Christ ensures that disciples are never isolated, but are continually strengthened and encouraged in their journey of faith.

<!-- p73 -->
## Who Does Discipleship?

<!-- p74 -->
In the body of Christ, everyone is first responsible to be a disciple and then to contribute to the disciple-making process. Gifts and roles are diverse, so our contributions may differ, but we all share responsibility. Those ultimately most responsible for the state and health of discipleship in the local church are the leaders (**elders and deacons - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 14:23</span>**), but everyone is to participate.

<!-- p75 -->
Discipleship is a living, growing process- like nurturing children toward maturity. Healthy discipleship brings strength and life. Just as parents strengthen and nourish children so that they grow healthy and mature, disciplers also help to nourish, strengthen, and mature disciples. The ultimate goal of discipleship is not 'transfer of knowledge,' but transformation of life.

<!-- p76 -->
We must all become complete (mature) people and make disciples. Although our gifts differ, all disciplers share common qualities.

<!-- p77 -->
### Qualities of a Discipler

<!-- p78 -->
A discipler not only follows Christ personally but is committed to helping others grow in faith. This role requires certain qualities that reflect spiritual maturity as well as practical dedication. Disciplers must continually rely on God's strength, wisdom, and grace to overcome the challenges that accompany leading others on their spiritual journey.

<!-- p79 -->
#### Rooted in Christ

<!-- p80 -->
The life foundation of a disciple maker must be deeply rooted in Christ. Jesus said, “I am the vine; you are the branches. If you remain in me and I in you, you will bear much fruit; apart from me you can do nothing” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:5</span>). Effective disciple makers know that the ability to grow and help others comes from drawing near to Jesus daily. However, resistance to personal growth and spiritual change can hinder this dependence. Disciple makers must continually cultivate their personal relationship with Jesus through prayer, Bible study, and obedience, modeling for the disciples they lead what it means to abide in Christ.

<!-- p81 -->
#### Faithful to the Mission

<!-- p82 -->
Disciple makers must hold fast to the commitment to the mission of making disciples, which Jesus commanded in the Great Commission (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19-20</span>). This mission requires intentional focus and prioritization, recognizing that making disciples is not an optional task but the church’s core purpose. However, challenges such as time constraints, busyness, and personal distractions can make it difficult to remain focused. Disciple makers must intentionally schedule time for mentoring, teaching, and building relationships, seeing disciple making as a lifestyle rather than an occasional activity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 19:10</span>).

<!-- p83 -->
#### Led by the Holy Spirit

<!-- p84 -->
Making disciples cannot be done by human strength alone; it requires the Holy Spirit’s guidance and empowerment. Jesus promised His disciples that when the Holy Spirit came upon them, they would receive power (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span]), enabling them to be witnesses and servants of the gospel. Disciple makers must be sensitive to the Spirit’s leading, relying on His wisdom to navigate complex situations, cultural barriers, and difficult conversations. Dependence on the Holy Spirit enables disciple makers to act in love, courage, and spiritual discernment, ensuring their efforts are Spirit-led rather than self-driven.

<!-- p85 -->
#### Humble and Teachable

<!-- p86 -->
Humility is the mark of an effective disciple maker. As Paul taught, “Do nothing out of selfish ambition or vain conceit. Rather, in humility value others above yourselves” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 2:3</span>). Disciple makers recognize that they themselves are lifelong learners who must remain teachable and willing to be admonished. They intentionally invite correction and feedback, modeling humility for those they lead. Pride can become a major obstacle to growth, both for disciple makers and for disciples. Embracing mutual accountability and maintaining a servant’s heart helps build trust and fosters an environment conducive to growth and transparency.

<!-- p87 -->
#### Patient and Persevering

<!-- p88 -->
Discipleship is rarely instantaneous-it is a long-term process that requires patience and perseverance. Paul reminds believers, “not grow weary in doing good, for at the proper time we will reap a harvest if we do not give up” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:9</span>). Growth in faith is often accompanied by setbacks, questions, and failures, but disciple makers are called to walk with others, offering encouragement and support at every step. When progress seems slow, it is easy to grow discouraged, but disciple makers trust that God is at work even when results are not immediately visible.

<!-- p89 -->
Becoming a disciple maker requires a blend of spiritual dependence, commitment, and humility. It is a calling that demands patience and perseverance while remaining Spirit-led and teachable. Disciple makers are not expected to be perfect, but they must be faithful—abiding in Christ, investing in others, and trusting God to bring growth and transformation in His timing. These qualities reflect Christ’s love and help others become mature disciples who can in turn make others.

<!-- p90 -->
## Practical Tools for Discipleship

<!-- p91 -->
Effective discipleship requires intentional tools and practices that foster spiritual growth, accountability, and community. These tools help disciples build a solid foundation of faith, deepen intimacy with God, and equip them to disciple others. Practical methods such as Bible study, prayer, small groups, and mentoring provide the structure and support needed for sustained growth.

<!-- p92 -->
### Bible Study

<!-- p93 -->
Deep engagement with Scripture is the foundation of discipleship. Jesus emphasized the importance of God’s Word in spiritual growth, declaring, “man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceeds out of the mouth of God” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 4:4</span>). Regular Bible study and memorization enable disciples to internalize truth, resist error, and apply God’s wisdom to daily life.

<!-- p94 -->
Disciples should be encouraged to immerse themselves in Scripture, not merely as an academic exercise but to know God and transform their hearts (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="119" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="105" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 119:105</span>). Practical approaches such as reading plans, study guides, and group discussion provide opportunities to explore doctrine and discuss application in community. By establishing a habit of regular study, disciples lay a solid foundation for spiritual maturity and effective ministry (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>).

<!-- p95 -->
### Prayer

<!-- p96 -->
Prayer is the lifeline of discipleship, connecting believers with God’s presence, power, and guidance. Jesus taught His disciples how to pray in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 11:1-4</span>, emphasizing dependence on God and spiritual alignment with His will. Through prayer, disciples learn to seek God’s wisdom, intercede for others, and receive strength for service.

<!-- p97 -->
Teaching disciples how to pray-whether individually or corporately-equips them to cultivate intimacy with God and discern His leading (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 4:6-7</span>). Incorporating practices such as prayer journals, prayer groups, and fasting helps cultivate spiritual discipline and empowerment, ensuring disciples remain Spirit-led and focused on the mission.

<!-- p98 -->
### Small Groups and Community

<!-- p99 -->
Discipleship thrives in community. The early church modeled relational discipleship through fellowship, prayer, and the breaking of bread (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42-47" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:42-47</span>). Small groups and accountability partnerships provide safe spaces for disciples to grow together, share struggles, and celebrate victories.

<!-- p100 -->
The local church is the central hub of discipleship relationships， providing opportunities such as small group Bible studies、prayer meetings、and fellowship activities。 These gatherings enable disciples to practice love， bear one another’s burdens（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:2</span>）， and encourage one another toward spiritual maturity（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:24-25</span>）。 Small groups also provide a framework for multiplying leaders， as mature disciples train and send others to lead groups、mentor new believers。

<!-- p101 -->
### Mentoring

<!-- p102 -->
Mentoring provides a personal and intentional approach to discipleship。 Jesus modeled this， investing deeply in the twelve disciples， walking with them through teaching、correction、and encouragement。 Likewise， one-on-one relationships allow disciple-makers to tailor guidance to individual needs， helping disciples grow in faith、character、and leadership。

<!-- p103 -->
Mentors also serve as accountability partners， providing support、prayer、and wisdom during seasons of growth and struggle。 Practical tools such as spiritual assessments、goal setting、and regular check-ins ensure that disciples remain focused and motivated。 Peer relationships within the mentoring structure also promote mutual growth， as both mentors and disciples sharpen one another through shared experiences（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="27" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 27:17</span>）。

## **Personal Reflection Questions**

<!-- p105 -->
1.  What does being a disciple of Jesus mean to you personally？ How do you actively follow Him in daily life？

<!-- p106 -->
2.  Reflect on Jesus’ model of discipleship。 Among His approaches - inviting、teaching、modeling， or empowering - which aspect impresses you the most？ Why？

<!-- p107 -->
3.  How can you multiply your faith by leading others to become disciples？ What challenges or fears might hinder you， and how will you overcome them？（Note, I said multiply your faith, not multiply your disciples!）

<!-- p108 -->
4.  What qualities of a disciple-maker do you see in yourself（for example， patience、humility、valuing relationships）？ Which qualities do you feel need further development？

<!-- p109 -->
5.  Think about the people in your life。 Whom might God be calling you to lead as a disciple？ How can you take the first step to build that relationship？

## Small Group Discussion Questions (20-30 minutes)

<!-- p111 -->
1.  **Icebreaker question：** Who has had the greatest impact on your spiritual growth？ What did they do that impacted you？

<!-- p112 -->
2.  **Exploring Jesus’ model：** Read <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 4:19</span>。 How does Jesus’ invitation to ‘Follow Me’ encapsulate the heart of discipleship？ What does this look like today？

<!-- p113 -->
3.  **The call to make disciples：** Discuss <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19-20</span>。 Why is making disciples central to the church’s mission？ As individuals， how can we participate in this mission？

<!-- p114 -->
4.  **Overcoming challenges：** What common obstacles are there in making disciples（for example， time、fear、lack of confidence）？ How can we support one another to overcome these obstacles？

<!-- p115 -->
5.  **Practical application：** What one specific step can you take this week to invest in someone’s spiritual growth？ As a group， how can we encourage one another in this process？
