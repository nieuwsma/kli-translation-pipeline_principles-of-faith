# Chapter 13 - Spiritual Authority

<!-- p1 -->
Jesus is the ultimate and complete source of spiritual authority. As the victorious Christ, He has ultimate authority over all things in heaven and on earth.

<!-- p2 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p3 -->
> **<u>Matthew 28:18-20</u>** (New King James Version)
>
> And Jesus came and spoke to them, saying, "All authority has been given to Me in heaven and on earth.
>
> Go therefore and make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit,
>
> teaching them to observe all things that I have commanded you; and lo, I am with you always, even to the end of the age." Amen.
>

<!-- p4 -->
</span>

<!-- p5 -->
In this chapter we will learn about Christ’s authority and power, what authority and power He has granted to us (His church), and how to operate in the authority of the name of Jesus. We will also study the existence of angels and evil spirits, and examine their authority, power, and nature.

<!-- p6 -->
Authority means having the right to act. Power is the ability to carry it out. Both are indispensable in the spiritual life. Authority is different from power. Let us look at the table below comparing Authority &amp; Power, and consider a few examples.

<!-- p7 -->
[See Table 7](../tables/table7.md)

<!-- p8 -->
Here are some examples to help understand the difference between power and authority.

<!-- p9 -->
Example 1: At work, an employee may have the talent and skill to sell company assets (power). If he also receives the corresponding permission from the company owner (authority), the exercise of that ability is legitimate. Selling company assets without such authority is theft.

<!-- p10 -->
Example 2: A police officer receives specialized training with weapons and in hand-to-hand combat to learn how to arrest and physically subdue criminals (power). The officer is entrusted with on-duty responsibilities supported by law (authority) to enforce criminal statutes.

## The source of Jesus' authority

<!-- p74 -->
Since we have carefully examined what authority is, we need to know who is the source of this authority. Jesus’ authority is both divine and delegated. It originates from God the Father, is confirmed through his death and resurrection, and operates in perfect alignment with the Father’s will. Though absolute, his authority respects human free will, manifesting God’s love rather than coercive control.

<!-- p75 -->
Jesus’ authority is rooted in his divine identity as the Son of God and is granted by the Father. The Father has entrusted all authority to Christ, including judgment and the rule over heaven and earth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 11:27</span>). Jesus’ authority reflects the unity and love within the Trinity, in which the Son perfectly carries out the Father’s plan (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="35" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:35</span>).

<!-- p76 -->
Although Jesus, as the Son of God, inherently possessed authority, his victory over sin, death, and Hades was fully established through his death and resurrection. Christ’s obedience in his sacrificial offering led to his exaltation (seated at the Father’s right hand), thereby securing his authority over all creation. By conquering death, he obtained absolute sovereignty over life and eternity.

## Laying on of Hands

<!-- p116 -->
The laying on of hands is an important biblical practice throughout the Old and New Testaments，symbolizing blessing、commissioning、healing、and the impartation of spiritual authority or gifts。It is not merely a physical gesture，but represents a spiritual connection and impartation under the guidance of God's authority。

<!-- p117 -->
In the Old Testament，the laying on of hands is often associated with blessing and consecration。例如，Jacob laid hands on his grandsons Ephraim and Manasseh to bless them（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="48" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 48:14</span>）。Similarly，Moses laid hands on Joshua，appointing him as successor and imparting to him authority and wisdom for leadership（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="NUM" data-chapter="27" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Numbers 27:18-23</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="DEU" data-chapter="34" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Deuteronomy 34:9</span>）。This act publicly affirmed God's choice and empowerment for the entrusted work。

<!-- p118 -->
In the New Testament，the laying on of hands is closely tied to the ministry of Jesus and the early church。Jesus often laid hands on the sick to heal them（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 6:5</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 4:40</span>），demonstrating His compassion and authority over sickness。The apostles continued this practice，laying on hands to impart the Holy Spirit（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:17</span>）、spiritual gifts（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 4:14</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 1:6</span>），and to bless believers。The laying on of hands also played a role in commissioning leaders and missionaries into ministry（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 13:3</span>）。

<!-- p119 -->
The laying on of hands symbolizes faith in and obedience to God's word。It is not a mechanical ritual，but a spiritual practice rooted in dependence on the Holy Spirit。For example，在<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:6</span>，the apostles laid hands on the chosen deacons，committing them to God's service and publicly recognizing their calling。在<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 19:6</span>，Paul laid hands on the believers，and they received the Holy Spirit，demonstrating the empowering role of the laying on of hands。

<!-- p120 -->
However, the Bible also issues a warning about this practice：

<!-- p121 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p122 -->
> **<u>1 Timothy 5:22</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Do not lay hands on anyone hastily, nor share in other people's sins； keep yourself pure。
>

<!-- p123 -->
</span>

<!-- p124 -->
This highlights the importance of discernment and spiritual maturity when appointing individuals to leadership roles。 As we learned in the chapter on the Church, leaders must be tested and proven to avoid promoting unqualified or immature believers, lest the body of Christ be harmed。

<!-- p125 -->
Here are some additional guidelines regarding the laying on of hands：

<!-- p126 -->
1.  Contextually appropriate - The person receiving the laying on of hands should be a willing participant。

<!-- p127 -->
2.  Physically appropriate - The laying on of hands does not involve sexual or sensual touching。 In practice, placing a hand on the shoulder, hand, or forehead (with permission) is typically a commonly accepted point of contact in the Western world。

<!-- p128 -->
3.  Culturally appropriate - In some cultures, unless related by blood or marriage, it is not appropriate for men and women to have physical contact。

<!-- p129 -->
The laying on of hands remains a powerful expression of faith and obedience in the Church today。 It is a means of blessing, healing, sending, and empowering, always pointing to the authority of Christ and the work of the Holy Spirit。 Whether praying for the sick, sending missionaries, or commissioning leaders, it reminds us that believers do not act in their own strength but are under the authority of Jesus Christ and aligned with His will。

<!-- p130 -->
Since we have studied the authority of Jesus and the authority of believers, we will next discuss other beings in the spiritual realm： angels and demons。

<!-- p131 -->
## Angels and Demons

<!-- p132 -->
### Angels

<!-- p133 -->
Angels are an important part of God's creation, serving as His messengers and agents to carry out His divine will。 Scripture reveals a spiritual realm inhabited by these beings who are powerful, wise, and obedient to God's commands。 Although angels are often unseen, their presence and activity influence human history and spiritual warfare。 Understanding their roles, classifications, and activities gives us deeper insight into God's plan and the unseen reality that surrounds us。

<!-- p134 -->
The word “angel” itself means “messenger,” highlighting their primary office。 Angels act in obedience to God's will, whether delivering messages, providing protection, executing judgment, or engaging in spiritual warfare。 They are not objects of worship but examples of obedience and reverence, always directing glory to God。 This chapter will explore the nature, classifications, and roles of angels, clarifying their importance in Scripture and their significance for our lives today。

<!-- p135 -->
In Scripture, angels are regarded as **messengers**, **servants**, and **warriors**。 Angels often serve as messengers, delivering important announcements, warnings, and encouragement。 They reveal God's plans and provide guidance in times of need（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26-38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 1:26-38</span>；<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 2:13</span>；<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 1:1</span>）。 Angels are actively involved in protecting, strengthening, and rescuing believers。 They minister to God's people in times of distress, providing supernatural assistance（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="91" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 91:11</span>；<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 12:7-11</span>；<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 4:11</span>）。 Angels engage in spiritual warfare, contending with demonic forces to advance God's kingdom and guard believers（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="DAN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Daniel 10:13</span>；<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:7-9</span>）。

<!-- p136 -->
#### The Nature of Angels

<!-- p137 -->
Angels are created beings, brought into existence by God before the foundation of the world。 They are distinct from humans and possess unique qualities that equip them for their heavenly duties。 Scripture teaches that angels are spiritual beings not bound by material limitations, yet they can take on visible form when interacting with humans。

<!-- p138 -->
Angels are described as immortal, neither dying nor aging。 They are innumerable, filling the vast realms of heaven。 Though often referenced with masculine terms, Scripture makes clear that angels are genderless and neither marry nor reproduce。 These attributes underscore their purpose as God's servants, wholly devoted to His will and commands。 Angels possess extraordinary qualities that reflect their divine mission： they are obedient, reverent, wise, and powerful。 These characteristics enable them to carry out God's will effectively and faithfully。

[See Table 8](../tables/table8.md)

<!-- p140 -->
Although the Bible emphasizes several roles and titles of angels, it does not provide an exhaustive classification system. However, some angels are identified by specific roles or functions：

<!-- p141 -->
- **The Angel of the LORD** - Often regarded as a pre-incarnate manifestation of Christ, appearing with divine authority。 (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="23" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 23:20-23</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JDG" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Judges 6:11-24</span>*)

<!-- p142 -->
- **Archangels** - High-ranking angels, such as Michael who leads armies, and Gabriel who delivers divine messages。 (*Jude 9; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:7</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 1:19-26</span>*)

<!-- p143 -->
- **Cherubim** - Guardians of God’s glory and presence, such as at the Garden of Eden and at the Ark of the Covenant。 (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 3:24</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 25:22</span>*)

<!-- p144 -->
- **Seraphim** - Worshipers of God, continually proclaiming His holiness。 (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 6:1-6</span>*)

<!-- p145 -->
- **Elect angels** - Faithful angels who resisted Satan’s rebellion and remain loyal to God。 (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:21</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 25:41</span>*)

<!-- p146 -->
These categories highlight the diversity and organization within the angelic realm, but their primary focus is always to accomplish God’s will。

<!-- p147 -->
#### Human Interaction with Angels

<!-- p148 -->
People often encounter angels in the Bible, but we are never taught to command them or pray to them。 Instead, angels act at God’s direction。 They serve believers as **God’s servants**（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 1:14</span>）。 Their interactions include protection, guidance, and deliverance, and they always point us to God’s power and sovereignty。

<!-- p149 -->
- **Warning against worshiping angels**: The Bible forbids worshiping angels or seeking encounters with them（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:18</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 22:8-9</span>).

<!-- p150 -->
- **Pray to God, not to angels**: Believers should pray to God, who sends angels according to His will（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="91" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 91:11</span>）。

<!-- p151 -->
- **Encouragement and trust**: Angels remind us of God’s care, but our faith should rest entirely in God, not in angels。

<!-- p152 -->
Angels are awe-inspiring creatures, created to serve God and minister to His people。 Though their work is often unseen, the Bible gives us glimpses of their activity, revealing their loyalty, power, and reverence。 Angels remind us of God’s provision and protection, yet they always point to His glory。 As believers, we are called to trust in God’s care, knowing that angels operate as His servants to accomplish His divine purposes。

<!-- p153 -->
### Demons

<!-- p154 -->
Just as studying angels is necessary to understand the spiritual world, so too is the study of demons。 Scripture clearly describes their activities, influence, and the victory over them through Jesus Christ。 Knowing their role in spiritual warfare helps believers stand firm in faith and not be afraid。

<!-- p155 -->
#### The Origin and Nature of Demons

<!-- p156 -->
Demons are angels who fell by following Satan in rebellion against God。*(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:7-9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 25:41</span>).* Satan, formerly called Lucifer（“light-bearer”）, was an exalted angel who sought to exalt himself above God and was therefore cast down from heaven along with the other rebellious angels。*(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 14:12-15</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EZK" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ezekiel 28:12-17</span>).*

<!-- p157 -->
Demons are spirits without physical bodies, but they can oppress and torment people and animals。*(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 5:11-13</span>).* They seek to deceive, tempt, oppress, and destroy。*(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 11:14</span>). Unlike God,* they are limited and are neither omnipresent (everywhere), omniscient (all-knowing), nor omnipotent (all-powerful) *.* Their ultimate defeat is certain, and they will face eternal punishment。*(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 20:10</span>)*

<!-- p158 -->
The Bible describes the devil’s work and activities as follows：

<!-- p159 -->
- Opposing God’s work *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Thessalonians 2:18</span>)*

<!-- p160 -->
- Deceives the world *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 4:4</span>)*

<!-- p161 -->
- Tempts and accuses believers *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 22:31</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:10</span>)*

<!-- p162 -->
- Torments and harms *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JOB" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Job 1:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 13:16</span>)*

<!-- p163 -->
The devil has many names and descriptions, each pointing to different aspects of his work.

<!-- p164 -->
[See Table 9](../tables/table9.md)

<!-- p165 -->
The Gospels and the Epistles further clarify the enemy’s work. Evil spirits, as disembodied beings, mostly have to work through vessels who are willing and open to them. God works through people, and so does the devil. Such oppression is often marked by torment, self-harm, and destructive behavior *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 8:27-29</span>)*. Their attacks bring fear, temptation, and discouragement *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 12:7</span>)*. The most common source of their power (not authority) in a person’s life is through the influence of lies and emotional strongholds *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 10:3-5</span>)*.

<!-- p166 -->
It should be noted that not every lie a person believes, every illness in their body, or every stronghold in their life is a direct result of demonic influence, but these wounds are places the enemy can exploit to keep us in defeat and oppression.

<!-- p167 -->
#### Jesus' authority over the works of the enemy

<!-- p168 -->
Do not be afraid; Jesus has complete authority over all the works of the enemy. In His ministry, Jesus demonstrated power over demons *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 1:34</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 5:1-20</span>)*. The works of the enemy, and Satan himself, have been defeated through Jesus’ death and resurrection *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:15</span>)*.

<!-- p169 -->
As believers, we have been given authority by Jesus to cast out demons in His name:

<!-- p170 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p171 -->
> **<u>Mark 16:17</u>** (NKJV)
>
> These signs will follow those who believe: In My name they will cast out demons; they will speak with new tongues;
>

<!-- p172 -->
</span>

<!-- p173 -->
Furthermore, as heirs of salvation, we have authority to submit to God and resist the devil, and he will flee from us! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 4:7</span>).

<!-- p174 -->
To deal with evil or unclean spirits, the pattern presented in Scripture is to exercise authority over them in Jesus’ name and command them to leave. The Holy Spirit promises to give us wisdom and discernment if we ask! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:5</span>). We do not need to turn this into a show or a movie-like drama, nor do we even need to talk with them—that is a trick the devil uses to distract us. Satan’s power is established and maintained only by the lies he can get us to believe.

<!-- p175 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="18" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p176 -->
> **<u>Matthew 18:18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> "Truly I say to you, whatever you bind on earth will be bound in heaven, and whatever you loose on earth will be loosed in heaven.
>

<!-- p177 -->
</span>

<!-- p178 -->
We speak to them in Jesus’ name and command them to leave.

<!-- p179 -->
When dealing with matters related to evil spirits, there are two important warnings in Scripture:

<!-- p180 -->
1.  **Only those who have a relationship with Jesus have authority over evil spirits.** <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 19:11-20</span> speaks of the sons of a Jewish chief priest named Sceva. They tried to intervene in the life of a man oppressed and tormented by demons, but the evil spirit refused to leave because they had no relationship with Jesus. The oppressed man pounced on them, stripped them of their clothes, and beat them. Just as you should not impersonate a police officer, **do not pretend to have a relationship with Jesus; instead, build a relationship with Jesus!**

<!-- p181 -->
2.  **When an evil spirit leaves, it departs for a time and tries to come back.** If it finds its former dwelling place empty, it will return in a worse way. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="43-45" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 12:43-45</span>。 When we minister to someone oppressed by the devil, that person needs to fill the place the evil spirit once occupied in their life. This at least means establishing a personal relationship with Jesus and strengthening that relationship by obeying the Holy Spirit. Evil or unclean spirits often operate in a person’s life on the basis of their beliefs, through the choices they make. Anyone released from the devil’s bondage needs to replace that old belief system and be transformed by the renewing of the mind（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:1-2</span>）。

<!-- p182 -->
We serve an awesome and mighty God, and in the face of that defeated enemy, we are ‘more than conquerors’（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="37" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:37</span>）。 We do not need to fear the already defeated enemy, because Jesus has given us His authority to crush his head! The devil’s time is short; he will ultimately be judged together with all the rulers and authorities on earth who reject Christ’s rule.
