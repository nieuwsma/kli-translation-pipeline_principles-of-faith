# Chapter 13 - Spiritual Authority

<!-- p1 -->
Jesus is the ultimate and complete source of spiritual authority. As the victorious Christ, He has ultimate authority over all things in heaven and on earth.

<!-- p2 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p3 -->
> **<u>Matthew 28:18-20</u>** (New King James Version)
>
> And Jesus came and spoke to them, saying, "All authority has been given to Me in heaven and on earth.
>
> Go therefore and make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit,
>
> teaching them to observe all things that I have commanded you; and lo, I am with you always, even to the end of the age." Amen.
>

<!-- p4 -->
</span>

<!-- p5 -->
In this chapter we will learn about Christ’s authority and power, what authority and power He has granted to us (His church), and how to operate in the authority of the name of Jesus. We will also study the existence of angels and evil spirits, and examine their authority, power, and nature.

<!-- p6 -->
Authority means having the right to act. Power is the ability to carry it out. Both are indispensable in the spiritual life. Authority is different from power. Let us look at the table below comparing Authority &amp; Power, and consider a few examples.

<!-- p7 -->
[See Table 7](../tables/table7.md)

<!-- p8 -->
Here are some examples to help understand the difference between power and authority.

<!-- p9 -->
Example 1: At work, an employee may have the talent and skill to sell company assets (power). If he also receives the corresponding permission from the company owner (authority), the exercise of that ability is legitimate. Selling company assets without such authority is theft.

<!-- p10 -->
Example 2: A police officer receives specialized training with weapons and in hand-to-hand combat to learn how to arrest and physically subdue criminals (power). The officer is entrusted with on-duty responsibilities supported by law (authority) to enforce criminal statutes.

<!-- p16 -->

<!-- p17 -->

<!-- p18 -->

<!-- p19 -->

<!-- p20 -->

## The source of Jesus' authority

<!-- p74 -->
Since we have carefully examined what authority is, we need to know who is the source of this authority. Jesus’ authority is both divine and delegated. It originates from God the Father, is confirmed through his death and resurrection, and operates in perfect alignment with the Father’s will. Though absolute, his authority respects human free will, manifesting God’s love rather than coercive control.

<!-- p75 -->
Jesus’ authority is rooted in his divine identity as the Son of God and is granted by the Father. The Father has entrusted all authority to Christ, including judgment and the rule over heaven and earth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 11:27</span>). Jesus’ authority reflects the unity and love within the Trinity, in which the Son perfectly carries out the Father’s plan (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="35" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:35</span>).

<!-- p76 -->
Although Jesus, as the Son of God, inherently possessed authority, his victory over sin, death, and Hades was fully established through his death and resurrection. Christ’s obedience in his sacrificial offering led to his exaltation (seated at the Father’s right hand), thereby securing his authority over all creation. By conquering death, he obtained absolute sovereignty over life and eternity.

## Jesus entrusts certain authority to his followers

<!-- p94 -->
Jesus entrusts believers with authority in specific areas. This authority is relational. Only in relationship with Jesus do we have this authority；apart from relationship with Jesus, we have no authority at all. Likewise, the power we receive from the Holy Spirit is ours only in relationship with him；apart from him, we can do nothing。

<!-- p95 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="12-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p96 -->
> **<u>John 14:12-14</u>** (NKJV)
>
> “Most assuredly, I say to you：he who believes in Me, the works that I do he will do also；and greater works than these he will do, because I go to My Father。
>
> And whatever you ask in My name, that I will do, that the Father may be glorified in the Son。
>
> If you ask anything in My name, I will do it。”
>

Jesus says these greater works are tied to his name（his rightful authority）, not to our name！These works are so that God may be glorified in the Son. Scripture says we must ask, and what we ask must accord with God's will；what we ask of God is not for personal enrichment, misuse, or abuse, but for the purposes of God's kingdom：
