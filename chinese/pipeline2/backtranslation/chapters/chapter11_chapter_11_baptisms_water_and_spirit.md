# Chapter 11 - Baptisms - Water and the Spirit

<!-- p1 -->
In our modern era, detached from a Jewish-rooted context, baptism is usually understood only as water baptism and the baptism of the Holy Spirit (Pentecost).

<!-- p2 -->
The Book of Hebrews was written to a Jewish audience. In our modern era, the vast majority of Christians do not have Jewish roots or tradition. Lacking Jewish roots and culture, many of us are missing the cultural background shared by first-century Messianic Jews (those Jewish believers who acknowledged Jesus). Since we did not grow up within Jewish tradition, we are usually unfamiliar with the various feasts, fasts, prayers, washings, rites, and rituals known to Jewish believers.

<!-- p3 -->
The Greek word for “various baptisms” or “washings” is: **Baptismos** (bap-tis-mos’ - **G909**) - the act of dipping or washing. It refers to ceremonial washings or rites of purification. It can mean immersion, purification rites, or washings practiced in Jewish and Christian contexts.

<!-- p4 -->
“Baptisms” is one of the foundational doctrines mentioned in Hebrews:

<!-- p5 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p6 -->
> **<u>Hebrews 6:1-2</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Therefore, leaving the elementary principles of Christ, let us go on to perfection, not laying again a foundation of repentance from dead works and of faith toward God,
>
> of the doctrine of baptisms, of laying on of hands, of resurrection of the dead, and of eternal judgment.
>

<!-- p8 -->
## Old Testament Washings - Historical Background

<!-- p9 -->
In the Old Testament, ceremonial washings occupied a central place in maintaining ritual purity and symbolizing holiness before God. These washings were prescribed in the Mosaic Law and applied both to the priests who served in the Tabernacle and to the people of Israel. For example, before entering the Tabernacle or approaching the altar, the priests had to wash their hands and feet at the bronze basin, symbolizing their readiness to serve before a holy God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="30" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 30:18-21</span>). Likewise, the ceremonies for cleansing those who were unclean due to impurity—such as touching a corpse or having a skin disease—included washing with water as a sign of the restoration of purity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LEV" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Leviticus 14:8</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="NUM" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Numbers 19:17-19</span>). These practices were outward expressions of an inward longing for holiness, emphasizing the importance of being set apart for God.

<!-- p10 -->
The ceremonial washings in the Old Testament had to be repeated regularly to maintain ritual purity. Unlike a permanent solution, these rites provided only temporary outward cleansing and could not address the deeper problem of sin. The author of Hebrews underscores this limitation, explaining that these practices were “symbolic for the present time,” and could not cleanse the conscience of the worshiper (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:9-10</span>). These washings served as continual reminders of human insufficiency and the need for something greater—a final cleansing that could purify not only the body but also the heart and soul.

<!-- p11 -->
The ceremonial washings of the Old Testament are fulfilled in Christ, who through His own sacrifice provides complete and eternal cleansing. Unlike the temporary rites of the Law, the blood of Christ brings perfect cleansing, purifying not only the flesh but also the conscience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:13-14</span>). Through His death and resurrection, Jesus accomplished what the Old Testament rites could only foreshadow—true reconciliation with God. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:22</span> calls believers to “draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, having our hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience and our bodies washed with pure water.” These words link Christ’s work with the imagery of Old Testament washings while demonstrating its far superior effect. His sacrifice does not merely cover sin but completely removes it (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 1:7</span>). Thus, the ceremonial washings of the Old Testament were like shadows, pointing to the deeper cleansing only Christ can provide, fulfilling humanity’s need for holiness through God’s intervention.

<!-- p12 -->
To reiterate, Christians are under no obligation to perform any ceremonial washings. Washings did not bring salvation under the Old Covenant, nor do they under the New. Ceremonial washings do not elevate our standing before God or earn us favor. Paul strongly condemned the Judaizers in Galatia (see Galatians), who attempted to make requirements from the Jewish setting (such as circumcision) necessary for salvation. Salvation is by faith in Jesus alone!

<!-- p13 -->
## John’s Baptism

<!-- p14 -->
In the New Testament canon, several kinds of baptisms are mentioned. We have already discussed baptisms and washings in Jewish tradition.

<!-- p15 -->
John the Baptist’s ministry centered on preparing the way for the coming of Jesus Christ. His baptism, described as “a baptism of repentance for the forgiveness of sins” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 1:4</span>), called people to confess their sins, turn their hearts to God, and prepare themselves for the coming of the Messiah. It was an outward sign of an inward heart of repentance, symbolizing a longing for spiritual cleansing and readiness for the kingdom of God. However, John’s baptism was temporary and preparatory - it pointed to the greater work that Christ would accomplish. Though it emphasized repentance, John’s baptism did not provide complete forgiveness or spiritual rebirth, because it lacked the indwelling and empowering of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:11-12</span>). Instead, John publicly declared that One greater than he would come, who would baptize not only with water but with the Holy Spirit and fire (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 3:16</span>).

<!-- p16 -->
John’s baptism was fulfilled and surpassed through the death, burial, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. The Christian baptism instituted by Jesus and practiced by the apostles marks a deeper reality—union with Christ, death to sin, and resurrection to new life (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 6:3-4</span>). Unlike John’s baptism, Christian baptism is directly connected to faith in Jesus’ atoning sacrifice and to the indwelling of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:38</span>). The outpouring of the Holy Spirit at Pentecost marked the inauguration of the New Covenant and the beginning of Spirit-empowered living, which John’s baptism could not provide. Therefore, John’s baptism is no longer practiced, because it has been replaced by the baptism ordained by Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19</span>); this baptism includes repentance, faith, and the sealing of believers with the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>).

<!-- p17 -->
## Christ’s Baptism

<!-- p18 -->
Christ’s baptism set a powerful example for His followers and laid the foundation for Christian water baptism. Although Jesus had no sin, He nevertheless submitted to John’s baptism in order to “fulfill all righteousness” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:15</span>). In this way, He identified with humanity’s need for repentance and pointed to His own death, burial, and resurrection. At His baptism, the Holy Spirit descended like a dove, and the Father declared, “This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:16-17</span>). This moment marked the beginning of Jesus’ public ministry and revealed the unity of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit, foreshadowing the spiritual transformation believers would experience through faith in Christ.

<!-- p19 -->
Later, Jesus gave His disciples the command concerning baptism, sending them, “Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19</span>). The water baptism instituted by Christ became an **act of obedience** and a **public confession of faith** for believers. It represents the believer’s identification with Jesus’ death, burial, and resurrection (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 6:3-4</span>). Going down into the water symbolizes dying to sin, and coming up out of the water signifies being raised to new life in Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:12</span>). However, it must be made clear that this outward act is not the source of salvation, but is a **visible sign** marking the **inward work of grace** already accomplished by the blood of Jesus.

<!-- p20 -->
Water baptism is a symbol of the cleansing power of Christ’s precious blood and the renewal of the Holy Spirit（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:26</span>；<span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>）. It indicates repentance, faith, and a commitment to follow Jesus. It is an outward witness of an inward transformation - a way for believers to publicly declare their loyalty to Christ and their participation in the body of Christ (the church)（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:41</span>）. Although it is commanded by Jesus and exemplified in the early church, it does not save. Salvation is by grace alone, through faith（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>）, and true cleansing is accomplished on a spiritual level through the blood of Christ（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 1:5</span>）. Water baptism is a response to that salvation, an act of obedience, not a requirement for being saved.

<!-- p21 -->
In the New Testament, water baptism is consistently associated with repentance and faith（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:38</span>；<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 16:16</span>）, and both require a personal decision to follow Christ - something infants cannot make. Baptism symbolizes the believer’s intentional identification with Christ’s death, burial, and resurrection（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 6:3-4</span>）, and their resolve to live in obedience to Him. Some denominations (e.g., Roman Catholic, Lutheran) practice infant baptism as a covenant sign similar to circumcision; however, since infants cannot express personal faith or repentance, the practice of infant baptism is not consistent with the biblical model of believer’s baptism. Instead, many churches hold baby dedication services, following Hannah’s example of dedicating Samuel to the Lord（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1SA" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Samuel 1:27-28</span>）and Mary and Joseph presenting Jesus in the temple（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 2:22</span>）. A dedication is not a substitute for baptism, but a commitment by parents to raise their children in the faith, teach them God’s word, and model a godly life until the child is mature enough to decide to follow Christ and be baptized.

<!-- p22 -->
Unlike the Old Testament ceremonial washings, which required repeated rituals to attain cleansing, Christian baptism is a one-time act, reflecting the already accomplished work of grace. The washings in the Old Testament symbolized external cleansing and foreshadowed the deeper spiritual cleansing that would come through Christ. In contrast, New Testament baptism symbolizes the inner renewal and regeneration accomplished by the Holy Spirit（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>）. It points to the believer’s participation in the New Covenant, where the cleansing of sin is permanent and based on Christ’s finished work.

<!-- p23 -->
Water baptism is closely related to the promise of the Holy Spirit. In<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:38</span>, Peter calls people to repent, be baptized, and receive the gift of the Holy Spirit. While water baptism publicly indicates a believer’s repentance and faith, the true baptism happens inwardly, when the Holy Spirit washes, regenerates, and seals the believer（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:13</span>）. This spiritual cleansing makes us part of the body of Christ and empowers us to live holy lives. The Holy Spirit’s role in baptism reminds us that salvation is not accomplished through rituals, but by God’s power to transform hearts.

<!-- p24 -->
It is important to distinguish the symbolic act of water baptism from the spiritual reality of being saved through the blood of Christ. The Bible clearly teaches that salvation is founded on faith in Jesus（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 10:9-10</span>）, not by works, including baptism（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>）. However, water baptism is an act of obedience that identifies the believer with Christ and with the church before others. It indicates an outward commitment to live as a disciple of Jesus, but it does not add to or complete the work of salvation.

<!-- p25 -->
Christ’s receiving water baptism set an example for believers, displaying humility, obedience, and readiness for service. For Christians today, water baptism is a public declaration of faith, symbolizing dying to sin and being raised to new life. Although it is commanded by Jesus and is an important step of obedience, it is not necessary for salvation. True cleansing happens through faith in the blood of Jesus, and the power for Christian living comes from the Holy Spirit’s work within believers. Baptism unites us with Christ and His church, reminding us of the inner transformation and renewal that God has accomplished.

<!-- p26 -->
Since it has been clarified that water baptism is the outward sign of faith, we now turn to the baptism of the Holy Spirit, which empowers believers for ministry and service.

<!-- p27 -->
## Baptism of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p28 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit is a profound and life-changing experience promised by God that empowers believers for ministry, service, and holy living. Every believer receives the Holy Spirit at regeneration - this is how we are made alive in the Spirit. But afterward, we should also receive the baptism of the Holy Spirit, so that we may receive boldness, spiritual gifts, and power for effective ministry.

<!-- p29 -->
Water baptism is the public confession of faith and symbolizes cleansing from sin through the blood of Christ, while spiritual baptism represents God’s empowering presence in the believer’s life. As two forms of baptism, together they constitute two complementary dimensions in the Christian journey - one is the outward sign of obedience, and the other is the source of inner power and renewal. The baptism of the Holy Spirit is the Spirit coming upon us, not in us. At salvation, all believers receive the indwelling Holy Spirit, but when the Spirit of God comes upon them, they receive power and boldness for service.

<!-- p30 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit was foretold by John the Baptist, who declared,

<!-- p31 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p32 -->
> **<u>Matthew 3:11</u>** (NKJV)
>
> I indeed baptize you with water unto repentance, but He who is coming after me is mightier than I, whose sandals I am not worthy to carry; He will baptize you with the Holy Spirit and fire.
>

<!-- p33 -->
</span>

<!-- p34 -->
This promise points to the greater work to be accomplished after Jesus’ resurrection - sending the Holy Spirit to indwell and empower His followers. Jesus also confirmed this, instructing the disciples to wait for the gift of the Holy Spirit, indicating that they would receive power to be His witnesses（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>）. The fulfillment of this promise took place on the Day of Pentecost, when the disciples were filled with the Holy Spirit, evidenced by speaking in tongues, prophesying, and boldly proclaiming the gospel（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:1-4</span>）.

<!-- p35 -->
This outpouring marked the beginning of the **Church Age**, and showed that the power of the Holy Spirit now comes upon all believers. This promise is not limited to the apostles, but extends to all who believe, as Peter said, *"This promise is for you and your children and for all who are far off-for all whom the Lord our God will call"*（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="39" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:39</span>）.

<!-- p36 -->
### Indwelling of the Holy Spirit vs Coming Upon of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p37 -->
When a person puts their faith in Jesus, the Holy Spirit imparts new life within them. This is what regeneration means. At that moment of faith, the Holy Spirit indwells the believer **within**, making them alive in the spirit and bringing them into God’s family（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:5-6</span>；<span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>）. This is the new birth, or being born again, that Jesus describes in John 3.

<!-- p38 -->
Scriptural basis:

<!-- p39 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>* - "He saved us, through the washing of regeneration and renewing of the Holy Spirit."

<!-- p40 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>* - The Holy Spirit seals believers as the **guarantee** of their inheritance.

<!-- p41 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:9</span>* - “If anyone does not have the Spirit of Christ, he does not belong to Him.”

<!-- p42 -->
This regenerating work is universal for all believers at salvation and establishes the permanent indwelling of the Holy Spirit. It does not depend on any external acts, such as water baptism, and ensures that people are saved by grace through faith.

<!-- p43 -->
By contrast, the baptism of the Holy Spirit is a distinct and subsequent experience that empowers believers for service and witness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>). Regeneration concerns new life and identity, whereas the baptism of the Holy Spirit focuses on **coming upon** power and the release of spiritual gifts. This baptism is often accompanied by signs and manifestations of the Spirit’s gifts.

<!-- p44 -->
Scriptural basis:

<!-- p45 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p46 -->
> **<u>Acts 1:8</u>** (NKJV)
>
> But when the Holy Spirit comes upon you, you will receive power; and you shall be My witnesses in Jerusalem, in all Judea and Samaria, and to the ends of the earth.”
>

<!-- p47 -->
</span>

<!-- p48 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:15-17</span>* - The Samaritans had already believed and been baptized, but they later received the Holy Spirit through prayer and the laying on of hands.

<!-- p49 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 19:6</span>* - The disciples in Ephesus, who were already believers, received the Holy Spirit when Paul laid hands on them.

<!-- p50 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit does not replace regeneration but builds upon it, equipping believers for spiritual service and giving them boldness in ministry. The baptism of the Holy Spirit is a free gift given to the saved and is not a requirement for salvation. It is not required that someone display a <u>specific</u> gift of the Spirit to prove their ‘baptism’ in the Spirit, but we should expect the baptism of the Holy Spirit to be manifested today as in Acts, accompanied by signs and boldness! Some mistakenly think that if a person does not speak in tongues, he is not saved or does not have the baptism of the Holy Spirit. Not everyone will receive all the gifts or all the manifestations of the Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29-31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:29-31</span>).

<!-- p51 -->
## How to receive the baptism of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p52 -->
The Bible describes the baptism of the Holy Spirit as a gift promised to believers and received by faith. It is distinct from salvation and is primarily for empowerment and witness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>). The baptism of the Holy Spirit is a gift of power given to believers, promised by Jesus and fulfilled at Pentecost. People receive it through repentance, faith, and asking God. Although the laying on of hands is often used as a means, ultimately it is God Himself who baptizes with the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p53 -->
The Bible presents speaking in tongues and prophesying as common signs, but the purpose of this baptism is broader- to provide boldness, power, and spiritual gifts for ministry. Every believer is encouraged to seek this experience as part of their spiritual journey, trusting that God will equip them to accomplish His work.

<!-- p54 -->
The Bible outlines several core principles about how to receive this baptism:

<!-- p55 -->
### **1. The baptism of the Holy Spirit is a free gift from God**

<!-- p56 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit is not something believers can earn-it is a free gift from God. God is willing to give good gifts to His children and is pleased to give the Holy Spirit to those who call upon Him with faith and a desire for empowerment.

<!-- p57 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p58 -->
> **<u>Luke 11:13</u>** (NKJV)
>
> If you then, being evil, know how to give good things to your children, how much more will your Father in heaven give the Holy Spirit to those who ask Him!”
>

<!-- p59 -->
</span>

<!-- p60 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="38-39" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p61 -->
> **<u>Acts 2:38-39</u>** (NKJV)
>

###  **2. Ask, seek, and receive by faith**

<!-- p64 -->
The Bible emphasizes that the baptism in the Holy Spirit is received by asking in prayer with faith. Faith, together with the willingness to ask and to receive, is the key to experiencing the baptism in the Holy Spirit. If you’re not sure whether God wants to give you the baptism in the Holy Spirit, just ask Him—He is a good Father! When He prompts you, you may feel in your body a tangible presence of God that you’ve never experienced before. And it’s okay if you don’t! God is a great God; He doesn’t need to be boxed into some perfect formula. If you sense God drawing your heart, surrender to Him!

## The Gifts of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p79 -->
When the Holy Spirit is poured out and people are baptized in the Holy Spirit, it is often accompanied by manifestations of the Spirit.

<!-- p80 -->
There are several lists of gifts in the New Testament：

<!-- p81 -->
- The motivational gifts given by God：<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:6-8</span>

<!-- p82 -->
- Discussed in the section on the Church。

<!-- p83 -->
- The fivefold ministry gifts given by Jesus：<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-13</span>

<!-- p84 -->
- Discussed in the section on the Church。

<!-- p85 -->
- The manifestation gifts given by the Holy Spirit：<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span>

<!-- p86 -->
- Discussed here。

<!-- p87 -->
Each group of these gifts has a different purpose and operates differently, yet together they point to the **diversity** and **unity** of gifts within the **body of Christ**。Each gift has a unique role in accomplishing God’s will。

<!-- p88 -->
### Purpose &amp; Operation of the Gifts

<!-- p89 -->
The primary purpose of the gifts of the Holy Spirit is to build up the body of Christ and give glory to God：

<!-- p90 -->
- **Edification** - to strengthen and encourage believers（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>）。

<!-- p91 -->
- **Unity** - to promote harmony and cooperation in the Church（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:12-13</span>）。

<!-- p92 -->
- **Evangelism** - to testify to unbelievers with God’s power（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="43" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:43</span>；<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:6-7</span>）。

<!-- p93 -->
- **Service** - to meet others’ needs with acts of mercy and generosity（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:6-8</span>）。

<!-- p94 -->
These gifts are not for personal glory but for the common good（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:7</span>）。When exercised in love and humility, they are most effective, reflecting the character of Christ（1 Corinthians 13）。

<!-- p95 -->
Paul emphasizes that spiritual gifts must be exercised appropriately and in order（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:40</span>）。This ensures that the use of gifts promotes unity, peace, and understanding, rather than causing confusion or division。

<!-- p96 -->
- Prophecy should be tested（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:29</span>）。

<!-- p97 -->
- When speaking a message in tongues to the congregation in the Church, pray that it may be interpreted（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:27-28</span>）。

<!-- p98 -->
- Gifts must always bring **edification** to the Church（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:12</span>）。

<!-- p99 -->
Such order does not quench the Holy Spirit but rather ensures that the gifts operate in a way that **honors God** and **benefits others**。

<!-- p100 -->
The exercise of spiritual gifts must always be rooted in love. Paul devotes an entire chapter (1 Corinthians 13) to explain that without love, even the greatest spiritual gifts are meaningless. Love provides the motivation and framework for using gifts to build others up rather than tear them down.

<!-- p101 -->
Furthermore, these gifts operate **according to the will of the Holy Spirit** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:11</span>). They cannot be manufactured or controlled by human effort- they are sovereignly given and empowered by the Holy Spirit, often occurring at moments of need or ministry. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 13:8</span> says that ultimately prophecy and tongues will cease. Theologically, those who believe the gifts of the Holy Spirit have ceased are called ‘cessationists’. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 13:10</span> says “When the perfect \[Jesus\] comes, the partial will pass away”. Since Jesus has not yet returned for His church, these gifts are still at work on earth.

<!-- p102 -->
It is crucial to recognize that the **gifts of the Holy Spirit** should not be separated from the **fruit of the Holy Spirit** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-23</span>). While the gifts manifest power, the fruit reveals character—love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control.

<!-- p103 -->
- **Gifts without fruit**: A believer may operate in gifts yet lack maturity or character, leading to pride, misuse, or harm.

<!-- p104 -->
- **Fruit without gifts**: A believer may have godly character yet lack the empowerment needed for effective ministry.

<!-- p105 -->
Both are necessary. The gifts equip believers to serve. The fruit is a sign of their spiritual health (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 7:16</span>) and bring a ‘pleasant taste’ to those who taste it.

<!-- p106 -->
### Explaining the 9 manifestation gifts of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p107 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span> lists the 9 manifestation gifts of the Holy Spirit:

<!-- p108 -->
1.  **Word of wisdom** - Supernatural insight into God’s will or direction for a situation. It applies divine wisdom to complex problems or decisions (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:10</span>).

<!-- p109 -->
2.  **Word of knowledge** - Supernatural revelation about a person, event, or situation—information that could not be known naturally (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 4:17-18</span>).

<!-- p110 -->
3.  **Faith** - The supernatural ability to trust God for miracles in impossible situations. This gift can inspire courage and confidence (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 3:6-7</span>).

<!-- p111 -->
4.  **Gifts of healings** - Healing physical, emotional, or spiritual illnesses by supernatural power, restoring wholeness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 3:6-8</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 9:17-18</span>).

<!-- p112 -->
5.  **Working of miracles** - The ability to perform acts that transcend natural laws, such as multiplying food or raising the dead (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40-41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 9:40-41</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 2:9-11</span>).

<!-- p113 -->
6.  **Prophecy** - Declaring God’s truth or will under the prompting of the Holy Spirit. Its purpose is to encourage, edify, and comfort (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>).

<!-- p114 -->
7.  **Discerning of spirits (discernment)** - The ability to discern whether a spiritual influence is from God, the enemy, or human (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 16:16-18</span>).

<!-- p115 -->
8.  **Various kinds of tongues** - Speaking languages unknown to the speaker, often as worship, prayer, or as a sign to unbelievers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:4</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:22</span>).

<!-- p116 -->
9.  **Interpretation of tongues** - The supernatural ability to interpret a message in tongues so that the church may be edified (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:27</span>).

<!-- p117 -->
Paul clearly points out that all the gifts are valuable and should work together for the common good of the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:7</span>). However, he also encourages believers to desire the greater gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:31</span>). The greater gifts are commonly understood as those that most directly edify the church, such as prophecy (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:1-5</span>).

<!-- p118 -->
- Special emphasis is placed on prophecy because it can strengthen, encourage, and comfort others (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>). Paul instructs believers to earnestly desire this gift, as it fosters clarity and understanding in the church.

<!-- p119 -->
- Gifts like tongues are extremely valuable for personal edification and prayer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:4</span>), but Paul teaches that when a message in tongues is given to the church one should pray for interpretation to ensure the church is edified (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:27-28</span>).

<!-- p120 -->
Although the Holy Spirit gives these gifts, we must submit and entrust ourselves to the Holy Spirit, allowing Him to use them. Like muscles and talents, as we practice obeying the Holy Spirit and let Him use His gifts, we will become more confident and more reliable in using them.
