# Chapter 10 - Sanctification-Prepared for God's Purposes

<!-- p1 -->
In the previous two chapters we discussed several important themes. We explored the role and ministry of the Holy Spirit, as well as the role and ministry of the Church.

<!-- p2 -->
We each have a part in the Church; we know training is needed, and cleansing is needed. To be truly prepared for God's purposes, we must be sanctified.

<!-- p3 -->
The definition of **sanctification** is to make something clean for a special purpose. This is closely related to the term **consecration**, which means to set something apart exclusively for special use.

<!-- p4 -->
Imagine a master sculptor preparing to carve a large block of marble. The master envisions what this raw, unshaped marble can be transformed into through his meticulous craftsmanship. He has a very specific design in mind and instructs his assistant to set this marble apart for that special design (**consecration**). As he refines and polishes the marble, adding texture, depth, and shape, he makes it clean for that special design (**sanctification**). The act of dedicating the marble to his special project is quick, but sculpting it into the final design takes considerable patience.

<!-- p5 -->
When we are saved, we are set apart, set apart unto God. Through the help of the Holy Spirit to cleanse our soul, we are transformed. This cleansing process is what sanctifies us.

<!-- p6 -->
When we receive Jesus through salvation, we are born again. We are justified, and our sins are forgiven. Our spirit is renewed and made alive in Christ. We are adopted into God's family, becoming a true temple of the Holy Spirit. Of such a marvelous transformation, the Scripture says: “*There is joy in the presence of the angels of God over one sinner who repents.*” (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 15:10</span>).

<!-- p7 -->
Receiving Jesus through salvation (**justification**) saves our spirit and cleanses us, enabling us to stand before God and receive His Holy Spirit. However, despite such a great salvation, **our soul** (our mind, will, and emotions) still **needs work**!

<!-- p8 -->
Is the work of salvation incomplete? No! The work of salvation is effective, but it must continue to be applied in the life of the believer through the renewal brought by the Holy Spirit. This process of renewal and transformation is the work of **sanctification**.

<!-- p9 -->
We know there is ongoing work to do after conversion because our painful memories remain unresolved, many beliefs stay the same, and many habits or rituals have not disappeared. Moreover, conversion has not delivered our mind from its carnal nature (carnal).

<!-- p10 -->
‘carnal’ comes from a Latin root meaning ‘flesh’ or ‘meat’. This carnal nature has been in us since birth, like animal instincts, existing to help us survive. The way the carnal nature operates is self-focused, self-directed, and self-centered. Children are a natural example of a carnal nature. Though they are innocent, their nature is primarily concerned with their own wants, desires, and needs; and if you don’t meet its every expectation, then ‘watch out!’

<!-- p11 -->
Part of growing up is maturing out of childish ways, but once under pressure, we easily revert to self-centered patterns of thinking and living. As James said:

<!-- p12 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p13 -->
> **<u>James 4:1</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Where do wars and fights come from among you? Do they not come from your desires for pleasure that war in your members?
>

<!-- p14 -->
</span>

<!-- p15 -->
Paul explains this carnal nature in more detail in his letter to the Romans (chapters 6 - 8). He says this carnal nature makes him do what he hates and causes him to do evil. He describes this inner conflict as a war between his human nature and the spirit God created.

<!-- p16 -->
This conflict between the flesh and the spirit exists within each of us, and we are responsible to deal with it. If we do not confront our carnal nature, even if our spirit has been redeemed, our soul will remain entangled in patterns of spiritual decay.

<!-- p17 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p18 -->
> **<u>Romans 8:6-8</u>** (NKJV)
>
> For to be carnally minded is death; but to be spiritually minded is life and peace.
>
> Because the carnal mind is enmity against God; for it is not subject to the law of God, nor indeed can be.
>
> So then, those who are in the flesh cannot please God.
>

<!-- p19 -->
</span>

<!-- p20 -->
Paul’s words here are both a warning and a promise to us. Although we have been born again, we still have fleshly minds that need to be brought into submission to the knowledge of Christ. When we do this, we walk in God’s life and peace.

<!-- p21 -->
Take heart, we have hope! We must walk according to the Spirit and be led by the Spirit of God. Doing so, we will not walk according to the flesh.

<!-- p22 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="1,13,14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p23 -->
> **<u>Romans 8:1, 13, 14</u>** (NKJV)
>
> There is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus, who do not walk according to the flesh, but according to the Spirit.
>
> For if you live according to the flesh you will die; but if by the Spirit you put to death the deeds of the body, you will live.
>
> For as many as are led by the Spirit of God, these are sons of God.
>

<!-- p24 -->
</span>

<!-- p25 -->
How do we learn to walk according to the Spirit? Is it merely *a conscious choice*? We learn to walk in the Spirit the same way we learned to walk in the natural. We are motivated to move and grow, so we struggle, strive, crawl, and totter until we can stand on our own. Our natural parents cannot walk for us, but they can support, encourage, help, and equip us as we learn to walk.

<!-- p26 -->
Similarly, God cannot live out our faith for us. He will teach us, but we must make the effort. As Paul said to the Philippians:

<!-- p27 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="12-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p28 -->
> **<u>Philippians 2:12-13</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Therefore, my beloved, as you have always obeyed, not as in my presence only, but now much more in my absence, work out your own salvation with fear and trembling;
>
> for it is God who works in you both to will and to do for His good pleasure.
>

<!-- p29 -->
</span>

<!-- p30 -->
We must work out our own salvation. This does not mean we climb onto a wooden cross and are nailed to it physically, but we do crucify the fleshly nature (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:24</span>). God promises that He will work in us and help us, but we must choose to walk this path out, and we must persevere.

<!-- p31 -->
It is right to say that “we” are dealing with our fleshly nature, but it is also right to say that “God” is dealing with it. The work of the cross (justification) was hard for Jesus—He had to sacrifice His life for us—but it is easy for us; it is a free gift. The work of the Spirit (sanctification) is easy for God—He walks in perfect truth and is willing and eager to dwell with us—but it is hard for us, because we must crucify the old nature, and our nature is not very willing to be crucified.

<!-- p32 -->
This process is cooperative. God helps, assists, guides, instructs, and corrects. We listen, obey, follow, and respond. The result is growth and change. This is the pruning Jesus said God would do in us. Jesus is the vine, His Father is the vinedresser, and we are the branches:

<!-- p33 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p34 -->
> **<u>John 15:1-4</u>** (NKJV)
>
> "I am the true vine, and My Father is the vinedresser.
>
> Every branch in Me that does not bear fruit He takes away; and every branch that bears fruit He prunes, that it may bear more fruit.
>
> You are already clean because of the word which I have spoken to you.
>
> Abide in Me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself, unless it abides in the vine, neither can you, unless you abide in Me.
>

<!-- p35 -->
</span>

<!-- p36 -->
Pruning is very beneficial for plants. Recall our chapter on repentance: we are called to turn from dead works (acts that no longer bear fruit) and turn to God. This turning away from the dead places in our lives and turning to God allows the great Gardener of our lives to prune the branches, improve growth, and bear more spiritual fruit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:2</span>).

<!-- p37 -->
Though pruning may feel painful, it reflects God’s loving care as He shapes us into vessels fit for His use (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 12:11</span>). Some may feel a sense of loss, or even fear, during seasons of pruning. However, God is gentle; a bruised reed He will not break (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="42" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 42:3</span>). Vines are pruned during the dormant season to minimize stress on the plant. A gardener who tends growth cuts away parts that have hardened into wood and no longer bear fruit as they once did, enabling the vine to direct its energy toward the shoots most likely to produce good fruit.

<!-- p38 -->
Just as a gardener guides growth by pruning branches, God may allow seasons of correction and discipline to refine our faith and character, yet His work is patient and loving. God prunes those who abide in Him. Our focus should be more on abiding in Christ and less on the pruning itself.

<!-- p39 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 12:11</span> reminds us that pruning "*yields the peaceable fruit of righteousness*" for those who have been trained by it.

<!-- p40 -->
How are we trained in righteousness? By being transformed through the renewing of the mind.

<!-- p41 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p42 -->
> **<u>Romans 12:1-2</u>** (NKJV)
>

> Do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind, that you may prove what

In the next section, I will describe each memory system, their purposes and functions, some practical steps for renewing this aspect of your mind, and resources for ongoing practice and learning.

<!-- p61 -->
### **Memory Systems and How to Renew Them**

<!-- p62 -->
#### **1. Explicit Memory (Declarative Memory)**

<!-- p63 -->
**Purpose：** Stores facts, events, and experiences we can consciously recall. It is divided into two types-**semantic** and **episodic** memory.

<!-- p64 -->
**Semantic Memory - Facts and Knowledge.** Semantic memory is where we store facts, definitions, and general knowledge- such as historical dates, Bible verses, and practical information. It helps us predict outcomes, make decisions, and avoid danger（for example, identifying poisonous plants）. While it gives us stability and order, it can also lead to rigidity- over-relying on rules instead of adapting to unexpected situations.

<!-- p65 -->
**How to renew it：**

<!-- p66 -->
- **Study the Bible** to shape our worldview (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="119" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="105" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 119:105</span>).

<!-- p67 -->
- **Memorize truth** and doctrine to stand firm in the faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 3:16</span>).

<!-- p68 -->
- **Read Christian books** such as *"Renewing the Mind" by Casey Treat* or *"Battlefield of the Mind" by Joyce Meyer* to cultivate godly patterns of thinking.

<!-- p69 -->
**Episodic Memory - Personal Experiences.** Episodic memory stores our personal experiences and links them to specific times and places- like a wedding day or a traumatic event. These memories provide context, help us learn from the past, and build relationships. However, episodic memory is susceptible to emotional bias and distortion. Past trauma can create fearful associations, making it difficult to move forward.

<!-- p70 -->
**How to renew it：**

<!-- p71 -->
- **Examine and confess**, bringing hidden pain into God’s light (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 1:9</span>).

<!-- p72 -->
- **Reinterpret past experiences** through the lens of God’s grace (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:28</span>).

<!-- p73 -->
- **Seek inner healing** through prayer and counseling (*“Your Wounds Are Showing” by Larry F. Low, "The Bondage Breaker" by Neil T. Anderson*, *"Emotionally Healthy Spirituality" by Peter Scazzero*).

<!-- p74 -->
#### **2. Implicit Memory (Nondeclarative Memory)**

<!-- p75 -->
**Purpose：** Unconscious memory used for habits, skills, and emotional associations. It operates automatically, helping us respond quickly to situations. It is divided into **procedural memory** and **emotional conditioning**.

<!-- p76 -->
**Procedural Memory - Skills and Habits.** Procedural memory allows us to perform tasks without thinking- such as driving, typing, or playing an instrument. It is efficient, saving our mental energy and time. However, bad habits and negative patterns can also become deeply ingrained and hard to break.

<!-- p77 -->
**How to renew it：**

<!-- p78 -->
- **Practice spiritual disciplines** such as prayer and fasting to build godly habits (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 5:14</span>).

<!-- p79 -->
- **Train for godliness** through repetition and obedience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 4:7</span>).

<!-- p80 -->
- **Read works such as** *"Atomic Habits" by James Clear*（practical）and *"Spiritual Disciplines for the Christian Life" by Donald Whitney*（biblical），to build habits that honor God。

<!-- p81 -->
**Emotional Conditioning - Emotional Associations.** Emotional conditioning associates sensory cues（such as smells or sounds）with emotions，triggering reactions before our conscious processing。This system protects us，but when past experiences are negative，it can also lead to irrational fears or emotional reactions。

<!-- p82 -->
**How to renew：**

<!-- p83 -->
- **Pray for healing for past wounds**（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="41" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 41:10</span>）。

<!-- p84 -->
- **Practice forgiveness**，releasing bitterness（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-32" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:31-32</span>）。

<!-- p85 -->
- **Replace fear with God’s promises**（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 1:7</span>）。

<!-- p86 -->
- **Seek guidance from the following books** *“Your Pain Is Showing” by Larry F. Low, "The Bondage Breaker" by Neil T. Anderson*, “*Soul Care" by Rob Reimer*，to overcome emotional triggers。

<!-- p87 -->
#### **3. Working Memory（Short-Term Memory）**

<!-- p88 -->
**Purpose：** Provides temporary storage for problem-solving and decision-making。It helps us focus，briefly hold information，and process tasks——such as remembering instructions or making quick decisions。While powerful，it is limited and vulnerable to distraction，and is especially prone to overload under stress。

<!-- p89 -->
**How to renew：**

<!-- p90 -->
- **Focus on and meditate on** God’s Word daily（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 1:2</span>）。

<!-- p91 -->
- **Renew your mind** through intentional thinking（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:2</span>）。

<!-- p92 -->
- **Practice mindfulness** and simplify mental clutter（*"Habits of Grace" by David Mathis*）。

<!-- p93 -->
- **Read** *"Celebration of Discipline" by Richard Foster*，to enhance focus through spiritual disciplines。

<!-- p94 -->
## Conclusion

<!-- p95 -->
Renewing the mind is not a quick fix——it is a lifelong, gradual process of transformation。Each memory system plays a role in shaping our thoughts, emotions, and behaviors。By consciously partnering with the Holy Spirit, applying Scripture, and cultivating godly habits, we can train our minds to align with God’s truth。Whether addressing past wounds, learning new habits, or staying focused in the present，God has given us **spiritual tools** and **practical wisdom** to help us walk in renewal and freedom（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 10:5</span>）。

<!-- p96 -->
Our world is filled with hardship, affliction, and trials。Through disciplined mind renewal, our inner life is transformed, and we can live a Godward, free life full of the fruit of His Spirit（<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-25</span>）。
