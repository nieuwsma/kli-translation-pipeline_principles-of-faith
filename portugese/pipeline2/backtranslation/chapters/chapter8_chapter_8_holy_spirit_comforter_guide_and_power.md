# Chapter 8 - Holy Spirit — Comforter, Guide, and Power

<!-- p1 -->
The Holy Spirit is not a new spirit, but is of God and has been with God from the beginning. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 1:2</span>, the Spirit of God hovers over the waters during creation. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="NEH" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Nehemiah 9:20</span>, the Scriptures say that it was the Spirit who instructed the people in the wilderness. It was by this same Holy Spirit that God anointed the kings, judges, and prophets in the Old Testament. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JOL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Joel 2:28-29</span>, there is the promise that the Spirit would be poured out on all humanity. It was by this same Holy Spirit that God raised Jesus from the dead, and now He dwells in us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:11</span>). There is only one Holy Spirit, and He has always been the same. The way He works with people has seemed different in different times, but His heart has never changed.

<!-- p2 -->
Jesus gave His disciples, and all of us, the powerful promise of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p3 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p4 -->
> **<u>John 14:16-18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And I will ask the Father, and He will give you another Comforter, that He may remain with you forever—
>
> the Spirit of truth, whom the world cannot receive, because it neither sees Him nor knows Him; but you know Him, because He dwells with you and will be in you.
>
> I will not leave you orphans; I will come to you.
>

<!-- p5 -->
</span>

<!-- p6 -->
While Jesus walked the earth in His physical body, He was limited like us; His body needed the same things our bodies need: rest, water, food, sleep, etc. This limitation not only affected Jesus, but also His followers, for He could only be in one place at a time. Often, as Jesus traveled, He was surrounded by a large crowd; they would try to cry out to Him and touch Him. Jesus often needed to spend time with His heavenly Father to recharge and pray.

<!-- p7 -->
For the Father's work to be multiplied, Jesus needed to raise up disciples and for them to carry on that work. Jesus knew that a crucial aspect of His ministry on earth depended on His connecting with and remaining in His Father. In fact, Jesus said that He could do nothing unless His Father did it through Him and showed Him what to do (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 5:19</span>). God, in His wisdom, sent His Holy Spirit to dwell in us, to lead and guide us, empowering us to carry out the Father's work.

<!-- p8 -->
## When was the Holy Spirit sent to all believers?

<!-- p9 -->
Jesus said:

<!-- p10 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p11 -->
> **<u>John 16:7</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Nevertheless, I tell you the truth: it is to your advantage that I go away; for if I do not go away, the Comforter will not come to you; but if I go, I will send Him to you.
>

<!-- p12 -->
</span>

<!-- p13 -->
After Jesus was crucified, His death made it possible for the Spirit of God to come to dwell within us. Until that time, the tangible Spirit of God was largely contained behind a thick veil in the temple. From time to time, individuals chosen by God were anointed (consecrated and set apart for a unique service), and received the Holy Spirit upon them. The anointing of God, and the Spirit upon them, was special, temporary, and conditional. David prayed, after his sin with Bathsheba: ‘*Do not take Your Holy Spirit from me!*’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="51" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 51:11</span>), because David had seen the Spirit withdraw from King Saul for his acts of disobedience.

<!-- p14 -->
We (humanity) could not receive the Holy Spirit within us because of our sinful nature, but when Jesus dealt with the problem of sin through His sacrifice, a way was opened, by faith, for us to become children and heirs of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:17</span>).

<!-- p15 -->
In John 20, after His resurrection, Jesus appears to His disciples in the middle of a locked room and breathes on them, saying ‘*Receive the Holy Spirit*’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 20:22</span>). Theology is somewhat debated as to what exactly occurred at that moment, but most Pentecostal interpretations see this as the disciples receiving the Spirit of God within them, signifying the new birth, being born again. However, this was not when they received the Spirit of God upon them, empowering them with boldness for ministry. It was when they received the Holy Spirit within them, enabling them to remain and commune with God. This will be discussed in greater depth later in this chapter, as well as in the chapters on sanctification and baptism.

<!-- p16 -->
Later, at the conclusion of the 40-day period between the resurrection and the ascension (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:3</span>), Jesus gives the promise of the Holy Spirit:

<!-- p17 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="4-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p18 -->
> **<u>Acts 1:4-9</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And being assembled together with them, He commanded them not to depart from Jerusalem, but to wait for the Promise of the Father, "which," He said, "you heard from Me;
>
> for John truly baptized with water, but you shall be baptized with the Holy Spirit not many days from now."
>
> Therefore, when they had come together, they asked Him, saying: "Lord, will You at this time restore the kingdom to Israel?"
>
> And He said to them: "It is not for you to know the times or seasons which the Father has set by His own authority.
>
> But you shall receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you; and you shall be My witnesses in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the ends of the earth."
>
> Now when He had spoken these things, while they watched, He was taken up, and a cloud received Him out of their sight.
>

<!-- p19 -->
</span>

<!-- p20 -->
The disciples were to wait for the promise of the outpoured Spirit, and that this would enable them to be witnesses to the ends of the earth. The Holy Spirit is an integral part of God’s plan for humanity, and is essential for us, as believers, to complete our mission, and for the world.

<!-- p21 -->
## Knowing the Holy Spirit

<!-- p22 -->
The Holy Spirit is, first and foremost, of God and proceeding from God. The Scriptures refer to the Spirit as the Spirit of God, or the Spirit of the Lord, or the Spirit of Christ; although these highlight different aspects, all are one and the same. The Holy Spirit, being fully God, is also eternal and is holy (blameless, innocent, pure, not profane). Some translations refer to the Holy Spirit as the Holy Ghost.

<!-- p23 -->
The word spirit, in Greek, is **Pneuma** (**NYOO-mah** - **G4151**), meaning breath or wind. The Hebrew word for spirit means the same. Breath is significant, for we need it in constant supply to live. It was the breath of God that He breathed into Adam to bring him to life; likewise, it was the breath of Jesus that He exhaled last before dying on the cross. Without breath there is no life. Breath, like the wind, can be felt and experienced, but it is not as easily observed as other ‘elements’ water, fire, earth.

<!-- p24 -->
There are several other fundamental adjectives to describe the Holy Spirit. Each of these adjectives is used in conjunction with ‘the Spirit of’, meaning that these properties are vital to the Spirit’s definition. Example, the *Spirit of Truth* or the *Spirit of Life*.

<!-- p25 -->
The Spirit of:

<!-- p26 -->
- **Truth** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:17</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span> Refers to the role of the Spirit in guiding believers into all truth, revealing the will of God and the reality of Christ.

<!-- p27 -->
- **Life** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:2</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:8</span> Highlights the role of the Spirit in imparting spiritual and eternal life to believers, freeing them from the law of sin and death.

<!-- p28 -->
- **Grace** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:29</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ZEC" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Zechariah 12:10</span> Reflects the Spirit’s work in dispensing God’s favor, forgiveness, and unmerited kindness to humanity.

<!-- p29 -->
- **Glory** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 4:14</span> Indicates the role of the Spirit in revealing and manifesting the glory of God, particularly in times of suffering and persecution.

<!-- p30 -->
- **Adoption** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:15</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 4:5</span> Refers to the Spirit’s work in making believers children of God, granting them the status of sons and daughters.

<!-- p31 -->
- **Holiness** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 1:4</span> Points to the sanctifying work of the Spirit, setting believers apart for God’s purposes.

<!-- p32 -->
- **Power** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 1:7</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span> Describes the empowering that the Spirit gives believers for boldness, ministry, and the ability to live out their faith effectively.

<!-- p33 -->
In all these attributes, the Holy Spirit is also properly recognized as the Helper or Comforter. Jesus refers to the Holy Spirit as the helper who would come, He uses the Greek word ***Paraklētos** (*pah-RAH-klay-tos - **G3875**), which means advocate, helper, called to be alongside.

<!-- p34 -->
An advocate speaks on behalf of another person, like a defense attorney. He intercedes and helps the person entrusted to him.

<!-- p35 -->
The helper helps. He helps to empower the work, but his function is primarily to assist the work. The helper is not solely responsible for carrying out the work, but comes to stand alongside in order to help accomplish it.

<!-- p36 -->
The Holy Spirit has various purposes and distinct roles in His ministry. His work is inward (in the hearts and lives of believers), upward (in service and acknowledgment of God) and outward (toward our mission field, the world).

<!-- p37 -->
## Roles of the Holy Spirit-Inward, upward, outward

<!-- p38 -->
### Inward-The Holy Spirit unites us to Jesus

<!-- p39 -->
Before Jesus performed any miracle or began His public ministry, He was baptized by John the Baptist and the Spirit of God descended upon him, and a voice from heaven proclaimed: ‘This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:17</span>).

<!-- p40 -->
When we accept Jesus by faith, we receive salvation and the seal of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p41 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p42 -->
> **<u>Ephesians 1:13-14</u>** (NKJV)
>
> In Him you also trusted, after you heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation; and, having also believed in Him, you were sealed with the Holy Spirit of promise,
>
> who is the guarantee of our inheritance, until the redemption of the purchased possession, to the praise of His glory.
>

<!-- p43 -->
</span>

<!-- p44 -->
This seal is not metaphorical, but literal. It serves as a sign of our redemption and is an advance on the full inheritance that we receive as children of God. Romans 8 describes that, through the new birth, we receive in our mortal bodies the very same Spirit of God who raised Christ from the dead, and we become the literal temple of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 6:16</span>).

<!-- p45 -->
This receiving of the Spirit of God transforms us and causes rivers of living water to flow from us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 7:38</span>). This renewal is immediate and complete, but it continues throughout our entire lives. The river of the Holy Spirit must never stop flowing in our lives!

<!-- p46 -->
Through salvation and the indwelling of the Holy Spirit, we can abide in Christ and He in us. The Spirit of God is no longer on the outside, but can dwell deeply within us. We can walk in fellowship and harmony with God from now into eternal life.

<!-- p47 -->
There is much more to say about the inner work of the Holy Spirit and His role in our sanctification, but that will be discussed in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p48 -->
### Upward - The Holy Spirit Testifies of Jesus

<!-- p49 -->
The Holy Spirit comes from God and is of God. Just as Jesus said He did nothing unless the Father showed Him (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 5:19</span>), the Holy Spirit also does nothing unless He is directed by the Father. Because the Holy Spirit is the Helper, He does not act on His own, but works in perfect harmony with the Father.

<!-- p50 -->
The Spirit testifies of Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:26</span>), glorifies Jesus, and announces and declares what Jesus has for us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:14</span>).

<!-- p51 -->
The Holy Spirit will testify of Jesus, will glorify Him, will teach and bring to our remembrance the teaching of Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:26</span>), and will guide us into all truth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span>). Jesus told His disciples that He had much more to teach them, but that they could not bear it yet, and that the Holy Spirit would guide them into all truth. This is another subtle point in theology. These teachings that Jesus needed His disciples to know were communicated in several ways. Jesus taught them personally both before His crucifixion and after the resurrection and before His ascension.

<!-- p52 -->
Jesus also promised that the Spirit of Truth, who was poured out upon them on the day of Pentecost, would lead them into all truth through divine revelation. Is there still divine revelation today? **Certainly yes!** The gifts of the Holy Spirit, including prophecy and words of knowledge and of wisdom, have not ceased! However, no prophecy or word coming from a spirit is of private interpretation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2PE" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Peter 1:20</span>). This is crucial. Some people, mistakenly, will claim that they are hearing the Holy Spirit, but their actions and words do not testify of Jesus. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 4:3</span> warns against any spirit that does not testify of Jesus, saying that such a spirit is of the antichrist. It bears repeating: if any spirit departs from the teaching of Christ, it is not the Holy Spirit but a profane spirit!

<!-- p53 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p54 -->
> **<u>1 John 4:1</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Beloved, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits, whether they are of God; because many false prophets have gone out into the world.
>

<!-- p55 -->
</span>

<!-- p56 -->
Throughout the Scriptures, we see examples of statements made in error, often by false prophets or by well-intentioned people who spoke contrary to the will of God. For example, Jeremiah warned against prophets who spoke visions from their own minds, proclaiming peace while destruction was approaching (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="23" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 23:16-17</span>). Similarly, in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 16:16-18</span>, the words of a young slave girl about Paul and Silas were correct, but inspired by a demonic spirit, not by the Holy Spirit. These examples remind us that even words that seem true can originate from ungodly sources.

<!-- p57 -->
Every claim of ‘God told me!’ or a ‘new’ teaching must be measured by the biblical tests of truth. To discern rightly, we must ask:

<!-- p58 -->
- Does this align with the Scriptures?

<!-- p59 -->
- Does this exalt Jesus Christ?

<!-- p60 -->
- Does this produce godly fruit?

<!-- p61 -->
By applying these tests, we can distinguish the true guidance of the Holy Spirit from errors or deception.

<!-- p62 -->
God has the habit of providing us with ample &amp; abundant wisdom and guidance (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:5</span> &amp; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="33" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 33:3</span>). God's divine wisdom will not violate the Scriptures, but will go beyond the scope of the Scriptures. His *entolē* *(*commandments*)* are immediate prescriptions to be obeyed; they are *rhema* words (as discussed in the chapter on faith).

<!-- p63 -->
This wisdom, understanding, and guidance are likely not word-for-word in the Scriptures. As an example, the Scriptures give great general direction about what to look for in a spouse, but do not give specific prescriptions about the exact person. God promises to direct our path, to lead us into all truth, and we can take comfort knowing that this wisdom and guidance of the Holy Spirit will never violate any teaching of Scripture.

<!-- p64 -->
### Outward-The Holy Spirit Empowers the Mission

<!-- p65 -->
Jesus gave us His mission, the Great Commission:

<!-- p66 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p67 -->
> **<u>Matthew 28:18-20</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And Jesus came and spoke to them, saying: "All authority has been given to Me in heaven and on earth.
>
> Therefore go and make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit,
>
> teaching them to observe all things that I have commanded you; and lo, I am with you always, even to the end of the age." Amen.
>

<!-- p68 -->
</span>

<!-- p69 -->
The Holy Spirit has an integral part in the mission, convicting the world of sin, righteousness, and judgment:

<!-- p70 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="8-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p71 -->
> **<u>John 16:8-11</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And when He has come, He will convict the world of sin, and of righteousness, and of judgment:
>
> of sin, because they do not believe in Me;
>
> of righteousness, because I go to My Father and you will see Me no more;
>
> of judgment, because the ruler of this world is judged.
>

<!-- p72 -->
</span>

<!-- p73 -->
Delving deeper, the Spirit brings conviction of these three truths:

<!-- p74 -->
1.  **Conviction of sin**: Revealing the reality of human rebellion against God, drawing people's attention to the depravity of their own lives.

<!-- p75 -->
2.  **Conviction of righteousness**: Demonstrating Jesus' perfect fulfillment of the Father's will, and that Jesus' sacrifice provided the unique atonement necessary for the forgiveness of sins.

<!-- p76 -->
3.  **Conviction of judgment**: Affirming the defeat of Satan, the certainty of God's justice, and that all will stand before a judgment for their works and deeds of life.

<!-- p77 -->
The Holy Spirit also empowers the mission. The Holy Spirit gives believers the power to be bold witnesses in the face of persecution to share the gospel. Boldness does not mean loudness. It means having courage despite fear. Power is strength, vigor, and ability. The Holy Spirit gives the strength and ability to be a witness of the Gospel despite threats and the fear of punishment.

<!-- p78 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p79 -->
> **<u>Acts 1:8</u>** (NKJV)
>
> But you shall receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you; and you shall be My witnesses in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the ends of the earth."

<!-- p80 -->
</span>

<!-- p81 -->
The entire book of Acts is filled with stories and accounts that follow this general cycle:

<!-- p82 -->
1.  **Prayer**: Believers ask for boldness and God's intervention.

<!-- p83 -->
2.  **Empowerment**: The Holy Spirit fills them with courage, power, and direction.

<!-- p84 -->
3.  **Proclamation**: They proclaim the gospel with boldness, often with signs, wonders, and miracles.

<!-- p85 -->
4.  **Opposition**: Resistance arises, resulting in persecution or challenges. Sometimes believers were martyred, other times imprisoned and/or beaten.

<!-- p86 -->
5.  **Renewal**: The church returns to prayer, seeking new empowerment, and the cycle continues.

<!-- p87 -->
The New Testament church grew largely in the face of determined opposition. Around the world, the church suffers violence at the hands of oppressors and wicked rulers, determined to suppress the knowledge of the truth of Jesus.

<!-- p88 -->
God knew that this opposition would occur; Jesus prophesied about it:

<!-- p89 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p90 -->
> **<u>Luke 12:11-12</u>** (NKJV)
>
> "Now when they bring you to the synagogues and magistrates and authorities, do not worry about how or what you should answer, or what you should say.
>
> For the Holy Spirit will teach you in that very hour what you ought to say."
>

<!-- p91 -->
</span>

<!-- p92 -->
Do not fear! Jesus promised us that:

<!-- p93 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p94 -->
> **<u>Matthew 16:18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And I also say to you that you are Peter, and on this rock I will build my church, and the gates of Hades shall not prevail against it.
>

<!-- p95 -->
</span>

<!-- p96 -->
This is an incredible and comforting promise. Gates are fixed defensive armaments. When Jesus says that the *gates of hell will not prevail*, He is painting the picture of a conquering army that knocks down the gates and captures the city!

<!-- p97 -->
The Holy Spirit brings supernatural boldness to proclaim the testimony of the Gospel in the face of persecution. The result of this empowerment can be described, but is better experienced. It is a supernatural boldness (greater than human) that motivates believers to share. This boldness in preaching is often accompanied by supernatural acts of demonstration of the Spirit and of power (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 2:1-5</span>). People are healed physically and emotionally, the dead are raised, those tormented by evil spirits are set free, people with hard hearts turn and repent.

<!-- p98 -->
Jesus said that we would do these works and greater ones still! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:12</span>). These collective works are supernatural, divine, and desperately needed in a world that is unraveling, miserable, full of pain, waiting for the mature sons of God to be clearly manifested! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:22-23</span>)

<!-- p99 -->
The instrument chosen by Jesus to bring about change on the earth is his church, empowered, bold, and led by the Holy Spirit.

## Building a Relationship with the Holy Spirit

<!-- p101 -->
Every relationship takes time and experience to develop deeply. The Holy Spirit is our advocate, our comforter, our helper, and our guide. He empowers us and sanctifies us.

<!-- p102 -->
Begin to think of the Holy Spirit as you would think of a person. In our relationship, both of us can work to build it up or do things that weaken our relationship. He can be ignored, quenched, blasphemed, or He can be heard, honored, and obeyed.

<!-- p103 -->
Do not ignore the voice of the Holy Spirit. He is always speaking to us and to the church. If you do not yet know how to discern the voice of the Holy Spirit, it takes time, willingness, and effort (like all healthy and beneficial relationships). But do not worry, you already have within you all the parts necessary to begin developing a personal relationship with the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p104 -->
The quickest and most reliable ways to build an effective relationship with the Holy Spirit are

<!-- p105 -->
1.  Cry out to Him **individually** in prayer, pause, and wait for Him to speak.

<!-- p106 -->
2.  Cry out to Him as part of a **group** of people who know Him (the church), pause, and wait for Him to speak.

<!-- p107 -->
I use the expression ‘cry out’ to mean literally, with your mouth, or in the way you speak (with your hands if you cannot speak out loud with your mouth). Why speak? A few reasons:

<!-- p108 -->
1.  We are speaking spiritual beings, made in the image of God. God speaks, and so do we.

<!-- p109 -->
2.  We use speech as the main form of communication. You can also write what you want to say (journaling) and speak it out loud.

<!-- p110 -->
3.  Speaking is an intentional act, and it indicates to the party you are speaking with that you would like to engage them in a conversation.

<!-- p111 -->
#### What should you say?

<!-- p112 -->
If you have never spoken to God, try introducing yourself. Of course, it will feel a little awkward, but that discomfort is normal and temporary. Besides introducing yourself, tell God that you want to speak with Him and that you want Him to speak with you.

<!-- p113 -->
Consider asking Him a question. It does not have to be a deep theological question; you can start simple. Can You show me Your love for me? Why do I have a certain feeling? What would You like to say to me, Lord?

<!-- p114 -->
#### And then?

<!-- p115 -->
Well, in all effective relationships you speak for a moment, then pause and listen. God will speak.

<!-- p116 -->
God speaks in many ways, but He wants to do so in a personal and internal way. It will probably not be an audible voice (as if your best friend said your name out loud), but it will be an inner voice that speaks to your heart and mind. Elijah refers to this as the ‘still small voice’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1KI" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Kings 19:11-13</span>).

<!-- p117 -->
The most common ways I have personally witnessed God speak to people, and I have been blessed to help dozens of people learn to speak to God directly, are some combination of the following:

<!-- p118 -->
- A certainty - the person who is speaking with God receives an immediate understanding that helps them.

<!-- p119 -->
- An image - also known in the Scriptures as a vision or a dream (a vision is when you are awake, a dream is when you are asleep), God will give you an image in your mind. Perhaps it is a memory, or a place you have been or that you know? Explore the image, what do you see? What do you feel as a result? What is happening?

<!-- p120 -->
- An inner voice - like an audible voice, but only you can hear, and spoken to your heart and mind.

<!-- p121 -->
#### What will God say?

<!-- p122 -->
I'm not sure! But that's the beauty of it. I can say that He is NOT the spirit of fear, but of perfect love, and that this will never violate the teaching of Scripture. For example, God will never tell you to have an extramarital affair.

<!-- p123 -->
What now?

<!-- p124 -->
Keep the dialogue going! It's really that simple.

<!-- p125 -->

<!-- p126 -->
- *Genuine Christianity* by Larry Low ([<u>www.larrylow.com</u>](http://www.larrylow.com))

<!-- p127 -->
- *Good Morning, Holy Spirit* by Benny Hinn ([<u>www.bennyhinn.org</u>](http://www.bennyhinn.org))

<!-- p128 -->
### Why build a relationship?

<!-- p129 -->
The Holy Spirit is the Helper. The Helper helps us! Work goes much more smoothly if we have a common language and relationship with Him. Can you imagine trying to work with someone at your job if you had no relationship, didn't speak the same language, and couldn't communicate?!

<!-- p130 -->
We serve a God who wants to communicate with us. Jesus said that His sheep know His voice (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:27</span>), we already have within us the natural capacities we need, but we may lack proficiency acquired through experience.

<!-- p131 -->
Speaking from my own experience, a personal and active relationship with the Holy Spirit is one of the best things about being a Christian. His presence brings us peace, comfort, and direction, but only if you know how to seek Him, hear Him, and obey Him.

<!-- p132 -->
Develop a sensitivity to the Holy Spirit. Like many married couples, over time you can communicate with your spouse in a unique way and sometimes with just a look. The look alone tells you everything you need to know about what just happened and what is going to happen. Build that kind of sensitivity and familiarity with the Holy Spirit. Include Him in your whole life, this is a wonderful part of your relationship with Him that will give great depth and richness to your walk of faith.

<!-- p133 -->
The Holy Spirit came to lead and guide us, to empower us to be the people of God, and to see the people of the Earth radically transformed, set free from the kingdom of darkness to the kingdom of light. To know Him, to build trust in Him is essential for everyday life and for accomplishing the work that Jesus has for us to do.

<!-- p134 -->
Our discussion of the work of the Holy Spirit does not end in this chapter. His work is discussed extensively throughout the remaining chapters of this book, especially the chapters on sanctification and baptism.
