# Chapter 1 - Sound Doctrine Matters

<!-- p1 -->
Doctrine simply means the central truths we believe and teach—truths passed on by God’s faithful people, rooted in the Scriptures and lived out in our daily walk.

<!-- p2 -->
When Jesus instructed His church to ‘teach’, He was instructing them to share His doctrine.

<!-- p3 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p4 -->
> **<u>Matthew 28:18-20</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And Jesus came and spoke to them, saying, "All authority has been given to Me in heaven and on earth.
>
> Go therefore and make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit,
>
> teaching them to observe all things that I have commanded you; and lo, I am with you always, even to the end of the age." Amen.
>

<!-- p5 -->
</span>

<!-- p6 -->
In other words, Jesus is instructing all of us to learn His teachings and replicate them in others.

<!-- p7 -->
[See Table 1](../tables/table1.md)

<!-- p8 -->
Jesus is the Way, the Truth, and the Life (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:6</span>), fully embodying the truth, wisdom, and revelation of God. This personification, the Word (logos), existed from the beginning and became flesh to dwell among us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:1</span>, 14). The structured teachings of Jesus are His doctrine (***didaskalia***), by observing (obeying, keeping, guarding) His commandments (***entolē)*** we become His disciples (***mathētēs***).

<!-- p9 -->
Imagine a master architect, the greatest builder in all of history, whose vision, knowledge, and wisdom surpass all others. He alone understands the perfect design for an enduring and unshakable structure. His understanding is not derived from others—it is inherent to Him. He is the source of all architectural wisdom, and all solid construction finds its origin in His design. This architect represents Jesus, the Logos—the full and complete personification of the truth, wisdom, and revelation of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:1</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:6</span>).

<!-- p10 -->
To bring His design to life, the architect creates a **detailed blueprint**—a structured scheme that contains the principles, proportions, and foundational truths needed to build a building that will stand firm. Without this plan, no one could build correctly. This plan represents the structured teaching of Jesus, His doctrine (***didaskalia***) — the framework through which divine truth is understood and applied (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 2:1</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 4:13</span>). His doctrine is not merely theoretical knowledge; it is the means by which His wisdom is communicated and preserved, so that all who follow Him can build their lives on the correct foundation.

<!-- p11 -->
However, a plan by itself is not enough. The architect also provides **precise instructions**, detailing how each part of the structure must be assembled, where each beam must be placed, and how each element must align with His perfect design. These specific instructions represent His commandments **(Entolē**)-the actionable directives that govern obedience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:15</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:20</span>). His commandments are not arbitrary rules; they are the necessary steps to ensure that the building remains firm, aligned with the original plan. To ignore or alter His commandments would be to compromise the integrity of the entire structure.

<!-- p12 -->
Now, imagine that the architect gathers a group of **apprentices**—those who desire to learn from Him, to build under His guidance, and one day to teach others how to erect buildings that endure. These apprentices carefully study His plan, listen attentively to His instructions, and faithfully apply His commandments in their work. They do not build according to their own ideas, but according to the master’s design. These apprentices represent the disciples **(Mathētēs**)-those who observe, obey, and internalize the teachings of Jesus, aligning their lives with His Word (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19-20</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 8:31</span>). The more they study with the architect, the more skilled they become, eventually teaching and training others in His ways.

<!-- p13 -->
## Why Should We Study Doctrine?

<!-- p14 -->
The teaching of Jesus is the foundation of our faith! He is the master architect who has a perfect design and plan. Only by studying the plans, obeying the architect’s specific instructions, and developing a solid working relationship can we build a life that will stand firm in the storms of life.

<!-- p15 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="127" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p16 -->
> **<u>Psalms 127:1</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Song of Ascents. Of Solomon. Unless the LORD builds the house, they labor in vain who build it; Unless the LORD guards the city, the watchman stays awake in vain.
>

<!-- p17 -->
</span>

<!-- p18 -->
We cannot expect our lives to remain firm in the face of hardships, challenges, tribulations, persecutions, or just ordinary life if we have not built our lives on the solid rock of faith.

<!-- p19 -->
It takes more than enrolling in the gym to make a person healthy and physically fit. One must continue in the discipline of the gym to obtain all the benefits of membership. Likewise, we cannot just attend a church or casually claim to be Christians; we must all be instructed, trained, equipped, disciplined in the faith—only then will we realize all the benefits of membership!

<!-- p20 -->
Jesus' commission extends beyond us. It is great that we become whole, healthy, disciplined, submissive to God, but we must also teach, train, equip, lead, and disciple others in the faith!

<!-- p21 -->
Why should we study doctrine and become disciplined? To build our lives on the rock!

<!-- p22 -->
However, this is more than just a benefit; it also serves as a warning to us. If we do not build our lives on the rock, we will build our lives on the sand, and a strong storm will knock our house down. (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 7:24-27</span>).

<!-- p23 -->
Our beliefs dictate our actions and our destiny. Proverbs teaches us that as a man thinks, so he is! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="23" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 23:7</span>). We cannot remain neutral - we are all building on something. Jesus calls us to build on the rock so that we can stand firm and walk in His salvation. The promise of truth and freedom is for us!

<!-- p24 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="31-32" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p25 -->
> **<u>John 8:31-32</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Then Jesus said to the Jews who believed in Him: "If you abide in My word, you are truly My disciples.
>
> And you shall know the truth, and the truth will set you free."
>

<!-- p26 -->
</span>

<!-- p27 -->
Being a disciple of Jesus is only possible by continuing in His word (His logos)! The result of continuing in His word is our knowledge of the truth, which makes us free!

<!-- p28 -->
Disciples, having been taught in the discipline, can fulfill the master's commandments. Every person in the New Testament church is called to:

<!-- p29 -->
1.  Be a disciple -\> subject ourselves to the discipline of sound doctrine.

<!-- p30 -->
2.  Make disciples -\> teach others to subject themselves to the discipline of sound doctrine.

<!-- p31 -->
### How Do We Build Our Lives on the Rock?

<!-- p32 -->
Don't worry, we don't need to do this alone.

<!-- p33 -->
1.  God has given us His Holy Spirit, who will lead and guide us into all truth (John 14).

<!-- p34 -->
2.  God has given us His words recorded in the Scriptures.

<!-- p35 -->
3.  God has given us gifted, spiritually mature leaders, to help build us up, make us mature, keep us safe, and organize us to carry out good works.

<!-- p36 -->
Christianity is a team sport, and we have a perfect coach who knows exactly what we need. He has given us specialists who know, from experience, how to help us grow and mature and, in time, you can be one of them too!

<!-- p37 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="11-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

> And He Himself gave some to be apostles, some prophets, some evangelists, and some pastors and teachers,
>
> for the equipping of the saints for the work of ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ,
>
> till we all come to the unity of the faith and of the knowledge of the Son of God, to a perfect man, to the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ;
>
> so that we may no longer be children, carried around by every wind of doctrine, by the trickery of men, in the craftiness with which they lead into error,
>
> but, following the truth in love, let us grow in all things into Him who is the head—Christ—
>

>

<!-- p39 -->
</span>

<!-- p40 -->
**An important observation:** the word ‘some’ indicates that not all of us are called with a gift of apostle, prophet, evangelist, pastor, or teacher, but all of us have received ministry gifts, talents, and special roles (discussed later in the chapter on the church).

<!-- p41 -->
God gave the church these special gifts to lead us to maturity, so that we can and will be:

<!-- p42 -->
- Able to do the work and bear fruit

<!-- p43 -->
- United in the faith

<!-- p44 -->
- Filled with the knowledge of God

<!-- p45 -->
- Mature

<!-- p46 -->
- Not like children

<!-- p47 -->
Without sound doctrine we will be <u>children, tossed and carried about.</u>

<!-- p48 -->
Children are an important part of God’s family, but children are inherently:

<!-- p49 -->
- Immature - unable to bear fruit or reproduce.

<!-- p50 -->
- Naive - unable to discern between good and bad agents.

<!-- p51 -->
- Self-centered - concerned with the temporal and with the now.

<!-- p52 -->
- Defenseless - vulnerable to harm.

### Sound doctrine offers a defense against the evil of the world

<!-- p54 -->
We do not have the luxury of being spiritually neutral in this life. We are either influenced by the Kingdom of God, or by the Kingdom of Darkness.

<!-- p55 -->
Paul charged his spiritual son in the Lord, Timothy, saying:

<!-- p56 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p57 -->
> **<u>2 Timothy 4:1-4</u>** (NKJV)
>
> I charge you therefore before God and the Lord Jesus Christ, who will judge the living and the dead at His appearing and His kingdom:
>
> Preach the word! Be ready in season and out of season. Convince, rebuke, exhort, with all longsuffering and teaching.
>
> For the time will come when they will not endure sound doctrine, but according to their own desires, because they have itching

<!-- p60 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p61 -->
> **<u>2 Timothy 3:1-9</u>** (NKJV)
>
> But know this: that in the last days perilous times will come:
>
> For men will be lovers of themselves, lovers of money, boasters, proud, blasphemers, disobedient to parents, ungrateful, ungodly,
>
> unloving, implacable, slanderers, without self-control, cruel, enemies of good,
>
> traitors, headstrong, arrogant, lovers of pleasures rather than of God,
>
> having a form of godliness, but denying its power. And from such people turn away!
>
> For of this sort are those who enter households and take captive gullible women, burdened with sins, led by various passions,
>
> always learning and never able to come to the knowledge of the truth.
>
> And just as Jannes and Jambres resisted Moses, so also these resist the truth: men of corrupt mind, disapproved concerning the faith;
>
> but they will go no further, for their folly will be manifest to all, as theirs also was.
>

<!-- p62 -->
</span>

<!-- p63 -->
The core of this message is that dangerous times will come. Men will be so turned toward themselves that, despite all their ‘teaching’, they will not submit to sound doctrine, will deny the power of God, and will walk with a corrupt and reprobate mind. Paul exhorts Timothy to be well prepared to preach sound doctrine.

<!-- p64 -->
As disciples, we study and submit ourselves to the discipline of sound doctrine, this provides a defense against the evil of the world, we become fully mature men and women of God, able to defend ourselves from the snares of the enemy and to protect those around us who have not yet reached full maturity.

<!-- p65 -->
We must be careful and diligent in our study, the healthy teaching of the Scriptures can be diverted by various perverse influences.

<!-- p66 -->
Sound doctrine can become distorted by the traditions of men. We see this truth evidenced by the need for the great reformers of Protestantism and in the history of the Old Testament. God raised up men to restore the fundamental truth that had been perverted by the traditions of men.

What are His doctrines, what will we study?

<!-- p88 -->
In this work we will mainly study fundamental doctrines of the New Testament church. These doctrines are fundamental because the Scriptures refer to them as such, but even more because they provide a solid foundation on which the learner can build their life.

<!-- p89 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p94 -->

<!-- p95 -->
3.  Faith toward God

<!-- p96 -->
4.  Doctrine of baptisms

<!-- p97 -->
5.  Laying on of hands

<!-- p98 -->
6.  Resurrection of the dead

<!-- p99 -->
7.  Eternal judgment

<!-- p100 -->
We will also study central elements of our Christian walk:

<!-- p101 -->
- Sanctification by the Holy Spirit

<!-- p102 -->
- The Church and our role in it

<!-- p103 -->
- Making disciples

<!-- p104 -->
- Spiritual authority

<!-- p105 -->
We will study each of these in the context of the church and study them so that we may ‘advance to perfection (maturity)’. Our study will focus on the various perspectives and aspects of the Godhead as they have been revealed to us: God the Father, God the Son, and God the Holy Spirit.
