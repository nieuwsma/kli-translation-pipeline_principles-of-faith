# Chapter 6 - Faith toward God

<!-- p1 -->
Faith is a fundamental concept in Christianity. Faith is a word so commonly used in Christianity that, at times, the meaning of the word is supplanted by subtle misconceptions and misunderstanding. But it is not something you can hold in your hands-it is something formed in your heart as you come to trust God. Persuasion is belief.

<!-- p2 -->
The author of Hebrews instructs us that we must have ‘faith toward God’.

### Defining faith

<!-- p10 -->
Faith is a commonly misunderstood and misrepresented aspect of Christianity. As the saying goes, “there’s a ditch on both sides of the road”! Because the word faith is so commonly used without proper explanation in our modern Christianity, it can end up absorbing erroneous definitions amplified by misunderstandings. Faith is often portrayed either as an overarching statement of abstract belief or as an almost mystical power or “magic,” something you possess in a specific, measured, limited quantity.

<!-- p11 -->
#### What faith is not

<!-- p12 -->
Faith misrepresented in an <u>abstract</u> way has no specific conviction or persuasion. This becomes evident with a bit of probing, as that representation of faith cannot be made specific. If you ask, ‘what do you believe’ or ‘why do you believe,’ the person with that understanding of faith often cannot clarify beyond ‘I believe.’ <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 3:15</span> says that we must be ready to give an answer for the hope that is in us.

<!-- p13 -->
Sometimes people think of faith as a magical power-but that is not what Scripture teaches. Faith concerns a real relationship built on trust. In Scripture, faith is much more dynamic and relational-it is rooted in persuasion, trust, and confidence in God’s character and promises. It is not an impersonal force, but an engaged response to God that shapes thoughts, feelings, and actions.

<!-- p14 -->
Why is belief relevant to persuasion?

<!-- p15 -->
Our beliefs shape the way we see and interact with the world around us. Belief shapes perception and precipitates our actions. That is how our minds are programmed-we live based on what we believe is possible.

<!-- p16 -->
We act based on what we perceive, understand, and believe to be possible. When we walk down a hallway, our brain detects the boundaries of the floor and wall and tells our feet automatically where we can step and what to avoid. This truth goes beyond our physical navigation of the world around us.

<!-- p17 -->
Suppose you believe it is impossible to become financially independent or to live a healthy, active lifestyle. In that case, your actions will follow that belief, and you, true to the belief, will not do nor likely even attempt what you believe to be impossible.

<!-- p18 -->
If you believe that God has an unloving, punitive character, that perception of Him will keep you from walking with Him as His child.

<!-- p19 -->
#### Faith is persuasion

<!-- p20 -->
So, what is faith? Persuasion! The Greek word ***pistis*** means persuasion, or the result of being persuaded. We must have persuasion toward God. In English we could say faith (persuasion) ‘**in** God’. If you are married, you are expected to have faith ‘**in’** your spouse. You believe that they will do what they said they would do, and you believe they are being authentic and genuine. That trust should extend beyond what they said, extending to them in general.

<!-- p21 -->
Developing faith, persuasion, and trust in a relationship takes time and experience. Hopefully, in life you have learned how to develop a trusting relationship with another person. The principles you learned that allow you to increase your trust in someone you know will also allow you to increase your faith in God. Faith in a relationship increases over time, in experience, with consistency, and through small and large actions that build your trust in the character and nature of the other person.

<!-- p22 -->
‘Faith toward God’ is relational. Relationships are built by two parties, not one. The good news about our faith in God is that God is responsible for His side of the relationship (made possible by the atoning death of Jesus, and by the filling of His Spirit in the new birth), and He helps us with our side as well (through the work of the Comforter - the Holy Spirit). God is doing almost all the work, our part (as we will discuss later in this chapter) is to walk *WITH* Him in obedience.

<!-- p23 -->
### Jesus Matures Our Faith

<!-- p24 -->
Jesus is the author and finisher of our faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 12:2</span>). Our faith was designed to be continually growing and expanding. We do not arrive at a single point at which we have ‘faith’ enough, but rather our faith increases. Consider a deep and personal relationship that you have in your life today. As in any relationship, we do not reach a specific milestone at which we now ‘know’ the person. We continue to get to know them and increase our knowledge about them, and this refines, shapes, and grows our relationship.

<!-- p25 -->
How do we make our faith in God grow? By continuing to experience Him through His many revelations, manifestations, and attributes. Again, consider a deep personal relationship in your life. You increase the depth of the relationship by engaging with that person in different ways, in different formats, through different means, in different contexts. You talk with them, text them, perhaps write to them. You see them individually and in group settings. You talk about what you value and, perhaps, challenge one another. The countless ways go on to infinity. Through these many expressions, you come to know the person more deeply.

<!-- p26 -->
We all must come to know God, through Jesus, as our savior. Think of the names of God (*see the appendix),* just because we know God as savior does not mean we have already experienced Him as deliverer, healer, counselor, Lord.

<!-- p27 -->
If you are at a point where you have been persuaded (faith) to know God as your savior, building that relationship will give you confidence in His character to know Him in other ways.

<!-- p28 -->
I am blessed to have several very deep relationships in my life. One of those relationships is with my wife. In our relationship, because of the level of trust (faith) we have in each other, my wife can tell me things that she will do, which I have not yet seen manifest, and I have confidence to believe her because of her proven character.

<!-- p29 -->
This example is how our deep relationship with God can be experienced, with confidence and conviction! We may not yet have fully experienced Him (in fact, we will never fully experience Him in this life), but as we mature in our relationship, we can trust in Him beyond the limit of what I know personally, and of what I have already seen happen.

<!-- p30 -->
Jesus increases our faith through experience with Him. A significant way in which we can and should experience Him is through His word.

### Faith Increases through the Word of God

<!-- p32 -->
Consider this passage from Romans:

> **<u>Romans 10:16-17</u>** (NKJV)
>
> But they have not all obeyed the gospel. For Isaiah says: "LORD, WHO HAS BELIEVED OUR REPORT?"
>
> So then faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God.
>

<!-- p35 -->
</span>

<!-- p36 -->
When we replace the word *faith* with *persuasion* it reads:

<!-- p37 -->
*‘So then persuasion comes by hearing and hearing by the Word of God’*. This truth is naturally self-evident. A primary way by which someone comes to have persuasion is to have been persuaded (convinced), and we become convinced when someone makes an ‘argument’-or provides a grounded and well-articulated explanation.

<!-- p38 -->
There are two Greek words for ‘Word’ in the Bible-**logos** and **rhema**. Understanding the difference helps us see how faith grows.

<!-- p39 -->
**Logos**: generally, it refers to the **concept, idea, reason, or broader expression of the word of God**. It encompasses a universal and fundamental truth or message and is often used to describe the eternal wisdom of God and Christ Himself as the living Word.

<!-- p40 -->
**Rhema (G4487)**: emphasizes a **specific, spoken word or utterance**. It usually refers to a particular word or message given at a specific moment, especially a spoken or revealed word that applies directly to a person’s situation.

<!-- p41 -->
*Logos* is like the entire message of a book, whereas *rhema* is like a particular passage or sentence that speaks directly to someone at a given moment. While *logos* is the overarching truth of God, *rhema* is when part of that truth is spoken to an individual in a personal and applicable way. Together, they illustrate the fullness of God’s communication: *logos* as the broad and foundational truths of God’s character and will, and *rhema* as the specific, applied word that brings that truth to life in a person’s life and situation.

<!-- p42 -->
### Example of a Rhema Word in Action

<!-- p43 -->
God promises revelation and wisdom to all who ask. When He speaks to us, that utterance is a *rhema* word. Rhema words come from God and serve to confirm, lead, and guide us. They do not replace the word of the *logos*, nor do they carry the authority of Scripture. *Rhema* words should be judged by the same standard as prophecy or interpretation of tongues (see the chapter on the Holy Spirit for an in-depth study of this topic).

<!-- p44 -->
At various points in my career, I prayed for divine guidance. A few years ago, I was contemplating starting the process of looking for a new opportunity and, in my time of prayer, I heard God say to me, ‘*Just wait on Me, I have an opportunity for you.*’ I was beginning to look for a new job because I was dissatisfied with my current position and felt frustrated. God’s word of direction, ‘*wait on Me’* helped me have peace and confidence in a season that otherwise would have been restless.

<!-- p45 -->
Three to four months went by; I kept working hard and faithfully, maintaining my positive attitude. God kept reminding me, *‘wait on Me’*, and then His promise came to pass. I had the opportunity to be promoted and received a generous pay raise.

<!-- p46 -->
I could have continued my job search; I could have left. I could have started somewhere new. Without a doubt someone else would have gotten that promotion! But because I sought the Lord’s counsel and was persuaded by His **rhema word**, I obeyed and received the benefit. God’s words are **living and personal**, and He promises to give us manifold and liberal wisdom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:5</span>).

<!-- p47 -->
I have dozens, if not hundreds, of times when I prayed and asked God what to do, and He gave me a rhema word. Not all had the same spectacular outcome, but all brought a sense of peace and confidence that I was obeying God and that He would work things out. They were the words I needed for the moment! Often, when God brings a rhema word, it comes from the logos. He will bring a passage of Scripture to my remembrance: “*Do not fear, for I am with you!*” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="41" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 41:10</span>), “*By His stripes we are healed”* (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="53" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 53:5</span>), “*Let us not grow weary while doing good!*” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:9</span>), countless examples continue!

<!-- p48 -->
Receiving a rhema word not only strengthens our faith, but also deepens our relationship with God as we trust Him more fully in our daily decisions. We will continue our study of learning to hear God in the chapter on the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p49 -->
Our faith is also strengthened by what we see God do, but sometimes what we see in the natural world challenges what we have heard from God. Reconciling the inconsistency between what we see and what we hear is an important ministry of the Holy Spirit, who promises to guide us into all truth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span>).

### Faith Greater Than We Can See

<!-- p51 -->
After His resurrection, Jesus appeared several times to many of His disciples. Thomas (one of the twelve) was not with the other disciples, so he missed the appearance of the risen Savior. Thomas believed in Jesus and trusted his fellow disciples, but he did not believe that Jesus had been raised in bodily form; he insisted that he could not actively believe until he experienced it personally.

<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="24-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

> And after eight days His disciples were again inside, and Thomas with them. Jesus came, the doors being shut, stood in the midst and said: "Peace to you!"
>

>
> And Thomas answered and said to Him: "My Lord and my God!"
>
> Jesus said to him: "Thomas, because you have seen Me, you have believed. Blessed are those who have not seen and have believed."

When Jesus appeared to His disciples and calmed them (I imagine it is quite alarming when someone appears in the midst of you, having come through locked doors), He spoke to Thomas. He did not criticize or blame Thomas, nor did He accuse him as part of the evil and perverse generation that seeks a sign (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 16:4</span>), but He revealed Himself to Thomas.

<!-- p60 -->
Jesus goes on to say, ‘*blessed are those who have not seen and have believed*d’. This is not Jesus encouraging ‘blind or ignorant faith’ nor rejecting the use of empirical evidence. Rather, He is saying that we are more blessed when we can believe without the need for empirical proof. Why? If our faith, our trust, were limited purely to what we had seen demonstrated empirically, we could never have any confidence in future work. We would have great confidence in what had been accomplished, but we would not walk in God’s peace for our tomorrows and for our future.

<!-- p61 -->
Using a relational example, how challenging would my relationship with my wife be if my attitude were consistently ‘<u>I’ll believe it when I see it!</u>’? Instead, a relationship based on trust says ‘I know you are a trustworthy person’.

### Faith produces obedience and good works

<!-- p80 -->
Our relationship of trust with God is strengthened through obedience, resulting in good works! These works do not save us nor make us deserve God’s love-they are the natural result of a life rooted in Him.

<!-- p81 -->
The Scriptures present a natural progression of faith. As we come to know God and experience His presence, we grow in our belief in Him. This belief impels us to act in ways that align with our faith, leading us to obey His commandments. Our obedience produces good works, which bring a harvest of positive and beneficial results.

<!-- p82 -->
Consider again this passage from Romans:

<!-- p83 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

Breaking this down in reverse order:

<!-- p87 -->
1.  Hearing the Word (**rhema**) of God produces faith (**pistis**)

<!-- p88 -->
2.  Faith, which is a state of belief or persuasion, will produce the action or the verb of believing.  Again, consider that the person who believes they can become debt-free acts accordingly!

<!-- p90 -->
3. Our act of believing is demonstrated by obedience.

<!-- p91 -->
The word for ‘obeyed’ is **hupakouō** (**hoop-ak-oo'-o**- G5219): **hupakouō** means *to hear under* (as a *subordinate*), that is, *to listen attentively*; by implication, *to heed* or *to conform to* a command or authority: - give ear to, be obedient to, obey.

<!-- p92 -->
In a workplace setting, when the boss or supervisor is giving instructions, they are not “speaking just for the sake of speaking”; instead, they are giving guidance they intend their employees to follow.

<!-- p93 -->
When we truly hear God, it should lead us to action - to trust in Him and obey. As the Scriptures say in:

<!-- p94 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

The counselor will teach them about the principles of financial freedom and help them set up a budget. That counseling will generate (trust / faith) in what they need to do to become free from debt. But trust is only the first step; the person must obey the principles they learned through disciplined practice to receive the good result and the blessing of being debt-free.

<!-- p98 -->
The book of James says that our belief (faith) must produce works! We already know what works are (for example, ‘dead works’ from <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 6:1</span>).

<!-- p103 -->
We do not receive salvation by works! When we receive salvation (by faith), we do works! Allow me to reiterate: you cannot work enough, nor make enough sacrifices to earn your salvation; it is the free gift of God that we receive by faith!

<!-- p104 -->
The result of these works (obedience) is that they produce a good and beneficial harvest. The time it takes to receive the harvest, or the measure of that harvest, varies, but it will produce a good harvest! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 8:22</span>).

It is better to properly follow what we are supposed to do than to need to make a sacrifice to make up for what we did not do correctly. Thankfully, there is grace from God when we stray from the path, miss the mark (sin), and need to turn back to God.

<!-- p108 -->
As we build our faith in God, it will naturally produce obedience in us. That obedience is for our benefit and produces a good harvest. Returning to my personal example from this chapter, I would not have experienced peace during that period, nor the blessing of a promotion, if I had not obeyed God's word to me to *wait on Him*!

### Practical steps to grow in your faith

<!-- p153 -->
Walking with God is relational, but there are skills to be learned if you want good relationships. In earthly relationships, the skills that produce strong and secure relationships are built upon:

<!-- p154 -->
- Open and free dialogue and communication

<!-- p155 -->
- Healthy conflict resolution

<!-- p156 -->
- Commitment

<!-- p157 -->
- Consistency

<!-- p158 -->
You must be consistently present, disclose (communicate) yourself (your thoughts and emotions), welcome the other person (listen), and walk with them if you want a strong and vibrant relationship! All these skills apply to our relationship with God.

<!-- p159 -->
Beyond the core relational skills, there are specific practices and disciplines that will strengthen your relationship with God:

<!-- p160 -->
- **Build and maintain a life of prayer; listen to and communicate with God.** Regular, sincere prayer is essential for a close relationship with God. Through prayer, you can share your concerns, joys, and gratitude, seeking His guidance and strength. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 4:6-7</span>*) Being sensitive to the voice of the Holy Spirit helps you discern God's will, find wisdom in decisions, and grow in spiritual maturity. Set aside time for quiet reflection to listen for His direction. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:14</span>*)

<!-- p161 -->
- **Devote time to worship.** Worship is a powerful way to draw near to God, expressing love and reverence. Through praise, songs, or quiet reflection, worship deepens your awareness of His presence. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="100" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 100:2-4</span>*)

<!-- p162 -->
- **Read, study, and meditate on Scripture.** God's Word reveals His character, His promises, and His instructions for life. Devoting time to the study of Scripture helps you grow in faith, understanding, and spiritual resilience. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>*)

<!-- p163 -->
- **Obey His commandments.** Obedience to God's instructions demonstrates your love, respect, and trust in Him. Walking in obedience aligns your life with His purpose and brings blessings. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:15</span>*)

<!-- p164 -->
- **Be actively involved in a local church and serve.** Getting involved in a community of faith provides spiritual support, encouragement, and opportunities to serve others. Being involved helps build a sense of belonging and strengthens your commitment to God's mission. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:24-25</span>*)

<!-- p165 -->
- **Build relationships with other believers.** Fellowship with other Christians offers accountability, encouragement, and shared wisdom. These relationships enrich your faith journey and help you grow through mutual support. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="27" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 27:17</span>*)

<!-- p166 -->
Our ‘faith toward God’ is not a one-time event for salvation, but an ongoing practice and relationship that we must cultivate throughout life. The benefits of a trusting and fruitful relationship are incredibly numerous, but we need to do the good work of fostering that trust and that growth so that it may flourish.
