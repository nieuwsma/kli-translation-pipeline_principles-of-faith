# Chapter 12 - Discipleship

<!-- p1 -->
Discipleship means following Jesus, learning from Him, and becoming more like Him in the way we live and love. It involves a lifelong journey of obedience, transformation, and service, guided by the Holy Spirit, and rooted in the Word of God.

<!-- p2 -->
Discipleship is the primary command in the Great Commission. Jesus defined this mission in:

<!-- p3 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p4 -->
> **<u>Matthew 28:18-20</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And Jesus came and spoke to them, saying: "All authority has been given to Me in heaven and on earth.
>
> Go therefore and make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit,
>
> teaching them to observe all things that I have commanded you; and lo, I am with you always, even to the end of the age." Amen.
>

<!-- p5 -->
</span>

<!-- p6 -->
We, His followers, must *“go and make disciples of all nations.”* Discipleship is about personal growth and multiplying that growth by teaching others to follow Christ. Discipleship is the vehicle by which we build the Kingdom of God; the teachings of Jesus are the message.

<!-- p7 -->
The call to be a disciple is more than attending church or learning Bible verses-it’s an invitation to live like Jesus and to cooperate with Him in advancing the Kingdom of God. Genuine discipleship helps believers become spiritually mature and effective for ministry (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:13</span>). It shapes both the heart and the skills needed to serve others and share the Gospel. As Paul instructed Timothy in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 2:2</span>, discipleship follows a pattern of spiritual multiplication, in which trained disciples continue raising up new followers to lead others to Christ.

<!-- p8 -->
Discipleship is both an individual calling and a global mission. On a personal level, it involves growing in faith, developing an active spiritual life, being in relationship with others &amp; God. At the same time, it drives the Church’s mission to reach all nations with the Gospel, ensuring that the message of Christ continues to expand and transform lives across generations.

<!-- p9 -->
## Why Discipleship Matters

<!-- p10 -->
The impact of discipleship goes beyond personal transformation-it has a Kingdom impact. Paul reminded Timothy in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 2:2</span> to entrust what he had learned to faithful men who would then teach others also. This generational pattern highlights how discipleship is the engine that drives the growth and sustainability of the Church. Discipleship matters because it was Jesus’ chosen method to spread the Gospel and build His Church.

<!-- p11 -->
Rather than focusing on large crowds, Jesus intentionally invested in a small group of followers, teaching them, modeling faithfulness, and preparing them to carry His mission forward. This personal and relational approach ensured that His message multiplied, as each disciple was empowered to make more disciples.

<!-- p12 -->
At its core, discipleship is central to fulfilling the Great Commission (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19-20</span>). Jesus did not call us merely to make converts, but to make disciples-followers who are grounded in the Word of God, empowered by the Holy Spirit, and prepared for ministry. Through discipleship, believers grow in their knowledge of the Scriptures, develop spiritual disciplines, and learn to walk in obedience to Christ. This prepares the Church to impact the world, advance the Gospel, and reflect the glory of God to all nations.

<!-- p13 -->
### Nature vs Nurture

<!-- p14 -->
In the study of biology, psychology, and anthropology, we often consider the impact of nature versus nurture. The question often posed is: is a person more the result of their inborn, embedded genetic heritage? Or are they more the result of their environment and upbringing? The truth is that each person is a mixture of nature and nurture. Both elements, in strong interaction, choreograph the development of a human soul.

<!-- p15 -->
When you accepted Jesus, the miracle of salvation caused you to be born again of incorruptible seed. You received a divine nature! Your spiritual DNA was rewritten! This divine nature stirs our hearts to be close to our heavenly Father, to know Him, to experience Him.

<!-- p16 -->
Just like a newborn, whose wonderful, beautiful, and innocent nature must be shaped by loving parents to become a healthy and mature individual, we too must be shaped and nurtured to be developed.

<!-- p17 -->
Discipleship is the process of spiritual development that builds on our divine nature and makes us spiritually mature. By investing in discipleship, we ensure that faith is passed on, leaders are raised up, and that the Church remains healthy and vibrant-both locally and globally.

<!-- p18 -->
### Didactic Model vs Apprentice Model

<!-- p19 -->
In modern Western education, the primary relationship the student has is with the material. Student learning is focused on content, material, books, videos, assignments, etc. Educational transformation happens through the curriculum. This teaching method is known as the *didactic model*.

<!-- p20 -->
The paradigm of the didactic model has not always been the norm. The model and method used in the time of Jesus were the apprentice model. This learning model emphasizes that knowledge is more 'absorbed than taught' and focuses more on the relationship between student and teacher than between student and curriculum.

<!-- p21 -->
The didactic model gradually became the standard method over roughly a millennium, from the early medieval universities of Europe in the 11<sup>th</sup> - 13<sup>th</sup> centuries, the invention of the printing press in the 15<sup>th</sup> century, the Enlightenment of the 17<sup>th</sup> &amp; 18<sup>th</sup> centuries, and the impact of the Industrial Revolution on information and education in the 19<sup>th</sup> &amp; 20<sup>th</sup> centuries.

<!-- p22 -->
The advantage of the didactic model is that it can scale more efficiently than the apprentice model. Massive open online courses (MOOCs) can have thousands of students at the same time, whereas even a very skilled master may have perhaps only a few dozen apprentices simultaneously.

<!-- p23 -->
The result of the didactic model is that the student masters the content; however, the student does not become like the teacher. The teacher is a facilitator of the curriculum. The teacher imparts knowledge.

<!-- p24 -->
In the apprentice model, the master shares more than knowledge; he imparts wisdom. The result of the apprentice model is that the student has become so like the master that he can reproduce the master's work and take on his own apprentices.

<!-- p25 -->
The didactic model has its use and place in the Kingdom of God, a good example is this book or any other Christian educational material. Jesus, as a rabbi, skilled and trained in the rabbinic method, practiced the apprentice model as the primary model for His disciples.

<!-- p26 -->
Jesus describes the apprentice model in:

<!-- p27 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p28 -->
> **<u>Luke 6:40</u>** (NKJV)
>
> A disciple is not above his teacher, but everyone who is perfectly trained will be like his teacher.
>

<!-- p29 -->
</span>

<!-- p30 -->
In the didactic model the student becomes a bearer of knowledge, in the apprentice model, the student becomes like the teacher. Let's study the key words of that passage in Luke.

<!-- p31 -->
[See Table 6](../tables/table6.md)

<!-- p32 -->
To summarize, the purpose and goal of the apprentice model used in discipleship is twofold:

<!-- p33 -->
**First, the disciple becomes fully trained**, like the teacher. This does not mean that the disciple imitates everything his teacher does. We are not being commanded to create clones, but rather to form to maturity men and women who can carry the message of Jesus to the ends of the earth.

<!-- p34 -->
**Second, the disciple now becomes a teacher for others**. The disciple must pass on what he has learned to another person, otherwise the message of the kingdom ends with him. This does not mean that he will necessarily have exactly the same gifts as his teacher, or that he will enter full-time ministry, but it does mean that he must reproduce so that his own training has been completed.

<!-- p35 -->
Every organism that does not reproduce is sterile, and its lineage is cut off. An animal that cannot reproduce is not healthy, a church that cannot reproduce is equally unhealthy.

## The Model and Process of Discipleship of Jesus

<!-- p37 -->
Jesus demonstrated the mature and complete example of discipleship in the Gospels with His chosen disciples. We should use these steps broadly as the basis for our own walk in becoming and making disciples.

<!-- p38 -->
The apprentice model has two roles: master and student. Every master was once a student and, it is hoped, every student will become a master. I will use the terms *disciple* to refer to the student and *discipler* to refer to the master/teacher.

<!-- p39 -->
Every discipleship relationship goes through a progression of eight phases. The phases, or stages, are not purely isolated from one another, often the relationship will flow between the phases. In each phase there is an emphasis for the disciple, and for the discipler. We will study in depth the attributes of disciplers later in this chapter.

<!-- p40 -->
The eight phases of discipleship are:

<!-- p41 -->
1.  Invitation/Call

<!-- p42 -->
2.  Teaching

<!-- p43 -->
3.  Demonstration

<!-- p44 -->
4.  Participation/Experimentation

<!-- p45 -->
5.  Correction

<!-- p46 -->
6.  Empowerment/Authorization

<!-- p47 -->
7.  Multiplication

<!-- p48 -->
8.  Remain Connected

<!-- p49 -->
### Invitation/Call

<!-- p50 -->
Discipleship begins with a personal invitation from Jesus to follow Him. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 4:19</span>, Jesus called His first disciples, saying, *“Follow Me, and I will make you fishers of men.”* This invitation was both relational and transformational- it required leaving behind old ways of life and entering into a new purpose centered on God’s mission. Jesus’ call was not to spectatorship, but to participation in His ministry and the work of the Kingdom.

<!-- p51 -->
Modern disciple-makers should invite others into authentic relationships that inspire spiritual growth and obedience. The call to discipleship is deeply personal. Jesus meets people where they are, attending to their unique needs and circumstances, but He also calls them to go deeper- to grow, serve, and become more like Him. This pattern reflects the relational nature of discipleship, where the focus is not merely on knowledge but on a transformational relationship with Christ.

<!-- p52 -->
### Teaching

<!-- p53 -->
Teaching is at the heart of discipleship. Jesus spent much of His ministry teaching the principles of the Kingdom of God, most notably in the Sermon on the Mount (Matthew 5-7). His teachings provided a foundation for living righteously, loving others, and walking in obedience to God. Instruction in the Word of God remains central to discipleship today, as Scripture is the primary tool for spiritual growth, correction, and instruction in righteousness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>).

<!-- p54 -->
Disciple-makers should commit to teaching the Bible faithfully, helping others understand it and apply it to their lives. Teaching involves more than transmitting knowledge- it requires modeling a Christlike life so that disciples see faith in action. Jesus embodied the truths He taught, showing that effective teaching is both verbal and visible. Likewise, disciple-makers should strive to live what they teach, providing practical examples of obedience and faithfulness.

<!-- p55 -->
### Demonstration

<!-- p56 -->
Jesus not only taught His disciples- He demonstrated how to live the truths He proclaimed. Jesus showed what it means to love, serve, and minister in practical ways. Some examples from the Gospels include Jesus washing the disciples’ feet as an act of humility and service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 13:12-15</span>), healing the sick, and feeding the hungry. Through His actions, He modeled compassion, faith, and boldness, leaving a clear example for His followers to imitate.

<!-- p57 -->
Discipleship requires showing, not just speaking. Modern disciple-makers should be transparent about their faith journey, including struggles and victories, so that others see how faith works in real life. Whether praying with someone in need, sharing testimonies, or serving the community, discipleship flourishes when it is rooted in practical demonstration. Actions often speak louder than words, making demonstration a powerful tool for training others in the faith.

<!-- p58 -->
### Participation/Experimentation

<!-- p59 -->
Jesus did not keep His disciples on the sidelines- He actively involved them in ministry experiences. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 9:1-6</span> and <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 10:5-15</span>, Jesus sent the disciples to preach the Gospel, heal the sick, and cast out demons. These hands-on opportunities allowed the disciples to learn by doing, gaining confidence and skills under His guidance.

<!-- p60 -->
Modern discipleship should also include practical involvement. Disciplers should invite disciples to participate in ministry, whether by leading prayer groups, sharing testimonies, or serving at outreach events. Providing safe environments for practice allows disciples to grow in boldness while being supported and encouraged. Learning happens best when it is applied, and participation is the key to turning theory into action.

<!-- p61 -->
### Correction

<!-- p62 -->
Growth often requires correction, and Jesus lovingly corrected His disciples when they misunderstood His teachings or failed in their tasks. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33-37" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 9:33-37</span>, He addressed their pride by teaching them about servant leadership. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-32" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 22:31-32</span>, He prayed for Peter, preparing him to recover from failure and strengthen others.

<!-- p63 -->
Correction in discipleship should be full of grace and constructive-rooted in Scripture and aimed at restoration (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 4:2</span>). It is not about condemnation, but about helping disciples grow and avoid pitfalls. Disciplers should provide accountability, using the Word of God to guide and encourage growth, while modeling humility by also receiving correction.

<!-- p64 -->
### Empowerment/Authorization

<!-- p65 -->
Jesus not only taught and corrected- He empowered His disciples to fulfill His mission. He gave them authority to preach the Gospel, heal the sick, and cast out demons (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:18-20</span>). After His resurrection, He promised the Holy Spirit as the source of power for their ministry (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>).

<!-- p66 -->
Modern discipleship should include empowering others to operate in their spiritual gifts and minister effectively. This involves identifying gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-13</span>), encouraging their use, and releasing disciples to serve. Empowerment also emphasizes dependence on the Holy Spirit for strength, boldness, and wisdom, ensuring that disciples are spiritually equipped for the work God has called them to do.

<!-- p67 -->
### Multiplication

<!-- p68 -->
The goal of discipleship is not merely personal growth-it is multiplication. Jesus modeled this by training the disciples to make more disciples (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19-20</span>). After His resurrection, the apostles continued this multiplying mission, spreading the Gospel and planting churches throughout the known world. Paul’s instructions in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 2:2</span> reinforce this principle, urging Timothy to teach others who will teach others.

<!-- p69 -->
Discipleship involves empowering disciples to disciple others, creating a chain reaction of faith that expands the Kingdom of God. This process requires intentionality-teaching disciples to mentor, teach, and train others, while sending them out with confidence and purpose (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 10:15</span>).

<!-- p70 -->
### Staying Connected

<!-- p71 -->
Discipleship does not end when someone is sent. Jesus modeled ongoing connection with His disciples, continuing to encourage and instruct them even after His resurrection (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="21" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 21:15-19</span>). The early church maintained this pattern, gathering regularly for teaching, fellowship, and prayer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42-47" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:42-47</span>).

<!-- p72 -->
Modern discipleship should also foster ongoing relationships, providing accountability, encouragement, and support as disciples continue to grow and lead. Maintaining unity in the Body of Christ ensures that disciples are never isolated, but remain strengthened and encouraged in their journey of faith.

<!-- p73 -->
## Who Does Discipleship?

<!-- p74 -->
Everyone in the body of Christ is responsible first for becoming disciples, and then for contributing to the process of making disciples. There is a diversity of gifts and functions, so our contributions may look different, but we are all responsible. The people ultimately most responsible for the state and health of discipleship in the local church are the leaders (**elders and deacons - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 14:23</span>**), but everyone has a role.

<!-- p75 -->
Discipleship is a living and growing process-like nurturing a child to maturity. Healthy discipleship brings strength and life to people. Like a parent who strengthens and nurtures a child and, in doing so, makes the child healthy and mature, disciplers help nurture, strengthen, and mature disciples. The ultimate goal of discipleship is not 'transfer of knowledge,' but transformation.

<!-- p76 -->
We all must become perfect (mature) and make disciples. Although we have diverse gifts, there are common characteristics for all disciplers.

<!-- p77 -->
### Characteristics of a Discipler

<!-- p78 -->
A discipler is someone who not only follows Christ but is also committed to helping others grow in their faith. This role requires specific characteristics that reflect both spiritual maturity and practical dedication. Disciplers must continually rely on God's strength, wisdom, and grace to overcome the challenges that come with leading others on their spiritual journeys.

<!-- p79 -->
#### Rooted in Christ

<!-- p80 -->
The foundation of a disciple maker's life must be deeply rooted in Christ. Jesus said, “I am the vine; you are the branches. If you remain in me and I in you, you will bear much fruit; apart from me you can do nothing” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:5</span>). Effective disciple makers know that the ability to grow and help others comes from staying close to Jesus every day. However, resistance to personal growth and spiritual change can hinder that dependence. Disciple makers must continually cultivate their personal relationship with Jesus through prayer, Scripture study, and obedience, modeling for those they are discipling what it means to remain in Christ.

<!-- p81 -->
#### Committed to the Mission

<!-- p82 -->
A disciple maker must remain committed to the mission of making disciples, as commanded by Jesus in the Great Commission (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19-20</span>). This mission requires intentional focus and prioritization, recognizing that making disciples is not an optional task, but the central purpose of the church. Even so, challenges such as time constraints, busyness, and personal distractions can make it difficult to maintain focus. Disciple makers should intentionally set aside time for mentoring, teaching, and relationship building, viewing discipleship as a lifestyle, and not an occasional activity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 19:10</span>).

<!-- p83 -->
#### Led by the Spirit

<!-- p84 -->
Making disciples cannot be accomplished by human strength alone; it requires the guidance and empowerment of the Holy Spirit. Jesus promised His disciples that they would receive power when the Holy Spirit came upon them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>), enabling them to be witnesses and ministers of the Gospel. Disciple makers must be sensitive to the Spirit’s leading, relying on His wisdom to navigate complex situations, cultural barriers, and difficult conversations. This dependence on the Holy Spirit allows disciple makers to act in love, boldness, and spiritual discernment, ensuring that their efforts are Spirit-led and not self-driven.

<!-- p85 -->
#### Humble and Teachable

<!-- p86 -->
Humility is a mark of an effective disciple maker. As Paul instructed, “Do nothing out of selfish ambition or vanity; but, humbly, consider others as superior to yourselves” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 2:3</span>). Disciple makers recognize that they, too, are lifelong learners and must remain teachable and accountable. They welcome correction and feedback, modeling humility for those they mentor. Pride can be a significant barrier to growth, both for disciple makers and for their disciples. Embracing accountability and maintaining a servant’s heart fosters trust and creates an environment of growth and transparency.

<!-- p87 -->
#### Patient and Persistent

<!-- p88 -->
Discipleship is rarely instantaneous-it is a long-term process that requires patience and persistence. Paul reminds believers “do not grow weary of doing good, because, at the right time, we will reap if we do not give up” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:9</span>). Growth in faith often involves setbacks, doubts, and failures, but disciple makers are called to walk alongside others, offering encouragement and support at every step. It is easy to become discouraged when progress seems slow, but disciple makers trust that God is at work, even when results are not immediately visible.

<!-- p89 -->
Being a disciple maker requires a combination of spiritual dependence, commitment, and humility. It is a calling that demands patience and persistence, remaining Spirit-led and teachable. Disciple makers are not expected to be perfect, but they must be faithful-remaining in Christ, investing in others, and trusting that God will bring growth and transformation in His time. These qualities reflect the love of Christ and help others become mature disciples who, in turn, can disciple others.

<!-- p90 -->
## Practical Tools for Discipleship

<!-- p91 -->
Effective discipleship requires intentional tools and practices that promote spiritual growth, accountability, and community. These tools help disciples develop a solid foundation in the faith, grow in intimacy with God, and be empowered to disciple others. Practical methods such as Bible study, prayer, small groups, and mentoring provide both the structure and the support needed for ongoing growth.

<!-- p92 -->
### Bible Study

<!-- p93 -->
Engaging deeply with Scripture is the foundation of discipleship. Jesus emphasized the importance of the Word of God in spiritual growth, declaring that “man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceeds from the mouth of God” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 4:4</span>). Regular Bible study and memorization enable disciples to internalize truth, combat falsehoods, and apply God’s wisdom to daily life.

<!-- p94 -->
Disciples should be encouraged to immerse themselves in Scripture, not merely as an academic exercise, but to know God and transform their hearts (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="119" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="105" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 119:105</span>). Practical approaches such as reading plans, study guides, and group discussions offer opportunities to explore doctrine and discuss application in community. By building habits of regular study, disciples lay a solid foundation for spiritual maturity and fruitful ministry (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>).

<!-- p95 -->
### Prayer

<!-- p96 -->
Prayer is the lifeline of discipleship, connecting believers to God’s presence, power, and guidance. Jesus taught His disciples to pray in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 11:1-4</span>, emphasizing both dependence on God and spiritual alignment with His will. Through prayer, disciples learn to seek God’s wisdom, intercede for others, and receive strength for ministry.

<!-- p97 -->
Teaching disciples to pray-both individually and collectively-empowers them to cultivate intimacy with God and discern His direction (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 4:6-7</span>). Incorporating prayer journals, prayer groups, and fasting practices promotes spiritual discipline and empowerment, ensuring that disciples remain Spirit-led and focused on the mission.

<!-- p98 -->
### Small Groups and Community

<!-- p99 -->
Discipleship flourishes in community. The early church modeled relational discipleship through fellowship, prayer, and breaking bread together (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42-47" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:42-47</span>). Small groups and accountability partnerships provide a safe space for disciples to grow together, share struggles, and celebrate victories.

<!-- p100 -->
The local church serves as the center for discipleship relationships, offering opportunities for group Bible studies, prayer meetings, and fellowship events. These gatherings allow disciples to practice love, bear one another’s burdens (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:2</span>) and encourage one another toward spiritual maturity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:24-25</span>). Small groups also provide a framework for multiplying leaders, as mature disciples train and send others to lead groups and mentor new converts.

<!-- p101 -->
### Mentoring

<!-- p102 -->
Mentoring provides a personal and intentional approach to discipleship. Jesus modeled this by investing deeply in the twelve disciples, walking with them through teaching, correction, and encouragement. Likewise, one-on-one relationships allow disciple-makers to tailor guidance to individual needs, helping disciples grow in faith, character, and leadership.

<!-- p103 -->
Mentors serve as accountability partners, offering support, prayer, and wisdom in times of growth and struggle. Practical tools such as spiritual assessments, goal-setting, and regular follow-ups ensure that disciples remain focused and motivated. Peer relationships within mentoring structures also foster mutual growth, as both the mentor and the disciple learn from shared experiences (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="27" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 27:17</span>).

## **Personal Reflection Questions**

<!-- p105 -->
1.  What does it mean to you, personally, to be a disciple of Jesus? How are you actively following Him in your daily life?

<!-- p106 -->
2.  Reflect on Jesus’ model of discipleship. Which aspect of His approach — inviting, teaching, demonstrating, or empowering — stands out most to you, and why?

<!-- p107 -->
3.  How can you multiply your faith by discipling others? What challenges or fears might hold you back, and how can you overcome them? (Notice that I said multiply your faith, not multiply your disciples!)

<!-- p108 -->
4.  Which characteristics of a disciple-maker (for example, patience, humility, relational focus) do you see in yourself? Which traits do you feel you need to develop more?

<!-- p109 -->
5.  Think about the people in your life. Whom might God be calling you to disciple? How can you take the first step to build that relationship?

## Questions for Group Discussion (20-30 minutes)

<!-- p111 -->
1.  **Icebreaker Question:** Who had the greatest influence on your spiritual growth, and what did that person do that impacted you?

<!-- p112 -->
2.  **Exploring Jesus’ Model:** Read <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 4:19</span>. How does Jesus’ invitation to ‘Follow Me’ encapsulate the heart of discipleship? What does this look like today?

<!-- p113 -->
3.  **The Call to Make Disciples:** Discuss <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19-20</span>. Why is making disciples central to the church’s mission? How can we, as individuals, participate in this mission?

<!-- p114 -->
4.  **Overcoming Challenges:** What are some common barriers to disciple-making (for example, time, fear, lack of confidence)? How can we support one another in overcoming these barriers?

<!-- p115 -->
5.  **Practical Application:** What is one specific step you can take this week to invest in someone’s spiritual growth? How can we, as a group, encourage one another in this process?
