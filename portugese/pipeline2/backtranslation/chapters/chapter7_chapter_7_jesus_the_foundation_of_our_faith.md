# Chapter 7 - Jesus - The Foundation of Our Faith

<!-- p1 -->
Based on our study of Jesus as the Messiah, in this chapter we continue looking at the life, person, and work of Jesus. We will see that Jesus is the Mediator of a new and better covenant—and that He perfectly fulfills the roles of Prophet, Priest, and King. It is in this New Covenant that we, as believers, have good standing before God and make the transition from a works-based covenant to a trust-based (faith-based) covenant. Finally, we will read and study the Apostles’ Creed as a way to complete our study about Jesus.

<!-- p2 -->
## Mediator of the New Covenant

<!-- p3 -->
The concept of a mediator involves an intermediary who bridges the gap between two parties, facilitating a relationship or agreement. In the story of the Exodus, Moses is the initial mediator of the covenant that God established with His people. Moses received the law, offered the sacrifice (priest), called God’s people to obedience (prophet), and judged them (judge / king). In the rest of the Old Testament after Moses, the mediating role was fulfilled through the role of the Prophet, the Levitical Priesthood, and the Judges and, later, the Kings of the nation.

<!-- p4 -->
Mediator roles in the Old Testament:

<!-- p5 -->
1.  **Prophet:** The prophets were God’s messengers, sent to speak His heart to the people and call them back when they strayed. Moses, for example, served as a prophet who mediated the Old Covenant by receiving God’s Law and teaching it to Israel. The prophets also foretold the coming of the Messiah and the final redemption of humanity. Other prominent examples of prophets include: Isaiah, Jeremiah, Elijah, Elisha, Samuel.

<!-- p6 -->
2.  **Levitical Priesthood:** The priests acted as mediators by offering sacrifices for the atonement of sins and interceding for the people before God. The High Priest, especially, played a vital role on the Day of Atonement, entering the Holy of Holies to sprinkle blood on the mercy seat, symbolizing reconciliation with God. The ministry of the priest is to intercede for the people.

<!-- p7 -->
3.  **Judge/King:** The Judges, and later the Kings, were charged with governing God’s people, applying His laws, judging among the people, delivering them from their enemies, and leading them in righteousness. The book of Judges tells the stories of these leaders—people like Deborah, Samson, and Gideon—whom God raised up to rescue and guide Israel. Israel later had a lineage of kings, many evil, some good (see 1 and 2 Kings, 1 and 2 Samuel, and 1<sup>st</sup> and 2<sup>nd</sup> Chronicles). Some prominent examples are Saul, David, and Solomon.

<!-- p8 -->
It is worth noting that all the mediators of the Old Covenant were fully human and not divine. As men, they were capable of sin, and often gave themselves over to sin. Some of the mediators were sinful and led God’s people astray and into profane worship: false prophets, lying and corrupt priests, and wicked kings.

<!-- p9 -->
### Christ as the Mediator of the New Covenant

<!-- p10 -->
The death, resurrection, and ascension of Jesus fulfill the Old Covenant and establish the New Covenant.

<!-- p11 -->
The New Covenant is a transformative relationship between God and His people, established through the life, death, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. Unlike the Old Covenant, which focused on external laws, the New Covenant emphasizes internal transformation and grace. It was inaugurated by Jesus at the Last Supper, when He declared that His blood would establish this covenant, replacing the repeated animal sacrifices of the Old Covenant with His once-for-all atonement (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 22:20</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:10</span>). Through this sacrifice, the New Covenant provides complete forgiveness and cleansing from sin, fulfilling the requirement for a perfect and permanent reconciliation with God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 31:34</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:12-18</span>).

<!-- p12 -->
One of the central features of the New Covenant is its internal nature, as God writes His law on the hearts of His people, fostering a heart-level relationship with Him through the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 31:33</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 3:3</span>). This change empowers believers to live out God’s will not by mere obedience to external commandments, but by the power of the Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:1-4</span>). The New Covenant also grants direct access to God, removing the need for an earthly priesthood. Jesus serves as the eternal High Priest, and believers are called a royal priesthood, able to draw near to God with confidence through Christ’s sacrifice (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 7:24-25</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:19-20</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:9</span>).

<!-- p13 -->
Unlike the Old Covenant, which was exclusive to Israel, the New Covenant is universal, extending to all who believe in Christ, regardless of ethnicity or background (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 3:28</span>). This covenant is based entirely on grace, offering salvation and forgiveness freely through faith in Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>). It is not sustained by human effort, but by the empowering work of the Holy Spirit, who transforms and enables believers to live in obedience and reflect God’s character (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EZK" data-chapter="36" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ezekiel 36:26-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:9-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-23</span>). Furthermore, the New Covenant is eternal, surpassing the temporary and conditional nature of the Old Covenant (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="32" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 32:40</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:13</span>). It represents the ultimate fulfillment of God’s redemptive plan, reconciling humanity to Himself and empowering believers to live as His people under His rule for all eternity.

<!-- p14 -->
Jesus is explicitly identified as the Mediator of the New Covenant in

<!-- p15 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p16 -->

>
> And for this reason He is the Mediator of the new covenant, by means of death, for the redemption of the transgressions under the first covenant, that those who are called may receive the promise of the eternal inheritance.
>

<!-- p17 -->
</span>

<!-- p18 -->
Through His atoning death and resurrection, Christ inaugurates this new covenant, replacing the old system of sacrifices and external laws with a covenant written on the hearts of believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 31:31-34</span>). He stands between God and humanity, not only interceding for His people, but also providing the means of reconciliation through His own blood (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:12</span>). Unlike the mediators of the Old Covenant, Jesus is sinless, eternal, and fully able to fulfill the divine requirements of righteousness and mercy, guaranteeing an enduring relationship between God and believers.

<!-- p19 -->
Jesus Fulfills and Perfects These Roles

<!-- p20 -->
1.  **Prophet:** Jesus is the supreme Prophet, not merely a messenger of the word of God, but the Word made flesh (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:14</span>). He fully and perfectly reveals the character, will, and plan of salvation of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 1:1-2</span>). His teachings, miracles, and prophetic declarations fulfill the prophecies of the Old Testament and provide the foundation for the New Covenant.

<!-- p21 -->
2.  **Priest:** Jesus is the perfect High Priest, offering Himself as the sacrifice, once for all, for sin (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 7:27</span>). Unlike the priests of the Old Covenant, who had to repeatedly offer sacrifices for their own sins and for those of the people, Jesus, being without sin, offered His own blood to secure eternal redemption (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:14</span>).

<!-- p22 -->
3.  **King &amp; Judge:** Jesus is the eternal King, reigning in righteousness and justice. As the Son of David, He fulfills the promise of an eternal kingdom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2SA" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Samuel 7:16</span>). His reign is not merely earthly, but cosmic, ruling over all creation and establishing peace through His victory over sin and death (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 2:9-11</span>). Jesus will return as king and judge and will judge ‘the living and the dead’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 10:42</span>).

<!-- p23 -->
In Christ, the roles of Prophet, Priest, and King converge and are perfectly fulfilled. As the Mediator of the New Covenant, He bridges the gap between a holy God and sinful humanity, enabling believers to approach God with confidence (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 4:16</span>) and securing the eternal inheritance promised in Him.

## The Apostles' Creed

<!-- p25 -->
The *Apostles' Creed* is not found in Scripture; it was created and modified based on the church’s earliest teachings. A creed is a formal confession of beliefs; think of it as an oath of allegiance combined with a statement of faith. Creeds are important because they are used to unify and separate those who believe in a certain way from those who do not. In the United States, it is common in civic functions, such as town council meetings or legislative gatherings, for participants to recite the Pledge of Allegiance. The pledge is widely used in the United States, but it is not used in other countries, because the citizens of those countries do not owe allegiance to the United States. The pledge is not what makes a person ‘American,’ but it represents a meaningful essential belief for those who are citizens.

<!-- p26 -->
The earliest recognizable versions of the creed were written in the 4th century A.D. The creed has changed slightly over the centuries and has small variations by denomination. Not all Christian denominations use the creed, but it is largely widely accepted, in some form, throughout Christendom.

<!-- p27 -->
The Apostles' Creed has been used as a teaching and catechism tool for centuries and communicates the most fundamental truths of the Christian faith, the foundational beliefs that must be acknowledged and held in order to be a Christian. Some denominations have a formal catechism process, by which a new believer joins that specific denomination and is indoctrinated in its beliefs.

<!-- p28 -->
A person does not need to fully understand these beliefs to draw near to God and receive salvation; salvation is by faith, not by a doctrinal test! However, as we continue in that faith, we all must come to understand these foundational truths.

## Brief explanation of the Apostles' Creed

<!-- p31 -->
The foundational truths in the Apostles' Creed are numerous and quite profound; a complete explanation would certainly require many full volumes to unfold. Although this chapter is about Jesus, many of the topics intersect with the other members of the Trinity. This will be an abbreviated explanation of the creed, focusing only on the most central themes. Several of these topics are studied in greater depth throughout the chapters of this book.

<!-- p32 -->
#### *I believe in God, the Father almighty, creator of heaven and earth.*

<!-- p33 -->
This affirms belief in God as the sovereign and almighty Creator and Father. The title “Father” emphasizes God’s loving relationship with His creation, particularly with His children by faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:12</span>). As Creator, He spoke and the universe came into existence, bringing order out of chaos (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 1:1-3</span>). His omnipotent power is displayed in the vastness of creation, while His fatherly care is seen in His continual sustenance and providence. This foundational statement establishes God as the origin and ruler of all life, worthy of worship and trust.

<!-- p34 -->
#### *I believe in Jesus Christ, His only Son, our Lord.*

<!-- p35 -->
Jesus Christ is the only begotten Son of God, eternally begotten, not created, and a participant in the divine nature (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:1-2</span>, 14). Jesus is the ‘Word’ from the beginning, and He became flesh and lived among us. As “Christ,” He is the anointed Messiah, fulfilling the Old Testament promises of a Savior. The title “Lord” acknowledges His authority over all creation and His rightful place as ruler and King (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 2:10-11</span>). He is not only Lord, but the Lord who saves (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 16:31</span>).

<!-- p36 -->
Jesus is not **only** sent from God; He **is** part of God. Jesus remains in the Father and the Father remains in Jesus. The Gospel of John repeatedly establishes that Jesus and the Father are one (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="30" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:30</span>), that Jesus did not act on His own (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 5:19</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="49-50" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 12:49-50</span>) and that everything He did was to glorify His Father (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="17" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 17:4</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:13</span>), and was done by the authority and power of His Father (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 8:28-29</span>).

<!-- p37 -->
#### *He was conceived by the power of the Holy Spirit and born of the Virgin Mary.*

<!-- p38 -->
This affirms the miraculous conception of Jesus by the Holy Spirit, highlighting His divine origin. Born of the Virgin Mary, Jesus fulfills the prophecy of <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 7:14</span>, which foretold the virgin birth of the Messiah. This event marks the incarnation, when the eternal Word of God took on human flesh (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:14</span>). Being both fully God and fully human, Jesus bridges the gap between God and humanity, enabling Him to be the perfect mediator and Savior.

<!-- p39 -->
Jesus was conceived by the seed of the Holy Spirit and did not inherit a sinful nature that we, born of the seed of men, have. Jesus was tempted and tested in all things, but He was perfect and without sin. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 4:15</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 5:21</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:22</span>.

<!-- p40 -->
#### *He suffered under Pontius Pilate, was crucified, dead, and buried.*

<!-- p41 -->
The suffering and death of Jesus were real, historical events that occurred under the Roman governor Pontius Pilate, grounding the Christian faith in verifiable history. His crucifixion was the fulfillment of Old Testament prophecies, such as <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="53" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 53:5</span>, which speaks of the Suffering Servant who would bear the sins of many. Jesus’ death was sacrificial, taking the penalty of sin on behalf of humanity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:24</span>). His burial confirms the reality of his death and prepares for the significance of the resurrection.

<!-- p42 -->
#### *He descended to the dead.*

<!-- p43 -->
After his death, Jesus descended to the realm of the dead, often called Hades or Sheol in Scripture. This act demonstrates his full identification with humanity, even in death, and signifies his victory over sin, death, and the devil (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 3:18-20</span>). In descending, He proclaimed his triumph and the consummation of his redemptive work.

<!-- p44 -->
#### *On the third day He rose again.*

<!-- p45 -->
The resurrection of Jesus is the cornerstone of our Christian faith, confirming his divinity and the truth of his teachings. By rising from the dead, He defeated sin and death, providing the assurance of eternal life for believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 15:17-22</span>). The resurrection also fulfills the Scriptures, including Jesus’ own predictions (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 16:21</span>). This miraculous event turned the disciples’ despair into bold faith, forming the foundation of the early Church’s witness.

<!-- p46 -->
#### *He ascended into heaven, and is seated at the right hand of the Father.*

<!-- p47 -->
The ascension of Jesus marks his return to the Father’s presence in glory. Seated at the right hand of the Father (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:20</span>), He assumes the role of our eternal High Priest, interceding for believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 7:25</span>). His position of authority also signifies his exaltation as King, reigning over all creation and directing the mission of the Church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:22</span>) to do greater works (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:12</span>) and fulfilling the Great Commission that He gave us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:18-20</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 16:15-18</span>). The ascension assures believers of his continuing presence and power through the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p48 -->
#### *He will come again to judge the living and the dead.*

<!-- p49 -->
This points to the future return of Christ, when He will bring final justice and restoration. Jesus will judge all people, rewarding the faithful and bringing justice to the impenitent (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 4:1</span>). His return will fulfill God’s definitive plan for creation, inaugurating His eternal Kingdom of righteousness and peace. This hope encourages believers to live faithfully and expectantly, knowing that their labor in the Lord is not in vain (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="58" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 15:58</span>).

<!-- p50 -->
#### *I believe in the Holy Spirit.*

<!-- p51 -->
The Holy Spirit, the third person of the Trinity, is the presence and power of God active in the world today. He convicts of sin, regenerates hearts, and empowers believers to live holy lives (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:8-13</span>). The Spirit unites Christians as one body, the Church, and equips them with spiritual gifts for service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:4-11</span>). He is the Comforter, Advocate, and Guide, indwelling believers as the seal of their eternal inheritance (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>).

<!-- p52 -->
#### *The holy catholic Church, the communion of saints.*

<!-- p53 -->
The "holy catholic Church" refers to the universal body of Christ, which comprises all believers across time and space. The Church is set apart for God’s purposes, called to reflect his holiness and share the gospel. The "communion of saints" emphasizes the unity of all Christians, living and dead, as members of the body of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:4-6</span>). This communion transcends earthly divisions, demonstrating the shared hope and mission of believers.

<!-- p54 -->
Jesus is the head of the church. The church is called both the bride of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="25-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:25-27</span>) and the body of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 1:18</span>).

<!-- p55 -->
#### *The forgiveness of sins.*

<!-- p56 -->
This highlights the heart of the gospel: through the sacrificial death of Jesus, God offers full forgiveness of sins to all who repent and believe (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:7</span>). This forgiveness restores the broken relationship between humanity and God, removing guilt and shame. It also calls believers to extend forgiveness to others, reflecting the grace they have received (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 6:14-15</span>).

<!-- p57 -->
#### *The resurrection of the body.*

<!-- p58 -->
Like Jesus, we will experience a bodily resurrection at the end of the age. This resurrection will transform mortal, perishable bodies into glorified, imperishable bodies, suited for eternal life (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42-44" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 15:42-44</span>). It underscores the Christian hope of restoration, not only for individuals but for all creation, as God makes all things new (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="21" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 21:4-5</span>).

<!-- p59 -->
#### *And eternal life. Amen.*

<!-- p60 -->
Eternal life with God is the supreme promise of the Christian faith. This life begins now through a relationship with Christ and will be fully realized in the new heavens and new earth, where believers will dwell with God forever (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 22:1-5</span>). This final assertion reflects the joy and confidence of salvation, echoing the confidence and the hope expressed throughout the Creed.

<!-- p61 -->
## Conclusion

<!-- p62 -->
In the fundamental truths expressed in the Apostles’ Creed, we see the life and work of Jesus as central to God’s plan for humanity. Each statement in the Creed captures key aspects of our belief in God, and who Jesus is-His divinity, humanity, mission, and final triumph. These truths point to His unique role as the Mediator of the New Covenant. As Mediator He invites us to draw near to God with confidence, trusting in His perfect sacrifice and ongoing intercession. This calls us to worship Him as the rightful King and to follow His teachings as the supreme Prophet. Ultimately, the study of Jesus leads to a deeper relationship with Him, transforming us into people who live in the light of His grace and truth.

<!-- p63 -->
Understanding who Jesus is and what He accomplished is the foundation of the Christian faith. The many names, titles, and roles of Jesus illuminate different facets of His character and mission, providing a more complete portrait of His love and authority.

<!-- p64 -->
As with our study about God, the Scriptures are filled with various names that refer to Jesus. Each name represents a specific aspect of His whole character, ministry, and role. These names were given to us to reveal an aspect of Jesus.
