<!-- p0 -->
# Chapter 9 - The Church &amp; Our Role in It

<!-- p1 -->
The church is how God reveals Himself on earth—through His people, doing His work. We are called to be His body; we are His hands and His feet to accomplish His work and His purpose. Without our efforts, the harvest will remain in the field and go uncollected.

<!-- p2 -->
## Who Is the Church?

<!-- p3 -->
The church, ***Ekklesia** (eh-klee-SEE-ah - **G1577**),* is derived from two Greek words:

<!-- p4 -->
- **"Ek"**: "Out of."

<!-- p5 -->
- **"Kaleo"**: "To call."

<!-- p6 -->
Together, **Ekklesia** literally means "called out" or "called-out assembly." In the city-states of ancient Greece, it referred to a gathering of citizens summoned for a specific purpose, such as legislative or judicial meetings. It was a secular term that denoted an assembly of people.

<!-- p7 -->
In the New Testament, it is used primarily to refer to the community of believers in Jesus Christ. It can denote:

<!-- p8 -->
- **The Local Church**: A specific gathering of believers in a particular location (e.g., the church in Corinth, Philippi, etc.).

<!-- p9 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p10 -->
> **<u>1 Corinthians 1:2</u>** (NKJV)
>
> To the church of God which is at Corinth, to those who are sanctified in Christ Jesus, called to be saints, with all who in every place call on the name of Jesus Christ our Lord, both theirs and ours:
>

<!-- p11 -->
</span>

<!-- p12 -->
- **The Universal Church**: The collective body of all believers across time and space.

<!-- p13 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p14 -->
> **<u>Matthew 16:18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And I also say to you that you are Peter, and on this rock I will build My church, and the gates of Hades shall not prevail against it.
>

<!-- p15 -->
</span>

<!-- p16 -->
The church is the people, not the building or the place where they meet. **Called Out**: It reflects the idea that Christians are called out of the world and set apart for God's purposes (e.g., <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:9</span>) for a specific mission and purpose (the Great Commission). Technically, the church is any two or more believers gathered (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="18" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 18:20</span>). Typically, the church is organized into regular meetings, perhaps with a set place and time, but wherever two or more people gather in the name of Jesus, that is where the church is!

<!-- p17 -->
The church was established by Jesus and grew in the first century AD through the work of the disciples who followed Him. The church we have and experience today in all its local congregations around the earth is a direct result of the original work of Christ and His apostles. As believers, we should be committed to a local church for fellowship, provision, empowerment for ministry, and to practice good works!

<!-- p18 -->
To help us understand what the church is, Scripture gives us powerful images that show what it is meant to be.

<!-- p19 -->
## Understanding the Church

<!-- p20 -->
There are several central metaphorical images used to describe attributes of the church:

<!-- p21 -->
1.  The Body

<!-- p22 -->
2.  The Bride

<!-- p23 -->
3.  The Temple

<!-- p24 -->
4.  The Family

<!-- p25 -->
5.  The Flock

<!-- p26 -->
6.  The Vine and the Branches

<!-- p27 -->
Each analogy highlights a unique aspect of the church and, together, they reveal the identity of the church as a united and diverse community under the leadership of Christ.

<!-- p28 -->
### The Body

<!-- p29 -->
The church is often described as **the Body of Christ**, emphasizing both its unity and its diversity. With Christ as the head, the church functions as a living organism in which each believer plays an essential and potentially varied role. Each member is uniquely gifted and contributes to the health and mission of the body according to his or her own function. This image highlights the interdependence of believers, specifically that the body must be connected in order to function and that our activity is collaborative, not competitive. When each part works together, the body grows and thrives in unity and love (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:12-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:15-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 1:18</span>).

<!-- p30 -->
### The Bride

<!-- p31 -->
The church is described as **the Bride of Christ**, which reflects an intimate and loving relationship with Jesus. Just as a bride is cherished and prepared for her husband, the church is sanctified and purified by Christ’s sacrificial love. This metaphor captures the devotion and faithfulness expected of the church, as well as the deep affection that Christ has for His people. The image also points to the final union between Christ and the church, celebrated at the wedding of the Lamb, where the church is presented in glory, without spot or blemish (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="25-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:25-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 19:7-9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="21" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 21:2</span>).

<!-- p32 -->
### The Temple

<!-- p33 -->
The church is described as **the Temple of God**, the dwelling place of His Spirit. Believers are described as living stones being built together upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets, with Christ Himself as the cornerstone. This sacred structure signifies God’s presence among His people and their role as a community of worship and prayer. The image of the temple emphasizes the importance of holiness and the church’s collective purpose in glorifying God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 3:16-17</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:19-22</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:4-5</span>).

<!-- p34 -->
### The Family

<!-- p35 -->
The church is described as **the Family of God**, emphasizing relational unity and care among its members. By the Spirit of adoption, believers are made children of God and members of His family. This family image highlights the love, belonging, and shared inheritance that believers have in Christ. It also reflects the call to treat one another with compassion and humility, as brothers and sisters in the family of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:19</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:15-17</span>). Moreover, through the image of the family, it shows how church members are to become mature, being taught and nurtured by spiritual mothers and fathers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:1-2</span>), so that we may move from babies who drink the pure milk of the Word to fully grown adults, able to eat the solid food of the Word (Hebrews 5).

<!-- p36 -->
### The Flock

<!-- p37 -->
The church is described as **the Flock**, sheep under the care of Jesus, the Good Shepherd. This image conveys the church’s dependence on Christ for guidance, provision, and protection. It also emphasizes the responsibility of church leaders to faithfully shepherd God’s flock as under-shepherds, mirroring Christ’s example of love and care. The image of the flock speaks of trust, obedience, and the close relationship between the Great Shepherd and His sheep (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:14-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:2</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>).

<!-- p38 -->
### The Vine and the Branches

<!-- p39 -->
The church is described as **the Vine and the Branches**, illustrating the essential connection between believers and Christ. Jesus is the true vine, and believers are the branches who must abide in Him to bear fruit. This metaphor highlights dependence on Christ for spiritual vitality, growth, and fruitfulness. Without Him, believers can do nothing, but by abiding in Him, they glorify God and fulfill their purpose. Jesus will prune so that He may bear more fruit in our lives (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:1-5</span>).

<!-- p40 -->
## Functions of the Church

<!-- p41 -->
The mission of the church, as well as the role of the Holy Spirit, can be understood through three dimensions: upward, inward, and outward, each reflecting a unique aspect of its calling in God’s plan. These dimensions guide the church in fulfilling its role as a community of worship, edification, and mission.

<!-- p42 -->
### Upward: Worship and Glorification of God

<!-- p43 -->
The first purpose of the church is to worship and glorify God. Corporate worship, where believers gather to exalt God together, is a visible reflection of His glory and a response to His greatness. As the psalmist declares, “*Worship the Lord in the splendor of holiness; tremble before Him, all the earth*” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="96" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 96:9</span>). In addition to corporate worship, the church is called to live lives of obedience and sacrifice, offering themselves as “living sacrifices” as an act of spiritual worship (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:1-2</span>). This upward focus keeps the church centered on the majesty of God and aligns its priorities to His will.

<!-- p44 -->
### Inward: Edifying the Body

<!-- p45 -->
The Great Commission commands us to ‘teach them to observe’; this command goes far beyond formal teaching methods like this book. Making disciples (discussed further in the chapter on discipleship) is to form the follower into the likeness of the discipline, like a father raises his child. Good parents do not hand a five-year-old an encyclopedia and challenge them to figure it out! Instead, they model, demonstrate, correct, guide, nurture, love, care for, and shepherd.

<!-- p46 -->
Parents sacrifice for their children because they love them, because they are part of the same body, because they share the same blood. As members of the body and family of Christ, we share the same body, we share the same blood. Communion reminds us, through its symbolism, that we have been united to one another through the sacrifice of Christ. The act of communion should be a point of unity, reminding us that we all need redemption and that salvation comes only from God. In this unity we see that no part of the body is greater than another, except our head, Christ.

<!-- p47 -->
### Outward: Reaching the World

<!-- p48 -->
The best-known part of the church’s mission extends outward to reach the world with the gospel. Jesus’ Great Commission calls His followers to evangelize and make disciples of all nations (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19-20</span>). Empowered by the Holy Spirit, the church is to be His witnesses to the ends of the earth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>).

<!-- p49 -->
Jesus is the light of the world (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 8:12</span>). Light shines in every direction outward from the source. His light shines through His manifestations: the Word, His Spirit, and us, His body. We are the light of the world; with our lives we must radiate His light into our darkened worlds and demonstrate good works that glorify God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 5:14-16</span>). In practice, this means that the church is called to advocate for justice and mercy, both spiritual and physical. As James says, our pure worship is to care for the vulnerable, the orphan, the widow (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:27</span>).

<!-- p50 -->
## The Leadership of the Church

<!-- p51 -->
Across all of Christendom there is a great variety of denominations, gatherings, meetings, and assemblies. There are very few universal rules for church leadership, but the rules we do have are very significant:

<!-- p52 -->
1.  Jesus is the only head of the Church. All serve Him (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 1:18</span>).

<!-- p53 -->
2.  There are high standards for those who lead; they are worthy of double honor, for they will receive double judgment (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:17</span>).

<!-- p54 -->
2.1  They must be mature, not proud, accountable (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:1-13</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 1:5-9</span>).

<!-- p55 -->
2.2.  They must be servants of all (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42-45" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 10:42-45</span>).

<!-- p56 -->
2.3.  They must devote themselves to the Word and to prayer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:4</span>).

<!-- p57 -->
2.4.  They are responsible for equipping the body for the work of the ministry.

<!-- p58 -->
3.  There are no greater or lesser members in the body. The Scriptures that support this are:

<!-- p59 -->
3.1.  There must be no partiality between rich and poor (James 2).

<!-- p60 -->
3.2. There is no division between Jew and Gentile, slave, or free, male or female (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 3:27-28</span>).

<!-- p61 -->
The structure of the Church, in addition to leadership rules, is important, but much of this is situational and a matter of tradition, rather than something universally dictated. “<u>This is how we've always done it!</u>” is a popular statement; if you go back far enough in time, there was usually a good reason for doing it that way. This is usually neither good nor bad, but a matter of taste, needs, and availability.

<!-- p62 -->
A helpful example of tradition being the basis for structure is family dinner time. The most important part of family dinner time is that everyone is fed and that the meal is nutritious. The variations are endless: what they eat, how they eat, how they season the food, etc. The various combinations are generally acceptable and, largely, a matter of taste and preference.

<!-- p63 -->
A church with 10,000 members has different needs and capabilities than a church of 50. The pattern established in the book of Acts and throughout the canon of the New Testament was the model of **elders** and **deacons**. Though leadership structures may look different among churches and denominations, ultimately that model represents the minimum viable leadership structure for the church. Let's take a look at these leadership positions and how they are selected.

<!-- p64 -->
### Elders (Overseers)

<!-- p65 -->
Roles and Responsibilities:

<!-- p66 -->
- **Spiritual Leadership** - Provide oversight, guidance, and pastoral care for the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:2</span>).

<!-- p67 -->
- **Teaching and Preaching** - Responsible for teaching sound doctrine and guarding against false teachings (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:17</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 1:9</span>).

<!-- p68 -->
- **Shepherd the Flock** - Care for the spiritual needs of the congregation, modeling humility and service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:2-3</span>).

<!-- p69 -->
- **Protect the Church** - Serve as watchmen, guarding the church from false doctrines and division (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29-31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:29-31</span>).

<!-- p70 -->
- **Equip the Saints** - Train and equip believers for works of ministry and discipleship (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-12</span>).

<!-- p71 -->
Selection Process:

<!-- p72 -->
- **Appointed in Each Church** - The elders were appointed by the apostles or their representatives, often through prayer and fasting (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 14:23</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 1:5</span>).

<!-- p73 -->
- **Confirmed by the Holy Spirit** - Their appointment was seen as Spirit-led, recognizing God's calling and gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>).

<!-- p74 -->
- **Tested and Approved** - Candidates needed to demonstrate maturity and faithfulness before leadership was entrusted to them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:10</span>). They cannot be recent converts! ((<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:6</span>)

<!-- p75 -->
### Deacons (Servants)

<!-- p76 -->
Roles and Responsibilities:

<!-- p77 -->
- **Practical Service** - Focused on meeting physical needs in the church, freeing the elders to focus on prayer and teaching (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-4</span>).

<!-- p78 -->
- **Support Ministry** - Assist with hospitality, care for the poor, and administrative tasks (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:3</span>).

<!-- p79 -->
- **Promote Unity** - Address issues of fairness and care to prevent division (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-7</span>).

<!-- p80 -->
- **Examples of Faith** - Serve as models in humility, faith, and service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:13</span>).

<!-- p81 -->
Selection Process (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-6</span>):

<!-- p82 -->
- **Recognized by the Congregation** - Chosen based on their reputation and spiritual character.

<!-- p83 -->
- **Approved by the Leaders** - The apostles evaluated and commissioned the chosen deacons through prayer and the laying on of hands.

<!-- p84 -->
- **Confirmed in Service** - The deacons were first tested to prove their faithfulness before being officially appointed (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:10</span>).

<!-- p85 -->
### Qualifications for Leadership

<!-- p86 -->
#### Qualifications for Elders and Deacons:

<!-- p87 -->
- Moral Character - Both must be above reproach, dignified, and demonstrate self-control (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:2</span>, 8).

<!-- p88 -->
- Family Life - Both must be faithful in marriage (“husband of one wife”) and manage their households well (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:4-5</span>, 12).

<!-- p89 -->
- Spiritual Maturity - Both must be tested and approved before appointment (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:6</span>, 10).

<!-- p90 -->
- Reputation - Both need a good reputation inside and outside the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:7</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
- Faith and Integrity - Both must hold the faith with a good conscience and live according to their beliefs (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:9</span>).

<!-- p92 -->
### Example Structures

<!-- p93 -->
Using what we have learned so far about rules and leadership structures, let's look at two examples of church structure found in the New Testament, Jerusalem and Antioch.

<!-- p94 -->
### Church in Jerusalem

<!-- p95 -->
The church in Jerusalem was led primarily by the **apostles**, who focused on teaching, prayer, and spiritual oversight (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:42</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:4</span>). As the community grew, the need for an administrative structure became evident, particularly to address practical issues such as caring for widows and distributing resources. This led to the appointment of **deacons** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-7</span>). These individuals, chosen for their spiritual maturity and good reputation, cared for practical ministries to allow the apostles to focus on their spiritual responsibilities. Over time, **elders** also emerged as leaders in the church in Jerusalem. For example, during the Jerusalem Council (Acts 15), both apostles and elders were involved in decision-making, showing a collaborative leadership structure.

<!-- p96 -->
### Church in Antioch

<!-- p97 -->
The church in Antioch had a more decentralized and diverse leadership structure compared to Jerusalem. It was led by a group of **prophets and teachers**, including Barnabas, Saul (Paul), Simeon, Lucius, and Manaen (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 13:1</span>). The church in Antioch was also much more oriented toward reaching the Gentiles, while the church in Jerusalem reflected its Jewish roots more. Although the New Testament does not explicitly mention deacons in Antioch, practical roles likely existed, given the growing size of the church and its involvement in sending aid to Jerusalem (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29-30" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 11:29-30</span>). The church in Antioch became a model for sending missionaries, with leaders praying, fasting, and laying hands on Paul and Barnabas to send them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 13:2-3</span>). This demonstrates a structure that balanced spiritual oversight (prophets and teachers) with missionary outreach and practical support.

<!-- p98 -->
Unlike Paul's corrections to the Corinthians and Galatians, the Scriptures do not criticize the leadership structures of Jerusalem or Antioch. This suggests that these churches, under the guidance of the Holy Spirit, were free to adapt their gatherings to meet their unique needs and circumstances within the scope of sound doctrine and apostolic teaching (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:42</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>).

<!-- p99 -->
Throughout these passages, certain terms are used that we have not yet defined and studied: *apostle, prophet, evangelist*. These gifts are part of the fivefold ministry that Jesus gave to the church.

<!-- p100 -->
### The five ministry gifts

<!-- p101 -->
Jesus established five distinct ministry gifts, often called the **five ministry gifts**, to equip believers for service and build up the Body of Christ.

<!-- p102 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p103 -->
> **<u>Ephesians 4:11-13</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And He Himself gave some to be apostles, some prophets, some evangelists, and some pastors and teachers,
>
> for the equipping of the saints for the work of ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ,
>
> till we all come to the unity of the faith and of the knowledge of the Son of God, to a perfect man, to the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ;
>

<!-- p104 -->
</span>

<!-- p105 -->
The five gifts given to equip the saints for the work of ministry are:

<!-- p106 -->
1.  **Apostles** - Pioneers and visionaries who lay foundations, plant churches, and advance the Kingdom of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:20</span>). Apostles model boldness and faith, demonstrating how to advance into new territories and take spiritual initiative.

<!-- p107 -->
2.  **Prophets** - Messengers and guides who proclaim God’s truth, bringing encouragement, edification, and direction (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>). Prophets inspire faith, call others to align with the Word of God, and demonstrate dependence on hearing the voice of God.

<!-- p108 -->
3.  **Evangelists** - Proclaimers of the Gospel who focus on reaching the lost and stirring others to action in sharing Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:5-8</span>). Evangelists teach disciples to share their faith effectively, model boldness in witness, and inspire compassion for the lost.

<!-- p109 -->
4.  **Pastors (shepherds)** - Caretakers and nurturers who protect, guide, and provide spiritual oversight for the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>). Pastors teach disciples the importance of community, caring for others, and accountability within the body of Christ.

<!-- p110 -->
5.  **Teachers** - Instructors and trainers who clarify doctrine and help believers grow in understanding and applying the Word of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 2:15</span>). Teachers equip disciples to study the Scriptures deeply, apply them in their daily lives, and teach others effectively.

<!-- p111 -->
## Leadership versus gifts

<!-- p112 -->
A commonly misunderstood aspect of leadership in the church is the separation between the authority and responsibility of leadership and specific gifts.

<!-- p113 -->
This confusion centers around our use of words like teacher, pastor, prophet, apostle, evangelist. These words are gifts. There is no **office** of ‘apostle’ in the New Testament church. There are people who do the work of an apostle, who have the gift or the calling of an apostle. Having the gift of an apostle does not confer authority or responsibility on them.

<!-- p114 -->
I am a skilled musician; I have played the saxophone for over 20 years. I played during school and college and played semi-professionally for several summers while working with an evangelistic team. Even though I have the skill, gift, and ability to be a saxophonist, I do not have the right to walk into your church and get on stage with the worship team.

<!-- p115 -->
Similarly, there are people gifted to be a pastor/shepherd, but that does not make them ‘THE PASTOR’ of the local church, nor does it make them ‘your’ pastor. The five ministry gifts do not lead the church; the leaders (elders and deacons) do. It is very common for leaders to have specific ministry gifts among the five, but that gift enhances their leadership; it does not create it. Likewise, it is common for people still in development with gifts among the five ministry gifts to serve and aspire to leadership, but God is interested first in character, then in gifts. We all must become mature, able to discern the Word of God, full of the Spirit, and practicing good works.

<!-- p116 -->
Every leader and every gift must first submit to being a disciple, being part of the body.

<!-- p117 -->
Jesus gave specific ministry gifts for the equipping of the saints for the work of ministry. These are different from motivational gifts (discussed later in this chapter) or manifestation gifts (discussed in the chapter on baptisms). As disciples grow, the five ministry gifts empower them for ministry, while elders and deacons provide oversight to ensure sound doctrine and practice.

<!-- p118 -->
## Your role in the church

<!-- p119 -->
So far in this chapter we have covered church leadership and the central analogies for the church. Let us develop the specific analogy of *the body* and consider a marathoner.

<!-- p120 -->
### You are part of the whole

<!-- p121 -->
The church, as the Body of Christ, depends on each member working together to fulfill its mission. Just as the body cannot function properly if a part is missing, the church needs every believer to participate actively, grow, and serve. Each member of the body has a very important role. Being part of the body means staying connected. A body can survive with reduced capacity without a member, but the member itself cannot live when separated from the body. We must be an active part of the church and not become dismembered. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:24</span> says that we should meet together and not abandon the assembly.

<!-- p122 -->
To truly work and function together, you need fellowship! You need to know others, love others, and ideally like others! Build strong relationships and friendships and repair them when they come under stress or are injured. When a joint gets hurt or inflamed, you need to work to rest it and/or set it back in place; so that the dislocated joint doesn’t end up breaking! A broken member that does not heal will end up being separated, and you cannot be part of the body, alone.

<!-- p123 -->
We do not all have the same function. Some of us may share the Gospel from a stage, others around a dinner table. But we are ALL called to be ministers of reconciliation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 5:18-19</span>). We have all been welcomed into the priesthood of all believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:9</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 1:6</span>). This means that we should all be able to do the work of the ministry by the power of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p124 -->
Toes work with feet, feet with ankles, and so on-each part contributing to the mission as a whole. Marathoners need to train if they want to be able to complete the race. You need to be ready-not just learning, but actually practicing what you have learned. The church, like a marathoner, flourishes when each member plays their role in unison.

<!-- p125 -->
There is a great number of people in your life that only you can possibly reach. The Pastor cannot be the only one trying to shepherd them, and the Evangelist cannot be the only one trying to reach them. God wants to use you to reach unbelievers through the power of His Holy Spirit. We all do the work of the ministry; the Holy Spirit helps us.

<!-- p126 -->
### You need training

<!-- p127 -->
Believers should train and equip themselves for their unique roles in the church, just as a runner strengthens specific muscles for the race. You will not succeed if you enter a marathon without having trained, and not all training is the same, you need to be properly prepared for the race. Jesus gave specific ministry gifts to empower believers, not to do all the work themselves, but to enable each member to serve effectively in their unique roles.

<!-- p128 -->
Training for a marathon will bring transformation. You need strength and you need to be transformed. It is our responsibility to submit ourselves to God and let Him transform us in renewal (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:1-2</span>). Transformation should lead you to maturity, enabling you to carry your own burdens and to support others. You should be able to bear MUCH fruit. This will be discussed extensively in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p129 -->
Training is hard, sometimes it is uncomfortable. Sometimes, the transformation you need to go through you would much rather skip. But, if you want to reach the goal, you need to stay on the narrow path. At certain times, you may need warning, comfort, or support-all offered with patience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Thessalonians 5:14</span>).

<!-- p130 -->
Every great marathoner has a coach to train them, you will need a coach to help you. Of course, you have the Holy Spirit, but He often relies on His coaches, the mature men and women of God, in the Body of Christ with whom you are called to work side by side.

<!-- p131 -->
As individuals, we need to submit, first, to God and, second, in this context, to a body of believers. Submission in the church means honoring godly leadership (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 13:17</span>) and submitting to one another in humility (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:21</span>).

<!-- p132 -->
Submission can be difficult, especially when you have previously experienced broken trust or failed leadership. This does not imply blind obedience or control, but reflects a spirit of reverence and cooperation. Worldly submission is demanded by those in authority and can be degrading and lead to resentment. Biblical submission, rooted in humility and discernment, strengthens the church and promotes accountability. Be willing to hear guidance and constructive feedback.

<!-- p133 -->
Now that we have laid a solid foundation - you are a significant part of the body, but you need training - let’s devote ourselves to discovering where you fit.

<!-- p134 -->
### Finding where you fit

<!-- p135 -->
Finding your role in the church involves seeking God’s direction, understanding your spiritual gifts, and actively engaging with your church community. It is a journey of prayer, exploration, and growth, in which you discover how God has uniquely empowered you to contribute to His kingdom. It is very important to note that your role in the church will likely change over time and will not be the same throughout your entire life.

<!-- p136 -->
Here is a practical approach to help you find your place, they are presented as a series of steps for greater clarity, but you will likely repeat the same step several times throughout your walk with God.

<!-- p137 -->
#### Step 1 - Seek God’s direction through prayer and the Word

<!-- p138 -->
Begin by **seeking God’s direction through prayer**. God is the ultimate source of your purpose and calling (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="29" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 29:11</span>). Pray regularly for clarity about how you can serve in the church, asking Him to reveal your spiritual gifts and passions. Trust that He will direct your steps as you surrender your plans to His will (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 3:5-6</span>).

<!-- p139 -->
**Study the Word of God** to deepen your understanding of the church’s mission and your role within it. Key passages such as 1 Corinthians 12 and <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-12</span> highlight the spiritual gifts and the diversity of roles in the Body of Christ. Reflect on examples of people in Scripture who discovered and fulfilled their roles, such as Paul, Timothy, and Lydia.

<!-- p140 -->
#### Step 2 - Identify your gifts, passions, and skills

<!-- p141 -->
Identifying your spiritual gifts is an essential step. You may find it helpful to take a spiritual gifts assessment. These gifts are given by God to build up the church. There are several classes of gifts listed in the New Testament:

<!-- p142 -->
- **Motivational Gifts** given by God: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:6-8</span>

<!-- p143 -->
- Discussed after this list.

<!-- p144 -->
- Fivefold Ministry Gifts given by Jesus: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-13</span>

<!-- p145 -->
- Discussed earlier in this chapter.

<!-- p146 -->
- Manifestation Gifts given by the Holy Spirit: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span>

<!-- p147 -->
- Discussed in the chapter on baptism.

<!-- p148 -->
The motivational gifts reflect how a believer is structured to think, feel, and act. They motivate believers to serve in specific ways that build up the church. Each gift complements the others, ensuring that the church operates as a unified body with diverse functions (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:4-5</span>). They operate as grace-endowed abilities (Greek: ***charis** - khar'-ece - **G5463 -** an undeserved gift*), which means they are given by God, not earned or learned. Although the gifts come from God, the person who has a gift can become more proficient in using it.

<!-- p149 -->
The motivational gifts are:

<!-- p150 -->
1.  **Prophecy** - A God-given inclination to declare truth boldly, often with a focus on conviction and righteousness, guiding others to align with the Word of God.\*

<!-- p151 -->
2.  **Service (Helps)** - Meets practical needs through acts of service and assistance to support others.

<!-- p152 -->
3.  **Teaching** - Explains and clarifies truth, helping others understand and apply the Word of God.

<!-- p153 -->
4.  **Exhortation (Encouragement)** - Motivates, encourages, and comforts others to inspire growth and perseverance.

<!-- p154 -->
5.  **Giving** - Provides resources generously to meet needs and advance the work of God without seeking recognition.

<!-- p155 -->
6.  **Leadership (Administration)** - Organizes and directs others to achieve goals with vision and efficiency.

<!-- p156 -->
7.  **Mercy** - Demonstrates compassion and care to comfort and support those who are hurting or in need.

<!-- p157 -->
\* The prophecy used in the context of Romans is understood as different from prophecy as a manifestation gift of the Holy Spirit (1 Corinthians). The Greek word used is the same in both passages, but the context is different. For a deeper study of the difference, I recommend reading:

<!-- p158 -->
- The Gift of Prophecy by Kenneth E. Hagin ([www.rhema.org](http://www.rhema.org))

<!-- p159 -->
<!-- -->

<!-- p160 -->
- *The Gifts and Ministries of the Holy Spirit* by Lester Sumrall

<!-- p161 -->
As you identify your gifts, consider your passions and abilities. God often calls us to serve in areas we are passionate about or in which we have skill (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 31:2-6</span>). Reflect on what excites you about serving God and others and think about how your professional skills, hobbies, and life experiences can contribute to ministry.

<!-- p162 -->
Seek guidance from leaders and mentors who can offer wisdom and discernment (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 15:22</span>). Meet with a pastor or ministry leader to discuss your interests and ask for honest feedback about where you might fit best. Pray about the feedback you receive. Consider joining a discipleship group to receive training and ongoing support.

<!-- p163 -->
#### Step 3 - Be faithful now

<!-- p164 -->
The Parable of the Talents (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-30" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 25:14-30</span>) reminds us that we need to be faithful with little to be entrusted with much (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 16:10</span>).

<!-- p165 -->
Get involved and serve in various ministries to explore where God is calling you. Start modestly, volunteering in areas of need in your church and in the ministries it supports. For example:

<!-- p166 -->
- Join the welcome team, the children's ministry, or outreach programs. Consider leading a discipleship-oriented group for new believers on this book *Principles of Faith,* using the group discussion questions and the activities at the end of each chapter to facilitate dialogue.

<!-- p167 -->
- Be open to stepping out of your comfort zone to discover new opportunities.

<!-- p168 -->
- Notice where God works through you and where you feel most effective.

<!-- p169 -->
Practice giving. We can give to the church in many important ways, and we are instructed to do so to fulfill the mission.

<!-- p170 -->
- Time - we can give our time and volunteer. By giving our time, we employ our efforts and talents. This can also be a great way to learn new skills and sharpen the ones we already have.

<!-- p171 -->
- Resources - we can give from our resources. More commonly today, this likely means contributing financially to the church and to ministries (not just the "institution," but the needy in the church). Contributing money is helpful, because the church can purchase needed resources with it, but people have given their material resources beyond money, for example extra houses, clothing, cars, food, building materials, tools, etc. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="32-35" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 4:32-35</span> we see an example of the early believers giving freely, sharing the proceeds from the sale of extra land and houses, and contributing so that 'no one was in need'.

<!-- p172 -->
Working alongside others in ministry can reveal your strengths and deepen your understanding of your role. Participate in small groups or church activities to foster connections. Listen to the burdens and needs of those around you and consider how you can support them. Building relationships within the church helps you connect with others and understand the needs of the community.

<!-- p173 -->
#### Step 4 - Seek help!

<!-- p174 -->
Jesus said that in life we would have tribulation! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:33</span>) We are encouraged not to carry the weight of our burdens alone, but to cast them on God, who cares for us! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:7</span>)

<!-- p175 -->
Sometimes, on our journey, we can get hurt. Marathon runners, even while doing "everything right," can push their bodies to the point of injury, or they can stumble and fall.

<!-- p176 -->
Not every 'fall' is related to sin, but some of it is; not every weakness is 'our fault', sometimes others will sin against us. Regardless of the cause of our weakness or injury, we should be restored and healed.

<!-- p177 -->
There is great freedom in seeking inner healing and restoration. We are called to cover the vulnerable parts of the body (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:23</span>) and to restore those who are caught in transgressions (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:1</span>).

<!-- p178 -->
There is much to say on this topic, and we explore the themes of healing as an overview in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p179 -->
#### Step 5 - Be patient

<!-- p180 -->
Pay attention to confirmation from God and from others. Often, God confirms your calling through fruitfulness, joy, and affirmation from the people around you. Reflect on where your efforts have borne fruit and listen to feedback about the impact of the ministry. This can help you recognize where you are most effective in serving the Body of Christ.

<!-- p181 -->
Be patient and trust in God’s timing. Discovering your role can take time and involve trial and error (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 1:6</span>). Trust that God will guide you as you remain faithful and open to His direction. During this period, commit to growth and preparation by participating in workshops, Bible studies, and other resources that equip you for service. Do this with the church; engage with the Body of Christ to discover your gifts.

<!-- p182 -->
Remember: the church is not a building or an institution; it is a living, pulsing body, made up of people like you. By taking up your role, you contribute to its mission and glorify Christ as its head.
