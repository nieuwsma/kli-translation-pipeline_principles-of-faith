# Chapter 11 - Baptisms-Water and Spirit

<!-- p1 -->
Baptism in our modern era, separated from Jewish roots, is typically thought of only in terms of water baptism and baptism in the Holy Spirit (Pentecost).

<!-- p2 -->
The book of Hebrews was written to a Jewish audience. The vast majority of Christians in our modern era have no Jewish roots or heritage. Without Jewish roots and culture, many of us lack the cultural context for things that first-century Messianic Jews (those believing Jews who professed Jesus) would share. Since we were not raised in the Jewish tradition, we typically do not know much about the feasts, fasts, prayers, washings, rites, and rituals that Jewish believers would have known.

<!-- p3 -->
The word for baptisms, or washings, in Greek is: **Baptismos** (bap-tis-mos’ - **G909**) - The act of dipping or washing. It refers to ceremonial washings or ritual purification. It can indicate immersion, purification rites, or washings practiced in Jewish and Christian contexts.

<!-- p4 -->
Baptisms is one of the foundational doctrines mentioned in Hebrews:

> **<u>Hebrews 6:1-2</u>** (NKJV)
>

## Old Covenant Washings - Historical Context

<!-- p9 -->
In the Old Covenant, ceremonial washings played a central role in maintaining ritual purity and symbolizing holiness before God. These washings were prescribed in the Law of Moses and applied both to the priests who served in the Tabernacle and to the people of Israel. For example, the priests were required to wash their hands and feet at the bronze basin before entering the Tabernacle or approaching the altar, symbolizing their readiness to serve in the presence of a holy God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="30" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 30:18-21</span>). Similarly, rituals for the purification of individuals from uncleanness, such as contact with a corpse or skin diseases, involved washing with water as a sign of restoration to purity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LEV" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Leviticus 14:8</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="NUM" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Numbers 19:17-19</span>). These acts served as outward demonstrations of an inward desire for holiness, emphasizing the importance of being set apart for God.

<!-- p10 -->
Ceremonial washings in the Old Testament required regular repetition to maintain ritual purity. Unlike permanent solutions, these rituals provided only a temporary external cleansing, leaving unresolved the deeper issue of sin. The writer of Hebrews highlights this limitation, explaining that these practices were “symbolic for the present time,” unable to purify the worshiper’s conscience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:9-10</span>). These washings acted as constant reminders of human imperfection and the need for something greater-a definitive cleansing that could wash not only the body but also the heart and soul.

<!-- p11 -->
The ceremonial washings of the Old Covenant find their fulfillment in Christ, who provides complete and eternal cleansing through His sacrifice. Unlike the temporary rituals of the law, the blood of Christ fully purifies, cleansing not only the flesh but also the conscience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:13-14</span>). Through His death and resurrection, Jesus accomplished what the Old Testament rituals only prefigured—true reconciliation with God. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:22</span> calls believers to “draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, having our hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience and our bodies washed with pure water.” This language relates Christ’s work to the imagery of Old Testament washings while showing its far superior efficacy. His sacrifice not only covers sin but removes it completely (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 1:7</span>). Thus, the ceremonial washings served as a shadow, pointing to the deeper cleansing that only Christ could provide, satisfying the human need for holiness through divine intervention.

<!-- p12 -->
To reiterate this point, Christians have no obligation to perform any ceremonial washing. Washings did not provide salvation in the old covenant and do not provide salvation in the new covenant. Ceremonial washing will not enhance our standing before God, nor obtain favor for us. Paul strongly condemns the Judaizers of Galatia (read Galatians) who attempted to mix requirements from the Jewish context as necessary for salvation (e.g., circumcision). Salvation is by faith in Jesus alone!

<!-- p13 -->
## The Baptism of John

<!-- p14 -->
Within the New Testament canon there are several baptisms mentioned. We have already discussed the baptisms and washings of the Jewish tradition.

<!-- p15 -->
The ministry of John the Baptist centered on preparing the way for the coming of Jesus Christ. His baptism, described as a baptism of repentance for the remission of sins (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 1:4</span>), called people to confess their sins, turn their hearts to God, and prepare for the arrival of the Messiah. It served as an outward sign of inward repentance, symbolizing the desire for spiritual cleansing and readiness for the kingdom of God. John’s baptism, however, was temporary and preparatory—it pointed to the greater work that Christ would accomplish. While John’s baptism focused on repentance, it did not provide complete forgiveness nor spiritual regeneration, as it lacked the indwelling and empowering of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:11-12</span>). Instead, John openly declared that one greater than he would come, who would baptize not only with water but with the Holy Spirit and with fire (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 3:16</span>).

<!-- p16 -->
John’s baptism was fulfilled and surpassed through the death, burial, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. Christian baptism, instituted by Jesus and practiced by the apostles, signifies a deeper reality—union with Christ, death to sin, and resurrection to new life (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 6:3-4</span>). Unlike John’s baptism, Christian baptism is directly connected to faith in the atoning sacrifice of Jesus and the indwelling of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:38</span>). The arrival of the Holy Spirit at Pentecost marked the new covenant and the beginning of a Spirit-empowered life, something John’s baptism could not provide. Therefore, John’s baptism is no longer practiced, as it was replaced by the baptism ordained by Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19</span>), which incorporates repentance, faith, and the sealing of believers by the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>).

<!-- p17 -->
## The Baptism of Christ

<!-- p18 -->
The baptism of Christ established a powerful example for His followers and laid the foundation for the Christian practice of water baptism. Although Jesus was without sin, He submitted to baptism by John to “fulfill all righteousness” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:15</span>). In doing so, He identified with humanity’s need for repentance and pointed to His own death, burial, and resurrection. At His baptism, the Holy Spirit descended like a dove, and the Father declared, “This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:16-17</span>). This moment marked the beginning of Jesus’ public ministry and revealed the unity of the Father, the Son, and the Spirit, foreshadowing the spiritual transformation that believers would experience through faith in Christ.

<!-- p19 -->
Afterward, Jesus commanded His followers to be baptized, commissioning them to “go and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19</span>). Water baptism, as instituted by Christ, became an **act of obedience** and a **public declaration of faith** for believers. It represents their identification with the death, burial, and resurrection of Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 6:3-4</span>). Going down into the waters symbolizes dying to sin, and rising from the waters illustrates being raised to new life in Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:12</span>). Still, it is crucial to clarify that this outward act is not the source of salvation, but a **visible sign** of the **internal work of grace** already accomplished by the blood of Jesus.

<!-- p20 -->
Water baptism is a **symbol** of the cleansing power of the blood of Christ and of the renewal of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:26</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>). It signifies repentance, faith, and a commitment to follow Jesus. It is an outward testimony of an inner transformation-a way for believers to publicly declare their loyalty to Christ and their participation in the body of Christ, the Church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:41</span>). Although it is commanded by Jesus and exemplified in the early Church, this does not **save** a person. Salvation is by grace through faith alone (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>), and true cleansing occurs spiritually through the blood of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 1:5</span>). Water baptism is a response to that salvation, an act of obedience and not a requirement for it.

<!-- p21 -->
In the New Testament, water baptism is consistently linked to **repentance and faith** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:38</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 16:16</span>), both of which require a personal decision to follow Christ-something babies are not able to make. Baptism symbolizes the believer’s deliberate identification with the death, burial, and resurrection of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 6:3-4</span>), as well as their commitment to live in obedience to Him. Some denominations (e.g., Catholics, Lutherans) practice infant baptism as a covenant sign similar to circumcision; however, since babies cannot express personal faith or repentance, the practice of **infant baptism** does not align with the biblical model of **believers’ baptism**. Instead, many churches practice **dedication ceremonies** for babies, following the example of Hannah dedicating Samuel to the Lord (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1SA" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Samuel 1:27-28</span>) and of Mary and Joseph presenting Jesus in the temple (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 2:22</span>). Dedication is not a substitute for baptism, but rather a commitment by parents to raise their child in the faith, teaching them the Word of God and modeling a godly life until the child is sufficiently mature to make their own decision to follow Christ and be baptized.

<!-- p22 -->
In contrast to the **ceremonial washings** of the Old Covenant, which required repeated rituals for purification, Christian baptism is a one-time event that reflects a completed work of grace. The washings of the Old Testament symbolized external cleansing and foreshadowed the deeper spiritual cleansing that comes through Christ. In contrast, New Testament baptism symbolizes the inner renewal and new birth accomplished by the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>). It points to the believer’s participation in the new covenant, where the cleansing from sin is permanent and based on the finished work of Christ.

<!-- p23 -->
Water baptism is closely linked to the **promise of the Holy Spirit**. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:38</span>, Peter urges people to repent, be baptized, and receive the gift of the Holy Spirit. Although water baptism publicly signifies the believer’s repentance and faith, the true baptism occurs inwardly when the Holy Spirit washes, regenerates, and seals the believer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:13</span>). This spiritual washing makes us members of the body of Christ and enables us to live holy lives. The **role of the Holy Spirit** in baptism reminds us that salvation is not accomplished through rituals, but by the transforming power of God.

<!-- p24 -->
It is important to distinguish between the **symbolic act of water baptism** and the spiritual reality of salvation by the blood of Christ. The Bible clearly teaches that salvation is based on faith in Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 10:9-10</span>) and not on works, including baptism (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>). However, water baptism is an **act of obedience** that publicly identifies the believer with Christ and with the Church. It demonstrates an outward commitment to live as a disciple of Jesus, but it neither adds to nor completes the work of salvation.

<!-- p25 -->
Christ’s water baptism set an example to be followed by believers, demonstrating humility, obedience, and preparation for ministry. For Christians today, water baptism serves as a public declaration of faith, symbolizing death to sin and resurrection to a new life. Although it is a commandment of Jesus and an important step of obedience, it is not necessary for salvation. True cleansing occurs through faith in the blood of Jesus, and the empowerment for the Christian life comes through the work of the Holy Spirit in the believer. Baptism unites us to Christ and to His Church, reminding us of the inner transformation and renewal that God has already accomplished.

<!-- p26 -->
Having established water baptism as an external sign of faith, we now turn to baptism in the Holy Spirit, which empowers believers for ministry and service.

<!-- p27 -->
## Baptism in the Holy Spirit

<!-- p28 -->
Baptism in the Holy Spirit is a profound and transformative experience promised by God to empower believers for ministry, service, and holy living. Every believer receives the Holy Spirit when they are born again-that is how we are made spiritually alive. But afterward, we must also receive baptism in the Holy Spirit, which empowers us for boldness, spiritual gifts, and effective ministry.

<!-- p29 -->
Water baptism is a public declaration of faith and symbolizes the cleansing of sin by the blood of Christ, baptism in the Spirit represents the empowering presence of God in the life of the believer. Together, these two modes of baptism form complementary aspects of the Christian walk-one as an external sign of obedience and the other as an internal source of power and renewal. Baptism in the Holy Spirit is the Spirit upon us rather than within. At salvation all believers receive the Spirit within them, but when the Spirit of God comes upon them, they receive power and boldness for ministry.

<!-- p30 -->
Baptism in the Holy Spirit was foretold by John the Baptist, who declared,

<!-- p31 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p32 -->
> **<u>Matthew 3:11</u>** (NKJV)
>
> I indeed baptize you with water unto repentance, but He who comes after me is mightier than I, whose sandals I am not worthy to carry. He will baptize you with the Holy Spirit and fire.
>

<!-- p33 -->
</span>

<!-- p34 -->
This promise pointed to a greater work that Jesus would accomplish after His resurrection-sending the Holy Spirit to dwell within and empower His followers. Jesus confirmed this when He instructed His disciples to wait for the gift of the Spirit, explaining that they would receive power to be His witnesses (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>). The fulfillment of this promise occurred on the Day of Pentecost, when the disciples were filled with the Holy Spirit, evidenced by speaking in tongues, prophecy, and boldness to proclaim the gospel (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:1-4</span>).

<!-- p35 -->
This outpouring marked the beginning of the **Church Age** and demonstrated that the power of the Spirit was now available to all believers. This promise was not limited to the apostles, but extends to all who believe, as Peter declared, *“The promise is for you and for your children and for all who are far off-for all whom the Lord our God will call”* (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="39" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:39</span>).

### Spirit Within vs Spirit Upon

<!-- p37 -->
When someone places their faith in Jesus, the Holy Spirit gives them new life on the inside. That is what it means to be born again (**regenerated**). At that moment of faith, the Holy Spirit dwells **WITHIN** the believer, making them spiritually alive and adopting them into the family of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:5-6</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>). This is the new birth or being born again, as Jesus described in John 3.

<!-- p38 -->
Biblical basis:

<!-- p39 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>* - “He saved us through the washing of regeneration and renewing of the Holy Spirit.”

<!-- p40 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>* - The Holy Spirit seals believers as a **guarantee** of their inheritance.

<!-- p41 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:9</span>* - "Whoever does not have the Spirit of Christ does not belong to Him."

<!-- p42 -->
This work of regeneration is universal for all believers at salvation and establishes a permanent indwelling of the Spirit. It does not depend on any external action, such as water baptism, and guarantees salvation by grace through faith.

<!-- p43 -->
In contrast, the baptism in the Holy Spirit is a distinct and subsequent experience that empowers believers with power for service and witness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>). While regeneration deals with new life and identity, the baptism in the Spirit focuses on empowerment **UPON** and the release of spiritual gifts. This baptism is often accompanied by signs and manifestations of the gifts of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p44 -->
Biblical basis:

<!-- p45 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p46 -->
> **<u>Acts 1:8</u>** (NKJV)
>
> But you will receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you; and you will be My witnesses in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the ends of the earth."
>

<!-- p47 -->
</span>

<!-- p48 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:15-17</span>* - The Samaritans had believed and were baptized, but later received the Holy Spirit through prayer and the laying on of hands.

<!-- p49 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 19:6</span>* - Disciples in Ephesus, already believers, received the Holy Spirit when Paul laid his hands on them.

<!-- p50 -->
The baptism in the Holy Spirit does not replace regeneration, but it expands it, equipping the believer for spiritual service and boldness in ministry. Being baptized in the Holy Spirit is a free gift for those who are saved, not a requirement to be saved. It is not a requirement that someone manifest a <u>specific</u> gift of the Holy Spirit to prove their ‘baptism’ in the Holy Spirit, but we should expect the baptism in the Holy Spirit to manifest today in the same way as in the book of Acts, with signs and boldness accompanying! Some people err in thinking that if a person does not speak in tongues, then they are not saved or do not have the baptism in the Holy Spirit. Not everyone will receive every gift or manifestation of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29-31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:29-31</span>).

<!-- p51 -->
## How to Receive the Baptism in the Holy Spirit

<!-- p52 -->
The baptism in the Holy Spirit is described in Scripture as a gift promised to believers and received by faith. It is separate from salvation and is primarily for empowerment and witness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>). The baptism in the Holy Spirit is a gift of empowerment for believers, promised by Jesus and fulfilled at Pentecost. It is received through repentance, faith, and asking God. Although the laying on of hands is often used as a method, it is ultimately God who baptizes with the Spirit.

<!-- p53 -->
The Scriptures present speaking in tongues and prophecy as a common sign, but the purpose of this baptism is much greater—providing boldness, power, and spiritual gifts for ministry. Every believer is encouraged to seek this experience as part of their spiritual journey, trusting that God will empower them for His work.

<!-- p54 -->
The Bible outlines several core principles about how this baptism is received:

<!-- p55 -->
### **1. The Baptism in the Holy Spirit is a free Gift from God**

<!-- p56 -->
The baptism in the Holy Spirit is not something that believers can earn—it is a free gift given by God. God wants to give good gifts to His children and is willing to give the Holy Spirit to those who ask with faith and a desire for empowerment.

<!-- p57 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p58 -->
> **<u>Luke 11:13</u>** (NKJV)
>
> If you then, being evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to those who ask Him!"
>

<!-- p59 -->
</span>

<!-- p60 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="38-39" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p61 -->
> **<u>Acts 2:38-39</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Then Peter said to them: "Repent, and let every one of you be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins; and you shall receive the gift of the Holy Spirit.

> For the promise is to you and to your children, and to all who are afar off, as many as the Lord our God will call."

<!-- p63 -->
###  **2. Ask, Seek, and Receive by Faith**

<!-- p64 -->
Scripture emphasizes that the baptism in the Holy Spirit is received by asking in prayer and with faith-filled expectation. Faith, combined with a willingness to ask and receive, is the key to experiencing the baptism in the Spirit. If you are not sure whether God wants to give you the baptism in the Holy Spirit, ask Him about it, He is a good Father! As He prompts you, you may feel a tangible presence of God in your body that you have never experienced before. It's okay if you don't feel it! God is a big God, and He doesn't need to be boxed into a perfect formula. If you feel God touching your heart, surrender to Him!

<!-- p65 -->
- **<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:15-17</span>** - Peter and John prayed and **laid their hands** on the believers, and they **received the Holy Spirit**.

<!-- p66 -->
- **<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="44-46" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 10:44-46</span>** - Cornelius and those of his household **received the Holy Spirit** while Peter was preaching-without hands being laid on them-showing that it is, ultimately, **God who baptizes**.

<!-- p67 -->

## The Gifts of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p79 -->
When the Holy Spirit was poured out, and people were baptized in the Holy Spirit, manifestations of the Holy Spirit often accompanied it.

<!-- p80 -->
There are several lists of gifts in the New Testament:

<!-- p81 -->
- Motivational Gifts given by God: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:6-8</span>

<!-- p82 -->
- Discussed in the chapter on the Church.

<!-- p83 -->
- Fivefold Ministry Gifts given by Jesus: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-13</span>

<!-- p84 -->
- Discussed in the chapter on the church.

<!-- p85 -->
- Manifestation Gifts given by the Holy Spirit: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span>

<!-- p86 -->
- Discussed here.

<!-- p87 -->
Each set of these gifts is given for different purposes and operates in different ways, but they collectively point to the diversity and unity of gifts within the Body of Christ. Each gift has a unique role in fulfilling God's purposes.

<!-- p88 -->
### Purpose &amp; Operations of the Gifts

<!-- p89 -->
The primary purpose of the gifts of the Holy Spirit is to build up the Body of Christ and bring glory to God:

<!-- p90 -->
- **Edification** - Strengthening and encouraging believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
- **Unity** - Promoting harmony and cooperation within the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:12-13</span>).

<!-- p92 -->
- **Evangelism** - Demonstrating the power of God as a testimony to unbelievers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="43" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:43</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:6-7</span>).

<!-- p93 -->
- **Service** - Meeting the needs of others through acts of mercy and generosity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:6-8</span>).

<!-- p94 -->
These gifts are not for personal glory, but for the common good (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:7</span>). They function best when exercised in love and humility, reflecting the character of Christ (1 Corinthians 13).

<!-- p95 -->
Paul emphasized that spiritual gifts should be exercised decently and in order (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:40</span>). This ensures that the use of the gifts promotes unity, peace, and understanding, rather than confusion or division.

<!-- p96 -->
- Prophecies should be evaluated (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:29</span>).

<!-- p97 -->
- When giving a message in tongues to the body, pray that it may be interpreted (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:27-28</span>).

<!-- p98 -->
- The gifts must always bring **edification** to the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:12</span>).

<!-- p99 -->
This order does not quench the Spirit, but ensures that the gifts operate in ways that honor God and benefit others.

<!-- p100 -->
The operation of spiritual gifts must always be grounded in love. Paul devotes an entire chapter (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 13</span>) to explain that without love, even the greatest spiritual gifts are insignificant. Love provides the motivation and the framework to use the gifts in ways that build others up rather than tear them down.

<!-- p101 -->
Moreover, the gifts operate **as the Spirit wills** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:11</span>). They cannot be manufactured or controlled by human effort-they are sovereignly granted and empowered by the Holy Spirit, often at moments of need or ministry. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 13:8</span> says that eventually prophecy and tongues will cease. Theologically, people who believe that the gifts of the Holy Spirit have ceased are known as 'Cessationists'. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 13:10</span> says “when the mayor \[Jesus\] comes, that desire is in part will be cause to cease”. Since Jesus has not yet returned for His church, the gifts are still in operation on the earth.

<!-- p102 -->
It is crucial to recognize that the **gifts of the Spirit** should not be separated from the **fruit of the Spirit** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-23</span>). While the gifts demonstrate power, the fruit reveals character-love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control.

<!-- p103 -->
- **Gifts without Fruit**: A believer may operate in gifts and yet lack maturity or character, leading to pride, misuse, or harm.

<!-- p104 -->
- **Fruit without Gifts**: A believer may have godly character but lack the empowerment to minister effectively.

<!-- p105 -->
Both are necessary. The gifts equip believers for service. The fruit is a sign of their spiritual health (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 7:16</span>) and provides a 'pleasant flavor' to those who consume it.

<!-- p106 -->
### Explaining the 9 Manifestation Gifts of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p107 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span> lists the 9 manifestation gifts of the Holy Spirit:

<!-- p108 -->
1.  **Word of Wisdom** - Supernatural insight into God's purposes or direction for a situation. Applies divine wisdom to complex problems or decisions (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:10</span>).

<!-- p109 -->
2.  **Word of Knowledge** - A supernatural revelation of information about a person, event, or circumstance that could not have been known naturally (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 4:17-18</span>).

<!-- p110 -->
3.  **Faith** - A supernatural ability to trust God for the miraculous, even in impossible situations. This gift inspires boldness and confidence (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 3:6-7</span>).

<!-- p111 -->
4.  **Gifts of Healing** - The supernatural power to heal physical, emotional, or spiritual ailments and restore wholeness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 3:6-8</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 9:17-18</span>).

<!-- p112 -->
5.  **Working of Miracles** - The ability to perform supernatural acts that defy natural laws, such as multiplying food or raising the dead (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40-41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 9:40-41</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 2:9-11</span>).

<!-- p113 -->
6.  **Prophecy** - Declaring the truth or will of God under the inspiration of the Holy Spirit. It should be encouraging, edifying, and comforting (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>).

<!-- p114 -->
7.  **Distinguishing (Discernment) Between Spirits** - The ability to discern whether a spiritual influence is from God, from the enemy, or of human origin (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 16:16-18</span>).

<!-- p115 -->
8.  **Various Kinds of Tongues** - Speaking in languages unknown to the speaker, often as an act of worship, prayer, or a sign to unbelievers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:4</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:22</span>).

<!-- p116 -->
9.  **Interpretation of Tongues** - The supernatural ability to interpret the message spoken in tongues so that the church may be edified (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:27</span>).

<!-- p117 -->
Paul makes it clear that all the gifts are valuable and should work together for the common good of the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:7</span>). However, He also encourages believers to desire the greater gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:31</span>). The greater gifts are often interpreted as those that most directly edify the church, such as prophecy (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:1-5</span>).

<!-- p118 -->
- Prophecy is especially emphasized because it strengthens, encourages, and comforts others (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>). Paul instructs believers to earnestly desire this gift, as it promotes clarity and understanding in the church.

<!-- p119 -->
- Gifts such as tongues are highly valued for personal edification and prayer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:4</span>), but Paul teaches that when a message in tongues is given to the Church to pray for interpretation to ensure that they edify the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:27-28</span>).

<!-- p120 -->
Although the Holy Spirit grants the gifts, we must yield and submit ourselves to the Holy Spirit so that He may use them. Like muscles and talents, when we practice yielding to the Holy Spirit so that He uses His gifts, we become more confident and reliable in using them.
