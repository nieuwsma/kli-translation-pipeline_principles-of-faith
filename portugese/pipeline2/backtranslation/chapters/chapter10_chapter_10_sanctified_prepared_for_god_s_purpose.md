# Chapter 10 - Sanctified - Prepared for God's Purpose

<!-- p1 -->
In the previous two chapters we discussed several important topics. We explored the role and ministry of the Holy Spirit, as well as the role and ministry of the church.

<!-- p2 -->
We have a role to play in the church and, although we know we need training, we also need purification. To truly be prepared for God's purposes we must be sanctified.

<!-- p3 -->
The definition of **sanctification** is to purify something for a special purpose. This is closely related to the term **consecration** which means to set something apart for a special purpose.

<!-- p4 -->
Imagine a master sculptor preparing to work with a large block of marble. The master envisions what this rough and formless marble can be transformed into through his careful work. He has a very specific design in mind, he orders his assistants to set aside that block of marble for a special design (**consecration**). As he refines and works the marble, adding texture, depth, and form, he purifies it (**sanctifies it**) for that special design. The act of dedicating the marble to his special project was quick, whereas the process of shaping the marble to its final design requires considerable patience.

<!-- p5 -->
We were set apart, consecrated to God at salvation. Through the act of purifying our souls with the help of the Spirit, we are transformed. This process of purification is what sanctifies us.

<!-- p6 -->
When we receive Jesus for salvation, we are born again. We were justified, our sins forgiven. Our spirit was regenerated, we became alive in Christ. We were adopted into the family of God and became the literal temple of the Holy Spirit. Such a wonderful transformation that the Scriptures say: “*there is joy in the presence of the angels of God over a sinner who repents.” (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 15:10</span>).

<!-- p7 -->
Receiving Jesus for salvation (**justification**) saves our spirit, purifies us and makes us presentable to God, fit to receive His Holy Spirit. However, despite so great a salvation, **our souls** (our mind, will, and emotions) still **need work**!

<!-- p8 -->
Was the work of salvation incomplete? No! The work of salvation is effective, but it must be continued in the life of the believer by the renewal brought by the Holy Spirit. This process of renewal and transformation is the work of **sanctification.**

<!-- p9 -->
We know there is ongoing work to be done after conversion because our painful memories have not been resolved, many of our beliefs remained the same, and many of our habits or rituals did not disappear. Furthermore, conversion did not separate our minds from their carnal nature (of the flesh).

<!-- p10 -->
The word ‘carnal’ comes from the Latin root meaning ‘flesh’. The carnal nature is within each of us from birth; like an animal’s instincts, it exists to help us survive. The way the carnal nature works is by being self-focused, self-oriented, egocentric. Children are natural examples of a nature of the flesh. Despite their innocence, their nature is focused mainly on their desires, longings, needs; and ‘watch out!’ if you do not meet all their expectations.

<!-- p11 -->
Part of growing up is maturing beyond our childish ways, but when under pressure we very easily resort to a self-oriented pattern of thinking and living. As James says:

<!-- p12 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p13 -->
> **<u>James 4:1</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Where do wars and quarrels come from among you? Do they not come from your passions that war in your members?
>

<!-- p14 -->
</span>

<!-- p15 -->
Paul explains this carnal nature in more detail in his epistle to the Romans (chapters 6 - 8). He says that this carnal nature keeps him doing what he hates and leads him to practice evil. He describes this inner conflict as a war between his human nature and his spirit made by God.

<!-- p16 -->
This conflict between flesh and spirit exists in each of us, and it is our job to do something about it. If we do not confront our carnal nature, our souls will remain entangled in patterns of spiritual decay, even though our spirits have been redeemed.

<!-- p17 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p18 -->
> **<u>Romans 8:6-8</u>** (NKJV)
>
> For the inclination of the flesh is death, but the inclination of the Spirit is life and peace.
>
> Because the inclination of the flesh is enmity against God; for it is not subject to the law of God, nor indeed can it be.
>
> So then, those who are in the flesh cannot please God.
>

<!-- p19 -->
</span>

<!-- p20 -->
These words of Paul are, for us, both a warning and a promise. Although we have been born again, we still have a carnal mind that needs to be submitted to the knowledge of Christ. As we do this, we walk in the life and peace of God.

<!-- p21 -->
Take heart, there is hope for us! We need to walk according to the Spirit and be led by the Spirit of God. In doing so, we will not walk according to the flesh.

<!-- p22 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="1,13,14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p23 -->
> **<u>Romans 8:1,13,14</u>** (NKJV)
>
> There is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus, who do not walk according to the flesh, but according to the Spirit.
>
> For if you live according to the flesh you will die; but if by the Spirit you put to death the deeds of the body, you will live.
>
> For as many as are led by the Spirit of God, these are sons of God.
>

<!-- p24 -->
</span>

<!-- p25 -->
How do we learn to walk in the Spirit? Is it *only a conscious choice*? We learn to walk in the Spirit the same way we learn to walk in the natural world. We are motivated to move and grow, so we struggle, we strive, we crawl, we totter, until we can stand on our own. Our natural parents cannot walk for us, but they can support, encourage, help, and enable us in our walking.

<!-- p26 -->
In the same way, God cannot live our faith for us. He will teach us, but we need to make the effort. As Paul said to the Philippians:

<!-- p27 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="12-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p28 -->
> **<u>Philippians 2:12-13</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Therefore, my beloved, as you have always obeyed, not as in my presence only, but now much more in my absence, work out your own salvation with fear and trembling;
>
> for it is God who works in you both to will and to do for His good pleasure.
>

<!-- p29 -->
</span>

<!-- p30 -->
We must work out our own salvation. This does not mean that we climb onto a wooden cross and are physically crucified, but we need to crucify our carnal nature (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:24</span>). God promises that He will work in us and help us, but we need to choose to walk that path and remain steadfast in it.

<!-- p31 -->
It is true to say that ‘we’ are dealing with our carnal nature, but it is also true to say that ‘God’ is dealing with it. The work of the cross (justification) was hard for Jesus, He had to sacrifice His life for ours, but easy for us, it is a free gift. The work of the Spirit (sanctification) is easy for God, He walks in perfect truth and is willing and eager to dwell with us, but it is hard for us, because we need to crucify our old nature, and our nature is not very interested in being crucified.

<!-- p32 -->
This process is collaborative. God helps, assists, guides, directs, and corrects. We listen, obey, follow, and respond. The result is growth and transformation. This is the pruning that Jesus said that God would do in us. Jesus is the vine, His Father is the vinedresser; we are the branches:

<!-- p33 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p34 -->
> **<u>John 15:1-4</u>** (NKJV)
>
> "I am the true vine, and My Father is the vinedresser.
>
> Every branch in Me that does not bear fruit He takes away; and every branch that bears fruit He prunes, that it may bear more fruit.
>
> You are already clean because of the word which I have spoken to you.
>
> Abide in Me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself, unless it abides in the vine, neither can you, unless you abide in Me.
>

<!-- p35 -->
</span>

<!-- p36 -->
Pruning is incredibly healthy for a plant. Recalling our chapter on repentance, we are called to turn away from dead works (those works that no longer bear fruit) and to turn to God. The result of this turning away from the dead places of our lives and this turning to God enables the master gardener of our lives to prune branches, improve growth, and produce greater spiritual fruit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:2</span>).

<!-- p37 -->
Although pruning may seem painful, it reflects God's loving care as He shapes us into vessels for His purpose (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 12:11</span>). Some may feel a sense of loss, even fear, as they go through the pruning process. However, God is gentle, and not even the bruised reed will He break (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="42" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 42:3</span>). Vines are pruned during their dormant season to minimize stress on the plant. The gardener who tends the growth will cut back the old, woody growth that no longer produces fruit as before; this allows the vine to direct energy to the branches with greater potential for good fruit.

<!-- p38 -->
Like a gardener who prunes branches to direct growth, God may allow seasons of correction and discipline to refine our faith and character, but His work is patient and kind. God prunes those who abide in Him. Our focus should be more on abiding in Christ and less on the work of pruning.

<!-- p39 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 12:11</span> reminds us that pruning “produces the peaceable fruit of righteousness” for those who are trained by it.

<!-- p40 -->
How are we trained by righteousness? By being transformed by the renewing of our minds.

<!-- p41 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p42 -->
> **<u>Romans 12:1-2</u>** (NKJV)
>
> I beseech you therefore, brethren, by the mercies of God, that you present your bodies as a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable to God, which is your reasonable service.
>
> And do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind, that you may experience what is the good, acceptable, and perfect will of God.
>

<!-- p43 -->
</span>

<!-- p44 -->
Paul sets out two simple requirements for transformation:

<!-- p45 -->
<u>1. Present your bodies to God as a living sacrifice</u>. A **living** sacrifice does not die physically during the sacrifice, but, since a living sacrifice cannot die, you must continually bring it to the altar!

<!-- p46 -->
<u>2. Renew your mind!</u> Renewal, based on the Greek word: **Anakainōsis** (*An-ak-ah'-ee-no-sis -* **G342**) means a renewal or restoration, focusing on an ongoing process of inner change and growth.

<!-- p47 -->
This renewal produces transformation. Transformation is based on the Greek word: **Metamorphoō** (*met-am-or-fo'-o -* **G3339**) means to change into another form; to transform or transfigure, emphasizing a complete change. Metamorphoō is where we get the English word metamorphosis, which is the process that caterpillars go through to become butterflies.

<!-- p48 -->
God wants to fundamentally transform our minds and our pattern of thinking. His desire is that we not be conformed to the way of the world, but that we would know what is the good (beneficial), pleasing (acceptable), and perfect (complete &amp; lacking nothing) will of God.

<!-- p49 -->
The requirements for transformation are really that simple, **although they require discipline**!

<!-- p50 -->
Our struggle against the flesh will not be won by our flesh, but by God’s equipment for the battle of the mind. His words (the sword of truth) are powerful to correct every imagination, every lofty thought, and to imprison (take captive) every thought that opposes obedience to God.

<!-- p51 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="3-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p52 -->
> **<u>2 Corinthians 10:3-6</u>** (NKJV)
>
> For though we walk in the flesh, we do not war according to the flesh.
>
> For the weapons of our warfare are not carnal but mighty in God for pulling down strongholds,
>
> casting down arguments and every high thing that exalts itself against the knowledge of God, bringing every thought into captivity to the obedience of Christ,
>
> and being ready to punish all disobedience, when your obedience is fulfilled.
>

<!-- p53 -->
</span>

<!-- p54 -->
When authorities arrest someone, they put handcuffs on them and place them in a cell. If that person escapes custody, they do not turn a blind eye; they will use incredible resources and will not rest until that person is brought back under control. We too must act with such zeal in the contest for our minds!

<!-- p55 -->
We are renewed by obedience and by experience with the truth. Jesus said that if we remained in His words, we would be His disciples, we would experience (<u>know</u>) the truth, and it would set us free (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-32" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 8:31-32</span>)!

<!-- p56 -->
The experience of the truth is different from the knowledge of the truth. Experience is the Greek word **Ginōskō** (Ghin-oce'-ko **- G1097**) means to know through personal experience or intimate understanding; it implies progressive learning.

<!-- p57 -->
It is not our academic knowledge of the truth that transforms us and renews the mind; it is our personal experience of the truth. We are sanctified by His truth! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="17" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 17:17</span>).

## How to Renew Your Mind

<!-- p59 -->
The renewing of our minds is not a process that serves everyone equally-depends on which part of the mind we are talking about! God designed our minds with incredible complexity, including several different memory systems, each serving a specific purpose. Some operate automatically in the background, while others require focused and intentional thought. These systems are gifts from God to help us survive, grow, and thrive, but they can also lead us astray when misused or left unrenewed. Understanding how these systems work gives us insight into how to cooperate with God’s process of transformation.

<!-- p60 -->
In the next section, I will describe each of the memory systems, their purpose and function, some practical steps to renew this aspect of your mind, as well as resources for ongoing practice and study.

<!-- p61 -->
### **Memory Systems and How to Renew Them**

<!-- p62 -->
#### **1. Explicit Memory (Declarative Memory)**

<!-- p63 -->
**Purpose:** Stores facts, events, and experiences that we can consciously recall. It is divided into two types-**semantic** and **episodic**.

<!-- p64 -->
**Semantic Memory - Facts and Knowledge.** Semantic memory is where we store facts, definitions, and general knowledge-things like historical dates, Bible verses, and practical information. It helps us predict outcomes, make decisions, and avoid danger (for example, recognizing poisonous plants). Although it gives us stability and order, it can also lead to rigidity-an excessive dependence on rules instead of adapting to unexpected situations.

<!-- p65 -->
**How to Renew It:**

<!-- p66 -->
- **Study the Scriptures** to shape our worldview (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="119" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="105" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 119:105</span>).

<!-- p67 -->
- **Memorize truths** and doctrines to remain firm in the faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 3:16</span>).

<!-- p68 -->
- **Read Christian books** such as *"Renewing the Mind" by Casey Treat* or *"Battlefield of the Mind" by Joyce Meyer* to develop godly thought patterns.

<!-- p69 -->
**Episodic Memory - Personal Experiences.** Episodic memory stores our personal experiences, linking them to specific times and places-like a wedding day or a traumatic event. These memories provide context, helping us learn from the past and build relationships. However, episodic memory is prone to emotional bias and distortion. Past traumas can create fear associations, making it difficult to move forward.

<!-- p70 -->
**How to Renew It:**

<!-- p71 -->
- **Reflect and confess** to bring hidden pain into the light of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 1:9</span>).

<!-- p72 -->
- **Reframe past experiences** through the lens of God’s grace (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:28</span>).

<!-- p73 -->
- **Seek inner healing** through prayer and counseling (*“Your Pain Is Showing” by Larry F. Low, "The Bondage Breaker" by Neil T. Anderson*, *"Emotionally Healthy Spirituality" by Peter Scazzero*).

<!-- p74 -->
#### **2. Implicit Memory (Nondeclarative)**

<!-- p75 -->
**Purpose:** Unconscious memory used for habits, skills, and emotional associations. It operates automatically, helping us respond quickly to situations. It is divided into **procedural memory** and **emotional conditioning**.

<!-- p76 -->
**Procedural Memory - Skills and Habits.** Procedural memory allows us to perform tasks without thinking-like driving, typing, or playing an instrument. It is efficient, saving us mental energy and time. However, bad habits and negative patterns can also become deeply ingrained and difficult to break.

<!-- p77 -->
**How to Renew It:**

<!-- p78 -->
- **Practice spiritual disciplines** such as prayer and fasting to build godly habits (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 5:14</span>).

<!-- p79 -->
- **Train in godliness** through repetition and obedience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 4:7</span>).

<!-- p80 -->
- **Read books like** *"Atomic Habits" by James Clear* (practical) and *"Spiritual Disciplines for the Christian Life" by Donald Whitney* (biblical) to establish habits that honor God.

<!-- p81 -->
**Emotional Conditioning - Emotional Associations.** Emotional conditioning links sensory cues (such as smells or sounds) to feelings, triggering responses before we consciously process them. This system protects us, but it can also cause irrational fears or emotional reactions when past experiences are negative.

<!-- p82 -->
**How to Renew It:**

<!-- p83 -->
- **Pray for healing** from past wounds (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="41" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 41:10</span>).

<!-- p84 -->
- **Practice forgiveness** and release bitterness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-32" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:31-32</span>).

<!-- p85 -->
- **Replace fear with God's promises** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 1:7</span>).

<!-- p86 -->
- **Seek guidance from books such as** *“Your Pain is Showing” by Larry F. Low, "The Bondage Breaker" by Neil T. Anderson*, “*Soul Care" by Rob Reimer* to overcome emotional triggers.

<!-- p87 -->
#### **3. Working Memory (Short-Term Memory)**

<!-- p88 -->
**Purpose:** Provides temporary storage for problem solving and decision making. It helps us focus, retain information for a brief period, and process tasks - such as remembering instructions or making quick decisions. Although it is powerful, it is also limited, prone to distraction, and easily overloaded, especially under stress.

<!-- p89 -->
**How to Renew It:**

<!-- p90 -->
- **Focus and meditate** on God's Word daily (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 1:2</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
- **Renew your thoughts** through intentional thought (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:2</span>).

<!-- p92 -->
- **Practice mindfulness** and simplify mental clutter (*"Habits of Grace" by David Mathis*).

<!-- p93 -->
- **Read** *"The Celebration of Discipline" by Richard Foster* to sharpen focus through spiritual disciplines.

<!-- p94 -->
## Conclusion

<!-- p95 -->
Renewing the mind is not a quick fix — it is a gradual, lifelong process of transformation. Each memory system plays a role in shaping how we think, feel, and act. By intentionally working with the Holy Spirit, applying Scripture, and developing godly habits, we can train our minds to align with God's truth. Whether we are dealing with wounds from the past, learning new habits, or staying focused in the moment, God has provided both **spiritual tools** and **practical wisdom** to help us walk in renewal and freedom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 10:5</span>).

<!-- p96 -->
Our world is full of problems, tribulations, and trials. Through the disciplined renewal of the mind, our souls are transformed, and we can live a life of freedom for God, filled with the fruit of His Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-25</span>).
