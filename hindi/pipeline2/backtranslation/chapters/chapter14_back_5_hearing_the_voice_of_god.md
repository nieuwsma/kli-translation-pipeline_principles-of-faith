# Hearing God's Voice

<!-- p1 -->
The following guest addendum by Rev. Larry Low ([<u>www.LarryLow.com</u>](http://www.larrylow.com)) complements our exploration of the role of the Holy Spirit, offering practical insights on hearing the voice of God. The following guest contribution is used by permission.

<!-- p2 -->
One morning during prayer, I asked **God** this question: “For the times that are coming upon us, what is the most important thing I can teach Your people?” And He answered me very quickly, and when He spoke, I felt the utmost urgency in it: “**Teach them to hear My voice.**”

<!-- p3 -->
When the Lord gives me such an instruction, I always pick up a pad and pencil and begin to write down what little I know, or what I think I know, and the Scriptures that support it. I like to do word-study on the subjects the Lord is leading me to teach. I love the anointing for preaching, but I truly love to teach the Word. Finding things that have always been there but were overlooked is a thrill.

<!-- p4 -->
David must have longed to discover God’s truths, for he prayed in Psalm 119:18: “Open my eyes, that I may see wondrous things from Your law.”

<!-- p5 -->
That is what I want to see-wondrous things from His Word.

<!-- p6 -->
As I was studying and preparing to teach the second message on hearing and discerning the voice of God, He said this in my heart:

<!-- p7 -->
“*Teach them to hear My voice,  
Teach them to honor and to obey.*”

<!-- p8 -->
“*Yes, teach them to hear My voice and I will always lead them away:  
Away from troubles that would destroy them,*”

<!-- p9 -->
“*Away from the trials they are in,  
Toward that place of peace, love, and joy where very few have ever been.*”

<!-- p10 -->
“*Toward a place of refreshing,  
Toward a place of divine ecstasy.*”

<!-- p11 -->
“*Oh yes, teach them to hear My voice, son  
and these people will be set free forever*”.

<!-- p12 -->
05/27/00 9:10 A.M.

<!-- p13 -->
The following eleven points will help you understand how the Lord will communicate with you:

<!-- p14 -->
1.  <u>Hearing God’s voice is not an audible sound, but a perception in your heart.</u>

<!-- p15 -->
There was a time when His word came so clear and firm that it might have been audible. It was so real that I answered Him out loud. However, there was no one there besides the Lord and me, so I cannot say for certain whether He spoke to me the same way we speak to one another.

<!-- p16 -->
There was also a time when Jesus was being baptized in the Jordan River and the Father said: “This is My Son, in whom I am well pleased,” but some who were present said it sounded like thunder, so I believe He could have spoken to me audibly as well, but for now, I want you to understand that when I say that God spoke to me, it is more a perception in my heart than a sound in my ear.

<!-- p17 -->
2.  <u>This perception is not produced by reasoning in your mind; however, it is a complete thought or understanding.</u>

<!-- p18 -->
The Lord has never said anything to me that left me confused. He has never said anything to me that left me asking ‘why’. It is always a perception with complete understanding. God is not the author of confusion (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:33</span>).

<!-- p19 -->
3.  <u>When He speaks, He speaks in complete thoughts.</u>

<!-- p20 -->
He never leaves us to fill in the blanks by guessing. In a moment, God can fill your heart with so much information that you could preach for a lifetime. One touch from God can fill you with a fire that will keep you burning for eternity. He is a perfect communicator.

<!-- p21 -->
4.  <u>When God speaks to us, He never drives; He leads.</u>

<!-- p22 -->
He is not a harsh taskmaster; He is a shepherd. Drovers drive, but shepherds lead. Jesus is the great Shepherd of the sheep, and we are the sheep of His pasture.

<!-- p23 -->
5.  <u>When He speaks to His children, He does not condemn; He explains.</u>

<!-- p24 -->
His children are those who have been born again by the confession of their faith in the shed blood of **Jesus** and His work at Calvary (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:3</span>, 5). Who would want to listen to someone who always condemns us for our mistakes? I believe that most of **God’s** children are afraid to hear **God** addressing them because they have been told that **God** will condemn them for this or that. Look at <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 3:19-20</span>. What does it say about who condemns or punishes us? It is our soul or heart that condemns a child of **God**, but the **Holy Spirit of God** knows everything and is greater than our hearts. He is there to be our Comforter. Read the following scriptures, and you will understand from them that the Holy Spirit convicts only sinners. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33-34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:33-34</span> teaches us that nothing can come between us and the love of **God**. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:16</span>, 26 teaches us that the Holy Spirit is our Comforter, not our condemner; our Counselor, not our punisher; our Advocate and Helper, not our hinderer. Also read <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 9:31</span> and 15:28, which are examples of how the Holy Spirit (the voice of **God**) supports, comforts, and empowers us.

<!-- p25 -->
6.  <u>The voice of God can be tested so that you may know it is God, not your thoughts or the voice of familiar spirits.</u>

<!-- p26 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 4:1-3</span> instructs us to "test every spirit" so that we can determine whether it is from God. When you feel that you are hearing from God, you have the right and responsibility to ask that voice a question (this is what it means to test or prove the spirit). The question to ask is: "Did Jesus Christ come in the flesh?" Familiar spirits will never admit one thing—that Jesus came to this earth in the flesh. He was fully man and fully God. No false spirit or religion will admit that Jesus came to this earth in the flesh and that He has delivered mankind from all their sins forever. Test the voice you hear. You should also know that God will never lead you outside His written Word, the Bible. If you are hearing something that cannot be supported by the Bible, you are not hearing from God. Reject it! Test the voice you hear to see whether it is from God.

<!-- p27 -->
7.  <u>The voice of God always glorifies Jesus Christ.</u>

<!-- p28 -->
The Holy Spirit is the voice of heaven. He is the One who is here on earth to work with us, and Jesus said that He will speak what He hears, and He will always glorify Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13-14</span>). He is not here to glorify your ministry, your deeds, or the church’s programs. All these things are good, and most of them are even necessary for our work, but the Holy Spirit will speak and glorify the person of Jesus. If the voice you are hearing is glorifying you, you are not hearing God. There is a difference between being glorified and being encouraged and empowered. Every time the Holy Spirit speaks to you, He will always encourage and empower you, even when He is correcting (guiding) you with the Word. But glorification is when you think you are the important one. Without Jesus you are nothing—He is the Somebody, not us.

<!-- p29 -->
8.  <u>The voice of God always reveals something to us.</u>

<!-- p30 -->
Look at <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:14</span>. He always takes what is of **Jesus** and reveals it to us. When considering the word "reveal," the Amplified adds the words "declare, disclose, and transmit." When **God** declares something to us, then He reveals it to us, and then, through your faith, He transmits it to us. Also study <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 2:10</span> and 12.

<!-- p31 -->
9.  <u>The voice of God always brings peace.</u>

<!-- p32 -->
Read <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:26</span> and 27, and <span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 3:14</span> and 15. His voice never unsettles us. It is a voice that brings perfect peace. There is inner peace because there is a "certain knowledge" that gives us boldness before God, which turns into perfect peace. If the voice you are hearing is unsettling you, you are not hearing from God—turn it off. Jesus has promised us peace, not as the world gives, but as He gives us through the comforting words of the Holy Spirit and His continual indwelling (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:27</span>).

<!-- p33 -->
10. <u>The voice of God always brings truth.</u>

<!-- p34 -->
Read <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:17</span>, 26; 16:13. In fact, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span> calls the Holy Spirit: "the Spirit of truth." God cannot lie! He is truth and therefore will speak only truth. I have heard people say: "I don’t believe what I am hearing is from God. It’s too good to be true." And it may seem too good to be true; therefore you must test the spirit by matching it with the Word of God, to make sure that it is true. Some people simply cannot believe because what they are hearing is always positive, and they have been taught that God condemns us for error. God will tell only the truth, and it will always be good news. The sad truth is that most people hear only bad news from the voice they are listening to and don’t even expect good news, so when good news comes, they question it as possibly untrue. **Truth is always good news!** It is **that truth** which sets us free, so what could **truth** be other than good news?

<!-- p35 -->
11. <u>The voice of **God** always leads us forward and upward.</u>

<!-- p36 -->
Deuteronomy 28:1-2, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="30" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 30:21</span>, and <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span> read

<!-- p37 -->
God never takes you down! He never puts you down; He only lifts you up. When God speaks to you, He speaks from the standpoint that everything the Word says about you has already been accomplished and you have already received it. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="33" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 33:3</span> says: "Call to Me, and I will answer you, and show you great and mighty things which you do not know." Does that sound demeaning to you or like doom and gloom? There is no "doom and gloom" in God, so He will not tell you anything calamitous or gloomy. For "doom and gloom" you will have to join the world’s news agency.

<!-- p38 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="29" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p39 -->
> **<u>Jeremiah 29:11</u>** (NKJV)
>
> For I know the thoughts that I think toward you, says the LORD, thoughts of peace and not of evil, to give you a future and a hope.

<!-- p40 -->
</span>

<!-- p41 -->
Now, if God thinks good thoughts toward us—of peace, a future, and hope—why would He say anything else? The answer is, He wouldn’t, and He doesn’t! He only leads us upward and onward with great hope, so that He may provide us with an expected future.

<!-- p42 -->
I hope this will encourage you to “call upon Him” and help you learn **how to hear His voice**. He has great things to tell you, but before all His good things can do you any good, He needs your listening ear.
