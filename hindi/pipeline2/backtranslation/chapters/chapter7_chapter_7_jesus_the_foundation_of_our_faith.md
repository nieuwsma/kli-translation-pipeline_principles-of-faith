# Chapter 7 - Jesus-our faith's foundation

<!-- p1 -->
Continuing our study of Jesus as the Christ, in this chapter we continue to look at Jesus’s life, character, and work. We will see that Jesus is the mediator of a new and better covenant-and that he perfectly fulfills the roles of prophet, priest, and king. In this new covenant we, as believers, are in good standing with God and move from a works-based covenant to a trust-based (faith-based) covenant. Finally, as a way to wrap up our study on Jesus, we will read and study the Apostles’ Creed.

<!-- p2 -->
## Mediator of the New Covenant

<!-- p3 -->
The concept of a mediator involves the role of a go-between who bridges the gap between two parties and makes a relationship or agreement possible. In the story of Exodus, Moses is the initial mediator who represented the covenant God made with his people. Moses received the Law, offered sacrifices (priest), called God’s people to obedience (prophet), and judged them (judge / king). After Moses, the rest of the Old Testament sees the role of mediator carried out by the prophets, the Levitical priesthood, and by the judges and later the nation’s kings.

<!-- p4 -->
Roles of mediators in the Old Testament:

<!-- p5 -->
1.  **Prophet:** Prophets were God’s messengers, sent to convey his will to the people and to call them back when they strayed. For example, Moses functioned as a prophet who, having received the Law from God and taught Israel, mediated the old covenant. The prophets also foretold the coming of the Messiah and the final redemption of humanity. Other prominent examples of prophets are: Isaiah, Jeremiah, Elijah, Elisha, Samuel.

<!-- p6 -->
2.  **Levitical priesthood:** The priests acted as mediators by offering sacrifices for the atonement of sins and by interceding before God on behalf of the people. In particular, the high priest played an important role on the Day of Atonement, entering the Holy of Holies and sprinkling blood on the mercy seat, which symbolized reconciliation with God. The ministry of the priest is to intercede for the people.

<!-- p7 -->
3.  **Judge/king:** The judges and later the kings were charged with ruling over God’s people, enforcing his statutes, judging among the people, delivering them from their enemies, and leading them in righteousness. The book of Judges tells the stories of these leaders—people like Deborah, Samson, and Gideon—whom God raised up to save and guide Israel. Later there was a series of kings in Israel—many evil, some good (see 1st and 2nd Kings, 1st and 2nd Samuel, and 1<sup>st</sup> and 2<sup>nd</sup> Chronicles). Some key examples are Saul, David, and Solomon.

<!-- p8 -->
Notably, all the mediators of the old covenant were entirely human, not divine. Being human, they could sin, and many times they did fall into sin. Some mediators were sinful and led the people away from God into profane worship: false prophets, false and corrupt priests, and wicked kings.

<!-- p9 -->
### Christ as the mediator of the New Covenant

<!-- p10 -->
Jesus’s death, resurrection, and ascension fulfill the old covenant and establish the new covenant.

<!-- p11 -->
The new covenant is a transformative relationship between God and his people, established through the life, death, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. In contrast to the old covenant, which focused on external regulations, the new covenant emphasizes inner transformation and grace. Jesus inaugurated this covenant at the Last Supper, where he declared that his blood would establish it, replacing the repeated animal sacrifices of the old covenant with his once-for-all atonement (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 22:20</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:10</span>). Through this sacrifice, the new covenant provides full forgiveness and cleansing from sin, meeting the need for perfect and lasting reconciliation with God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 31:34</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:12-18</span>).

<!-- p12 -->
One of the chief features of the new covenant is its internal nature, as God writes his law on the hearts of his people, establishing a heart-level relationship with them through the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 31:33</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 3:3</span>). This transformation moves believers beyond merely keeping external commandments and enables them, by the power of the Spirit, to live out God’s will (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:1-4</span>). The new covenant also grants direct access to God, ending the need for an earthly priesthood. Jesus is the eternal High Priest, and believers are called a royal priesthood, who can approach God with boldness through Christ’s sacrifice (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 7:24-25</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:19-20</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:9</span>).

<!-- p13 -->
In contrast to the old covenant, which was limited to Israel, the new covenant is universal—it extends to everyone who believes in Christ, regardless of ethnicity or background (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 3:28</span>). This covenant is entirely based on grace, freely granting salvation and forgiveness through faith in Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>). It is not sustained by human effort, but by the empowering work of the Holy Spirit—who transforms believers and enables them to live in obedience and reflect God’s character (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EZK" data-chapter="36" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ezekiel 36:26-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:9-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-23</span>). At the same time, the new covenant is eternal, surpassing the temporary and conditional nature of the old covenant (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="32" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 32:40</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:13</span>). It represents the ultimate fulfillment of God’s plan of salvation—reconciling humanity to himself and enabling believers to live forever under his rule as his people.

<!-- p14 -->
Jesus is explicitly identified as the mediator of the new covenant

<!-- p15 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

>
> And for this reason he is the mediator of the new covenant, so that by means of death the redemption of the transgressions committed under the first covenant might take place, and those who are called may receive the promise of the eternal inheritance.
>

<!-- p17 -->
</span>

<!-- p18 -->
By his atoning death and resurrection, Christ inaugurates this new covenant, which replaces the old system of sacrifices and external regulations with a covenant written on the hearts of believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 31:31-34</span>). He stands between God and humanity—not only interceding on behalf of his people, but also providing, by his own blood, the means of reconciliation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:12</span>). Unlike the mediators of the old covenant, Jesus is sinless and eternal, fully able to meet the divine requirements of justice and mercy, thus securing a lasting relationship between God and believers.

<!-- p19 -->
Jesus fulfills and perfects these roles

<!-- p20 -->
1.  **Prophet:** Jesus is the supreme Prophet; he is not only the messenger of the word of God, but the Word made flesh (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:14</span>). He reveals God’s nature, will, and plan of salvation fully and perfectly (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 1:1-2</span>). His teachings, miracles, and prophetic declarations fulfill the prophecies of the Old Testament and lay the foundation of the New Covenant.

<!-- p21 -->
2.  **Priest:** Jesus is the perfect High Priest, who offered himself once for all as a sacrifice for sin (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 7:27</span>). Unlike the priests of the Old Covenant, who had to offer sacrifices repeatedly for their own sins and for the people’s, Jesus, who is sinless, offered his own blood to secure eternal redemption (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:14</span>).

<!-- p22 -->
3.  **King &amp; Judge:** Jesus is the eternal King, who reigns in righteousness and justice. As the Son of David, he fulfills the promise of an everlasting kingdom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2SA" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Samuel 7:16</span>). His kingdom is not limited to the earth alone, but is cosmic—he reigns over all creation and establishes peace through his victory over sin and death (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 2:9-11</span>). Jesus will return as King and Judge, and will judge ‘both the living and the dead’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 10:42</span>).

<!-- p23 -->
In Christ, the roles of Prophet, Priest, and King come together and are perfectly fulfilled. As the Mediator of the New Covenant, he bridges the gap between the holy God and sinful humanity, enabling believers to draw near to God with boldness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 4:16</span>) and securing in him the promised eternal inheritance.

## The Apostles' Creed

<!-- p25 -->
*The Apostles' Creed* is not found in Scripture; it was composed and revised from time to time on the basis of the early teachings of the church. A “creed” (statement of faith) is a formal affirmation of beliefs—think of it as a declaration of faith with a pledge or oath. Creeds are important because they both unite those who believe in a particular way and distinguish them from those who do not. In public events such as city council meetings or legislative assemblies in the United States, those present often recite the Pledge of Allegiance. This pledge is widely used in the United States, but not in other countries, because the citizens of those countries do not owe allegiance to the United States. This pledge does not make a person ‘American,’ but for those who are citizens it represents an important foundational belief.

<!-- p26 -->
The first identifiable versions of this creed were written in the fourth century A.D. Over the centuries the creed has undergone minor changes, and some variations are found depending on denomination. Not every Christian denomination uses this creed, yet in some form it has been widely accepted throughout the Christian world.

<!-- p27 -->
The Apostles’ Creed has been used for centuries as a tool for teaching and catechesis and communicates the most fundamental truths of the Christian faith—the basic beliefs that must be confessed and held in order to be Christian. Some denominations have a formal process of catechesis by which a new believer is incorporated into that particular denomination and instructed in its teachings.

<!-- p28 -->
A person does not need to fully understand these beliefs to come to God and be saved; salvation is by faith, not by passing a doctrinal exam! But as we grow in that faith, we all should understand these foundational truths.

## A brief overview of the Apostles' Creed

<!-- p31 -->
The fundamental truths contained in the Apostles’ Creed are many and exceedingly deep; it would indeed take many volumes to give their full description. Although this chapter is about Jesus, many of these topics also relate to the other persons of the Godhead. This is a brief exposition of the creed, focusing only on the most central themes. Many of these topics are explored in greater depth in other chapters of this book.

<!-- p32 -->
#### *I believe in God, the Father Almighty, Creator of heaven and earth.*

<!-- p33 -->
This affirms belief in God as the Almighty, sovereign Creator and Father. The title “Father” underscores God’s loving relationship with his creation, especially with those who become his children through faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:12</span>). As Creator, he called the universe into existence by his word, bringing order out of chaos (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 1:1-3</span>). His almighty power is revealed in the vastness of creation, while his fatherly care is seen in its continual preservation and governance. This foundational statement declares God to be the source and ruler of all life, worthy of worship and trust.

<!-- p34 -->
#### *I believe in Jesus Christ, his only Son, our Lord.*

<!-- p35 -->
Jesus Christ is the unique Son of God—begotten from eternity, not made—and shares in the divine nature (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:1-2</span>, 14). Jesus is the Word from the beginning, and he became flesh and dwelt among us. As the “Christ” he is the anointed Messiah who fulfills the Old Testament promises regarding the Savior. The title “Lord” acknowledges his authority over all creation and his rightful place as King and Ruler (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 2:10-11</span>). He is not only Lord, but the Lord who saves (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 16:31</span>).

<!-- p36 -->
Jesus was not **only** sent by God; he **is** a part of him. Jesus abides in the Father and the Father abides in Jesus. The Gospel of John repeatedly proves that Jesus and the Father are one (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="30" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:30</span>), that Jesus did nothing of himself (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 5:19</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="49-50" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 12:49-50</span>), that whatever he did he did for his Father’s glory (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="17" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 17:4</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:13</span>), and that all of it was done by his Father’s authority and power (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 8:28-29</span>).

<!-- p37 -->
#### *He was conceived by the power of the Holy Spirit and born of the Virgin Mary.*

<!-- p38 -->
This affirms the miraculous conception of Jesus by the Holy Spirit, which underscores his divine origin. By being born of the Virgin Mary, Jesus fulfilled the prophecy of <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 7:14</span>, which foretold the virgin birth of the Messiah. This event is the sign of the Incarnation, where the eternal Word of God took on human flesh (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:14</span>). Being fully God and fully man, Jesus bridges the gap between God and humanity, making him the perfect Mediator and Savior.

<!-- p39 -->
Jesus was conceived by the seed of the Holy Spirit, and he did not inherit the sinful nature that we, who are born of the seed of man, possess. Jesus was tempted and tested in every way, yet he remained perfect and sinless. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 4:15</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 5:21</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:22</span>.

<!-- p40 -->
#### *He suffered under Pontius Pilate, was crucified, died, and was buried.*

<!-- p41 -->
Jesus’ suffering and death were real, historical events that occurred under the Roman governor Pontius Pilate, grounding the Christian faith in verifiable history. His crucifixion was the fulfillment of Old Testament prophecies, such as <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="53" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 53:5</span>, which speaks of the suffering servant who would bear the sins of many. Jesus’ death was sacrificial; He took upon Himself the penalty of sin on behalf of humanity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:24</span>). His burial confirms the reality of His death and sets the stage for the significance of the resurrection.

<!-- p42 -->
#### *He descended to the dead.*

<!-- p43 -->
After His death, Jesus descended to the realm of the dead, often called Hades or Sheol in Scripture. This act, even in death, demonstrates His complete solidarity with humanity and signals His victory over sin, death, and Satan (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 3:18-20</span>). By descending He proclaimed His triumph and the completion of His redemptive work.

<!-- p44 -->
#### *On the third day He rose again.*

<!-- p45 -->
The resurrection of Jesus is the cornerstone of our Christian faith; it confirms His divinity and the truth of His teachings. Rising from the dead, He conquered sin and death and gave believers the assurance of eternal life (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 15:17-22</span>). The resurrection fulfilled both Scripture and Jesus’ own predictions (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 16:21</span>). This miraculous event turned the disciples’ despair into bold faith and laid the foundation for the testimony of the early Church.

<!-- p46 -->
#### *He ascended into heaven, and is seated at the right hand of the Father.*

<!-- p47 -->
The ascension of Jesus is the sign of His return, in glory, to the Father’s presence. Seated at the Father’s right hand (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:20</span>), He fulfills the role of our eternal High Priest, interceding for believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 7:25</span>). His exalted position also displays His glorification as King—He reigns over all creation and directs the Church’s mission (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:22</span>) so that it may do greater works (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:12</span>) and fulfill the Great Commission He gave us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:18-20</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 16:15-18</span>). His ascension assures believers of His continuing presence and power through the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p48 -->
#### *He will come again to judge the living and the dead.*

<!-- p49 -->
This points to Christ’s future return, when He will bring final judgment and restoration. Jesus will judge all people—rewarding the faithful and bringing judgment upon those who do not repent (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 4:1</span>). His coming will complete God’s ultimate plan for creation, establishing His eternal kingdom of righteousness and peace. This hope encourages believers to live with faithfulness and expectancy, knowing that their labor in the Lord is not in vain (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="58" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 15:58</span>).

<!-- p50 -->
#### *I believe in the Holy Spirit.*

<!-- p51 -->
The Holy Spirit, the third Person of the Trinity, is God’s active presence and power in the world today. He convicts of sin, gives new birth to hearts, and empowers believers to live holy lives (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:8-13</span>). The Spirit unites Christians as one body—the Church—and equips them with spiritual gifts for service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:4-11</span>). He is the Comforter, Advocate, and Guide, who dwells in believers and remains as the seal of their eternal inheritance (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>).

<!-- p52 -->
#### *The holy catholic Church, the communion of saints.*

<!-- p53 -->
The "holy catholic Church" refers to the universal body of Christ, comprising all believers across time and space. The Church is set apart for God’s purposes, called to reflect His holiness and share the gospel. The "communion of saints" emphasizes the unity of all Christians, living and dead, as members of Christ’s body (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:4-6</span>). This communion rises above earthly divisions and displays the shared hope and mission of believers.

<!-- p54 -->
Jesus is the head of the Church. The Church is called both the bride of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="25-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:25-27</span>) and the body of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 1:18</span>).

<!-- p55 -->
#### *The forgiveness of sins.*

<!-- p56 -->
This underscores the heart of the gospel: through Jesus’ sacrificial death, God grants full forgiveness of sins to all who repent and believe (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:7</span>). This forgiveness restores the broken relationship between humanity and God, removing guilt and shame. It also calls believers to forgive others, reflecting the grace they have received (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 6:14-15</span>).

<!-- p57 -->
#### *The resurrection of the body.*

<!-- p58 -->
Like Jesus, at the end of time we too will experience the resurrection of the body. This resurrection will transform perishable, mortal bodies into glorious, imperishable bodies fit for eternal life (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42-44" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 15:42-44</span>). It emphasizes the Christian hope of restoration not only for individuals but for all creation as well, for God makes all things new (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="21" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 21:4-5</span>).

<!-- p59 -->
#### *And the life everlasting. Amen.*

<!-- p60 -->
Eternal life with God is the supreme promise of the Christian faith। This life begins now through a relationship with Christ and will be fully realized in the new heavens and the new earth, where believers will dwell with God forever (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 22:1-5</span>)। This concluding declaration reflects the joy and assurance of salvation, echoing the faith and hope expressed throughout the entire confession of faith।

<!-- p61 -->
## Conclusion

<!-- p62 -->
Among the basic truths expressed in the Apostles’ Creed, we find the life and work of Jesus at the center of God’s plan for humanity। In each statement of the creed are captured the key aspects of our belief about God, and who Jesus is-his divinity, humanity, mission, and final victory-are captured। These truths point to his unique role as the mediator of the new covenant। As mediator he invites us to come to God with confidence, trusting in his perfect sacrifice and ongoing intercession। This calls us to worship him as the rightful king and to follow his teachings as the supreme prophet। Ultimately, studying Jesus deepens our relationship with him, and transforms us into people who live in the light of his grace and truth।

<!-- p63 -->
Understanding who Jesus is and what he has accomplished is the foundation of the Christian faith। The many names, titles, and roles of Jesus highlight different aspects of his nature and mission, giving a more complete picture of his love and authority।

<!-- p64 -->
As in our study of God, so too Scripture is filled with diverse names in reference to Jesus। Each name represents a specific aspect of his whole character, ministry, and role। These names are given to reveal a facet of Jesus to us। For your personal study, a reference table is included in the appendix: *Table 12 - Names of Jesus।* Read these names and study the Scripture references।
