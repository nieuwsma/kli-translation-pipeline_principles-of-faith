# Chapter 11 - Baptisms - Water and Spirit

<!-- p1 -->
In our modern age, separated from Jewish roots, baptism is often understood only in terms of water baptism and the baptism of the Holy Spirit (Pentecost).

<!-- p2 -->
The book of Hebrews was written to Jewish readers. In our modern age, most Christians do not have Jewish roots or heritage. Without Jewish roots and culture, many of us lack the cultural context that first-century Messianic Jews (those Jewish believers who believed in Jesus) shared. Since we were not raised in the Jewish tradition, we generally do not know much about the feasts, fasts, prayers, washings, rites, and rituals that Jewish believers would have known.

<!-- p3 -->
In Greek, the word for baptisms, or washings, is: **Baptismos** (bap-tis-mos’ - **G909**) — the act of immersing or washing. It denotes ritual washings or ceremonial purification. It can refer to immersion, rites of purification, or washings practiced in Jewish and Christian contexts.

<!-- p4 -->
Baptisms is one of the foundational teachings mentioned in Hebrews:

<!-- p5 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p6 -->
> **<u>Hebrews 6:1-2</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Therefore, leaving the discussion of the elementary principles of Christ, let us go on to perfection; not laying again the foundation of repentance from dead works and of faith toward God,
>
> of the doctrine of baptisms, of laying on of hands, of resurrection of the dead, and of eternal judgment.
>

<!-- p8 -->
## Old Covenant Washings - Historical Context

<!-- p9 -->
In the Old Covenant, ritual washings played a central role in maintaining ceremonial purity and symbolizing holiness before God. These washings were prescribed in the Law of Moses and applied both to the priests who served in the tabernacle of meeting and to the people of Israel. For example, the priests were required to wash their hands and feet at the bronze laver before entering the tabernacle of meeting or approaching the altar, symbolizing their readiness to minister in the presence of the holy God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="30" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 30:18-21</span>). Likewise, procedures for cleansing from defilement—such as touching a dead body or skin diseases—involved washing with water as a sign of restoration to purity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LEV" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Leviticus 14:8</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="NUM" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Numbers 19:17-19</span>). These acts were outward demonstrations of an inward desire for holiness, underscoring the importance of being set apart for God.

<!-- p10 -->
In the Old Testament, ritual washings had to be repeated regularly to maintain ceremonial purity. Unlike a permanent solution, these ordinances provided only temporary external cleansing and left the deeper issue of sin unresolved. The author of Hebrews highlights this limitation, explaining that these practices were “symbolic for the present time,” and were not able to cleanse the worshiper’s conscience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:9-10</span>). These washings were a continual reminder of human imperfection and the need for something greater—an ultimate cleansing that could purify not only the body but also the heart and spirit.

<!-- p11 -->
The ritual washings of the Old Covenant find their fulfillment in Christ, who through His sacrifice provides complete and eternal cleansing. In contrast to the temporary ordinances of the Law, the blood of Christ cleanses fully—not only the body but also the conscience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:13-14</span>). Through His death and resurrection Jesus accomplished what the Old Testament rites only foreshadowed—true reconciliation with God. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:22</span> calls believers to “draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith; having our hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience and our bodies washed with pure water.” This language links Christ’s work to the imagery of Old Testament washings, while showing its far greater effectiveness. His sacrifice not only covers sin but removes it entirely (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 1:7</span>). Thus, those ritual washings were a shadow pointing to the deeper cleansing that only Christ could provide, fulfilling humanity’s need for holiness through divine intervention.

<!-- p12 -->
Reiterating this, Christians are not obligated to perform any ritual washings. Under the Old Covenant, the washings did not provide salvation, nor do they provide salvation in the New Covenant. Ritual washing will neither increase our standing before God nor earn us favor. Paul strongly rebukes the Judaizers in Galatia (read Galatians) who tried to require Jewish markers (e.g., circumcision) for salvation. Salvation is by faith in Jesus alone!

<!-- p13 -->
## John’s Baptism

<!-- p14 -->
The canonical books of the New Testament mention several kinds of baptisms. We have already discussed the baptisms and washings of Jewish tradition.

<!-- p15 -->
The focus of John the Baptist’s ministry was to prepare the way for the coming of Jesus Christ. His baptism, called a baptism of repentance for the forgiveness of sins (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 1:4</span>), called people to confess their sins, turn their hearts to God, and be ready for the arrival of the Messiah. It was an outward sign of inward repentance, symbolizing the desire for spiritual cleansing and readiness for the kingdom of God. But John’s baptism was temporary and preparatory—it pointed to the greater work that Christ would accomplish. John’s baptism centered on repentance, but it did not grant full forgiveness or spiritual new life, because it did not include the indwelling and empowering of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:11-12</span>). By contrast, John openly declared that One greater than he was coming, who would baptize not only with water but with the Holy Spirit and fire (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 3:16</span>).

<!-- p16 -->
John’s baptism was fulfilled—and far surpassed—by the death, burial, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. Christian baptism, which Jesus established and the apostles adopted, signifies a deeper reality—union with Christ, dying to sin, and rising to new life (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 6:3-4</span>). Unlike John’s baptism, Christian baptism is directly connected to faith in Jesus’ atoning sacrifice and to the indwelling of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:38</span>). The coming of the Holy Spirit on the Day of Pentecost marked the beginning of the New Covenant and a Spirit-empowered life, which John’s baptism could not provide. For this reason, John’s baptism is no longer practiced, because it has been replaced by the baptism commanded by Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19</span>), which encompasses repentance, faith, and the sealing of believers by the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>).

<!-- p17 -->
## Christ’s Baptism

<!-- p18 -->
Christ’s baptism became a powerful example for His followers and established the foundation for Christian water baptism. Although Jesus was sinless, He accepted baptism from John “to fulfill all righteousness” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:15</span>). In doing so, He identified Himself with humanity’s need for repentance and pointed forward to His own death, burial, and resurrection. At His baptism the Holy Spirit descended like a dove, and the Father declared, “This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:16-17</span>). This moment marked the beginning of Jesus’ public ministry and revealed the unity of the Father, Son, and Spirit, foreshadowing the spiritual transformation believers would experience in Christ.

<!-- p19 -->
Later Jesus commanded His followers to “go and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19</span>). The water baptism instituted by Christ became an **act of obedience** and a **public confession of faith** for believers. It identifies their lives with Jesus’ death, burial, and resurrection (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 6:3-4</span>). Going under the water symbolizes dying to sin, and coming up out of the water signifies being raised to new life in Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:12</span>). Yet it is necessary to clarify that this outward act is not the source of salvation, but rather a **visible sign** of the **inner work of grace**, already accomplished by the blood of Jesus.

<!-- p20 -->
Water baptism is a **symbol** of the cleansing power of Christ’s blood and the renewal of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:26</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>). It signifies repentance, faith, and a commitment to follow Jesus. It is the outward testimony of an inner transformation-believers’ way of publicly declaring their allegiance to Christ and their participation in the body of Christ, that is, the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:41</span>). Although it was commanded by Jesus and demonstrated in the early church, it does not **save** a person. Salvation is by grace alone, through faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>), and true cleansing happens spiritually through the blood of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 1:5</span>). Water baptism is a response to that salvation, an act of obedience, not a necessary condition for it.

<!-- p21 -->
In the New Testament, water baptism is consistently associated with **repentance and faith** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:38</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 16:16</span>), both of which require a personal decision to follow Christ-something infants are not able to do. Baptism symbolizes a believer’s deliberate identification with Christ’s death, burial, and resurrection (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 6:3-4</span>), and also their commitment to live an obedient life to Him. Some denominations (e.g., Catholic, Lutheran) practice infant baptism as a covenant sign similar to circumcision; however, because infants cannot express personal faith or repentance, the practice of **infant baptism** does not accord with the biblical pattern of **believer’s baptism**. Instead, many churches hold **dedication ceremonies** for infants, following the example of Hannah dedicating Samuel to the Lord (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1SA" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Samuel 1:27-28</span>) and of Mary and Joseph presenting Jesus in the temple (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 2:22</span>). Dedication is not a substitute for baptism, but a pledge by parents to raise their child in the faith, teach them God’s Word, and model a devout life until the child is old enough to decide to follow Christ and be baptized.

<!-- p22 -->
In contrast to the Old Covenant’s **ceremonial washings**, which required repeated rituals for purity, Christian baptism is a once-for-all event that reflects the finished work of grace. The Old Testament washings symbolized external purification and foreshadowed the deeper spiritual cleansing to come through Christ. By contrast, New Testament baptism symbolizes the inner renewal and new birth accomplished by the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>). It points to the believer’s participation in the New Covenant, where cleansing from sin is permanent and grounded in Christ’s finished work.

<!-- p23 -->
Water baptism is closely connected to the **promise of the Holy Spirit**. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:38</span>, Peter calls the people to repent, be baptized, and receive the gift of the Holy Spirit. While water baptism publicly portrays the believer’s repentance and faith, true baptism happens within when the Holy Spirit washes, regenerates, and seals the believer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:13</span>). This spiritual washing makes us members of Christ’s body and empowers us to live a holy life. The **role of the Holy Spirit** in baptism reminds us that salvation is accomplished not by rites but by God’s transforming power.

<!-- p24 -->
It is important to distinguish between the **symbolic act of water baptism** and the spiritual reality of salvation accomplished by the blood of Christ. The Bible clearly teaches that salvation is based on faith in Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 10:9-10</span>) and not on works, including baptism (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>). However, water baptism is an **act of obedience** that publicly identifies the believer with Christ and the church. It shows an outward commitment to live as a disciple of Jesus, but it does not add to or complete the work of salvation.

<!-- p25 -->
Christ’s water baptism set an example for believers to follow, demonstrating humility, obedience, and readiness for ministry. For Christians today, water baptism serves as a public declaration of faith, symbolizing death to sin and resurrection to new life. Although it is commanded by Jesus and is an important step of obedience, it is not necessary for salvation. True cleansing comes by faith in the blood of Jesus, and the power for the Christian life comes from the work of the Holy Spirit in the believer. Baptism unites us with Christ and His church, reminding us of the inner transformation and renewal that God has already accomplished.

<!-- p26 -->
Having established water baptism as the outward sign of faith, we now turn to the baptism of the Holy Spirit, which empowers believers for ministry and service.

<!-- p27 -->
## Baptism of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p28 -->
Baptism in the Holy Spirit is a profound and transformative experience that God has promised to empower believers for ministry, service, and holy living. Every believer receives the Holy Spirit at new birth-this is how we are made spiritually alive. But subsequently, we should also be baptized in the Holy Spirit, which empowers us with enduring boldness, spiritual gifts, and effective ministry.

<!-- p29 -->
Whereas water baptism is the public declaration of faith and symbolizes the cleansing of sin by Christ’s blood, Spirit baptism represents God’s powerful presence in the believer’s life. Together, these two kinds of baptism form complementary aspects of the Christian walk-one an outward sign of obedience and the other an inward source of power and renewal. The baptism of the Holy Spirit is about the Spirit being upon us, not within. At salvation all believers receive the Spirit within them, but when God’s Spirit comes upon them, they receive power and boldness for ministry.

<!-- p30 -->
John the Baptist foreshadowed the baptism in the Holy Spirit, saying,

<!-- p31 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p32 -->
> **<u>Matthew 3:11</u>** (NKJV)
>
> I indeed baptize you with water for repentance, but He who comes after me is mightier than I; I am not worthy to carry His sandals. He will baptize you with the Holy Spirit and fire.
>

<!-- p33 -->
</span>

<!-- p34 -->
This promise pointed to a greater work that Jesus would accomplish after His resurrection-sending the Holy Spirit to indwell and empower His followers. Jesus confirmed this when He commanded His disciples to wait for the gift of the Spirit, explaining that they would receive power to be His witnesses (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>). This promise was fulfilled on the day of Pentecost, when the disciples were filled with the Holy Spirit and gave evidence by speaking in other tongues, prophesying, and boldly proclaiming the gospel (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:1-4</span>).

<!-- p35 -->
This outpouring marked the beginning of the **Church Age** and indicated that the power of the Spirit is now available to all believers. This promise was not limited to the apostles but extends to all who believe, as Peter declared, *“This promise is for you and your children, and for all who are far off-for all whom the Lord our God will call”* (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="39" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:39</span>).

<!-- p36 -->
### The Spirit within versus the Spirit upon

<!-- p37 -->
When a person puts their faith in Jesus, the Holy Spirit gives them new life from within. This is what it means to be born again (**regenerated**). At that very moment of faith, the Holy Spirit dwells **within** the believer, makes them spiritually alive, and adopts them into God’s family (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:5-6</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>). This is the new birth, or being born again, as Jesus described in John 3.

<!-- p38 -->
Scriptural basis:

<!-- p39 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>* - “He saved us through the washing of regeneration and renewal by the Holy Spirit.”

<!-- p40 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>* - The Holy Spirit seals believers as a **guarantee** of their inheritance.

<!-- p41 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:9</span>* - "Whoever does not have the Spirit of Christ is not His."

<!-- p42 -->
This work of regeneration is universal for all believers at the time of salvation and establishes the Spirit’s permanent indwelling. It is not dependent on any external act, such as water baptism, and guarantees salvation by grace through faith.

<!-- p43 -->
In contrast, the baptism in the Holy Spirit is a distinct and subsequent experience that equips believers with power for service and witness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>). Whereas regeneration relates to new life and identity, the baptism of the Spirit focuses **on** empowerment and the release of spiritual gifts. This baptism is often accompanied by signs and manifestations of the gifts of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p44 -->
Scriptural basis:

<!-- p45 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p46 -->
> **<u>Acts 1:8</u>** (NKJV)
>
> But when the Holy Spirit comes upon you, you will receive power; and in Jerusalem, and all Judea and Samaria, and to the end of the earth, you will be my witnesses."
>

<!-- p47 -->
</span>

<!-- p48 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:15-17</span>* - The Samaritans had believed and been baptized, but later they received the Holy Spirit through prayer and the laying on of hands.

<!-- p49 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 19:6</span>* - The disciples in Ephesus, who already believed, received the Holy Spirit when Paul laid hands on them.

<!-- p50 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit does not replace regeneration, but builds upon it, and equips the believer for boldness in spiritual service and ministry. Receiving baptism in the Holy Spirit is a free gift for those who are saved, not a condition for obtaining salvation. It is not necessary that a person manifest any <u>specific</u> gift of the Holy Spirit to prove their ‘baptism in the Holy Spirit,’ but we should expect that even today the baptism of the Holy Spirit will be manifested as it was in the book of Acts, with signs and boldness! Some people err in thinking that if a person does not speak in other tongues, they are not saved, or do not have the baptism of the Holy Spirit. Not everyone will receive every gift or every manifestation of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29-31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:29-31</span>).

<!-- p51 -->
## How to Receive the Baptism of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p52 -->
In Scripture, the baptism of the Holy Spirit is described as a gift promised to believers and received by faith. It is distinct from salvation and is primarily for empowerment and witness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>). The baptism of the Holy Spirit is a gift of empowerment for believers, promised by Jesus and fulfilled on the Day of Pentecost. It is received through repentance, faith, and asking God. Although the laying on of hands is often used, ultimately it is God who baptizes with the Spirit.

<!-- p53 -->
Scripture presents speaking in other tongues and prophecy as common signs, but the purpose of this baptism is far greater—to provide boldness, power, and spiritual gifts for ministry. Every believer is encouraged to seek this experience as part of their spiritual journey, trusting that God will equip them for His work.

<!-- p54 -->
The Bible sets out some key principles regarding how this baptism is received:

<!-- p55 -->
### **1. The baptism of the Holy Spirit is a free gift of God**

<!-- p56 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit is not something believers can earn—it is a free gift given by God. God wants to give good gifts to His children and is eager to give the Holy Spirit to those who ask with faith and a desire for empowerment.

<!-- p57 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p58 -->
> **<u>Luke 11:13</u>** (NKJV)
>
> If you then, who are evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to those who ask Him!"
>

<!-- p59 -->
</span>

<!-- p60 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="38-39" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p61 -->
> **<u>Acts 2:38-39</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Then Peter said to them, "Repent, and let each one of you be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of your sins; and you will receive the gift of the Holy Spirit.
>

<!-- p63 -->
###  **2. Ask in faith, seek, and receive**

<!-- p64 -->
The Scriptures emphasize that the baptism of the Holy Spirit is received by asking in prayer and with faith-filled expectation. Faith, combined with the willingness to ask and receive, is the key to experiencing the baptism of the Spirit. If you are not sure that God wants to give you the baptism of the Holy Spirit, ask Him about it; He is a good Father! When He prompts you, it is possible that you may feel a tangible presence of God in your body that you have never experienced before. If that does not happen, that’s okay! God is great, and He does not need to be bound to any one perfect method. If you feel that God is drawing your heart, yield yourself to Him!

<!-- p65 -->
- **<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:15-17</span>** - Peter and John prayed and **laid hands** on the believers, and they **received the Holy Spirit**.

<!-- p66 -->
- **<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="44-46" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 10:44-46</span>** - Cornelius and his household **received the Holy Spirit** while Peter was preaching-without laying hands on them-which shows that ultimately **God is the one who baptizes**.

## Gifts of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p79 -->
When the Holy Spirit was poured out, and people were baptized in the Holy Spirit, manifestations of the Holy Spirit often accompanied it.

<!-- p80 -->
There are several lists of gifts in the New Testament:

<!-- p81 -->
- Motivational gifts given by God: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:6-8</span>

<!-- p82 -->
- Discussed in the Church chapter.

<!-- p83 -->
- The fivefold ministry gifts given by Jesus: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-13</span>

<!-- p84 -->
- Discussed in the Church chapter.

<!-- p85 -->
- Manifestation gifts given by the Holy Spirit: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span>

<!-- p86 -->
- Discussed here.

<!-- p87 -->
Each of these groups of gifts is given for different purposes and functions differently, yet together they point to the **diversity** and **unity** found among the gifts within **the body of Christ**. Each gift plays a unique role in fulfilling God's purposes.

<!-- p88 -->
### Purpose &amp; Operation of the Gifts

<!-- p89 -->
The primary purpose of the gifts of the Holy Spirit is to build up the body of Christ and to glorify God:

<!-- p90 -->
- **Edification** - to strengthen and encourage believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
- **Unity** - to promote harmony and cooperation within the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:12-13</span>).

<!-- p92 -->
- **Evangelism** - to demonstrate God's power as a testimony to unbelievers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="43" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:43</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:6-7</span>).

<!-- p93 -->
- **Service** - to meet others' needs through acts of mercy and generosity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:6-8</span>).

<!-- p94 -->
These gifts are not for personal glory but for the common good (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:7</span>). When exercised in love and humility, they work most effectively and reflect the character of Christ (1 Corinthians 13).

<!-- p95 -->
Paul emphasized that spiritual gifts should be exercised with decency and order (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:40</span>). This ensures that the use of the gifts promotes unity, peace, and understanding rather than confusion or division.

<!-- p96 -->
- Prophecies should be evaluated (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:29</span>).

<!-- p97 -->
- When a message in other tongues is given for the church, pray that it may be interpreted (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:27-28</span>).

<!-- p98 -->
- Gifts should always bring **edification** in the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:12</span>).

<!-- p99 -->
This order does not quench the Spirit; rather, it ensures that the gifts operate in a way that **honors God** and **benefits others**.

<!-- p100 -->
The use of spiritual gifts must always be rooted in love. Paul devotes an entire chapter (1 Corinthians 13) to explaining that even the greatest spiritual gifts are meaningless without love. Love provides the motivation and framework by which the gifts are used in ways that build others up rather than tear them down.

<!-- p101 -->
Additionally, the gifts operate in the manner the Spirit wills (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:11</span>). They can neither be produced nor controlled by human effort-they are sovereignly given and empowered by the Holy Spirit, often at moments of need or ministry. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 13:8</span> says that eventually prophecy and tongues will cease. Theologically, those who believe that the gifts of the Holy Spirit have ceased are called ‘Cessationists’. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 13:10</span> says “When the perfect \[Jesus\] comes, that which is partial will be done away.” Because Jesus has not yet returned for His church, the gifts are still at work on the earth.

<!-- p102 -->
It is extremely important to recognize that the gifts of the Spirit must not be separated from the fruit of the Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-23</span>). Whereas the gifts display power, the fruit reveals character-love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control.

<!-- p103 -->
- Gifts without fruit: A believer can operate in the gifts, yet lack maturity or character, leading to pride, misuse, or harm.

<!-- p104 -->
- Fruit without gifts: A believer may have a devout character, but may lack the power to minister effectively.

<!-- p105 -->
Both are necessary. The gifts equip believers for service. The fruit is an indicator of their spiritual health (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 7:16</span>) and provides a ‘pleasant taste’ to those who receive it.

<!-- p106 -->
### Explanation of the 9 manifestation gifts of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p107 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span> lists the 9 manifestation gifts of the Holy Spirit:

<!-- p108 -->
1.  **Word of wisdom** - Supernatural insight about God’s purposes or direction for a situation. It applies divine wisdom to complex problems or decisions (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:10</span>).

<!-- p109 -->
2.  **Word of knowledge** - A supernatural disclosure of information about a person, event, or circumstance that could not have been known naturally (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 4:17-18</span>).

<!-- p110 -->
3.  **Faith** - The supernatural ability to trust God for miraculous matters, even when circumstances are impossible. This gift inspires courage and confidence (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 3:6-7</span>).

<!-- p111 -->
4.  **Gifts of healings** - The supernatural power to heal physical, emotional, or spiritual illnesses and restore wholeness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 3:6-8</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 9:17-18</span>).

<!-- p112 -->
5.  **Working of miracles** - The ability to perform supernatural acts that defy natural laws, such as multiplying food or raising the dead (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40-41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 9:40-41</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 2:9-11</span>).

<!-- p113 -->
6.  **Prophecy** - Declaring God’s truth or will under the inspiration of the Holy Spirit. It should be encouraging, edifying, and comforting (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>).

<!-- p114 -->
7.  **Discernment of spirits (discernment)** - The ability to assess whether a spiritual influence is from God, from the enemy, or from a human source (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 16:16-18</span>).

<!-- p115 -->
8.  **Different kinds of tongues** - Speaking languages unknown to the speaker, often as worship, prayer, or a sign to unbelievers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:4</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:22</span>).

<!-- p116 -->
9.  **Interpretation of tongues** - The supernatural ability to interpret messages spoken in tongues so that the church may be edified (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:27</span>).

<!-- p117 -->
Paul makes it clear that all the gifts are valuable and should work together for the common good of the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:7</span>). Nevertheless, he also encourages believers to desire the greater gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:31</span>). The greater gifts are often considered those that most directly edify the church, such as prophecy (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:1-5</span>).

<!-- p118 -->
- Prophecy is emphasized in particular because it strengthens, encourages, and comforts others (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>). Paul teaches believers to earnestly desire this gift because it promotes clarity and understanding in the church.

<!-- p119 -->
- Gifts like tongues are extremely valuable for personal edification and prayer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:4</span>), but Paul teaches that when a message in tongues is given for the church, pray for interpretation so that they can ensure the church is built up (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:27-28</span>).

<!-- p120 -->
Although the Holy Spirit gives gifts, we must bow before and surrender to the Holy Spirit in order to use them. Like muscles and talents, as we practice yielding to the Holy Spirit so that we can use his gifts, we become more confident and reliable in their use.
