<!-- p1 -->
Faith is a foundational concept in Christianity। The word ‘faith’ is used so commonly in Christianity that subtle misconceptions and misunderstandings sometimes distort its meaning। But it is not something you can hold in your hands—it is formed in your heart as you learn to trust God। Conviction is faith।

<!-- p2 -->
The author of the letter to the Hebrews teaches us that we are to have ‘faith toward God’।

### Definition of Faith

<!-- p10 -->
Faith is an aspect of Christianity that is often misunderstood and misrepresented। As the saying goes, “There is a ditch on both sides of the road”! Because in modern Christianity the word ‘faith’ is very common without proper explanation, it ends up taking on exaggerated, mistaken definitions fueled by misunderstandings। Faith is often portrayed either as a broad statement of abstract belief, or almost as some mystical power or “magic”—something you possess in a definite, measured, and limited quantity।

<!-- p11 -->
#### What Faith Is Not

<!-- p12 -->
When faith is misrepresented in an <u>abstract form</u>, it contains no specific conviction। A little probing makes this clear because this conception of faith fails to become specific। If you ask, ‘What do you believe?’ or ‘Why do you believe?’ a person who holds this kind of understanding of faith often cannot explain beyond ‘I believe।’ <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 3:15</span> says that we should be ready to give the reason for the hope within us।

<!-- p13 -->
Sometimes people think of faith as some kind of magical power, but Scripture does not teach this। Faith is about a real relationship built on trust। In Scripture, faith is far more dynamic and relational—it is rooted in conviction, trust, and assurance in God’s character and promises। It is not an impersonal force, but an engaged response toward God that shapes thoughts, feelings, and actions।

<!-- p14 -->
How is faith related to conviction?

<!-- p15 -->
Our beliefs shape the way we see and interact with the world around us। Faith molds our perception and motivates our actions। Our brains are built this way—we live according to what seems possible to us।

<!-- p16 -->
We act on what we consider, understand, and perceive to be possible। When we walk down a hallway, our brain recognizes the boundaries of the floor and walls and automatically tells our feet where to step and what to avoid। This truth extends beyond navigating the physical world around us।

<!-- p17 -->
Suppose you believe that it is impossible for you to be financially independent or to live a healthy and active lifestyle। Then your actions will follow that belief, and, consistent with your belief, you will neither do what seems impossible to you nor likely even attempt it।

<!-- p18 -->
If you believe that God’s nature is loveless and punitive, then this perception of Him will keep you from walking with Him as His child।

<!-- p19 -->
#### Faith is Conviction

<!-- p20 -->
So, what is faith? Assurance! The Greek word ***pistis*** means assurance, or the result of being persuaded. We are to have assurance toward God. In English we can say faith (persuasion) ‘**in** God’. If you are married, hopefully you have ‘**in’** faith in your spouse. You believe that they will do what they have said they will do, and you believe that they are authentic and true. This trust, going beyond what they have said, should also extend toward them in general.

<!-- p21 -->
It takes time and experience to develop faith, assurance, and trust in a relationship. Hopefully in life you have learned how trustworthy relationships are developed with another person. The same principles you have learned that allow you to increase your trust in an acquaintance will also enable you to increase your faith in God. Trust in a relationship grows over time, in experience, through consistency, and through small and large actions that build your confidence in the other person’s character and nature.

<!-- p22 -->
‘Faith toward God’ is relational. Relationships are made of two parties, not one. The good news about our faith in God is that God Himself takes responsibility for His side of the relationship (made possible by Jesus’ atoning death, and by the infilling of His Spirit in the new birth), and He also helps us on our side (through the work of the Comforter - the Holy Spirit -). God is doing almost all the work; our part (as we will discuss further in this chapter) is to walk with Him in obedience, *together*.

### Jesus matures our faith

<!-- p24 -->
Jesus is the author and finisher of our faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 12:2</span>). Our faith is made to continually grow and expand. We do not reach a point where we have enough ‘faith’, rather our faith keeps growing. Today consider a deep and personal relationship in your life. As in any relationship, we do not reach a specific milestone where we now ‘know’ that person. We continue to know them, and increase our knowledge about them, and this refines, shapes, and grows our relationship.

<!-- p25 -->
How do we increase our faith in God? By continually experiencing Him in His many manifestations, revelations, and attributes. Again, consider a deep personal relationship in your life. You deepen a relationship with a person by connecting with them in different ways, different formats, different mediums, different settings. You talk to them, send them messages, perhaps even write to them. You meet them personally, and in group settings. You discuss the things you value, and perhaps you even challenge one another. This happens in countless ways. Amid these many expressions you begin to know that person more deeply.

<!-- p26 -->
All of us, through Jesus, should know God as our Savior. Recall the names of God (*see Appendix),* simply because we know God as Savior does not mean that we have yet experienced Him as Deliverer, Healer, Counselor, Lord.

<!-- p27 -->
If you are in a situation where you have become assured (faith) to know God as your Savior, then building this relationship will give you such confidence in His character that you can know Him in other ways as well.

<!-- p28 -->
I am blessed with many extremely deep relationships in my life. One such relationship is with my wife. In our relationship, because of the level of trust (faith) we have in one another, my wife can tell me things she will do, which I have not yet seen happen, and because of her proven character I have the confidence to believe her.

<!-- p29 -->
This example shows how our deep relationship with God can be experienced with trust and confidence! It may be that we have not yet fully experienced Him (indeed we will never be able to experience Him fully in this life), but as we mature in our relationship, we can trust Him beyond the limit of what I personally know, and what I have already seen happen.

<!-- p30 -->
Jesus increases our faith through the experiences we have with Him. One important way we can and should experience Him is through His Word.

<!-- p36 -->
When we replace the word *faith* with *assurance*, it reads:

<!-- p37 -->
*‘Then assurance comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God’.* This truth is naturally self-evident. A major way that someone comes to assurance is that they have been persuaded (have been assured), and we are assured when someone presents an ‘argument’-or a reasoned and clear explanation.

<!-- p38 -->
There are two Greek words in the Bible for ‘Word’-**logos** and **rhema**. Understanding the difference between them shows us how faith grows.

<!-- p39 -->
**Logos**: Generally, it denotes the **concept, thought, reason, or broad expression of the Word of God**. It encompasses a universal, foundational truth or message and is often used to describe God’s eternal wisdom and Christ Himself as the living Word.

<!-- p40 -->
**Rhema (G4487)**: emphasizes a **specific, spoken word or utterance**. It generally refers to a particular word or message given at a specific moment, especially a spoken or revealed word that applies directly to a person's situation.

<!-- p41 -->
*Logos* is like the overall message of a book, whereas *rhema* is like a specific passage or sentence that speaks directly to a person at a particular moment. Where *logos* is God's overarching truth, *rhema* is when a portion of that same truth is spoken to a person in a personal and applied way. Together, they reflect the fullness of God's communication: *logos* as the broad, foundational truths of God's character and will, and *rhema* as the specific, applied word that makes that truth come alive in a person's life and circumstances.

<!-- p42 -->
### Example of a rhema word in action

<!-- p43 -->
God promises revelation and wisdom to all who ask. When He speaks to us, that utterance is a *rhema* word. Rhema words are from God and serve to confirm, guide, and direct us. They are not a substitute for the *logos* word, nor do they carry the authority of Scripture. *Rhema* words should be evaluated by the same standard as prophecy or the interpretation of tongues (see the chapter on the Holy Spirit for an in-depth study on this topic).

<!-- p44 -->
At many stages in my career, I have prayed for divine guidance. A few years ago, I was considering beginning the process of looking for a new opportunity, and during my prayer time I heard God say to me, ‘*Just wait on Me, I have an opportunity for you.*’ I was starting to look for a new job because I was dissatisfied with my current role and felt discouraged. The word of God’s direction, ‘*Wait on Me*’, gave me peace and confidence in an otherwise restless time.

<!-- p45 -->
Three or four months passed; I kept working diligently and faithfully, keeping my attitude positive. God would remind me, *‘Wait on Me’*, then His promise was fulfilled. I received a promotion opportunity and a generous increase in pay.

<!-- p46 -->
I could have continued my job search; I could have left. I could have started fresh somewhere else. There is no doubt that that promotion would have gone to someone else! But because I sought the Lord’s counsel and was assured by His **rhema word**, I obeyed and benefited. God’s words are **living and personal**, and He promises to give us manifold and generous wisdom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:5</span>).

<!-- p47 -->
I have had opportunities dozens, if not hundreds, of times when I prayed and asked God what to do, and He gave me a rhema word. Not every outcome was as remarkable, but all of them gave the peace and confidence that I was obeying God and that He would make everything right. They were exactly the words I needed for that moment! Many times, when God brings a rhema word, it is from the logos itself. He brings to my remembrance a portion of Scripture, “*Do not fear, for I am with you!*” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="41" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 41:10</span>), “*By His stripes we are healed*” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="53" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 53:5</span>), “*Do not grow weary in doing good!*” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:9</span>), and so many such examples go on!

<!-- p48 -->
Receiving a rhema word not only strengthens our faith but also deepens our relationship with God, as we trust Him more fully in our daily decisions. We will continue our study of learning to hear from God in the chapter on the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p49 -->
Our faith is also strengthened by the work we see God doing, but sometimes what we see in the natural world challenges what we hear from God. Reconciling the discrepancy between what we see and what we hear is an important ministry of the Holy Spirit, who promises to lead us into all truth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span>).

Thomas believed in Jesus and trusted his fellow disciples, but he did not believe that Jesus had risen bodily; he insisted that unless he experienced it personally, he could not actively believe it.

<!-- p60 -->
Jesus goes on to say, ‘*Blessed are those who have not seen and have believed*’. This is not Jesus encouraging ‘blind or ignorant faith’ or rejecting the use of empirical evidence. Rather, He is saying that when we can believe without the need for empirical proof, we are more blessed. Why? If our faith, our trust, were limited only to what we have seen empirically demonstrated, we could never have faith for a future work. We would have great confidence in what has already been accomplished, but we would not walk in God’s peace for our tomorrow and our future.

<!-- p61 -->
Taking relationships as an example, if my attitude were constantly ‘<u>I’ll believe it when I see it!</u>’, how challenging would my relationship with my wife be? In contrast, a trust-based relationship says, ‘I know that you are a trustworthy person.’

### Practical steps to grow your faith

<!-- p153 -->
Walking with God is relational, but if you want good relationships there are certain skills to learn. In human relationships, the skills that yield strong and secure relationships are based on:

<!-- p154 -->
- Open and free dialogue and communication

<!-- p155 -->
- Healthy conflict resolution

<!-- p156 -->
- Commitment

<!-- p157 -->
- Consistency

<!-- p158 -->
If you want a strong, vibrant relationship, you need to show up consistently, reveal yourself (communicate — our thoughts and feelings), receive the other person (listen), and walk with them! All these skills apply to our relationship with God as well.

<!-- p159 -->
Beyond the basic relational skills, there are certain specific practices and disciplines that will strengthen your relationship with God:

<!-- p160 -->
- **Build and maintain a prayer life; listen to God and communicate with Him.** Regular, heartfelt prayer is essential for a close relationship with God. Through prayer you can share your concerns, joys, and gratitude, asking for His guidance and strength. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 4:6-7</span>*) Being sensitive to the voice of the Holy Spirit helps you discern God’s will, gain wisdom in decisions, and grow in spiritual maturity. Set aside quiet, reflective time to hear His direction. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:14</span>*)

<!-- p161 -->
- **Spend time in worship.** Worship is a powerful way to draw near to God, expressing love and reverence. Through praise, song, or quiet contemplation, worship deepens your awareness of His presence. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="100" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 100:2-4</span>*)

<!-- p162 -->
- **Read, study, and meditate on Scripture.** God’s Word reveals His character, promises, and instructions for life. Dedicating time to studying Scripture increases your faith, understanding, and spiritual resilience. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>*)

<!-- p163 -->
- **Obey His commands.** Following God’s instructions demonstrates your love, respect, and trust for Him. Walking in obedience aligns your life with His purposes and brings blessings. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:15</span>*)

<!-- p164 -->
- **Be actively present and serve in a local church.** Connecting within a church community provides spiritual support, encouragement, and opportunities to serve others. Staying engaged creates a sense of belonging and strengthens your commitment to God’s mission. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:24-25</span>*)

<!-- p165 -->
- **Build relationships with other believers.** Fellowship with other Christians provides accountability, encouragement, and shared wisdom. These relationships enrich your journey of faith and help you grow through mutual support. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="27" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 27:17</span>*)

<!-- p166 -->
Our ‘faith toward God’ is not a one-time event for salvation, but a continual practice and relationship that we must cultivate throughout our lives. The benefits of a trustworthy and beneficial relationship are exceedingly many, but we must do the good work of nurturing that faith and growth so that it continues to flourish.
