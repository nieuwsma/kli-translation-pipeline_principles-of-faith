# Chapter 13 - Spiritual Authority

<!-- p1 -->
Jesus is the supreme and complete source of spiritual authority. As the victorious Christ, He has ultimate authority over all things in heaven and on earth.

<!-- p2 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p3 -->
> **<u>Matthew 28:18-20</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And Jesus came near and said to them, "All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to Me.
>
> Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit,
>
> and teach them to observe all things that I have commanded you; and behold, I am with you always, to the end of the age." Amen.
>

<!-- p4 -->
</span>

<!-- p5 -->
In this chapter we will study the authority and power of Christ, which authorities and powers He has entrusted to us (His church), and how to operate in the authority of the name of Jesus. We will also study angels and demonic beings and examine their authority, power, and natures.

<!-- p6 -->
Authority means having the right to act. Power is the ability to carry it out. Both are necessary in the spiritual life. Authority is different from power. Let us look at the following table, which compares authority and power, and consider some examples.

<!-- p7 -->
[See Table 7](../tables/table7.md)

<!-- p8 -->
Here are some examples to illustrate the difference between power and authority.

<!-- p9 -->
Example 1: At the workplace, an employee may have the talent and skill to sell the company's assets (power). This power is valid only if they also receive the corresponding permission (authority) from the owners of the company. Without this authority, selling the company's assets is theft.

<!-- p10 -->
Example 2: A police officer is given special training in weapons and hand-to-hand combat to learn how to apprehend and physically subdue a criminal (power). Police officers are given the duty (authority), backed by laws, to enforce criminal laws.

## The source of Jesus' authority

<!-- p74 -->
Now that we have seen exactly what authority is, we need to know who the source of that authority is. Jesus' authority is both divine and delegated. It originates from God the Father, is confirmed by His death and resurrection, and operates in complete harmony with the Father's will. Although it is supreme, His authority still respects human free will, demonstrating God's love rather than coercive control.

<!-- p75 -->
Jesus' authority is rooted in His divine identity as the Son of God and is granted by the Father. The Father has entrusted all authority to Christ, including judgment and rule over heaven and earth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 11:27</span>). Jesus' authority reflects the unity and love within the Trinity, where the Son perfectly fulfills the Father's plan (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="35" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:35</span>).

<!-- p76 -->
Although Jesus, as the Son of God, possessed inherent authority, His victory over sin, death, and hell was fully established through His death and resurrection. In His sacrifice, Christ's obedience brought about His exaltation (seated at the right hand of the Father), thereby securing His authority over all creation. By conquering death, Christ obtained supreme dominion over life and eternity.

## Laying on of Hands

<!-- p116 -->
The laying on of hands is an important Bible-based practice that is used as a symbol of blessing, appointment, healing, and the transmission of spiritual authority or gifts in both the Old Testament and the New Testament. It is not merely a physical act—it represents a spiritual connection and impartation under God's direction.

<!-- p117 -->
In the Old Testament, the laying on of hands was often associated with blessing and dedication. For example, Jacob laid his hands on his grandsons, Ephraim and Manasseh, and blessed them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="48" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 48:14</span>). Likewise, Moses laid hands on Joshua to appoint him as his successor, and imparted leadership authority and wisdom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="NUM" data-chapter="27" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Numbers 27:18-23</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="DEU" data-chapter="34" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Deuteronomy 34:9</span>). This act publicly demonstrated God's choice and empowerment for the work ahead.

<!-- p118 -->
In the New Testament, the laying on of hands is closely connected with the ministry of Jesus and the early church. Jesus often laid hands on the sick and healed them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 6:5</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 4:40</span>), revealing both compassion and His authority over sickness and disease. The apostles continued this practice, using it to impart the gift of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:17</span>), spiritual gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 4:14</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 1:6</span>), and to bless believers. It was also used in appointing leaders and missionaries for ministry (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 13:3</span>).

<!-- p119 -->
The laying on of hands is a symbolic act of faith and obedience to God's word. It is not a mechanical ritual, but a spiritual practice rooted in dependence on the Holy Spirit. For example, in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:6</span>, the apostles laid hands on those chosen to serve as deacons, dedicating them to God's service and publicly acknowledging their calling. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 19:6</span>, Paul laid hands on the believers, and they received the Holy Spirit, showing its role in spiritual empowerment.

<!-- p120 -->
However, the Bible also gives warnings about this practice:

<!-- p121 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p122 -->
> **<u>1 Timothy 5:22</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Do not lay hands on anyone hastily, nor share in other people's sins; keep yourself pure.
>

<!-- p123 -->
</span>

<!-- p124 -->
This highlights how important discernment and spiritual maturity are when appointing individuals to leadership roles. As we studied in the chapter on the church, leaders must be tested and proven in order to avoid promoting unqualified or immature believers who could harm the body of Christ.

<!-- p125 -->
Some additional guidelines for the practice of laying on of hands:

<!-- p126 -->
1.  Context-appropriate - The person on whom hands are being laid should be a willing participant.

<!-- p127 -->
2.  Physically appropriate - Laying on of hands does not involve sexual or sensual touch. In practice, placing a hand on the shoulder, arm, or forehead (with permission) are generally acceptable locations in the Western world.

<!-- p128 -->
3.  Culturally appropriate - In some cultures it is not considered appropriate for men and women to touch one another unless they are related by blood or by marriage.

<!-- p129 -->
Even today, the laying on of hands remains a powerful expression of faith and obedience in the church. It is a means of blessing, healing, commissioning, and empowerment, always pointing to the authority of Christ and the work of the Holy Spirit. Whether praying for the sick, sending missionaries, or appointing leaders, it is a reminder that believers act not by their own power, but under the authority of Jesus Christ and in alignment with his plans.

<!-- p130 -->
Now that we have studied the authority of Jesus and the authority of believers, we will discuss other beings in the spiritual realm, angels and demons.

## Angels and Demons

<!-- p132 -->
### Angels

<!-- p133 -->
Angels are an important part of God's creation, who, as his messengers and representatives, carry out his divine plans. The Bible reveals a spiritual realm where these beings dwell, who are powerful, wise, and obedient to God's commands. Although angels often remain unseen, their presence and actions nonetheless influence human history and spiritual warfare. Understanding their roles, classifications, and activities gives us a deeper understanding of God's plan and the unseen realities that exist around us.

<!-- p134 -->
The very meaning of the word "angel" is "messenger," which reflects their primary role. Angels act in obedience to God's will, whether delivering messages, providing protection, executing judgment, or engaging in spiritual warfare. They are not objects of worship, but examples of obedience and reverence, always pointing to the glory of God. This chapter explores the nature, classifications, and roles of angels, highlighting the Scriptures and their significance in our lives today.

<!-- p135 -->
In the Scriptures, angels are seen as **messengers**, **servants**, and **warriors**. Angels often act like messengers, delivering important announcements, warnings, and encouragement. They reveal God's plans and provide guidance in times of need (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26-38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 1:26-38</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 2:13</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 1:1</span>). Angels are actively involved in protecting, empowering, and delivering believers. They minister to God's people in times of distress and provide supernatural help (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="91" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 91:11</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 12:7-11</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 4:11</span>). Angels engage in spiritual warfare, combating demonic powers to advance God's kingdom and protect believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="DAN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Daniel 10:13</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:7-9</span>).

<!-- p136 -->
#### The Nature of Angels

<!-- p137 -->
Angels are created beings whom God brought into existence before the foundation of the world. They are distinct from humans and possess unique characteristics that equip them for their heavenly roles. The Bible teaches that angels are spiritual beings not bound by physical limitations; yet, when interacting with humans, they can assume visible form.

<!-- p138 -->
Angels are described as immortal, that is, they neither die nor age. Their number is countless, and their vast hosts fill the heavenly realms. Although masculine pronouns are often used for them, the Scriptures make it clear that angels are sexless and neither marry nor reproduce. These qualities emphasize that they are servants of God, wholly devoted to his will and commands. Angels possess extraordinary qualities that reflect their divine purpose. They are obedient, reverent, wise, and powerful. These traits enable them to fulfill God's will effectively and faithfully.

<!-- p140 -->
Although the Bible illuminates many functions and titles of angels, it does not provide a detailed classification system. Even so, some angels are identified by particular roles or functions:

<!-- p141 -->
- **Angel of the Lord** - Often regarded as a pre-incarnate manifestation of Christ, who appears with divine authority. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="23" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 23:20-23</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JDG" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Judges 6:11-24</span>*)

<!-- p142 -->
- **Archangels** - High-ranking angels such as Michael, who leads the armies, and Gabriel, who delivers divine messages. (*Jude 9; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:7</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 1:19-26</span>*)

<!-- p143 -->
- **Cherubim** - Guardians of God's glory and presence, as in Eden and on the Ark of the Covenant. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 3:24</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 25:22</span>*)

<!-- p144 -->
- **Seraphim** - Worshipers of God, who continually proclaim His holiness. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 6:1-6</span>*)

<!-- p145 -->
- **Elect angels** - Those faithful angels who opposed Satan's rebellion and remained loyal to God. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:21</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 25:41</span>*)

<!-- p146 -->
These classifications emphasize diversity and order within the realm of angels; nevertheless, their main focus is always to fulfill God's purposes.

<!-- p147 -->
#### Human interaction with angels

<!-- p148 -->
In Scripture humans often encounter angels, yet we are never taught to command them or pray to them. Instead, angels act according to God's command. They serve believers as **God's servants** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 1:14</span>). Their interactions include protection, guidance, and deliverance, which always point us back to God's power and sovereignty.

<!-- p149 -->
- **Warnings against angel-worship**: Scripture forbids worshiping angels or seeking encounters with them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:18</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 22:8-9</span>).

<!-- p150 -->
- **Prayer to God, not to angels**: Believers should pray to God, who sends angels according to His will (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="91" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 91:11</span>).

<!-- p151 -->
- **Encouragement and trust**: Angels remind us of God's care, but our faith must rest entirely on God, not on angels.

<!-- p152 -->
Angels are awe-inspiring beings created to serve God and to minister to His people. Though their work often remains unseen, Scripture gives glimpses of their activities, revealing their faithfulness, power, and reverence. Angels remind us of God's provision and protection, yet they always point to His glory. As believers, we are called to trust in God's care, knowing that angels act as His servants to accomplish His divine purposes.

<!-- p153 -->
### Demons

<!-- p154 -->
Just as the study of angels is necessary to understand the spiritual realm, so is the study of demons. Scripture clearly describes their activity, influence, and their defeat through Jesus Christ. Recognizing their role in spiritual warfare helps believers remain firm in faith without fear.

<!-- p155 -->
#### Origin and nature of demons

<!-- p156 -->
Demons are the fallen angels who sided with Satan's rebellion. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:7-9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 25:41</span>).* Satan, whose former name was Lucifer (“light-bearer”), a high-ranking angel, sought to exalt himself above God and was cast out of heaven with the other rebellious angels. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 14:12-15</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EZK" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ezekiel 28:12-17</span>).*

<!-- p157 -->
Demons are spirits without physical bodies, yet they can oppress and torment humans and animals. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 5:11-13</span>).* They seek to deceive, tempt, oppress, and destroy. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 11:14</span>). Unlike God,* they have limitations, and they are not omnipresent (everywhere), omniscient (all-knowing), omnipotent (able to do everything)*.* Their final defeat is certain, and they will face eternal punishment. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 20:10</span>)*

<!-- p158 -->
Scripture describes the works and activities of Satan as follows:

<!-- p159 -->
- Opposes the work of God *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Thessalonians 2:18</span>)*

<!-- p160 -->
- deceives the world *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 4:4</span>)*

<!-- p161 -->
- tempts believers and accuses them *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 22:31</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:10</span>)*

<!-- p162 -->
- afflicts and harms *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JOB" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Job 1:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 13:16</span>)*

<!-- p163 -->
Satan has many names and descriptions, each describing different aspects of his work.

[See Table 9](../tables/table9.md)

<!-- p165 -->
The Gospels and the Epistles further clarify the work of the enemy. Demons, because they are disembodied, often do their work through willing and open vessels. Just as God works through people, so does Satan. This oppression is often marked by torment, self-harm, and destructive behavior *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 8:27-29</span>).* Their attacks produce fear, temptation, and discouragement *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 12:7</span>).* The most common source of their power (not authority) in a person's life is through the influence of lies and emotional strongholds *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 10:3-5</span>).* 

<!-- p166 -->
It is noteworthy that not every lie a person believes, every weakness of their body, or every stronghold in their life is a direct result of demonic influence; however, these wounded places are places where the enemy can exploit us and keep us in a state of distress and oppression.

<!-- p167 -->
#### Jesus' authority over the works of the enemy

<!-- p168 -->
Do not be afraid, Jesus has full and complete authority over the works of the enemy. During his ministry Jesus showed power over demons *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 1:34</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 5:1-20</span>).* The works of the enemy and Satan himself were defeated by Jesus' death and resurrection *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:15</span>).* 

<!-- p169 -->
As believers, we have been given the authority granted by Jesus to cast out demons in his name:

<!-- p170 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p171 -->
> **<u>Mark 16:17</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And these signs will follow those who believe: In My name they will cast out demons; they will speak with new tongues;
>

<!-- p172 -->
</span>

<!-- p173 -->
Additionally, as heirs of salvation we have the authority to submit ourselves to God, resist the devil, and he will flee from us! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 4:7</span>).

<!-- p174 -->
The way shown in Scripture for dealing with an evil or unclean spirit is that we exercise authority over it in Jesus' name and command it to leave. If we ask, the Holy Spirit promises to give us wisdom and discernment! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:5</span>). We do not need to make it a spectacle, a movie-like drama, or a conversation with them; that is Satan's ploy to distract us. Satan's power is established and sustained only by the lies he can get us to believe.

<!-- p175 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="18" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p176 -->
> **<u>Matthew 18:18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> "Truly I say to you, whatever you bind on earth will be bound in heaven, and whatever you loose on earth will be loosed in heaven.
>

<!-- p177 -->
</span>

<!-- p178 -->
We speak to them in Jesus' name and command them to leave.

<!-- p179 -->
Regarding dealing with demons, Scripture gives two major cautions:

<!-- p180 -->
1.  **Only those who have a relationship with Jesus have authority over demons**. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 19:11-20</span> tells about the sons of a Jewish chief priest named Sceva. They tried to intervene in the life of a man who was oppressed and tormented by Satan, and the evil spirit refused to come out because they had no relationship with Jesus. The man who was afflicted leapt on them, stripped them naked, and beat them. Just as you should not impersonate a police officer, **do not impersonate someone who has a relationship with Jesus; rather, have a relationship with Jesus yourself**!

<!-- p181 -->
2.  **When an evil spirit comes out, it goes away for a time and will try to return**. If it finds the place where it used to live empty, it will return in an even worse state. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="43-45" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 12:43-45</span>. When we minister to someone afflicted by Satan, that person must fill the empty space in their life that the evil spirit had occupied. At the very least, this means establishing a personal relationship with Jesus and strengthening it through obedience to the Holy Spirit. Often an evil or unclean spirit has power to operate in a person’s life through choices made on the basis of their beliefs. Anyone delivered from Satan must replace that old belief system and be transformed by the renewing of their mind (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:1-2</span>).

<!-- p182 -->
We serve a wonderful and Almighty God, and we are ‘more than conquerors’ over a defeated enemy (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="37" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:37</span>). We have no need to fear our defeated enemy, because Jesus has given us His authority to crush his head! Satan’s time is short, and in the end he will be judged along with every principality and power on earth that rejects the lordship of Christ.
