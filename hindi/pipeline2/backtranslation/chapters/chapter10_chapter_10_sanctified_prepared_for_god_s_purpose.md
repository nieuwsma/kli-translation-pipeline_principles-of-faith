# Chapter 10 - Sanctified—Prepared for God's Purposes

<!-- p1 -->
In the last two chapters we discussed several important topics. We explored the role and ministry of the Holy Spirit, and the role and ministry of the Church.

<!-- p2 -->
We also have a part in the Church, and although we need training, we also need purification. In order to truly be ready for God’s purposes, we must be sanctified.

<!-- p3 -->
The definition of **sanctification** is to purify something for a particular purpose. This word is closely related to **consecration**, which means to set something apart for a particular purpose.

<!-- p4 -->
Imagine a master sculptor preparing to work on a large solid block of marble. The master envisions what this raw and uncarved marble can be transformed into by his careful craftsmanship. He has a very specific design in mind; he instructs his assistants to set this block of marble apart for a specific design (**consecration**). As he refines the marble and works on it, adding texture, depth, and shape, he purifies it for that particular design (**sanctifies**). The act of dedicating the marble to his special project was swift, whereas the process of shaping the marble into its final design takes considerable patience.

<!-- p5 -->
At salvation we were set apart, dedicated to God. By the work of purifying our souls with the help of the Spirit, we are transformed. It is this process of purification that sanctifies us.

<!-- p6 -->
When we received Jesus through salvation, we were born anew. We were justified, our sins were forgiven. Our spirit was regenerated; we became alive in Christ. We were adopted into God’s family and became the very temple of the Holy Spirit. Such a wonderful transformation that Scripture says, “*There is joy in the presence of the angels of God over one sinner who repents.” (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 15:10</span>).

<!-- p7 -->
Receiving Jesus through salvation (**justification**) saves our spirit, purifies us, and makes us fit to stand before God, so that we can receive His Holy Spirit. Nevertheless, despite such a great deliverance, our souls (our mind, will, and emotions) still require work!

<!-- p8 -->
Was the work of salvation incomplete? No! The work of salvation is effective, but it must continue in the believer’s life through the renewal brought by the Holy Spirit. This process of renewal and transformation is the work of **sanctification**.

<!-- p9 -->
We know that work remains to be done even after conversion because our painful memories did not go away, many of our beliefs remained the same, and many of our habits or customs did not disappear. In addition, conversion did not separate our minds from their fleshly (carnal) nature.

<!-- p10 -->
The word ‘carnal’ comes from a Latin root meaning ‘flesh’ or ‘body.’ The carnal nature is in each of us from birth; like an animal’s instincts, it is meant to help us survive. The way of the carnal nature is to be self-centered, self-directed, self-absorbed. Children are natural examples of the carnal nature. Despite their innocence, their disposition is primarily focused on their desires, wants, and needs; and if you do not meet its every expectation—‘Beware!’

<!-- p11 -->
Part of growing up is maturing beyond childish things, but when pressure comes we very easily revert to self-focused patterns of thinking and living. As James says:

<!-- p12 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p13 -->
> **<u>James 4:1</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Where do wars and fights come from among you? Do they not come from your desires for pleasure that war in your members?
>

<!-- p14 -->
</span>

<!-- p15 -->
Paul explains this carnal nature in more detail in his letter to the Romans (chapters 6–8). He says that this carnal nature keeps him doing what he hates and urges him to do evil. He describes this inner struggle as a war between his human-born nature and his God-born spirit.

<!-- p16 -->
This struggle between flesh and spirit exists in all of us, and it is our responsibility to do something about it. If we do not confront our carnal nature, then even though our spirits have been redeemed, our souls will remain entangled in patterns of spiritual decay.

<!-- p17 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p18 -->
> **<u>Romans 8:6-8</u>** (NKJV)
>
> For to be carnally minded is death, but to be spiritually minded is life and peace.
>
> Because the carnal mind is enmity against God; for it is not subject to the law of God, nor indeed can be.
>
> So then, those who are in the flesh cannot please God.
>

<!-- p19 -->
</span>

<!-- p20 -->
These words of Paul are both a warning to us and a promise. Although we have been born again, there is still a carnal mind within us that must be brought under the knowledge of Christ. In doing so, we walk in God's life and peace.

<!-- p21 -->
Take courage, there is hope for us! We must walk according to the Spirit and be led by the Spirit of God. By doing so, we will not walk according to the flesh.

<!-- p22 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="1,13,14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p23 -->
> **<u>Romans 8:1,13,14</u>** (NKJV)
>
> There is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus, who do not walk according to the flesh, but according to the Spirit.
>
> For if you live according to the flesh you will die; but if by the Spirit you put to death the deeds of the body, you will live.
>
> For as many as are led by the Spirit of God, these are the sons of God.
>

<!-- p24 -->
</span>

<!-- p25 -->
How do we learn to walk in the Spirit? Is it *just a conscious choice*? We learn to walk in the Spirit the way we learn to walk naturally. We are motivated to move and grow, so we wrestle, struggle, crawl, and wobble until we learn to stand on our feet. Our natural parents cannot walk for us, but they can support, encourage, assist, and enable us in our walking.

<!-- p26 -->
Similarly, God cannot live our faith on our behalf. He will teach us, but the effort is ours to make. As Paul said to the Philippians:

<!-- p27 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="12-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p28 -->
> **<u>Philippians 2:12-13</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Therefore, my beloved, as you have always obeyed, not as in my presence only, but now much more in my absence, work out your own salvation with fear and trembling;
>
> For it is God who works in you both to will and to do according to His good pleasure.
>

<!-- p29 -->
</span>

<!-- p30 -->
We must work out our salvation. This does not mean that we should climb onto a wooden cross and be crucified physically, but we must crucify our fleshly nature (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:24</span>). God promises that He will work in us and help us, but the choice to walk is ours to make, and we must remain steadfast in it.

<!-- p31 -->
It is true to say that ‘we’ are dealing with our fleshly nature, but it is also true that ‘God’ is dealing with it. The work of the cross (justification) was hard for Jesus—He had to sacrifice His life for us—but it is easy for us; it is a free gift. The work of the Spirit (sanctification) is easy for God—He walks in perfect truth and is willing and eager to dwell with us—but it is hard for us, because we must crucify our old nature, and our nature is not very interested in being crucified.

<!-- p32 -->
This process is based on participation. God helps, supports, guides, directs, and corrects. We listen, obey, follow, and respond. The result is growth and transformation. This is the pruning about which Jesus said that God would do within us. Jesus is the true vine, His Father is the vinedresser, and we are the branches:

<!-- p33 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p34 -->
> **<u>John 15:1-4</u>** (NKJV)
>
> "I am the true vine, and My Father is the vinedresser.
>
> Every branch in Me that does not bear fruit He takes away; and every branch that bears fruit He prunes, that it may bear more fruit.
>
> You are already clean because of the word which I have spoken to you.
>
> Abide in Me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself unless it abides in the vine, neither can you, unless you abide in Me.
>

<!-- p35 -->
</span>

<!-- p36 -->
Pruning is extremely beneficial for a plant. Recalling our chapter on repentance, we are called to turn from dead works (the works that are no longer bearing fruit) and to turn to God. Turning away from the dead places in our lives and turning toward God—this results in the Master Gardener of our lives pruning the branches, improving growth, and producing more spiritual fruit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:2</span>).

<!-- p37 -->
Although pruning may feel painful, it reflects God's loving care as He shapes us into vessels for His purpose (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 12:11</span>). Some may experience loss, even fear, during the process of pruning. Even so, God is gentle, and He will not break a bruised reed (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="42" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 42:3</span>). The pruning of grapevines is done in their dormant season so that stress on the plant is minimized. The gardener who oversees the growth cuts away the old, woody growth that no longer bears fruit as before; this causes the vine to direct its energy to the branches that have the greatest likelihood of good fruit.

<!-- p38 -->
Just as a gardener prunes branches to direct growth, so God may allow seasons of correction and discipline to refine our faith and character, yet His work is filled with patience and kindness. God prunes those who abide in Him. Our focus should be more on abiding in Christ, and less on the work of pruning.

<!-- p39 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 12:11</span> reminds us that pruning “*produces the peaceable fruit of righteousness*” for those who are trained by it.

<!-- p40 -->
How are we trained in righteousness? By being transformed through the renewing of our mind.

<!-- p41 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p42 -->
> **<u>Romans 12:1-2</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Therefore, brothers, I beseech you by the mercies of God that you present your bodies as a living sacrifice, holy and acceptable to God; this is your reasonable service.
>
> And do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind, so that you may prove what is the good, acceptable, and perfect will of God.
>

<!-- p43 -->
</span>

<!-- p44 -->
Paul presents two simple requirements for transformation:

<!-- p45 -->
<u>1. Present your bodies as a living sacrifice to God</u>. A **living** sacrifice does not physically die at the time of sacrifice, but because a living sacrifice cannot die, you must bring it to the altar again and again!

<!-- p46 -->
<u>2. Renew your mind!</u> Renewal, based on the Greek word: **Anakainōsis** (*An-ak-ah'-ee-no-sis -* **G342**), means renewal or renovation, focusing on the continual process of inner change and growth.

<!-- p47 -->
This renewal brings transformation. Transformation is based on the Greek word: **Metamorphoō** (*met-am-or-fo'-o -* **G3339**), which means to change into another form; to transform or transfigure, emphasizing complete change. From Metamorphoō comes the English word metamorphosis, the process by which caterpillars become butterflies.

<!-- p48 -->
God wants to fundamentally transform our minds and our way of thinking. It is His desire that we not conform to the pattern of the world, but that we know what God's good (beneficial), pleasing (acceptable), and perfect (complete and lacking nothing) will is.

<!-- p49 -->
The requirements for transformation really are just this simple, **although they require discipline**!

<!-- p50 -->
Our fight against the flesh will not be won by our flesh, but will be won with the equipment God has given for the battle of the mind. His Word (the sword of truth) is powerful to correct every imagination, arrogant thought, and to take captive (imprison) every thought that opposes the command of God.

<!-- p51 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="3-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p52 -->
> **<u>2 Corinthians 10:3-6</u>** (NKJV)
>
> For though we walk in the flesh, we do not war according to the flesh.
>
> For the weapons of our warfare are not carnal but mighty in God for pulling down strongholds,
>
> casting down arguments and every high thing that exalts itself against the knowledge of God, and bringing every thought into captivity to the obedience of Christ,
>
> and being ready to punish all disobedience when your obedience is fulfilled.
>

<!-- p53 -->
</span>

<!-- p54 -->
When authorities arrest someone, they shackle him and throw him into a cell. If that person escapes custody, they do not sit idly by; they deploy remarkable resources and do not rest until they bring that person back under control. We should show the same diligence in the struggle of our minds!

<!-- p55 -->
We are renewed by obedience to the truth and by experience. Jesus said that if we abide in His words, we will be His disciples, we will experience (<u>know</u>) the truth, and it will set us free (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-32" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 8:31-32</span>)!

<!-- p56 -->
Experiencing the truth is different from knowledge of the truth. The Greek word used for experience is **Ginōskō** (Ghin-oce'-ko **- G1097**); it means to know through personal experience or intimate understanding; it implies progressive learning.

<!-- p57 -->
It is not our academic knowledge that transforms us and renews our minds, but our personal experience of the truth. We are sanctified by His truth! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="17" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 17:17</span>).

## How to renew your mind

<!-- p59 -->
The renewing of our mind is not a one-size-fits-all process—it depends on which part of the mind we're talking about! God has fashioned our minds with amazing complexity, including many different memory systems, and each has its own specific purpose. Some run automatically in the background, while others require focused, deliberate thought. These systems are God's gifts that help us survive, grow, and thrive, but when they are misused or not renewed, they can also lead us astray. Understanding how these systems work gives us insight into how to partner with God's process of transformation.

<!-- p60 -->
In the next section I will describe each memory system, their purpose and function, some practical steps to renew this aspect of your mind, and resources for continued practice and study.

<!-- p61 -->
### **Memory systems and how to renew them**

<!-- p62 -->
#### **1. Explicit Memory (Declarative Memory)**

<!-- p63 -->
**Purpose:** Stores facts, events, and experiences that we can consciously recall. It is divided into two types-**semantic** and **episodic** memory.

<!-- p64 -->
**Semantic memory - facts and knowledge.** Semantic memory is the place where we store facts, definitions, and general knowledge- things like historical dates, Bible verses, and practical information. It helps us predict outcomes, make decisions, and avoid danger (e.g., identifying poisonous plants). While it gives us stability and order, it can also lead to rigidity- over-reliance on rules, rather than adapting to unexpected circumstances.

<!-- p65 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p66 -->
- **Study the Scriptures** to shape our worldview (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="119" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="105" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 119:105</span>).

<!-- p67 -->
- **Memorize truths and principles** to stand firm in faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 3:16</span>).

<!-- p68 -->
- **Read Christian books** such as *"Renewing the Mind" by Casey Treat* or *"Battlefield of the Mind" by Joyce Meyer* to develop godly patterns of thinking.

<!-- p69 -->
**Episodic memory - personal experiences.** Episodic memory stores our personal experiences, linking them to specific times and places- such as a wedding day or a traumatic event. These memories provide context, helping us learn from the past and form connections. However, episodic memory is susceptible to emotional bias and distortion. Past traumas can create fear-based associations, making it hard to move forward.

<!-- p70 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p71 -->
- **Reflect and confess** to bring hidden pain into God’s light (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 1:9</span>).

<!-- p72 -->
- **Reframe past experiences** through the lens of God’s grace (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:28</span>).

<!-- p73 -->
- **Seek inner healing through prayer and counseling** (*“Your Pain is Showing” by Larry F. Low, "The Bondage Breaker" by Neil T. Anderson*, *"Emotionally Healthy Spirituality" by Peter Scazzero*).

<!-- p74 -->
#### **2. Implicit Memory (Nondeclarative Memory)**

<!-- p75 -->
**Purpose:** Unconscious memory used for habits, skills, and emotional associations. It operates automatically, helping us respond quickly to situations. It is divided into **procedural memory** and **emotional conditioning**.

<!-- p76 -->
**Procedural memory - skills and habits.** Procedural memory allows us to perform tasks without thinking - like driving, typing, or playing an instrument. It is efficient, saving our mental energy and time. However, bad habits and negative patterns can also become deeply ingrained and be difficult to break.

<!-- p77 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p78 -->
- **Practice spiritual disciplines like prayer and fasting** to form godly habits (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 5:14</span>).

<!-- p79 -->
- **Train in godliness through repetition and obedience** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 4:7</span>).

<!-- p80 -->
- **Read books such as** *"Atomic Habits" by James Clear* (practical) and *"Spiritual Disciplines for the Christian Life" by Donald Whitney* (Bible-based) to establish habits that honor God.

<!-- p81 -->
**Emotional Conditioning - Emotional Associations.** Emotional conditioning links sensory cues (such as smells or sounds) with emotions, triggering responses even before we consciously process them. This system protects us, but when past experiences are negative, it can also produce irrational fears or emotional reactions.

<!-- p82 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p83 -->
- **Pray for healing** from past wounds (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="41" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 41:10</span>).

<!-- p84 -->
- **Practice forgiveness** and let go of bitterness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-32" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:31-32</span>).

<!-- p85 -->
- **Replace fear with God’s promises** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 1:7</span>).

<!-- p86 -->
- **Seek guidance from books such as** *“Your Pain is Showing” by Larry F. Low, "The Bondage Breaker" by Neil T. Anderson*, “*Soul Care" by Rob Reimer* to overcome emotional triggers.

<!-- p87 -->
#### **3. Working Memory (Short-Term Memory)**

<!-- p88 -->
**Purpose:** Provides temporary storage for problem-solving and decision-making. It helps us focus, retain information for a short time, and process tasks—such as remembering instructions or making quick decisions. Though powerful, it is limited, prone to distraction, and, especially under stress, is easily overloaded.

<!-- p89 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p90 -->
- **Focus and meditate** on God’s Word daily (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 1:2</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
- **Renew your thoughts** through intentional thinking (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:2</span>).

<!-- p92 -->
- **Practice mindfulness** and simplify mental clutter (*"Habits of Grace" by David Mathis*).

<!-- p93 -->
- **Read** *"The Celebration of Discipline" by Richard Foster* to sharpen focus through spiritual disciplines.

<!-- p94 -->
## Conclusion

<!-- p95 -->
The renewal of the mind is not a quick fix—it is a lifelong, gradual process of transformation. Each memory system plays a role in shaping how we think, feel, and act. By working intentionally with the Holy Spirit, applying Scripture, and developing devotional habits, we can train our minds to align with God’s truth. Whether it is facing past wounds, learning new habits, or staying focused in the present moment—God has given both spiritual tools and practical wisdom to help us walk in renewal and freedom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 10:5</span>).

<!-- p96 -->
Our world is filled with crises, tribulations, and trials. Through the disciplined renewal of the mind, our souls are transformed, and we can live a life of freedom, filled with the fruit of His Spirit, for God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-25</span>).
