# Chapter 8 - The Holy Spirit- Comforter, Guide, and Power

<!-- p1 -->
The Holy Spirit is not a new spirit, but belongs to God and has been with God from the beginning. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 1:2</span>, at creation the Spirit of God was hovering over the waters. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="NEH" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Nehemiah 9:20</span>, the Scriptures say that it was the Spirit who instructed the people in the wilderness. It was by this same Holy Spirit that God anointed kings, judges, and prophets in the Old Testament. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JOL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Joel 2:28-29</span> the promise is made that the Spirit will be poured out on all people. By this same Holy Spirit God raised Jesus from the dead, and now He dwells in us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:11</span>). The Holy Spirit is one, and He has always been the same. How He works with people has appeared different at different times, but His heart has never changed.

<!-- p2 -->
Jesus gave His disciples and all of us the powerful promise of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p3 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p4 -->
> **<u>John 14:16-18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And I will pray the Father, and He will give you another Helper, that He may abide with you forever—
>
> the Spirit of truth, whom the world cannot receive, because it neither sees Him

<!-- p20 -->
The disciples had to wait for the promise of the Spirit to be poured out, and this was to empower them to be witnesses to the ends of the earth. The Holy Spirit is an integral part of God’s plan for humanity, and for us, as believers, is essential for fulfilling our mission and indispensable for the world.

<!-- p21 -->
## Introduction to the Holy Spirit

<!-- p22 -->
The Holy Spirit, first and foremost, is from God and belongs to God. Scripture calls the Spirit the Spirit of God, the Spirit of the Lord, or the Spirit of Christ; although these highlight different aspects, they are all the same. The Holy Spirit, being fully God, is both eternal and holy (blameless, spotless, pure, not profane). Some translations also say “Holy Ghost” for the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p23 -->
In Greek, the word for “spirit” is **Pneuma** (**NYOO-mah** - **G4151**), which means breath or wind. The Hebrew word for “spirit” has the same meaning. Breath is important because we need its continual supply to live. God breathed His breath into Adam to give him life; likewise, the last breath Jesus took before dying on the cross was also breath. Without breath there is no life. Breath, like wind, can be felt and experienced, but it is not as easily seen as other “elements” like water, fire, or earth.

<!-- p24 -->
There are many other key descriptors used to describe the Holy Spirit. Each of these descriptors is used with “the Spirit of,” which means these qualities are essential for defining the Spirit. For example, “the Spirit of truth” or “the Spirit of life.”

<!-- p25 -->
The Spirit of:

<!-- p26 -->
- **Truth** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:17</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span> refers to the Spirit’s role in guiding believers into all truth, revealing God’s will and the reality of Christ.

<!-- p27 -->
- **Life** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:2</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:8</span> highlights the Spirit’s role in imparting spiritual and eternal life to believers, and in freeing them from the law of sin and death.

<!-- p28 -->
- **Grace** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:29</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ZEC" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Zechariah 12:10</span> depicts the Spirit’s work in granting God’s grace, forgiveness, and unmerited mercy to humanity.

<!-- p29 -->
- **Glory** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 4:14</span> indicates the Spirit’s role in revealing and manifesting God’s glory, especially in times of suffering and persecution.

<!-- p30 -->
- **Adoption** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:15</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 4:5</span> refers to the Spirit’s work of making believers God’s children, granting them the status of sons and daughters.

<!-- p31 -->
- **Holiness** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 1:4</span> points to the Spirit’s sanctifying work, which sets believers apart for God’s purposes.

<!-- p32 -->
- **Power** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 1:7</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span> describes the Spirit’s work in empowering believers for boldness, ministry, and the ability to live out their faith effectively.

<!-- p33 -->
In all these qualities, the Holy Spirit is rightly considered a Helper or Comforter. Jesus refers to the Holy Spirit as the coming Helper, using the Greek word ***Paraklētos** (*pah-RAH-klay-tos - **G3875**), which means advocate, helper, one called alongside.

<!-- p34 -->
An advocate speaks on behalf of another, like a defense attorney. They intercede and assist the person for whom they are appointed.

<!-- p35 -->
A helper helps. The helper empowers the work, but the helper’s role is primarily to assist in that work. The helper is not solely responsible for performing the task, but comes alongside to help accomplish it.

<!-- p36 -->
There are many specific purposes and roles in the Holy Spirit’s ministry. His work is inward (in the hearts and lives of believers), upward (in service to God and in His exaltation), and outward (toward our mission field, that is, the world).

<!-- p37 -->
## The roles of the Holy Spirit—Inward, Upward, Outward

<!-- p38 -->
### Inward—the Holy Spirit unites us with Jesus

<!-- p39 -->
Before Jesus performed any miracles or began His public ministry, He was baptized by John the Baptist and the Spirit of God descended upon Him, and a voice from heaven said, ‘This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:17</span>).

<!-- p40 -->
When we receive Jesus by faith, we receive salvation and the seal of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p41 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p42 -->
> **<u>Ephesians 1:13-14</u>** (NKJV)
>
> In Him you also, after hearing the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation, trusted in Him; and in Him, when you believed, you were sealed with the Holy Spirit of promise,
>
> who is the guarantee of our inheritance until the redemption of the purchased possession, to the praise of His glory.
>

<!-- p43 -->
</span>

<!-- p44 -->
This seal is not metaphorical, but real. It is the sign of our redemption and the down payment of the full inheritance we are to receive as God's children. Romans 8 tells us that through the new birth we receive in our mortal bodies the very Spirit of God who raised Christ from the dead, and we become the real temple of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 6:16</span>).

<!-- p45 -->
Receiving the Spirit of God transforms us, and causes rivers of living water to flow from within us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 7:38</span>). This renewal is immediate and complete, yet it continues throughout our entire lives. The river of the Holy Spirit should never stop flowing in our lives!

<!-- p46 -->
Through salvation and the indwelling of the Holy Spirit we can abide in Christ and He in us. The Spirit of God is no longer left outside, but can dwell with us deeply. From now into eternal life we can walk in fellowship and harmony with God.

<!-- p47 -->
There is much to be said about the inner work of the Holy Spirit and His role in sanctification, but this will be discussed in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p48 -->
### Upward - The Holy Spirit testifies about Jesus

<!-- p49 -->
The Holy Spirit is from God and is God's. Just as Jesus said that He does nothing except what the Father shows Him (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 5:19</span>), so the Holy Spirit also does nothing without the Father's direction. Because the Holy Spirit is the Helper, He does not act on His own, but works in perfect harmony with the Father.

<!-- p50 -->
The Spirit testifies of Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:26</span>), glorifies Jesus, and tells and declares what Jesus has for us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:14</span>).

<!-- p51 -->
The Holy Spirit will bear witness to Jesus, glorify Him, teach, and bring Jesus' teachings to our remembrance (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:26</span>), and will guide us into all truth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span>). Jesus told His disciples that He had much more to teach them, but they could not bear it yet; but the Holy Spirit would lead them into all truth. This is another subtle point in theology. The teachings that Jesus wanted His disciples to know were given to them in many ways. Jesus taught them directly, even before the crucifixion, and also after the resurrection and before the ascension.

<!-- p52 -->
Jesus also promised that the Spirit of truth, who was poured out on them on the day of Pentecost, would lead them into all truth through divine revelation. Does divine revelation still happen today? Certainly yes! The gifts of the Holy Spirit, including prophecy and words of knowledge and wisdom, have not ceased! However, no prophecy or word from any spirit is of private interpretation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2PE" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Peter 1:20</span>). This is extremely important. Some people will mistakenly claim that they are hearing the Holy Spirit, but their deeds and words do not bear witness to Jesus. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 4:3</span> warns against any spirit that does not testify of Jesus, and says that such a spirit is of the antichrist. This bears repeating: if any spirit strays from the teaching of Christ, it is not the Holy Spirit but an unholy spirit!

<!-- p53 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p54 -->
> **<u>1 John 4:1</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Beloved, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits, whether they are from God; for many false prophets have gone out into the world.
>

<!-- p55 -->
</span>

<!-- p56 -->
Throughout Scripture, we see examples of false claims, often made by false prophets or by well-meaning people who spoke against God's will. For example, Jeremiah warned against prophets who spoke visions from their own minds and claimed peace even when destruction was near (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="23" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 23:16-17</span>). Likewise, in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 16:16-18</span>, the words of a slave girl about Paul and Silas were correct, but they were inspired not by the Holy Spirit, but by an evil spirit. These examples remind us that words that appear to be true can still come from ungodly sources.

<!-- p57 -->
Every claim of 'God told me!' or a 'new' teaching must be tested by the biblical tests of truth. To discern properly, we should ask:

<!-- p58 -->
- Is this in accordance with Scripture?

<!-- p59 -->
- Does this exalt the Lord Jesus Christ?

<!-- p60 -->
- Does it produce godly fruit?

<!-- p61 -->
By applying these criteria, we can distinguish the true guidance of the Holy Spirit from errors or deception.

<!-- p62 -->
God continues to provide us with sufficient &amp; abundant wisdom and guidance (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:5</span> &amp; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="33" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 33:3</span>). God’s divine wisdom will not violate Scripture, but will go beyond the scope of Scripture. His *entolē* *(*commandments*)* are instructions to be obeyed immediately; they are *rhema* words (as discussed in the chapter on faith).

<!-- p63 -->
This wisdom, understanding, and guidance will likely not be found word-for-word in Scripture. For example, Scripture gives very general direction about what to look for in a spouse, but does not give specific instructions about the exact person. God promises to direct our path and to lead us into all truth, and we can be assured that this wisdom and the leading of the Holy Spirit will never violate any teaching of Scripture.

It means having courage despite fear. Power means strength, might, and ability. The Holy Spirit gives the power and ability to be witnesses to the gospel despite threats and fear of punishment.

<!-- p78 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

5.  **Renewal**: The church returns to prayer, seeks fresh empowerment, and the cycle continues.

(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:22-23</span>)

The instrument Jesus has chosen for transformation on the earth is His church, empowered, emboldened, and guided by the Holy Spirit.

## Building a Relationship with the Holy Spirit

<!-- p101 -->
Every relationship takes time and experience to develop deeply. The Holy Spirit is our advocate, our comforter, our helper, and our guide. He empowers us and sanctifies us.

<!-- p102 -->
Begin to think of the Holy Spirit as a person. In our relationship, we can either work to build it or do things that weaken it. He can be ignored, quenched, blasphemed, or He can be listened to, honored, and obeyed.

<!-- p103 -->
Do not ignore the voice of the Holy Spirit. He always speaks to us and to the Church. If you do not yet know how to distinguish the voice of the Holy Spirit, it takes time, willingness, and effort (as in every healthy and beneficial relationship). But do not worry; within you are already all the necessary components needed to begin developing a personal relationship with the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p104 -->
The fastest and most reliable ways to build an effective relationship with the Holy Spirit are

<!-- p105 -->
1.  In prayer, call on Him **personally**, pause, and wait for Him to speak.

<!-- p106 -->
2.  As part of a **group** of those who know Him (the Church), call on Him, pause, and wait for Him to speak.

<!-- p107 -->
I use the phrase ‘call out’ literally, to mean speaking with your mouth, or however you speak (with your hands if you cannot speak aloud with your mouth). Why speak? A few reasons:

<!-- p108 -->
1.  We are speaking spirits made in the image of God. God speaks, and we speak too.

<!-- p109 -->
2.  Speaking is the primary way we communicate. You can also write what you want to say (journaling) and speak it out loud.

<!-- p110 -->
3.  Speaking is an intentional act, and it signals to the one you are addressing that you want to engage in conversation.

<!-- p111 -->
#### What should you say?

<!-- p112 -->
If you have never spoken to God, try introducing yourself. Yes, it will feel a bit awkward, but that discomfort is okay and temporary. Along with your introduction, tell God that you want to talk with Him and that you want Him to talk with you as well.

<!-- p113 -->
Consider asking Him a question. It does not have to be a deep theological question; it can start simple. Could You show me Your love for me? Why do I feel a certain way? What would You like to tell me?

<!-- p114 -->
#### Then what?

<!-- p115 -->
Well, in every effective relationship you speak for a while, then pause and listen. God will speak.

<!-- p116 -->
God speaks in many ways, but He wants to do it personally and inwardly. It will likely not be an audible voice (like your best friend saying your name out loud) but an inner voice that speaks to your heart and mind. Elijah calls it a ‘still small voice’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1KI" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Kings 19:11-13</span>).

<!-- p117 -->
The most common ways in which I have personally seen God speak to people—and I have had the blessing of helping dozens of people learn to speak directly with God—are some combination of the following:

<!-- p118 -->
- An insight - the person who is speaking with God immediately receives an understanding that helps them.

<!-- p119 -->
- An image - also known in Scripture as a vision or a dream (a vision when you are awake, a dream when you are asleep), God will give an image in your mind. Perhaps it is a memory, or a place where you have been or that you know? Explore the image: what do you see? As a result what do you feel? What is happening?

<!-- p120 -->
- An inner voice - like an audible voice, but one that only you can hear, and which is spoken in your heart and mind.

<!-- p121 -->
#### What would God say?

<!-- p122 -->
Not sure! But that’s the fun of it. I can say that He is not a spirit of fear, but of perfect love, and He will never violate the teaching of Scripture. e.g., God will never tell you to have an extramarital affair.

<!-- p123 -->
Now what?

<!-- p124 -->
Keep the conversation going! It really is that simple.

### Why build a relationship?

<!-- p129 -->
The Holy Spirit is the Helper. The Helper helps us! If we have a shared language and relationship with Him, things go much more smoothly. Can you imagine trying to work on the job with someone with whom you have no relationship, you don’t speak the same language, and you can’t communicate?!

<!-- p130 -->
We serve a God who wants to communicate with us. Jesus said that His sheep recognize His voice (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:27</span>), the natural capacities we need are already inherent within us, but it’s possible that we lack practiced proficiency.

<!-- p131 -->
From personal experience, having a personal and active relationship with the Holy Spirit is one of the best things about being a Christian. His presence gives us peace, comfort, and direction, but only if you know how to seek Him, listen to Him, and obey Him.

<!-- p132 -->
Develop a sensitivity to the Holy Spirit. Just as many married people, over time, are able to communicate with their spouse in a particular way—and sometimes with just a glance. That one glance tells you everything about what just happened and what’s about to happen. Build that same kind of sensitivity and familiarity with the Holy Spirit. Involve Him in your whole life; this is a wonderful part of your relationship with Him that will add great depth and richness to your walk of faith.

<!-- p133 -->
The Holy Spirit has come to lead and guide us, to empower us to become God’s people, and to see the people of the earth fully transformed and delivered from the kingdom of darkness into the kingdom of light. Knowing Him, and building trust with Him, is essential for daily life and for accomplishing the work Jesus has given us to do.

<!-- p134 -->
Our discussion of the work of the Holy Spirit does not conclude in this chapter. His work is discussed in detail in the remaining chapters of this book, especially in the chapters on sanctification and baptism.
