# Chapter 9 - The Church and Our Role in It

<!-- p1 -->
The Church is the means by which God reveals Himself on the earth—through His people who do His work. We are called to be His body; we are His hands and feet to accomplish His work and purpose. Without our efforts the harvest will remain in the field and will not be reaped.

<!-- p2 -->
## Who Is the Church?

<!-- p3 -->
Church, ***Ekklesia** (eh-klee-SEE-ah - **G1577**),* is derived from two Greek words:

<!-- p4 -->
- **"Ek"**: "out of."

<!-- p5 -->
- **"Kaleo"**: "to call."

<!-- p6 -->
Together, **Ekklesia** literally means "the called-out ones" or "a called assembly." In the ancient Greek city-states, it referred to the assembly of citizens summoned for a specific purpose, such as legislative or judicial meetings. It was a secular term denoting a gathering of people.

<!-- p7 -->
In the New Testament, it is used primarily for the community of those who believe in Jesus Christ. It can refer to:

<!-- p8 -->
- **Local church**: A specific assembly of believers in a particular place (e.g., the church of Corinth, Philippi, etc.).

<!-- p9 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p10 -->
> **<u>1 Corinthians 1:2</u>** (NKJV)
>
> To the church of God which is in Corinth: to those who are sanctified in Christ Jesus, called to be saints, together with all who in every place call on the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, both theirs and ours:
>

<!-- p11 -->
</span>

<!-- p12 -->
- **The universal Church**: The collective body of all believers across time and space.

<!-- p13 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p14 -->
> **<u>Matthew 16:18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And I also say to you that you are Peter, and on this rock I will build My church, and the gates of Hades shall not prevail against it.
>

<!-- p15 -->
</span>

<!-- p16 -->
The Church is people, not the building or place where they meet. **Called-out people**: this idea indicates that Christians are called out of the world and set apart for God's purposes (e.g., <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:9</span>) for a specific mission and purpose (the Great Commission). The church can technically be called any meeting with two or more believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="18" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 18:20</span>). Generally, the church is organized into regular gatherings, possibly with a set place and time; but wherever two or more people gather in Jesus' name, there is the church!

<!-- p17 -->
The Church was established by Jesus, and it grew in the first century AD through the work of the disciples who followed Him. The church we have today and experience in all its local congregations across the earth is the direct result of the foundational work of Christ and His apostles. As believers we should be committed to a local church for fellowship, provision, equipping for service, and doing good works!

<!-- p18 -->
To help us understand what the church is, Scripture gives us some powerful images that show what it is intended to be.

<!-- p19 -->
## Understanding the Church

<!-- p20 -->
Several key metaphorical images are used to depict the characteristics of the Church:

<!-- p21 -->
1.  Body

<!-- p22 -->
2.  Bride

<!-- p23 -->
3.  Temple

<!-- p24 -->
4.  Family

<!-- p25 -->
5.  Flock

<!-- p26 -->
6.  Vine and Branches

<!-- p27 -->
Each analogy highlights a distinct aspect of the Church, yet together they reveal the Church’s identity as a unified and diverse community under the leadership of Christ.

<!-- p28 -->
### Body

<!-- p29 -->
The Church is often called the **Body of Christ**, which emphasizes both its unity and diversity. With Christ as the head, the Church functions as a living body, where every believer plays an indispensable and potentially diverse role. Each member is endowed with distinct gifts and contributes to the health and mission of the body according to their respective role. This image underscores believers’ interdependence, particularly that the body must be connected in order to function, and that our work is cooperative, not competitive. When every part works together, the body grows and thrives in unity and love (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:12-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:15-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 1:18</span>).

<!-- p30 -->
### Bride

<!-- p31 -->
The Church is described as the **Bride of Christ**, which depicts an intimate and loving relationship with Jesus. Just as a bride is cherished and prepared for her husband, so the Church is sanctified and purified by Christ’s sacrificial love. This metaphor reflects the devotion and faithfulness expected of the Church, as well as the deep affection that Christ has for His people. This portrayal also points to the ultimate union between Christ and the Church, which will be celebrated at the marriage supper of the Lamb, where the Church is presented in glory without spot or blemish (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="25-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:25-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 19:7-9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="21" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 21:2</span>).

<!-- p32 -->
### Temple

<!-- p33 -->
The Church is called the **Temple of God**, which is the dwelling place of His Spirit. Believers are described as living stones being joined together on the foundation of the apostles and prophets, and Christ Himself is the cornerstone. This sacred structure signifies God’s presence among His people and their role as a community of worship and prayer. The image of the temple highlights the importance of holiness and the Church’s collective purpose in glorifying God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 3:16-17</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:19-22</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:4-5</span>).

<!-- p34 -->
### Family

<!-- p35 -->
The Church is called the **Family of God**, emphasizing relational unity and care among its members. By the Spirit of adoption, believers are made children of God and members of His household. This familial image highlights the love, belonging, and shared inheritance that believers have in Christ. It also reflects the calling that we, as brothers and sisters in God’s family, show compassion and humility toward one another (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:19</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:15-17</span>). Further, through this family image it is shown how the members of the Church are to become mature: that they be taught and nurtured by spiritual mothers and fathers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:1-2</span>), that we grow from infants drinking the pure milk of the Word into mature adults who can eat the solid food of the Word (Hebrews 5).

<!-- p36 -->
### Flock

<!-- p37 -->
The Church is described as a **flock**, that is, sheep under the care of Jesus, the Good Shepherd. This image expresses the Church’s dependence on Christ for guidance, nourishment, and protection. It also emphasizes the responsibility of the Church’s leaders to watch over God’s flock faithfully as under-shepherds, reflecting Christ’s example in love and care. The image of the flock speaks of trust, obedience, and the close relationship between the Great Shepherd and His sheep (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:14-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:2</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>).

<!-- p38 -->
### Vine and Branches

<!-- p39 -->
The Church is described as the **vine and branches**, which portrays the essential relationship between believers and Christ. Jesus is the true vine, and believers are the branches who must abide in Him to bear fruit. This metaphor underscores dependence on Christ for spiritual vitality, growth, and fruitfulness. Without Him believers can do nothing, but by abiding in Him they glorify God and fulfill their purpose. Jesus will prune us so that more fruit may be produced in our lives (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:1-5</span>).

<!-- p40 -->
## Roles of the Church

<!-- p41 -->
The mission of the Church, like the role of the Holy Spirit, can be understood through three dimensions: upward, inward, and outward; each of these reflects a specific aspect of its calling in God’s plan। These dimensions guide the Church to fulfill its role as a worshiping, edifying, and missionary community।

<!-- p42 -->
### Upward: Worship and Glorifying God

<!-- p43 -->
The first purpose of the Church is to worship God and glorify Him। Corporate worship, where believers come together to exalt God, is a visible reflection of His glory and a response to His greatness। As the psalmist declares, “*Worship the Lord in the beauty of holiness; tremble before Him, all the earth*” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="96" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 96:9</span>)। Beyond corporate worship, the Church is called to live lives of obedience and sacrifice, offering themselves as a “living sacrifice,” which is an act of spiritual worship (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:1-2</span>)। This upward focus keeps the Church centered on the glory of God and aligns its priorities with His will।

<!-- p44 -->
### Inward: Building up the Body

<!-- p45 -->
The Great Commission gives us the command to ‘*teach them to obey*,’ a command that goes far beyond formal teaching methods such as this book। Discipleship formation (discussed further in the chapter on discipleship) is to shape the follower in the pattern of discipline, as parents raise their child। Good parents do not hand a five-year-old an encyclopedia and challenge them to make sense of it! Rather, they model, show, correct, guide, nurture, love, care, and shepherd।

<!-- p46 -->
Parents sacrifice for their children because they love them, because they are part of the same body, because they share the same blood। As members of the Body and family of Christ, we share one body, we share the same blood। The Lord’s Supper, through its symbolism, reminds us that by Christ’s sacrifice we are joined to one another। The act of the Lord’s Supper is to be a point of unity, reminding us that we all need redemption, and that salvation is only from God। In this unity we see that, apart from our head, Christ, no part of the body is greater than another।

<!-- p47 -->
### Outward: Reaching the World

<!-- p48 -->
The most well-known part of the Church’s mission extends outward to reach the world with the gospel। Jesus’ Great Commission calls His followers to proclaim the gospel to all nations and make disciples (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19-20</span>)। Empowered by the Holy Spirit, the Church is called to be His witnesses to the ends of the earth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>)।

<!-- p49 -->
Jesus is the light of the world (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 8:12</span>)। Light shines outward from its source in all directions। His light shines through His revelations: the Word, His Spirit, and us, His body। We are the light of the world; in our lives let us radiate His light into our darkened worlds and show such good works as glorify God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 5:14-16</span>)। Practically, this means the Church is called to advocate for justice and mercy—both spiritual and physical। As James says, our pure worship is to care for the vulnerable, the fatherless, and the widows (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:27</span>)।

<!-- p50 -->
## Leadership of the Church

<!-- p51 -->
There are many kinds of denominations, meetings, assemblies, and fellowships in the Christian world। There are very few universal rules for church leadership, but the rules we do have are extremely important:

<!-- p52 -->
1.  Jesus alone is the head of the Church। All serve Him (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 1:18</span>).

<!-- p53 -->
2.  Those who lead have high standards, they are worthy of double honor, because they will receive double judgment (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:17</span>).

<!-- p54 -->
2.1  They must be mature, free from pride, responsible (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:1-13</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 1:5-9</span>).

<!-- p55 -->
2.2.  They must be the servant of all (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42-45" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 10:42-45</span>).

<!-- p56 -->
2.3.  They must devote themselves to the Word and to prayer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:4</span>).

<!-- p57 -->
2.4.  They are responsible for equipping the body for the work of ministry।

<!-- p58 -->
3.  No member in the body is greater or lesser। Some verses that support this are:

<!-- p59 -->
3.1.  There should be no favoritism between the rich and the poor (James 2)।

<!-- p60 -->
3.2.  There is no division between Jew and Gentile, slave or free, male or female (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 3:27-28</span>).

<!-- p61 -->
Beyond the rules of leadership, the structure of the church is important, but much of it is dependent on circumstances and, rather than being universally determined, is a matter of tradition. “<u>We have always done it this way!</u>” is a popular saying; if you go back far enough, it will often be found that there was a good reason for doing so. This is generally neither good nor bad, but a matter of taste, needs, and availability.

<!-- p62 -->
A useful example of structure being formed on the basis of tradition is mealtime with the family. The most important thing about family mealtimes is that everyone gets food and the food is nutritious. The variations are countless: what is eaten, how it is eaten, how the food is seasoned, etc. Different combinations are generally acceptable and are mostly a matter of taste and preference.

<!-- p63 -->
The needs and capacities of a church with 10,000 members differ from those of a church with 50 members. The pattern established in the book of Acts and in the writings of the New Testament was the model of **elders** and **deacons**. Although the leadership structures among churches and denominations may appear different, ultimately this model represents the minimally viable structure of leadership for the church. Let us look at these leadership offices and the process of their selection.

<!-- p64 -->
### Elder (Overseer)

<!-- p65 -->
Role and responsibilities:

<!-- p66 -->
- **Spiritual leadership** - Providing oversight, guidance, and pastoral care for the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:2</span>).

<!-- p67 -->
- **Teaching and preaching** - Responsible for teaching sound doctrine and guarding against false teachings (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:17</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 1:9</span>).

<!-- p68 -->
- **Shepherding the flock** - Caring for the spiritual needs of the congregation, setting an example of humility and service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:2-3</span>).

<!-- p69 -->
- **Protecting the church** - Serving as watchmen, safeguarding the church from false doctrine and division (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29-31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:29-31</span>).

<!-- p70 -->
- **Equipping the saints** - Training and equipping believers for works of service and discipleship (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-12</span>).

<!-- p71 -->
Selection process:

<!-- p72 -->
- **Appointed in every church** - Elders were appointed by the apostles or their delegates with prayer and fasting (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 14:23</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 1:5</span>).

<!-- p73 -->
- **Confirmed by the Holy Spirit** - Their appointment was considered Spirit-led, recognizing God’s calling and gifting (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>).

<!-- p74 -->
- **Tested and approved** - Candidates had to demonstrate maturity and faithfulness before being entrusted with leadership (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:10</span>)। They should not be recently converted! ((<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:6</span>)

<!-- p75 -->
### Deacon (Servant)

<!-- p76 -->
Role and responsibilities:

<!-- p77 -->
- **Practical service** - Focused on meeting the material needs of the church so that the elders can focus on prayer and teaching (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-4</span>).

<!-- p78 -->
- **Supportive service** - Assisting in hospitality, care for the poor, and administrative tasks (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:3</span>).

<!-- p79 -->
- **Promoting unity** - Addressing issues of fairness and care to prevent division (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-7</span>).

<!-- p80 -->
- **Examples of faith** - Serve as models in humility, faith, and service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:13</span>).

<!-- p81 -->
Selection process (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-6</span>):

<!-- p82 -->
- **Recognized by the congregation** - Chosen on the basis of their reputation and spiritual character.

<!-- p83 -->
- **Approved by the leaders** - The apostles, by prayer and the laying on of hands, confirmed the chosen deacons and appointed them.

<!-- p84 -->
- **Proven in service** - Before official appointment, deacons were first tested to prove their faithfulness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:10</span>).

<!-- p85 -->
### Qualifications for leadership

<!-- p86 -->
#### Qualifications for both elders and deacons:

<!-- p87 -->
- Moral character - Both should be blameless, dignified, and exhibit self-control (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:2</span>, 8).

<!-- p88 -->
- Family life - Both should be faithful in marriage (“husband of one wife”) and manage their households well (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:4-5</span>, 12).

<!-- p89 -->
- Spiritual maturity - Both should be tested and proven before appointment (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:6</span>, 10).

<!-- p90 -->
- Reputation - Both should have a good reputation within and outside the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:7</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
- Faith and integrity - Both should hold the faith with a clear conscience and live according to their faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:9</span>).

<!-- p92 -->
### Example structures

<!-- p93 -->
Using what we have learned so far about the rules and structures of leadership, let us look at two examples of church structure in the New Testament, Jerusalem and Antioch.

<!-- p94 -->
### The church in Jerusalem

<!-- p95 -->
The leadership of the church in Jerusalem was primarily carried out by the apostles, who focused on teaching, prayer, and spiritual oversight (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:42</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:4</span>). As the community grew, the need for an administrative structure became clear to address practical matters, especially the care of widows and the distribution of resources. This led to the appointment of deacons (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-7</span>). Chosen for their spiritual maturity and good reputation, these individuals handled practical ministries so that the apostles could focus on their spiritual responsibilities. Over time, elders also emerged as leaders in the church in Jerusalem. For example, during the Jerusalem council (Acts 15), both apostles and elders were involved in decision-making, reflecting a collaborative leadership structure.

<!-- p96 -->
### The church in Antioch

<!-- p97 -->
The church in Antioch had a more decentralized and diverse leadership structure compared to Jerusalem. It was led by a group of prophets and teachers, including Barnabas, Saul (Paul), Simeon, Lucius, and Manaen (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 13:1</span>). The church in Antioch was more oriented toward reaching Gentiles, whereas the church in Jerusalem more reflected its Jewish roots. Although the New Testament does not explicitly mention deacons in Antioch, given the church’s growing size and its involvement in sending relief to Jerusalem, it is likely that practical roles existed there (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29-30" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 11:29-30</span>). The church in Antioch became a model for the commissioning of missionaries, where the leaders prayed, fasted, and laid hands on Paul and Barnabas to send them out (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 13:2-3</span>). This reflects a structure that balanced spiritual oversight (prophets and teachers) with missionary outreach and practical aid.

<!-- p98 -->
In contrast to Paul’s corrections to the Corinthians and Galatians, Scripture does not criticize the leadership structures of Jerusalem or Antioch. This indicates that, under the guidance of the Holy Spirit, these churches were free to shape their assemblies according to their specific needs and circumstances within the framework of sound doctrine and apostolic teaching (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:42</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>).

<!-- p99 -->
Some terms are used in these passages that we have not yet defined and studied: *apostle, prophet, evangelist*. These gifts are part of the fivefold ministry that Jesus gave to the church.

<!-- p100 -->
### The Fivefold Ministry Gifts

<!-- p101 -->
Jesus established five distinct ministry gifts, often called the fivefold ministry gifts, to equip believers for service and to build up the body of Christ.

<!-- p102 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p103 -->
> **<u>Ephesians 4:11-13</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And He Himself gave some to be apostles, some prophets, some evangelists, and some pastors and teachers,
>
> for the equipping of the saints for the work of ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ,
>
> till we all come to the unity of the faith and of the knowledge of the Son of God, to a perfect man, to the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ;
>

<!-- p104 -->
</span>

<!-- p105 -->
Five gifts that are given to equip the saints for the work of ministry:

<!-- p106 -->
1.  **Apostles** - pioneers and visionaries who lay foundations, establish churches, and advance the kingdom of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:20</span>). Apostles model courage and faith, showing how to step into new territories and take spiritual initiative.

<!-- p107 -->
2.  **Prophets** - messengers and guides who declare God’s truth, bringing encouragement, edification, and direction (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>). Prophets inspire faith, call others to align with God’s Word, and demonstrate dependence on hearing God’s voice.

<!-- p108 -->
3.  **Evangelists** - heralds of the gospel who focus on reaching the lost and mobilize others to share Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:5-8</span>). Evangelists teach disciples to share their faith effectively, exemplify boldness in witness, and stir compassion for the lost.

<!-- p109 -->
4.  **Pastors (shepherds)** - caregivers and nurturers who protect, guide, and provide spiritual oversight for the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>). Pastors teach disciples the value of fellowship, caring for others, and accountability within the body of Christ.

<!-- p110 -->
5.  **Teachers** - instructors and guides who clarify doctrine and help believers grow in understanding and application of God’s Word (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 2:15</span>). Teachers equip disciples to study Scripture deeply, apply it to their daily lives, and teach others effectively.

<!-- p111 -->
## Leadership vs. Gifts

<!-- p112 -->
One commonly misunderstood aspect of leadership in the church is the distinction between leadership authority and responsibility and specific gifts.

<!-- p113 -->
This confusion centers around our use of words like teacher, paster, prophet, apostle, evangelist. These words are gifts. In the New Testament church there is no ‘office’ of ‘apostle’. There are people who do the work of an apostle, who have the gift or calling of an apostle. Having the gift of an apostle does not give them authority or responsibility.

<!-- p114 -->
I am a skilled musician; I have been playing the saxophone for more than twenty years. I played throughout school and college and also played semi-professionally for several summers while working with an evangelistic team. Despite having the skill, gift, and ability of being a saxophonist, I do not have the right to come to your church and stand on the platform with the worship team.

<!-- p115 -->
Similarly, some people are proficient in the gift of pastor/shepherd, but that does not make them the ‘pastor’ of a local church, nor does it make them ‘your’ pastor. The fivefold ministry gifts do not lead the church; leaders (elders and deacons) do. Often leaders have specific fivefold ministry gifts, but that gift enhances their leadership; it does not produce it. Likewise, people who are still developing in the fivefold gifts serve and also desire leadership, but God is interested first in character, then in gifts. We all must become mature, able to test God’s Word, filled with the Spirit, and doers of good works.

<!-- p116 -->
Every leader and every gift must first be under discipleship, become a member of the body, and be submitted.

<!-- p117 -->
Jesus gave specific ministry gifts to equip the saints for the work of ministry. These are different from the motivational gifts (which are discussed later in this chapter) or the manifestational gifts (which are discussed in the chapter on baptisms). As disciples grow, the fivefold ministry gifts equip them for ministry, while elders and deacons provide oversight to ensure sound doctrine and conduct.

<!-- p118 -->
## Your Role in the Church

<!-- p119 -->
Up to now in this chapter we have covered the leadership of the church and the main metaphors for the church. Let us move forward with the specific metaphor of the *body* by considering a marathon runner.

<!-- p120 -->
### You are a part of the whole

<!-- p121 -->
The church, as the body of Christ, depends on every member working together to fulfill its mission. Just as a body cannot function properly in the absence of a limb, so the church requires every believer to actively participate, grow, and serve. Every member of the body has a very important role. Being part of the body means staying connected. A body can live with diminished capacity without a limb, but that limb itself cannot survive separated from the body. We should be an active part of the church and not be cut off. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:24</span> says that we should gather together and not forsake the assembly.

<!-- p122 -->
To truly work and walk together you need fellowship! You need to know others, love others, and ideally even like others! Build strong relationships and friendships, and repair them when they are strained or wounded. When a joint is injured or swollen, you must make efforts to give it rest and/or restore it; so that a joint that has slipped out of place does not become broken! A limb that is broken and does not heal is eventually removed, and alone you cannot be part of the body.

<!-- p123 -->
We do not all have the same work. Some of us can share the gospel from the platform, and some at the dinner table. But we are all called to the ministry of reconciliation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 5:18-19</span>). We have all been included in the priesthood of believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:9</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 1:6</span>). This means that by the power of the Holy Spirit we should all be able to do the work of ministry.

<!-- p124 -->
Toes work with the feet, the feet with the ankles, and so on - each part contributes to the overall mission. Marathon runners must train if they want to finish the race. You must be ready - not only by learning, but by actually practicing what you have learned. The church, like a marathon runner, thrives when each member plays their part in unity.

<!-- p125 -->
There are many people in your life whom possibly only you can reach. It cannot be only the pastor who tries to shepherd them, nor only the evangelist who tries to reach them. God wants to use you, by the power of His Holy Spirit, to reach unbelievers. We all do the work of ministry; the Holy Spirit helps us.

<!-- p126 -->
### You need training

<!-- p127 -->
Believers must be trained and equipped in the church for their specific roles, just as a runner strengthens specific muscles for a race. If you jump straight into a marathon without training you will not succeed, and not all training is the same; you must be adequately prepared for the race. Jesus gave specific ministry gifts to equip believers, so that they themselves would not do all the work, but would enable every member for effective service in his unique role.

<!-- p128 -->
Training for a marathon will bring change. You need strength and you also need to be changed. Our duty is to submit ourselves to God and allow Him to transform us through renewal (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:1-2</span>). Change should lead you toward maturity, enabling you to carry your own burdens and support others. You should be able to bear much fruit. This topic will be discussed in detail in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p129 -->
Training is hard, sometimes uncomfortable. Sometimes the change you need to go through is what you would most like to avoid. But if you want to reach the goal, you must stay on the narrow path. Many times you will need warning, comfort, or support—all to be given with patience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Thessalonians 5:14</span>).

<!-- p130 -->
Every great marathon runner has a coach to train him; you too will need a trainer for help. Undoubtedly, you have the Holy Spirit, but He often relies on His coaches, mature men and women of God in the body of Christ, with whom you are called to work.

<!-- p131 -->
Personally, we must first and foremost be submitted to God, and secondly, in this context, to a congregation of believers. Submission in the church means honoring godly leadership (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 13:17</span>) and humbly submitting to one another (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:21</span>).

<!-- p132 -->
Submission can be difficult, especially when you have previously experienced broken trust or failed leadership. It does not mean blind obedience or control, but reflects an attitude of respect and cooperation. Worldly submission is demanded by those seated in authority, and it can be demeaning and give rise to bitterness. Biblical submission, rooted in humility and discernment, strengthens the church and promotes accountability. Be ready to listen to guidance and constructive feedback.

<!-- p133 -->
Now that we have laid a strong foundation - that you are a meaningful part of the body but that you need training - let us get to finding where you fit.

<!-- p134 -->
### Where you fit

<!-- p135 -->
Finding your role in the church involves seeking God’s guidance, understanding your spiritual gifts, and actively engaging with your church fellowship. It is a journey of prayer, exploration, and growth, in which you discover how God has uniquely equipped you to contribute to His kingdom. It is very important to note that your role in the church may change over time and will likely not remain the same throughout your whole life.

<!-- p136 -->
Here is a practical way to help you find your place. For clarity these are presented as a series of steps, but you will likely repeat the same step many times during your walk with God.

<!-- p137 -->
#### Step 1 - Seek God’s guidance through prayer and the Word

<!-- p138 -->
Begin by **seeking God’s guidance through prayer**. God is the ultimate source of your purpose and calling (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="29" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 29:11</span>). Pray regularly for clarity about how you can serve in the church, and ask Him to reveal your spiritual gifts and passions. Trust that as you submit your plans to His will, He will direct your steps (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 3:5-6</span>).

<!-- p139 -->
**Study God’s Word** so that your understanding of the church’s mission and your role in it deepens. Key passages such as 1 Corinthians 12 and <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-12</span> highlight the diversity of spiritual gifts and roles in the body of Christ. Reflect on examples in Scripture of individuals who discovered and fulfilled their roles, such as Paul, Timothy, and Lydia.

<!-- p140 -->
#### Step 2 - Identify your gifts, passions, and skills

<!-- p141 -->
Identifying your **spiritual gifts** is an essential step. You may find it helpful to take a spiritual gifts assessment. These gifts are given by God to build up the church. Several categories of gifts are listed in the New Testament:

<!-- p142 -->
- **Motivational gifts** given by God: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:6-8</span>

<!-- p143 -->
- Discussed after this list.

<!-- p144 -->
- Fivefold ministry gifts given by Jesus: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-13</span>

<!-- p145 -->
- Discussed earlier in this chapter.

<!-- p146 -->
- Manifestation gifts given by the Holy Spirit: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span>

<!-- p147 -->
- Discussed in the chapter on baptism.

<!-- p148 -->
**Motivational gifts** indicate how a believer naturally thinks, feels, and acts. They motivate believers to serve in specific ways that build up the church. Each gift complements another, enabling the church to function as a unified body with diverse roles (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:4-5</span>). They function as grace-endowed abilities (Greek: ***charis** - khar'-ece - **G5463 -** an unearned gift*), that is, they are given by God, not earned or learned. Although the gifts come from God, the one who has a gift can still become more proficient in its use.

<!-- p149 -->
The motivational gifts are:

<!-- p150 -->
1.  **Prophecy** - A God-given inclination to boldly proclaim truth, often focused on conviction and righteousness, guiding others to align with God's word.\*

<!-- p151 -->
2.  **Service (Helps)** - Meets practical needs through acts of service and help in order to support others.

<!-- p152 -->
3.  **Teaching** - Explains and clarifies truth, helping others understand and apply God's word.

<!-- p153 -->
4.  **Exhortation (Encouragement)** - Motivates, encourages, and comforts others to inspire growth and perseverance.

<!-- p154 -->
5.  **Giving** - Generously supplies resources to meet needs and advance God's work, without seeking recognition.

<!-- p155 -->
6.  **Leadership (Administration)** - Organizes and directs others with vision and efficiency to accomplish goals.

<!-- p156 -->
7.  **Mercy** - Shows compassion and care to comfort and support those who are suffering or in need.

<!-- p157 -->
\* Prophecy as used in the context of Romans is understood as different from prophecy as a manifestation gift of the Holy Spirit (1 Corinthians). The Greek word used in both passages is the same, but the context is different. For a more in-depth study of the difference, I recommend reading:

<!-- p158 -->
- *The Gift of Prophecy* - by Kenneth E. Hagin ([www.rhema.org](http://www.rhema.org))

<!-- p159 -->
<!-- -->

<!-- p160 -->
- *Gifts and Ministries of the Holy Spirit* by Lester Sumrall

<!-- p161 -->
When you identify your gifts, consider your **passions and skills**. God often calls us to serve in the very areas we are excited about or in which we are proficient (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 31:2-6</span>). Think about what excites you about serving God and others, and also how your professional skills, hobbies, and life experiences can contribute to ministry.

<!-- p162 -->
Seek guidance from **leaders and mentors** who can offer wisdom and insight (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 15:22</span>). Meet with a pastor or ministry leader to discuss your interests and ask for honest feedback about where you might be the best fit. Pray over the feedback you receive. Consider joining a discipleship group to receive training and ongoing support.

<!-- p163 -->
#### Step 3 - Be faithful now

<!-- p164 -->
The Parable of the Talents (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-30" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 25:14-30</span>) reminds us that we must be faithful in little things to be entrusted with greater things (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 16:10</span>).

<!-- p165 -->
**Get involved and serve** in various ministries so that you can discover where God is calling you. Start small by volunteering in areas of need in your church and in the ministries they support. For example:

<!-- p166 -->
- Join the hospitality team, children’s ministry, or outreach programs. Consider leading a discipleship-focused group for new believers on this book, *Principles of Faith,* using group discussion questions and end-of-chapter activities to enhance dialogue.

<!-- p167 -->
- Be open to stepping out of your comfort zone to explore new opportunities.

<!-- p168 -->
- Pay attention to where God works through you and where you feel most effective.

<!-- p169 -->
Practice giving. We can give to the church in many important ways, and we are instructed to do so to fulfill the mission.

<!-- p170 -->
- Time - We can give our time and volunteer. When we give our time, we use our efforts and our talents. This can also be a good way to learn new skills and grow existing ones.

<!-- p171 -->
- Resources - We can give our resources. Today, most commonly this is likely giving financially to the church and ministries (not just to the “institution,” but also to those in need within the church). Giving money is useful, because the church can purchase necessary resources with it, but people have gone beyond money and given their physical resources as well, such as extra homes, clothing, cars, food, building materials, tools, etc. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="32-35" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 4:32-35</span> we see an example of the early believers who gave freely, distributing and giving the proceeds from the sale of extra lands and houses so that ‘no one was in need’.

<!-- p172 -->
Working shoulder to shoulder with others in ministry can reveal your strengths and deepen your understanding of your role. Participate in small groups or church activities to develop relationships. Listen to the burdens and needs of those around you and consider how you can support them. Building relationships within the church helps connect you to others and understand the needs of the community.

<!-- p173 -->
#### Step 4 - Get help!

<!-- p174 -->
Jesus said that in life we will face tribulation! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:33</span>) We are encouraged not to carry the weight of our burdens ourselves, but to cast them on the God who cares for us! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:7</span>)

<!-- p175 -->
Sometimes on our journey we can get injured. Marathon runners, even if they are doing “everything right,” can put so much strain on their bodies that they get injured, or they may stumble and fall.

<!-- p176 -->
Not every ‘fall’ is related to sin, though some are; not every weakness is ‘our fault’; sometimes others will sin against us. Whatever the cause of our weakness or injury, we are to be restored and healed.

<!-- p177 -->
There is great joy and freedom in seeking inner healing and restoration. We are called to cover the more delicate parts of the body (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:23</span>) and to restore those who have been caught in wrongdoing (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:1</span>).

<!-- p178 -->
There is much to say on this subject, and we offer a brief overview of themes of healing in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p179 -->
#### Step 5 - Be patient

<!-- p180 -->
Pay attention to confirmation that comes from God and from other people. Often, God confirms your calling through fruitfulness, joy, and the support of the people around you. Reflect on where your efforts have borne fruit, and listen to the feedback you receive about the impact of your service. This can help you recognize where you are most effective in serving the body of Christ.

<!-- p181 -->
Be patient and trust God’s timing. Finding your role can take time and may involve many attempts and trial-and-error (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 1:6</span>). Have confidence that as you remain faithful and open to his leading, God will guide you. During this time, stay committed to growth and training—by participating in workshops, Bible studies, and other means that equip you for service. Do this with the church; connect with the body of Christ to discover your gifts.

<!-- p182 -->
Remember, the church is not a building or an institution; it is a living, breathing body made up of people like you. By stepping into your role, you contribute to its mission and glorify Christ as its head.
