# Chapter 10 - Sanctified-Prepared for God’s Purpose

<!-- p1 -->
In the previous two chapters we discussed several important topics. We also examined the role and ministry of the Holy Spirit, and the role and ministry of the church.

<!-- p2 -->
We have a role in the church, and just as we know that we need training, we also need purification. To be truly prepared for God’s purposes, we must be sanctified.

<!-- p3 -->
The definition of **sanctification** is to purify something for a particular purpose. This word is closely related to **dedication**, which means to set something apart for a specific purpose.

<!-- p4 -->
Imagine that a master sculptor is preparing to work with a large piece of white stone. This master envisions what this raw and shapeless stone could become through his careful craftsmanship. With a very specific design in mind, he tells his assistants to set this piece of stone aside for a particular design (**dedication**). As he refines and carves the stone, giving it layers, depth, and shape, he purifies it for that specific design (**sanctifies it**). The act of dedicating the stone to his special project was swift, but the process of shaping the stone into its final design requires considerable patience.

<!-- p5 -->
At salvation we were set apart, dedicated to God. Through the work of purifying our souls with the help of the Spirit we are transformed. This process of purification is what sanctifies us.

<!-- p6 -->
When we received Jesus through salvation, we were born again. We were justified, our sins were forgiven. Our spirit was brought to life anew; we live in Christ. We were adopted into the family of God and became the true temple of the Holy Spirit. Such an amazing transformation that Scripture says, “*There is joy in the presence of the angels of God over one sinner who repents.” (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 15:10</span>).

<!-- p7 -->
Receiving Jesus through salvation (**justification**) saves our spirit, purifies us, and makes us presentable before God, so that we may receive His Holy Spirit. However, despite such a great salvation, **our souls** (our mind, will, and emotions) still **need work**!

<!-- p8 -->
Was the work of salvation incomplete? No! The work of salvation is effective, but it must continue through the renewal brought by the Holy Spirit in the believer’s life. This process of renewal and transformation is the work of **sanctification**.

<!-- p9 -->
We know that the work continues even after conversion because our painful memories were not resolved, many of our beliefs remained the same, and many of our habits or customs did not disappear. Furthermore, conversion did not separate our minds from their carnal (fleshly) nature.

<!-- p10 -->
The word ‘carnal’ comes from a Latin root meaning ‘flesh’ or ‘meat’. The carnal nature is in everyone from birth, like an animal’s instinctive nature, which is for our survival. The carnal nature operates in a self-centered, self-focused, self-important way. Children are natural examples of the carnal nature. Despite their innocence, their nature remains primarily focused on their wants, desires, and needs; and ‘watch out!’ if you do not meet their every expectation.

<!-- p11 -->
Part of growing up is maturing out of our childish ways, but when pressure comes we very easily revert to that pattern of thinking and living that is self-centered. As James says:

<!-- p12 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p13 -->
> **<u>James 4:1</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Where do wars and quarrels come from among you? Do they not come from your desires for pleasure that wage war in your members?
>

<!-- p14 -->
</span>

<!-- p15 -->
Paul, in his letter to the Romans (chapters 6 - 8), explains this carnal nature in more detail. He says that this carnal nature makes him do what he hates and compels him to do evil. He describes this inner conflict as a war between his human nature and his God-ward spirit.

<!-- p16 -->
This conflict between flesh and spirit exists in each of us, and it is our job to do something about it. If we do not confront our carnal nature, our souls will remain trapped in patterns of spiritual decline, even though our spirits have been redeemed.

<!-- p17 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p18 -->
> **<u>Romans 8:6-8</u>** (NKJV)
>
> For to be carnally minded is death, but to be spiritually minded is life and peace.
>
> Because the carnal mind is enmity against God; for it is not subject to the law of God, nor indeed can be.
>
> So then, those who are in the flesh cannot please God.
>

<!-- p19 -->
</span>

<!-- p20 -->
These words of Paul are both a warning and a promise for us. Even though we have been born again, we still have a fleshly mind that needs to be brought under the knowledge of Christ. As we do so, we walk in God’s life and peace.

<!-- p21 -->
Take courage, there is hope for us! We are to walk according to the Spirit and be led by the Spirit of God. In doing so, we will not walk according to the flesh.

<!-- p22 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="1,13,14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p23 -->
> **<u>Romans 8:1,13,14</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Therefore there is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus, who do not walk according to the flesh, but according to the Spirit.
>
> For if you live according to the flesh you will die; but if by the Spirit you put to death the deeds of the body, you will live.
>
> For as many as are led by the Spirit of God, these are the sons of God.
>

<!-- p24 -->
</span>

<!-- p25 -->
How do we learn to walk in the Spirit? Is it *only a conscious choice*? We learn to walk in the Spirit the same way we learn to walk naturally. We are driven to move and grow, so we strain, struggle, crawl, and stumble, until we stand on our feet. Our natural parents cannot walk for us, but they can support, encourage, help, and enable us in our walking.

<!-- p26 -->
In the same way, God cannot walk our faith for us. He will teach us, but we must make the effort. As Paul told the Philippians:

<!-- p27 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="12-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p28 -->
> **<u>Philippians 2:12-13</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Therefore, my beloved, as you have always obeyed, not only in my presence but now much more in my absence, work out your own salvation with fear and trembling;
>
> for it is God who works in you both to will and to do for His good pleasure.
>

<!-- p29 -->
</span>

<!-- p30 -->
We must work out our own salvation. This does not mean that we climb onto a wooden cross and are physically crucified, but we must crucify our fleshly nature (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:24</span>). God promises that He will work in us and help us, but we must choose to walk this path, and persevere in it.

<!-- p31 -->
It is right to say that ‘we’ are dealing with our fleshly nature, but it is also right to say that ‘God’ is dealing with it as well. The work of the cross (justification) was hard for Jesus—He had to sacrifice His life for us—but it is easy for us; it is a free gift. The work of the Spirit (sanctification) is easy for God—He walks in all truth and is ready and willing to dwell with us—but it is hard for us, because we must crucify our old nature, and our nature is not very interested in being crucified.

<!-- p32 -->
This process is shared. God helps, comforts, guides, directs, and corrects. We listen, obey, follow, and respond. The result is growth and change. This is the pruning that Jesus said God would do in us. Jesus is the vine, His Father is the vinedresser, we are the branches:

<!-- p33 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p34 -->
> **<u>John 15:1-4</u>** (NKJV)
>
> "I am the true vine, and My Father is the vinedresser.
>
> Every branch in Me that does not bear fruit He takes away; and every branch that bears fruit He prunes, that it may bear more fruit.
>
> You are already clean because of the word which I have spoken to you.
>
> Abide in Me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself, unless it abides in the vine, neither can you, unless you abide in Me.
>

<!-- p35 -->
</span>

<!-- p36 -->
Pruning is exceedingly beneficial for a plant. Recalling our chapter on repentance, we are called to turn from dead works (those works that no longer bear fruit) and to turn to God. The result of turning our backs on the dead places in our lives and turning to God is that the Master Gardener of our lives is enabled to prune the branches, improve growth, and produce even greater spiritual fruit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:2</span>).

<!-- p37 -->
Although pruning may seem painful, it displays God’s loving care as He fashions us into vessels for His purpose (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 12:11</span>). Some people may feel a sense of loss, even fear, while going through the process of pruning. Even so, God is gentle, and He will not break a bruised reed (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="42" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 42:3</span>). Vines are pruned during their dormant season so that the stress on the plant is reduced. The gardener who tends the growth cuts back that old and hardened growth which no longer bears fruit as before; this enables the vine to invest energy in those branches with the greatest potential for good fruit.

<!-- p38 -->
Just as a gardener prunes branches to direct growth, so God may allow seasons of correction and discipline to refine our faith and character, yet His work is patient and compassionate. God prunes those who abide in Him. Our focus should be more on abiding in Christ, and less on the work of pruning.

<!-- p39 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 12:11</span> reminds us that pruning “*yields the peaceable fruit of righteousness*” for those who are trained by it.

<!-- p40 -->
How are we trained by righteousness? By being transformed through the renewal of our minds.

<!-- p41 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p42 -->
> **<u>Romans 12:1-2</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Therefore, brothers, I beseech you by the mercies of God to present your bodies as a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable to God—this is your reasonable service.
>
> And do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewal of your mind, so that you may prove what is the good, acceptable, and perfect will of God.
>

<!-- p43 -->
</span>

<!-- p44 -->
For transformation Paul points out two simple requirements:

<!-- p45 -->
<u>1. Present your bodies as a living sacrifice before God</u>. A **living** sacrifice does not die physically at the time of offering, but because a living sacrifice cannot die, you must continually bring it to the altar!

<!-- p46 -->
<u>2. Renew your mind!</u> Renewal, based on the Greek word: **Anakainōsis** (*An-ak-ah'-ee-no-sis -* **G342**), means renewal or making anew, which focuses on the ongoing process of inner change and growth.

<!-- p47 -->
This renewal brings transformation. Transformation is based on the Greek word: **Metamorphoō** (*met-am-or-fo'-o -* **G3339**), which means to change into another form; to transform or transfigure, which emphasizes a complete change. From Metamorphoō comes the English word metamorphosis, the process caterpillars go through to become butterflies.

<!-- p48 -->
God wants to fundamentally change our minds and our patterns of thought. His desire is that we not be conformed to the ways of the world, but that we know what the good (beneficial), acceptable, and perfect (complete &amp; lacking nothing) will of God is.

<!-- p49 -->
The requirements for transformation really are just this simple, **though they require discipline**!

<!-- p50 -->
Our battle against the flesh will not be won by our own flesh, but with God’s equipment for the war of the mind. His Word (the sword of truth) is powerful to correct every imagination and proud thought, and to bring into custody (into captivity) every thought that goes against obeying God.

<!-- p51 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="3-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p52 -->
> **<u>2 Corinthians 10:3-6</u>** (NKJV)
>
> For though we walk in the flesh, we do not wage war according to the flesh.
>
> For the weapons of our warfare are not carnal, but mighty in God for pulling down strongholds,
>
> casting down arguments and every high thing that exalts itself against the knowledge of God, bringing every thought into captivity to the obedience of Christ,
>
> and being ready to punish all disobedience when your obedience is fulfilled.
>

<!-- p53 -->
</span>

<!-- p54 -->
When authorities arrest someone, they put them in chains and place them in a jail cell. If that person escapes custody, they do not close their eyes; they use extraordinary resources and do not rest until that person is brought back under control. We should work with the same zeal in the contest for our minds!

<!-- p55 -->
We are made new through obedience and experience with the truth. Jesus said that if we abide in His words, then we will be His disciples, we will experience (<u>know</u>) the truth, and it will set us free (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-32" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 8:31-32</span>)!

<!-- p56 -->
Experiencing the truth is different from knowledge of the truth. The Greek word for experience is **Ginōskō** (Ghin-oce'-ko **- G1097**); it means to know through personal experience or deep understanding; and it points to gradual learning.

<!-- p57 -->
What transforms and renews us and our minds is not academic knowledge of the truth, but our personal experience of the truth. We are sanctified by His truth! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="17" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 17:17</span>).

## How to renew your mind

<!-- p59 -->
The renewal of our minds is not a one-size-fits-all process—it depends on which part of the mind we are talking about! God has designed our minds with astonishing complexity, including many different memory systems, each serving a particular purpose. Some run automatically in the background, while others require focused, intentional thinking. These systems are gifts from God that help us survive, grow, and flourish, but when they are misused or left unrenewed, they can also lead us astray. Understanding how these systems work gives us the wisdom to know how to cooperate with God’s transforming process.

<!-- p60 -->
In the next section I will describe each memory system, their purpose and function, some practical steps to renew this aspect of our mind, and resources for ongoing practice and study.

<!-- p61 -->
### **Memory systems and how to renew them**

<!-- p62 -->
#### **1. Explicit memory (declarative memory)**

<!-- p63 -->
**Purpose:** Stores facts, events, and experiences that we can consciously recall. It is divided into two types—**Semantic** and **Episodic** memory.

<!-- p64 -->
**Semantic memory - facts and knowledge.** In semantic memory we store facts, definitions, and general knowledge—such as historical dates, Bible verses, and practical information. It helps us predict outcomes, make decisions, and avoid danger (for example, recognizing poisonous plants). While it gives us stability and order, it can also lead to rigidity—over-reliance on rules instead of adapting to unexpected situations.

<!-- p65 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p66 -->
- **Study Scripture** so that our worldview is formed (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="119" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="105" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 119:105</span>).

<!-- p67 -->
- **Memorize truths** and teachings so that we stand firm in faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 3:16</span>).

<!-- p68 -->
- **Read Christian books** such as *"Renewing the Mind" by Casey Treat* or *"Battlefield of the Mind" by Joyce Meyer* to develop godly patterns of thinking.

<!-- p69 -->
**Episodic memory - personal experiences.** Episodic memory stores our personal experiences, linking them to specific times and places—such as a wedding day or a traumatic event. These memories provide context, helping us learn from the past and build relationships. However, episodic memory tends toward emotional bias and distortion. Past traumas can create fear-linked associations, making it difficult to move forward.

<!-- p70 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p71 -->
- **Reflect and confess** so that hidden pain comes into the light of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 1:9</span>).

<!-- p72 -->
- **Reframe past experiences** through the lens of God’s grace (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:28</span>).

<!-- p73 -->
- **Seek inner healing through prayer and counseling** (*"Your Pain is Showing" by Larry F. Low, "The Bondage Breaker" by Neil T. Anderson*, *"Emotionally Healthy Spirituality" by Peter Scazzero*).

<!-- p74 -->
#### **2. Implicit memory (non-declarative memory)**

<!-- p75 -->
**Purpose:** Unconscious memory used for habits, skills, and emotional associations. It operates automatically, helping us respond quickly to situations. It is divided into **procedural memory** and **emotional conditioning**.

<!-- p76 -->
**Procedural memory - skills and habits.** Procedural memory enables us to perform tasks without thinking—such as driving, typing, or playing an instrument. It is efficient, conserving our mental energy and time. However, bad habits and negative patterns can also become deeply ingrained and be hard to break.

<!-- p77 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p78 -->
- **Practice spiritual disciplines** such as prayer and fasting so that godly habits are formed (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 5:14</span>).

<!-- p79 -->
- **Train yourself in godliness** through repetition and obedience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 4:7</span>).

<!-- p80 -->
- Read books like *"Atomic Habits" by James Clear* (practical) and *"Spiritual Disciplines for the Christian Life" by Donald Whitney* (biblical) to establish God-honoring habits.

<!-- p81 -->
**Emotional Conditioning - Emotional Associations.** Emotional conditioning links sensory cues (like smells or sounds) with emotions, causing us to react before we consciously process them. This system protects us, but when past experiences have been negative, it can also lead to irrational fears or emotional reactions.

<!-- p82 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p83 -->
- **Pray** for healing from past wounds (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="41" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 41:10</span>).

<!-- p84 -->
- **Practice forgiveness** and release bitterness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-32" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:31-32</span>).

<!-- p85 -->
- **Replace fear with God’s promises** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 1:7</span>).

<!-- p86 -->
- **Seek guidance from books like** *“Your Pain is Showing” by Larry F. Low, "The Bondage Breaker" by Neil T. Anderson*, “*Soul Care" by Rob Reimer* to overcome emotional triggers.

<!-- p87 -->
#### **3. Working Memory (Short-term Memory)**

<!-- p88 -->
**Purpose:** Provides temporary storage for problem-solving and decision-making. It helps us focus, retain information for a short time, and process tasks—like remembering instructions or making quick decisions. Although powerful, it’s also limited, sensitive to distraction, and easily overloaded, especially under stress.

<!-- p89 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p90 -->
- **Meditate and reflect** on God’s Word daily (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 1:2</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
- **Renew your thoughts** through **intentional thinking** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:2</span>).

<!-- p92 -->
- **Practice mindfulness** and simplify mental clutter (*"Habits of Grace" by David Mathis*).

<!-- p93 -->
- **Read** *"The Celebration of Discipline" by Richard Foster* to sharpen focus through spiritual disciplines.

<!-- p94 -->
## Conclusion

<!-- p95 -->
Renewing the mind is not a quick fix—it is a lifelong process of gradual transformation. Each memory system plays a role in shaping how we think, feel, and act. By working intentionally with the Holy Spirit, applying Scripture, and developing devotional habits, we can train our minds to align with God’s truth. Whether it’s healing old wounds, learning new habits, or staying focused in the present moment, God has given us both **spiritual tools** and **practical wisdom** to walk in renewal and freedom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 10:5</span>).

<!-- p96 -->
Our world is filled with trouble, tribulation, and trials. Through the disciplined renewal of the mind, our souls are transformed, and we can live a life of freedom for God, filled with the fruit of His Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-25</span>).
