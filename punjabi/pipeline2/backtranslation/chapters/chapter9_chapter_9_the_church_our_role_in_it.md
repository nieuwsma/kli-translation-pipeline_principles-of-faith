# Chapter 9 - The Church &amp; Our Role in It

<!-- p1 -->
The Church is the way by which God—on the earth—through His people, doing His work—reveals Himself. We are called to be His body; we are His hands and feet to fulfill His work and purpose. Without our efforts, the harvest will remain in the field and will not be gathered.

<!-- p2 -->
## Who Is the Church?

<!-- p3 -->
The Church, ***Ekklesia** (eh-klee-SEE-ah - **G1577**),* is derived from two Greek words:

<!-- p4 -->
- **"Ek"**: "out of."

<!-- p5 -->
- **"Kaleo"**: "to call."

<!-- p6 -->
Taken together, **Ekklesia** literally means "the called-out ones" or "the assembly called out." In ancient Greek city-states, it referred to a gathering of citizens summoned for a specific purpose, such as legislative or judicial meetings. It was a secular term used for an assembly of people.

<!-- p7 -->
In the New Testament, it is used primarily for the community of those who believe in Jesus Christ. It can refer to:

<!-- p8 -->
- **The local church**: A specific gathering of believers in a particular place (for example, the church in Corinth, Philippi, etc.).

<!-- p9 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p10 -->
> **<u>1 Corinthians 1:2</u>** (NKJV)
>
> To the church of God which is at Corinth, to those who are sanctified in Christ Jesus, called to be saints, with all who in every place call on the name of Jesus Christ our Lord, both theirs and ours:
>

<!-- p11 -->
</span>

<!-- p12 -->
- **The universal church**: The collective body of all believers across time and place.

<!-- p13 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p14 -->
> **<u>Matthew 16:18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And I also say to you that you are Peter, and on this rock I will build My church, and the gates of Hades shall not prevail against it.
>

<!-- p15 -->
</span>

<!-- p16 -->
The Church is the people, not the building or place where they meet. The called-out ones: this idea indicates that Christians are called out of the world and set apart for God’s purposes (for example, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:9</span>) for a specific mission and purpose (the Great Commission). Technically, wherever two or more believers gather together (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="18" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 18:20</span>) that is the church. Typically, the church is organized into regular meetings, perhaps with a set place and time, but wherever two or more people meet in Jesus’ name, there the church is!

<!-- p17 -->
The Church was established by Jesus, and it grew through the work of the disciples who followed Him in the first century AD. The church that we have and experience today in all its local congregations throughout the earth is the direct result of the original work of Christ and His apostles. As believers, we should be committed to a local church for fellowship, edification, equipping for service, and doing good works!

<!-- p18 -->
To help us understand what the church is, Scripture gives us powerful images that show what it is designed to be.

<!-- p19 -->
## Understanding the Church

<!-- p20 -->
Several key metaphorical images are used to illustrate the characteristics of the church:

<!-- p21 -->
1.  Body

<!-- p22 -->
2.  Bride

<!-- p23 -->
3.  Temple

<!-- p24 -->
4.  Family

<!-- p25 -->
5.  Flock

<!-- p26 -->
6.  Vine and Branches

<!-- p27 -->
Each metaphor highlights a unique aspect of the church, but together they reveal the church’s identity as a unified and diverse community under the leadership of Christ.

<!-- p28 -->
### Body

<!-- p29 -->
The church is often called the **Body of Christ**, which highlights both its unity and diversity. With Christ as the head, the church functions like a living organism, where every believer plays an essential and often different role. Each member is endowed with unique gifts and contributes to the health and mission of the body according to their particular function. This image illuminates the interdependence of believers, specifically that for the body to function it must be connected, and that our work is collaborative, not competitive. When each part works together, the body grows and flourishes in unity and love (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:12-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:15-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 1:18</span>).

<!-- p30 -->
### Bride

<!-- p31 -->
The church is described as the **Bride of Christ**, portraying an intimate and loving relationship with Jesus. Just as a bride is cherished and prepared for her husband, so the church is sanctified and purified by Christ’s sacrificial love. This metaphor captures the devotion and faithfulness expected of the church, and Christ’s deep affection for his people. This image also points to the final union between Christ and the church, celebrated at the marriage supper of the Lamb, where the church is presented in glory without spot or blemish (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="25-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:25-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 19:7-9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="21" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 21:2</span>).

<!-- p32 -->
### Temple

<!-- p33 -->
The church is called the **Temple of God**, the dwelling place of his Spirit. Believers are described as living stones, being built together on the foundation of the apostles and prophets, with Christ himself as the cornerstone. This holy structure signifies God’s presence among his people and their role as a community of worship and prayer. The temple image underscores the importance of holiness and the church’s shared purpose of glorifying God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 3:16-17</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:19-22</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:4-5</span>).

<!-- p34 -->
### Family

<!-- p35 -->
The church is called the **Family of God**, emphasizing familial unity and care among its members. Through the Spirit of adoption, believers are made children of God and members of his household. This family image highlights the love, belonging, and shared inheritance believers have in Christ. It also reflects the call to treat one another with kindness and humility as brothers and sisters in God’s family (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:19</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:15-17</span>). Furthermore, through the image of family it shows how church members are to mature: that they be taught and nurtured by spiritual mothers and fathers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:1-2</span>), so that we grow from infants who drink the pure milk of the Word to full adults able to eat the solid food of that Word (Hebrews 5).

<!-- p36 -->
### Flock

<!-- p37 -->
The church is also described as a **flock**, sheep under the care of Jesus, the Good Shepherd. This image conveys the church’s dependence on Christ for guidance, provision, and protection. It also underscores the responsibility of church leaders to shepherd God’s flock faithfully as under-shepherds, reflecting the example of Christ’s love and care. The flock image speaks of trust, obedience, and the close relationship between the Great Shepherd and his sheep (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:14-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:2</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>).

<!-- p38 -->
### Vine and Branches

<!-- p39 -->
The church is also described as the **Vine and the Branches**, which depicts the vital connection between believers and Christ. Jesus is the true vine, and believers are the branches who must abide in him to bear fruit. This metaphor emphasizes dependence on Christ for spiritual vitality, growth, and fruitfulness. Apart from him, believers can do nothing, but by remaining in him they glorify God and fulfill their purpose. Jesus will prune so that he may bring forth more fruit in our lives (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:1-5</span>).

<!-- p40 -->
## Roles of the Church

<!-- p41 -->
The mission of the church, like the role of the Holy Spirit, can be understood through three dimensions: upward, inward, and outward; each reflects a unique aspect of its calling in God’s plan. These dimensions help the church fulfill its role as a worshiping, edifying, and missional community.

<!-- p42 -->
### Upward: Worship and Glorifying God

<!-- p43 -->
The church’s first purpose is to worship God and glorify Him. Corporate worship, where believers gather together to exalt God, is a visible reflection of His glory and our response to His greatness. As the psalmist declares, “*Worship the Lord in the beauty of holiness; let all the earth tremble before Him*” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="96" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 96:9</span>). Beyond corporate worship, the church is called to live lives of obedience and sacrifice, offering themselves as “living sacrifices” as an act of spiritual worship (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:1-2</span>). This upward focus keeps the church centered on the greatness of God and aligns its priorities with His will.

<!-- p44 -->
### Inward: Building up the Body

<!-- p45 -->
The Great Commission commands us to ‘teach them to observe’; this command goes far beyond formal teaching methods like this book. Making disciples (discussed further in the chapter on discipleship) is shaping the follower into that example, as parents raise their child. Good parents do not put an encyclopedia in the hands of a five-year-old and challenge them to figure it out on their own! Instead, they model, demonstrate, correct, guide, nurture, love, care, and guard.

<!-- p46 -->
Parents make sacrifices for their children because they love them, because they are part of the same body, because they share the same blood. As members of Christ’s body and family we too share the same body, we share the same blood. Communion, through its symbolism, reminds us that through Christ’s sacrifice we have been joined to one another. The act of Communion should be a point of unity, reminding us that we all need salvation, and that salvation is only from God. In this unity we see that, apart from our Head Christ, no part of the body is greater than another.

<!-- p47 -->
### Outward: Reaching the World

<!-- p48 -->
The most well-known part of the church’s mission extends outward to reach the world with the gospel. Jesus’ Great Commission calls His followers to preach the gospel and make disciples of all nations (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19-20</span>). Empowered by the Holy Spirit, the church is to be His witness to the ends of the earth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>).

<!-- p49 -->
Jesus is the light of the world (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 8:12</span>). Light shines outward in all directions from its source. His light shines through His revelations: the Word, His Spirit, and we, His body. We are the light of the world; with our lives we are to reflect His light into our darkened worlds and display such good works that glorify God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 5:14-16</span>). Practically, this means that the church is called to raise its voice for justice and mercy in both spiritual and physical senses. As James says, our pure worship is this: that we care for the vulnerable, the orphans, the widows (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:27</span>).

<!-- p50 -->
## Leadership of the Church

<!-- p51 -->
Across the Christian world there are countless denominations, meetings, gatherings, and councils. There are very few universal rules for the leadership of the church, but the rules we do have are very important:

<!-- p52 -->
1.  Jesus is the only Head of the church. Everyone serves Him (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 1:18</span>).

<!-- p53 -->
2.  There are high standards for those who lead; they are worthy of double honor, because they will receive double judgment (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:17</span>).

<!-- p54 -->
2.1  They should be mature, not puffed up with pride, and accountable (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:1-13</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 1:5-9</span>).

<!-- p55 -->
2.2.  They must be servants of all (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42-45" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 10:42-45</span>).

<!-- p56 -->
2.3.  They should devote themselves to the Word and to prayer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:4</span>).

<!-- p57 -->
2.4.  They bear the responsibility of equipping the body for the work of ministry.

<!-- p58 -->
3.  No members of the body are greater or lesser. The scriptures that affirm this are:

<!-- p59 -->
3.1.  There must be no partiality between rich and poor (James 2).

<!-- p60 -->
3.2.  There is no distinction between Jew and Gentile, slave or free, male or female (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 3:27-28</span>).

<!-- p61 -->
Beyond leadership rules, church structure is important, but much of it is situational and a matter of tradition rather than a universal command. “<u>We’ve always done it this way!</u>” is a popular saying; if you go far enough back, there was usually a good reason why they did it that way. It is generally neither good nor bad, but rather a matter of preference, needs, and availability.

<!-- p62 -->
A helpful example of tradition being the foundation for structure is family dinner time. The most important thing about family dinner time is that everyone eats and the food is nutritious. There is no limit to the variations: what they eat, how they eat, how they season their food, etc. Different combinations are generally acceptable and are largely a matter of taste and preference.

<!-- p63 -->
The needs and capacities of a church with 10,000 members are different from those of a church with 50 members. The pattern established in the Book of Acts and in the canon of the New Testament was the model of **elders** and **deacons**. Although leadership structures may look different across churches and denominations, ultimately this model represents the minimal functional structure of leadership for the church. Let’s look at these leadership offices and how they are chosen.

<!-- p64 -->
### Elders (overseers)

<!-- p65 -->
Roles and responsibilities:

<!-- p66 -->
- **Spiritual leadership** - Providing oversight, guidance, and pastoral care for the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:2</span>).

<!-- p67 -->
- **Teaching and preaching** - Responsible for teaching sound doctrine and guarding against false teaching (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:17</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 1:9</span>).

<!-- p68 -->
- **Shepherding the flock** - Caring for the spiritual needs of the congregation, setting an example of humility and service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:2-3</span>).

<!-- p69 -->
- **Protecting the church** - Serving as watchmen, protecting the church from false teaching and division (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29-31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:29-31</span>).

<!-- p70 -->
- **Equipping the saints** - Training and equipping believers for works of ministry and discipleship (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-12</span>).

<!-- p71 -->
Selection process:

<!-- p72 -->
- **Appointed in every church** - Elders were appointed by the apostles or their representatives, often through prayer and fasting (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 14:23</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 1:5</span>).

<!-- p73 -->
- **Affirmed by the Holy Spirit** - Their appointment was considered Spirit-led, recognizing God’s call and gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>).

<!-- p74 -->
- **Tested and approved** - Candidates had to demonstrate maturity and faithfulness before being entrusted with leadership (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:10</span>)। They must not be recent converts! ((<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:6</span>)

<!-- p75 -->
### Deacons (servants)

<!-- p76 -->
Roles and responsibilities:

<!-- p77 -->
- **Practical service** - Focused on meeting physical needs in the church, so that the elders can focus on prayer and teaching (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-4</span>).

<!-- p78 -->
- **Supportive ministry** - Helping with hospitality, care for the poor, and administrative tasks (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:3</span>).

<!-- p79 -->
- **Promoting unity** - Addressing issues of fairness and care to prevent division (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-7</span>).

<!-- p80 -->
- **Examples of faith** - become examples in humility, faith, and service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:13</span>).

<!-- p81 -->
Selection process (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-6</span>):

<!-- p82 -->
- **Identified by the congregation** - chosen on the basis of their reputation and spiritual character।

<!-- p83 -->
- **Approved by the leaders** - the apostles examined the chosen deacons through prayer and the laying on of hands and appointed them।

<!-- p84 -->
- **Confirmation in service** - the deacons were first tested to prove their faithfulness before official appointment (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:10</span>)।

<!-- p85 -->
### Qualifications for leadership

<!-- p86 -->
#### Qualifications for both elders and deacons:

<!-- p87 -->
- Moral character - both should be above reproach, respectable, and exhibiting self-control (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:2</span>, 8)。

<!-- p88 -->
- Family life - both should be faithful in marriage (“husband of one wife”) and should manage their household well (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:4-5</span>, 12)。

<!-- p89 -->
- Spiritual maturity - both should be tested and proven before appointment (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:6</span>, 10)。

<!-- p90 -->
- Reputation - both should have a good reputation inside and outside the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:7</span>)।

<!-- p91 -->
- Faith and integrity - both should hold the faith with a clear conscience and live out their faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:9</span>)।

<!-- p92 -->
### Illustrative frameworks

<!-- p93 -->
Based on what we have learned so far about the rules and structures for leadership, let us look at two examples of church organization found in the New Testament: Jerusalem and Antioch।

<!-- p94 -->
### The church in Jerusalem

<!-- p95 -->
The church in Jerusalem was led primarily by the apostles, who focused on teaching, prayer, and spiritual care (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:42</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:4</span>)। As the community grew, the need for administrative structure became clear, especially to address practical issues such as the care of widows and the distribution of resources। This led to the appointment of **deacons** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-7</span>)। Chosen on the basis of spiritual maturity and good reputation, these individuals handled practical ministries so that the apostles could concentrate on their spiritual responsibilities। Over time, **elders** also emerged as leaders in the church in Jerusalem। For example, during the Jerusalem Council (Acts 15), both the apostles and the elders participated in decision-making, indicating a shared leadership structure।

<!-- p96 -->
### The church in Antioch

<!-- p97 -->
The leadership of the church in Antioch was more decentralized and diverse than that of Jerusalem। It was led by a group of **prophets and teachers**, including Barnabas, Saul (Paul), Simeon, Lucius, and Manaen (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 13:1</span>)। The church in Antioch was also much more focused on reaching Gentiles, whereas the church in Jerusalem reflected its Jewish roots more strongly। Although the New Testament does not explicitly mention deacons in Antioch, given the growing size of the church and its participation in sending relief to Jerusalem, it is likely that practical roles existed (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29-30" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 11:29-30</span>)। The church in Antioch became a model for the appointment of missionaries, where the leaders prayed, fasted, and laid hands on Paul and Barnabas to send them out (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 13:2-3</span>)। This illustrates a structure that balances spiritual care (prophets and teachers) with missional outreach and practical support।

<!-- p98 -->
Unlike Paul's rebukes to the Corinthians and Galatians, the Scriptures do not criticize the leadership structures of Jerusalem or Antioch। This suggests that, under the guidance of the Holy Spirit, these churches were free to adapt their gatherings to their unique needs and circumstances within the framework of sound teaching and apostolic doctrine (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:42</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>)।

<!-- p99 -->
Some terms have been used in these sections that we have not yet defined and studied: *apostles, prophets, evangelists*। These gifts are part of the fivefold ministry that Jesus gave to the church।

<!-- p100 -->
### The Fivefold Ministry Gifts

<!-- p101 -->
Jesus established five distinct ministry gifts to equip believers for service and to build up the body of Christ, often referred to as the **Fivefold Ministry Gifts**.

<!-- p102 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p103 -->
> **<u>Ephesians 4:11-13</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And He Himself gave some to be apostles, some prophets, some evangelists, and some pastors and teachers,
>
> for the equipping of the saints for the work of ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ,
>
> till we all come to the unity of the faith and of the knowledge of the Son of God, to a perfect man, to the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ;
>

<!-- p104 -->
</span>

<!-- p105 -->
The five gifts given to equip the saints for the work of ministry are:

<!-- p106 -->
1.  **Apostles** - pioneers and visionaries who lay foundations, plant churches, and advance the kingdom of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:20</span>). Apostles model courage and faith, showing how to step into new frontiers and take spiritual initiative.

<!-- p107 -->
2.  **Prophets** - messengers and guides who proclaim God’s truth, bringing encouragement, edification, and direction (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>). Prophets inspire faith, call others to align with God’s Word, and model dependence on hearing God’s voice.

<!-- p108 -->
3.  **Evangelists** - heralds of the gospel who focus on reaching the lost and mobilize others to action in sharing Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:5-8</span>). Evangelists train disciples to share their faith effectively, model boldness in witness, and stir compassion for the lost.

<!-- p109 -->
4.  **Pastors (shepherds)** - caregivers and nurturers who protect, guide, and provide spiritual oversight for the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>). Pastors teach disciples the importance of community, caring for others, and accountability within the body of Christ.

<!-- p110 -->
5.  **Teachers** - instructors and trainers who clarify doctrine and help believers grow in understanding and applying God’s Word (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 2:15</span>). Teachers equip disciples to study Scripture deeply, apply it in daily life, and teach others effectively.

## Leadership versus Gifts

<!-- p112 -->
A commonly misunderstood aspect of leadership in the church concerns the distinction between leadership authority and responsibility and specific gifts.

<!-- p113 -->
This confusion often arises because of the words we use—such as teacher, pastor, prophet, apostle, evangelist. These terms are gifts. There is no “office” of apostle in the New Testament church. There are people who do the work of an apostle, who have the gift or calling of an apostle. Having the gift of apostle does not confer authority or responsibility.

<!-- p114 -->
I am an accomplished musician; I have been playing the saxophone for more than twenty years. I played through school and college and also played semi-professionally for a few summers while working with an evangelistic team. Despite having the skill, gift, and ability to play the saxophone, I do not have the right to come into your church and stand on the platform with the worship team.

<!-- p115 -->
Likewise, some people are gifted as pastors/shepherds, but that neither makes them “the pastor” of a local church nor “your” pastor. The fivefold ministry gifts do not lead the church; leaders (elders and deacons) do. Often leaders have particular fivefold ministry gifts, but those gifts enhance their leadership; they do not constitute it. Similarly, it is common for people with fivefold gifts who are still developing to minister and to desire leadership, but God is interested first in character, then in gifts. Let us all be mature, able to discern God’s Word, filled with the Spirit, and doing good works.

<!-- p116 -->
Every leader and every gift must first be submitted to being a disciple and a part of the body.

<!-- p117 -->
Jesus gave specific ministry gifts to equip the saints for the work of ministry. These are different from the motivational gifts (discussed later in this chapter) or the manifestation gifts (discussed in the chapter on baptisms). As disciples grow, the fivefold ministry gifts equip them for ministry, while elders and deacons provide oversight to ensure sound doctrine and practice.
