# Chapter 9 - The Church &amp; Our Role in It

<!-- p1 -->
The Church is the way by which God—on the earth—through His people, doing His work—reveals Himself. We are called to be His body; we are His hands and feet to fulfill His work and purpose. Without our efforts, the harvest will remain in the field and will not be gathered.

<!-- p2 -->
## Who Is the Church?

<!-- p3 -->
The Church, ***Ekklesia** (eh-klee-SEE-ah - **G1577**),* is derived from two Greek words:

<!-- p4 -->
- **"Ek"**: "out of."

<!-- p5 -->
- **"Kaleo"**: "to call."

<!-- p6 -->
Taken together, **Ekklesia** literally means "the called-out ones" or "the assembly called out." In ancient Greek city-states, it referred to a gathering of citizens summoned for a specific purpose, such as legislative or judicial meetings. It was a secular term used for an assembly of people.

<!-- p7 -->
In the New Testament, it is used primarily for the community of those who believe in Jesus Christ. It can refer to:

<!-- p8 -->
- **The local church**: A specific gathering of believers in a particular place (for example, the church in Corinth, Philippi, etc.).

<!-- p9 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p10 -->
> **<u>1 Corinthians 1:2</u>** (NKJV)
>
> To the church of God which is at Corinth, to those who are sanctified in Christ Jesus, called to be saints, with all who in every place call on the name of Jesus Christ our Lord, both theirs and ours:
>

<!-- p11 -->
</span>

<!-- p12 -->
- **The universal church**: The collective body of all believers across time and place.

<!-- p13 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p14 -->
> **<u>Matthew 16:18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And I also say to you that you are Peter, and on this rock I will build My church, and the gates of Hades shall not prevail against it.
>

<!-- p15 -->
</span>

<!-- p16 -->
The Church is the people, not the building or place where they meet. The called-out ones: this idea indicates that Christians are called out of the world and set apart for God’s purposes (for example, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:9</span>) for a specific mission and purpose (the Great Commission). Technically, wherever two or more believers gather together (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="18" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 18:20</span>) that is the church. Typically, the church is organized into regular meetings, perhaps with a set place and time, but wherever two or more people meet in Jesus’ name, there the church is!

<!-- p17 -->
The Church was established by Jesus, and it grew through the work of the disciples who followed Him in the first century AD. The church that we have and experience today in all its local congregations throughout the earth is the direct result of the original work of Christ and His apostles. As believers, we should be committed to a local church for fellowship, edification, equipping for service, and doing good works!

<!-- p18 -->
To help us understand what the church is, Scripture gives us powerful images that show what it is designed to be.

<!-- p19 -->
## Understanding the Church

<!-- p20 -->
Several key metaphorical images are used to illustrate the characteristics of the church:

<!-- p21 -->
1.  Body

<!-- p22 -->
2.  Bride

<!-- p23 -->
3.  Temple

<!-- p24 -->
4.  Family

<!-- p25 -->
5.  Flock

<!-- p26 -->
6.  Vine and Branches

<!-- p27 -->
Each metaphor highlights a unique aspect of the church, but together they reveal the church’s identity as a unified and diverse community under the leadership of Christ.

<!-- p28 -->
### Body

<!-- p29 -->
The church is often called the **Body of Christ**, which highlights both its unity and diversity. With Christ as the head, the church functions like a living organism, where every believer plays an essential and often different role. Each member is endowed with unique gifts and contributes to the health and mission of the body according to their particular function. This image illuminates the interdependence of believers, specifically that for the body to function it must be connected, and that our work is collaborative, not competitive. When each part works together, the body grows and flourishes in unity and love (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:12-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:15-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 1:18</span>).

<!-- p30 -->
### Bride

<!-- p31 -->
The church is described as the **Bride of Christ**, portraying an intimate and loving relationship with Jesus. Just as a bride is cherished and prepared for her husband, so the church is sanctified and purified by Christ’s sacrificial love. This metaphor captures the devotion and faithfulness expected of the church, and Christ’s deep affection for his people. This image also points to the final union between Christ and the church, celebrated at the marriage supper of the Lamb, where the church is presented in glory without spot or blemish (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="25-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:25-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 19:7-9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="21" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 21:2</span>).

<!-- p32 -->
### Temple

<!-- p33 -->
The church is called the **Temple of God**, the dwelling place of his Spirit. Believers are described as living stones, being built together on the foundation of the apostles and prophets, with Christ himself as the cornerstone. This holy structure signifies God’s presence among his people and their role as a community of worship and prayer. The temple image underscores the importance of holiness and the church’s shared purpose of glorifying God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 3:16-17</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:19-22</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:4-5</span>).

<!-- p34 -->
### Family

<!-- p35 -->
The church is called the **Family of God**, emphasizing familial unity and care among its members. Through the Spirit of adoption, believers are made children of God and members of his household. This family image highlights the love, belonging, and shared inheritance believers have in Christ. It also reflects the call to treat one another with kindness and humility as brothers and sisters in God’s family (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:19</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:15-17</span>). Furthermore, through the image of family it shows how church members are to mature: that they be taught and nurtured by spiritual mothers and fathers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:1-2</span>), so that we grow from infants who drink the pure milk of the Word to full adults able to eat the solid food of that Word (Hebrews 5).

<!-- p36 -->
### Flock

<!-- p37 -->
The church is also described as a **flock**, sheep under the care of Jesus, the Good Shepherd. This image conveys the church’s dependence on Christ for guidance, provision, and protection. It also underscores the responsibility of church leaders to shepherd God’s flock faithfully as under-shepherds, reflecting the example of Christ’s love and care. The flock image speaks of trust, obedience, and the close relationship between the Great Shepherd and his sheep (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:14-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:2</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>).

<!-- p38 -->
### Vine and Branches

<!-- p39 -->
The church is also described as the **Vine and the Branches**, which depicts the vital connection between believers and Christ. Jesus is the true vine, and believers are the branches who must abide in him to bear fruit. This metaphor emphasizes dependence on Christ for spiritual vitality, growth, and fruitfulness. Apart from him, believers can do nothing, but by remaining in him they glorify God and fulfill their purpose. Jesus will prune so that he may bring forth more fruit in our lives (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:1-5</span>).
