# Chapter 8 - The Holy Spirit-Comforter, Guide, and Power

<!-- p1 -->
The Holy Spirit is not a new spirit, but is God’s and has been with God from the very beginning. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 1:2</span>, during creation, the Spirit of God was hovering over the waters. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="NEH" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Nehemiah 9:20</span>, the Scriptures say that it was the Spirit who taught the people in the wilderness. It was by that same Holy Spirit that God anointed the kings, judges, and prophets in the Old Testament. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JOL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Joel 2:28-29</span> there is the promise of the Spirit that He would be poured out upon all humanity. It was that same Holy Spirit by whom God raised Jesus from the dead, and He now dwells in us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:11</span>). There is only one Holy Spirit, and He has always remained the same. The way He has worked with people has appeared different at different times, but His heart has never changed.

Jesus gave His disciples, and all of us, a powerful promise of the Holy Spirit.

The disciples were to wait for the promised outpouring of the Spirit, and this was to empower them to be witnesses to the ends of the earth. The Holy Spirit is an integral part of God’s plan for humanity, and is essential for us as believers to fulfill our mission and for the world.

## Getting to Know the Holy Spirit

<!-- p22 -->
The Holy Spirit, first and foremost, is from God and belongs to God. Scripture calls the Spirit the Spirit of God, or the Spirit of the Lord, or the Spirit of Christ; though these highlight different aspects, they are all the same. Being fully God, the Holy Spirit is both eternal and holy (sinless, blameless, pure, not impure). Some translations call the Holy Spirit the Holy Ghost.

<!-- p23 -->
In Greek the word for ‘Spirit’ is **Pneuma** (**NYOO-mah** - **G4151**); it means breath or wind. In Hebrew the word for Spirit has the same meaning. Breath is important because we need it continually to live. It was God’s breath that He breathed into Adam to give him life; likewise it was Jesus’ breath that He released one last time before dying on the cross. Without breath there is no life. Like wind, breath can be felt and experienced, but it is not as easily seen as other ‘elements’ such as water, fire, and earth.

<!-- p24 -->
There are also a few other fundamental descriptors used to describe the Holy Spirit. Each of these is used together with ‘of the Spirit’, which means these qualities are essential to defining the Spirit. For example, the Spirit of truth or the Spirit of life.

<!-- p25 -->
Of the Spirit:

<!-- p26 -->
- **Truth** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:17</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span> points to the Spirit’s role in guiding believers into all truth, revealing God’s will and the reality of Christ.

<!-- p27 -->
- **Life** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:2</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:8</span> highlights the Spirit’s role in imparting spiritual and eternal life to believers, freeing them from the law of sin and death.

<!-- p28 -->
- **Grace** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:29</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ZEC" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Zechariah 12:10</span> depicts the Spirit’s work in bestowing God’s favor, forgiveness, and unmerited kindness to humanity.

<!-- p29 -->
- **Glory** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 4:14</span> indicates the Spirit’s role in manifesting and making evident the glory of God, especially in times of suffering and persecution.

<!-- p30 -->
- **Adoption** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:15</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 4:5</span> points to the Spirit’s work in making believers children of God, granting them the status of sons and daughters.

<!-- p31 -->
- **Holiness** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 1:4</span> refers to the sanctifying work of the Spirit, who sets believers apart for God’s purposes.

<!-- p32 -->
- **Power** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 1:7</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span> describes the empowering of believers by the Spirit for boldness, service, and the ability to live out their faith effectively.

<!-- p33 -->
In all these attributes the Holy Spirit is also rightly known as the Helper or Comforter. Jesus calls the Holy Spirit the coming Helper; he uses the Greek word ***Paraklētos** (*pah-RAH-klay-tos - **G3875**), which means advocate, helper, called alongside.

<!-- p34 -->
An advocate speaks on behalf of another, like a defense attorney. They intercede and assist the person entrusted to their care.

<!-- p35 -->
A helper helps. They aid in empowering the work, but their primary task is to assist. A helper is not solely responsible to do all the work, but stands alongside to help bring it to completion.

<!-- p36 -->
In the ministry of the Holy Spirit there are many different purposes and roles. His work is inward (in the hearts and lives of believers), upward (in service to God and in acknowledging Him), and outward (toward our mission field, the world).

### Inward-The Holy Spirit connects us to Jesus
