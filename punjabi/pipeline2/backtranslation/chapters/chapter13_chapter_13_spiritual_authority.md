# Chapter 13 - Spiritual Authority

<!-- p1 -->
Jesus is the ultimate and perfect source of spiritual authority. As the victorious Christ, He has ultimate authority over all things in heaven and on earth.

<!-- p2 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p3 -->
> **<u>Matthew 28:18-20</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And Jesus came and spoke to them, saying, "All authority has been given to Me in heaven and on earth.
>
> Go therefore and make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit,
>
> teaching them to observe all things that I have commanded you; and behold, I am with you always, even to the end of the age." Amen.
>

<!-- p4 -->
</span>

<!-- p5 -->
In this chapter we will study Christ’s authority and power, which authorities and powers He has delegated to us (His church), and how to operate in the authority of the name of Jesus. We will also study angelic and demonic beings and examine their authority, power, and nature.

<!-- p6 -->
Authority means the right to act. Power is the ability by which it is carried out. Both are important in spiritual life. Authority is different from power. Let us look at the table below that compares authority &amp; power and consider some examples.

<!-- p7 -->
[See Table 7](../tables/table7.md)

<!-- p8 -->
Here are some examples that help to understand the difference between power and authority.

<!-- p9 -->
Example 1: At work, an employee may have the qualifications and skills to sell the company’s assets (power). If he also has the relevant permission (authority) from the company’s owners, then this power is lawful. Without this authority, selling the company’s assets is theft.

<!-- p10 -->
Example 2: A police officer is given special training in weapons and hand-to-hand combat so that he can learn to arrest a criminal and physically subdue him (power). Police officers are given the job responsibility (authority), supported by laws, to enforce criminal statutes.

## The Source of Jesus' Authority

<!-- p74 -->
Now that we have fully seen what authority is, we need to know the source of that authority. Jesus' authority is both divine and delegated. It originates from God the Father, is affirmed through His death and resurrection, and functions in complete harmony with the Father's will. Though He is perfect, His authority respects human free will, reflecting God's love rather than coercive control.

<!-- p75 -->
Jesus' authority is rooted in His divine identity as the Son of God, and it is bestowed by the Father. The Father has entrusted all authority to Christ, including judgment and rule over heaven and earth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 11:27</span>). Jesus' authority reflects the unity and love within the Trinity, where the Son perfectly fulfills the Father's plan (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="35" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:35</span>).

<!-- p76 -->
Although as the Son of God Jesus had inherent authority, His victory over sin, death, and hell was fully established through His death and resurrection. Christ's obedience in His sacrifice led to His glorification (being seated at the right hand of the Father), which secured His authority over all creation. By triumphing over death, Christ gained complete dominion over life and eternity.

## The practice of laying on of hands

<!-- p116 -->
The laying on of hands is an important biblical practice that has been used in both the Old Testament and the New Testament as a symbol of blessing, commissioning, healing, and the impartation of spiritual authority or gifts. It is not merely a physical act—it is a symbol of spiritual connection and transfer under the direction of God’s authority.

<!-- p117 -->
In the Old Testament, the laying on of hands was often associated with blessing and dedication. For example, Jacob laid his hands on his grandsons, Ephraim and Manasseh, and blessed them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="48" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 48:14</span>). Similarly, Moses laid hands on Joshua, appointing him as his successor, imparting leadership authority and wisdom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="NUM" data-chapter="27" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Numbers 27:18-23</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="DEU" data-chapter="34" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Deuteronomy 34:9</span>). This act publicly signaled God’s choice and empowerment for the task ahead.

<!-- p118 -->
In the New Testament, the laying on of hands is closely connected with the ministry of Jesus and the early Church. Jesus regularly laid hands on the sick and healed them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 6:5</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 4:40</span>), demonstrating both compassion and authority over sickness. The apostles continued this practice, for the impartation of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:17</span>), the impartation of spiritual gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 4:14</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 1:6</span>), and to bless believers. It also played a role in appointing leaders and missionaries for ministry (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 13:3</span>).

<!-- p119 -->
The laying on of hands symbolizes faith and obedience to God’s word. It is not a mechanical ritual, but a spiritual practice rooted in dependence on the Holy Spirit. For example, in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:6</span>, the apostles laid hands on those chosen to serve as deacons, dedicating them to God’s service and publicly recognizing their calling. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 19:6</span>, Paul laid hands on believers, and they received the Holy Spirit, demonstrating its role in spiritual empowerment.

<!-- p120 -->
However, the Bible also gives warnings about this practice:

<!-- p121 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p122 -->
> **<u>1 Timothy 5:22</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Do not lay hands on anyone hastily, nor share in other people’s sins; keep yourself pure.
>

<!-- p123 -->
</span>

<!-- p124 -->
This indicates how important discernment and spiritual maturity are when appointing individuals to roles of leadership. As we studied in the chapter about the church, leaders must be tested and approved so as to avoid elevating unqualified or immature believers who could harm the body of Christ.

<!-- p125 -->
Some additional guidelines regarding the laying on of hands:

<!-- p126 -->
1.  Context-appropriate - the person on whom hands are being laid should be a willing participant.

<!-- p127 -->
2.  Physically appropriate - the laying on of hands does not involve sexual or erotic touching. In practice, placing a hand on the shoulder, hand, or forehead (with permission) is generally accepted in the Western world.

<!-- p128 -->
3.  Culturally appropriate - in some cultures it is not considered appropriate for men and women to touch one another unless they are related by blood or marriage.

<!-- p129 -->
The laying on of hands remains a powerful expression of faith and obedience in the church today. It is a means of blessing, healing, ordination, and empowerment, always pointing to Christ’s authority and the work of the Holy Spirit. Whether praying for the sick, sending missionaries, or appointing leaders, it reminds us that believers do not act by their own strength but operate under the authority of Jesus Christ and in agreement with his purposes.

<!-- p130 -->
Now that we have studied the authority of Jesus and the authority of the believer, we will consider other beings of the spiritual realm, angels and demons.

<!-- p131 -->
## Angels and Demons

<!-- p132 -->
### Angels

<!-- p133 -->
Angels are an important part of God’s creation, serving as his messengers and agents to accomplish his divine purposes. The Bible reveals a spiritual realm in which these beings dwell—powerful, intelligent, and obedient to God’s commands. Although angels often remain unseen, their presence and actions affect human history and spiritual warfare. Understanding their roles, classifications, and activities gives us deeper insight into God’s plan and the unseen realities that surround us.

<!-- p134 -->
The word “angel” itself means “messenger,” highlighting their primary function. Angels act in obedience to God’s will—whether delivering messages, providing protection, executing judgment, or engaging in spiritual battles. They are not objects of worship but serve as examples of obedience and reverence, always pointing to the glory of God. This chapter examines the nature, classifications, and roles of angels, shedding light on their significance in Scripture and in our lives today.

<!-- p135 -->
In the Scriptures, angels are seen as **messengers**, **servants**, and **warriors**. Angels often act as messengers, delivering important announcements, warnings, and encouragement. They reveal God’s plans and provide guidance in times of need (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26-38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 1:26-38</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 2:13</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 1:1</span>). Angels actively participate in the protection, strengthening, and deliverance of believers. They minister to God’s people in times of crisis and provide supernatural help (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="91" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 91:11</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 12:7-11</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 4:11</span>). Angels are involved in spiritual warfare, fighting against demonic forces to advance the kingdom of God and protect believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="DAN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Daniel 10:13</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:7-9</span>).

<!-- p136 -->
#### The Nature of Angels

<!-- p137 -->
Angels are created beings, brought into existence by God before the foundation of the world. They are distinct from humans, with unique characteristics that equip them for their heavenly roles. The Bible teaches that angels are spiritual beings, not bound by physical limitations, yet they can take on visible form when interacting with humans.

<!-- p138 -->
Angels are described as immortal, meaning they neither die nor age. They are innumerable, filling the heavenly realms with vast hosts. Although masculine pronouns are often used for them, Scripture clarifies that angels are without sex and neither marry nor produce offspring. These qualities highlight their purpose as servants of God, wholly devoted to his will and commands. Angels possess distinctive qualities that reflect their divine purpose. They are obedient, reverent, intelligent, and powerful. These traits enable them to carry out God’s will effectively and faithfully.

[See Table 8](../tables/table8.md)

<!-- p140 -->
Although the Bible highlights many roles and titles for angels, it does not provide a comprehensive classification system. Even so, some angels are identified by specific roles or tasks:

<!-- p141 -->
- **Angel of the Lord** - Often considered a pre-incarnate manifestation of Christ, appearing with divine authority. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="23" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 23:20-23</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JDG" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Judges 6:11-24</span>*)

<!-- p142 -->
- **Archangels** - High-ranking angels, such as Michael, who leads armies, and Gabriel, who delivers divine messages. (*Jude 9; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:7</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 1:19-26</span>*)

<!-- p143 -->
- **Cherubim** - Guardians of God's glory and presence, as in Eden and over the Ark of the Covenant. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 3:24</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 25:22</span>*)

<!-- p144 -->
- **Seraphim** - Worshipers of God, continually proclaiming His holiness. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 6:1-6</span>*)

<!-- p145 -->
- **Elect angels** - Faithful angels who resisted Satan's rebellion and remained loyal to God. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:21</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 25:41</span>*)

<!-- p146 -->
This classification highlights the diversity and order of the angelic realm, yet their primary focus always remains on fulfilling God's purposes.

<!-- p147 -->
#### Human interaction with angels

<!-- p148 -->
In Scripture, humans often encounter angels, but we are never instructed to command them or pray to them. Instead, angels act according to God's direction. They serve believers as **servants of God** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 1:14</span>). Their interactions include protection, guidance, and deliverance, and they always point us back to God's power and sovereignty.

<!-- p149 -->
- **Warnings about the worship of angels**: Scripture forbids worshiping angels or seeking encounters with them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:18</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 22:8-9</span>).

<!-- p150 -->
- **Pray to God, not angels**: Believers should pray to God, who sends angels according to His will (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="91" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 91:11</span>).

<!-- p151 -->
- **Encouragement and trust**: Angels remind us of God's care, but our faith should rest entirely in God, not in angels.

<!-- p152 -->
Angels are wondrous beings created to serve God and minister to His people. Though their work is often unseen, Scripture gives glimpses of their activity, revealing their faithfulness, power, and reverence. Angels remind us of God's provision and protection, but they always point to His glory. As believers, we are called to trust in God's care, knowing that angels, as His servants, work to accomplish His divine purposes.

<!-- p153 -->
### Evil spirits

<!-- p154 -->
Just as studying angels is essential to understanding the spiritual realm, so too is the study of evil spirits necessary. Scripture clearly describes their activity, influence, and defeat through Jesus Christ. Recognizing their role in spiritual warfare helps believers stand firm in faith without fear.

<!-- p155 -->
#### Origin and nature of evil spirits

<!-- p156 -->
Evil spirits are the fallen angels who followed Satan's rebellion against God. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:7-9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 25:41</span>).* Satan, formerly known as Lucifer (“light-bearer”), a high-ranking angel, sought to exalt himself above God and was cast out of heaven along with other rebellious angels. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 14:12-15</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EZK" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ezekiel 28:12-17</span>).* 

<!-- p157 -->
Evil spirits are spirits who do not have physical bodies but can torment and harass humans and animals. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 5:11-13</span>).* They seek to deceive, tempt, oppress, and destroy. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 11:14</span>). Unlike God,* they have limits, and they are not omnipresent (everywhere), omniscient (all-knowing), or omnipotent (all-powerful)*.* Their ultimate defeat is certain, and they will face eternal punishment. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 20:10</span>)*

<!-- p158 -->
Scripture depicts Satan's works and activities as follows:

<!-- p159 -->
- Opposes the work of God *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Thessalonians 2:18</span>)*

<!-- p160 -->
- Deceives the whole world *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 4:4</span>)*

<!-- p161 -->
- Tests and accuses believers *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 22:31</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:10</span>)*

<!-- p162 -->
- Afflicts and harms *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JOB" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Job 1:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 13:16</span>)*

<!-- p163 -->
There are many names and descriptions for Satan, each highlighting a different aspect of his work.

<!-- p164 -->
[See Table 9](../tables/table9.md)

<!-- p165 -->
The Gospels and the Epistles further clarify the work of the enemy. Evil spirits, being bodiless, often do their work through willing and open vessels. God works through people; Satan does the same. This oppression is often marked by torment, self-harm, and destructive behavior *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 8:27-29</span>).* Their attacks produce fear, temptation, and despair *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 12:7</span>).* The most common source of their power (not authority) is through the influence of lies and emotional strongholds in a person's life *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 10:3-5</span>).*

<!-- p166 -->
It is important to note that not every lie a person believes, every weakness in their body, or every stronghold in their life is a direct result of demonic influence, but these wounded areas are places where the enemy can take advantage of us and keep us in discouragement and oppression.

<!-- p167 -->
#### Jesus' authority over the works of the enemy

<!-- p168 -->
Do not be afraid; Jesus has full and complete authority over the works of the enemy. During his ministry Jesus displayed his power over evil spirits *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 1:34</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 5:1-20</span>).* The works of the enemy and Satan himself were defeated through Jesus' death and resurrection *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:15</span>).* 

<!-- p169 -->
As believers we have been given authority by Jesus to cast out evil spirits in his name:

<!-- p170 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p171 -->
> **<u>Mark 16:17</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And these signs will follow those who believe: In My name they will cast out demons; they will speak with new tongues;
>

<!-- p172 -->
</span>

<!-- p173 -->
Furthermore, as heirs of salvation we have the authority to submit ourselves to God, resist the devil, and he will flee from us! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 4:7</span>).

<!-- p174 -->
The pattern shown in Scripture for dealing with an evil or unclean spirit is to take authority over it in Jesus' name and command it to go. If we ask, the Holy Spirit promises to give us wisdom and discernment! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:5</span>). We do not need to make it a show, or a movie-like drama, or to converse with them; that is a tactic of Satan to distract us. Satan's power is maintained and sustained only through the lies he can get us to believe.

<!-- p175 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="18" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p176 -->
> **<u>Matthew 18:18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> "Assuredly, I say to you, whatever you bind on earth will be bound in heaven, and whatever you loose on earth will be loosed in heaven.
>

<!-- p177 -->
</span>

<!-- p178 -->
We address them in Jesus' name and command them to leave.

<!-- p179 -->
There are two major warnings in Scripture when dealing with demonic matters:

<!-- p180 -->
1.  **Only those who have a relationship with Jesus have authority over demonic powers**. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 19:11-20</span> describes the sons of a Jewish chief priest named Sceva. They attempted to intervene in the life of a man tormented and afflicted by a demon, but the evil spirit refused to leave because they had no relationship with Jesus. The tormented man leaped on them, stripped off their clothes, and beat them. Just as you should not impersonate a police officer, **do not imitate someone who has a relationship with Jesus; instead, build your own relationship with Jesus**!

<!-- p181 -->
2.  **When an evil spirit goes out, it goes away for a time and will try to return**. If it finds the place where it used to dwell empty, it will come back in an even worse state. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="43-45" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 12:43-45</span>. When we minister to someone tormented by the devil, that person must fill the empty space in their life that the evil spirit had occupied. This means at the very least establishing a personal relationship with Jesus and strengthening it by obeying the Holy Spirit. Often an evil or unclean spirit gains the right to operate in someone's life through choices made according to their beliefs. Anyone who is delivered from the devil must change that old belief system and be transformed by the renewing of their mind (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:1-2</span>).

<!-- p182 -->
We serve an amazing and powerful God, and over the defeated enemy we are 'more than conquerors' (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="37" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:37</span>) over the defeated enemy. We have no need to fear our defeated enemy, because Jesus has given us His authority so that we can crush his head! Satan’s time is short, and in the end he will be judged, along with every principality and power on earth that rejects the Lordship of Christ.
