# Chapter 7 - Jesus-Our Faith's Foundation

<!-- p1 -->
Building on our study of acknowledging Jesus as the Messiah, in this chapter we will continue to look further at the life, person, and work of Jesus. We will see that Jesus is the mediator of a new and better covenant - and that he perfectly fulfills the roles of prophet, priest, and king. It is in this new covenant that we believers have good standing before God and move from a works-based covenant to a trust-based (faith-based) covenant. Finally, to conclude our study on Jesus, we will read and study the Apostles' Creed.

<!-- p2 -->
## The Mediator of the New Covenant

<!-- p3 -->
The idea of a mediator is that an intermediary person becomes a bridge between two parties and facilitates a relationship or agreement. In the story of the Exodus, Moses is the first mediator who mediates the covenant God made with his people. Moses received the Law, offered sacrifices (priest), called God's people to obedience (prophet), and judged them (judge/king). After Moses, throughout the rest of the Old Covenant, this mediating role was carried out through the office of the prophets, the Levitical priesthood, and first the judges and later the kings of the nation.

<!-- p4 -->
The roles of mediators in the Old Covenant:

<!-- p5 -->
1.  **Prophet:** Prophets were God's messengers, sent to speak his heart to the people and to call them back when they strayed. For example, Moses was a prophet who fulfilled a mediating role in the Old Covenant by receiving the Law from God and teaching it to Israel. The prophets also foretold the coming of the Messiah and the ultimate redemption of humanity. Other notable examples of prophets are: Isaiah, Jeremiah, Elijah, Elisha, Samuel.

<!-- p6 -->
2.  **Levitical Priesthood:** Priests served as mediators by offering sacrifices for the atonement of sins and by interceding before God for the people. The high priest, in particular, entered the Holy of Holies on the Day of Atonement and sprinkled blood on the mercy seat, symbolizing reconciliation with God. The ministry of the priest is to intercede for the people.

<!-- p7 -->
3.  **Judge/King:** Judges, and later kings, were responsible to rule over God's people, enforce his laws, render justice among the people, deliver them from enemies, and lead in righteousness. The Book of Judges tells the stories of these leaders—people like Deborah, Samson, and Gideon—whom God raised up to save and guide Israel. Later there was a line of kings in Israel—many evil, some good (see 1st and 2nd Kings, 1st and 2nd Samuel, and 1<sup>st</sup> and 2<sup>nd</sup> Chronicles). Some well-known examples are Saul, David, and Solomon.

<!-- p8 -->
It is noteworthy that all the mediators of the Old Covenant were fully human and not divine. Being human, they were capable of sin and at times fell under sin. Some mediators even sinned themselves and pushed the people away from God and toward impure worship: false prophets, false and corrupt priests, and wicked kings.

### Christ, the Mediator of the New Covenant

<!-- p10 -->
Jesus' death, resurrection, and ascension fulfill the Old Covenant and establish the New Covenant.

<!-- p11 -->
The New Covenant is a transformative relationship between God and his people, established through the life, death, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. Unlike the Old Covenant, which was centered on external laws, the New Covenant emphasizes inner transformation and grace. It was inaugurated at the Last Supper, where Jesus declared that his blood would establish this covenant, replacing the Old Covenant's repeated animal sacrifices with his once-for-all atonement (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 22:20</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:10</span>). Through this sacrifice, the New Covenant provides full forgiveness and cleansing from sin, meeting the need for a complete and permanent reconciliation with God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 31:34</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:12-18</span>).

<!-- p12 -->
One of the central features of the New Covenant is its internal nature, for God writes his law on the hearts of his people and, through the Holy Spirit, brings about a personal relationship with himself (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 31:33</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 3:3</span>). This transformation enables believers to live out God's will not merely by following external commands, but by the empowerment of the Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:1-4</span>). The New Covenant also grants direct access to God, removing the need for an earthly priesthood. Jesus is the eternal High Priest, and believers are called a royal priesthood, who can approach God with boldness through Christ's sacrifice (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 7:24-25</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:19-20</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:9</span>).

<!-- p13 -->
Unlike the Old Covenant, which was limited to Israel alone, the New Covenant is universal and is for everyone who believes in Christ, regardless of race or background (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 3:28</span>). This covenant is entirely based on grace, freely offering salvation and forgiveness through faith in Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>). It does not stand on human effort, but on the empowerment of the Holy Spirit—who transforms and enables believers to live obediently and to reflect God's character—(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EZK" data-chapter="36" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ezekiel 36:26-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:9-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-23</span>). Furthermore, the New Covenant is everlasting, surpassing the temporary and conditional nature of the Old Covenant (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="32" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 32:40</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:13</span>). It represents the ultimate fulfillment of God's redemptive plan, reconciling humanity to himself and enabling believers to live as his people under his reign forever.

Through his atoning death and resurrection, Christ inaugurates this New Covenant, replacing the system of old sacrifices and external laws with a covenant written on the hearts of believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 31:31-34</span>). He stands between God and humanity, not only interceding on behalf of his people, but also providing the means of reconciliation through his own blood (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:12</span>). Unlike the mediators of the Old Covenant, Jesus is sinless, eternal, and fully able to meet the divine requirements of justice and mercy, guaranteeing a lasting relationship between God and believers.
