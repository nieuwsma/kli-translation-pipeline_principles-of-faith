# Hearing God's Voice

<!-- p1 -->
The following guest appendix by Rev. Larry Low ([<u>www.LarryLow.com</u>](http://www.larrylow.com)) complements our exploration of the role of the Holy Spirit, offering practical insights about hearing God's voice. The following guest contribution is used with permission.

<!-- p2 -->
One morning during prayer, I asked **God** this question: “What is the most important thing I can teach Your people for the times that are coming toward us?” And He answered me very quickly, and when He said: “**Teach them to hear My voice.**” I felt this very urgent.

<!-- p3 -->
When the Lord gives me direction like this, I always pick up a pad and pencil and begin to write what little I know, or what I think I know, and the Scriptures that support it. I like to do word studies on the subjects the Lord is leading me to teach. I love the anointing to preach, but I truly love to teach the Word. It is an exciting journey to discover the things that have always been there but have been overlooked.

<!-- p4 -->
David certainly must have longed to unfold God’s truths, for he prayed in Psalm 119:18: “Open my eyes so that I may behold wondrous things out of Your law.”

<!-- p5 -->
That is what I want to see-wondrous things from His Word.

<!-- p6 -->
As I was studying and preparing to teach a second message about hearing and discerning God’s voice, He said this to my heart:

<!-- p7 -->
“*Teach them to hear My voice,  
teach them to honor and obey.*

<!-- p8 -->
*Yes, teach them to hear My voice and I will always lead them away:  
away from the troubles that could destroy them,*

<!-- p9 -->
*away from the trials they are in,  
away to that place of peace, love, and joy, where few have ever dwelt.*

<!-- p10 -->
*away to a place of refreshing,  
to a place of divine ecstasy.*

<!-- p11 -->
*Oh yes, teach them to hear My voice son  
and these people will be free forever*.”

<!-- p12 -->
05/27/00 9:10 A.M.

<!-- p13 -->
The eleven points below will help you understand how the Lord will communicate with you:

<!-- p14 -->
1.  <u>Hearing God’s voice is not an audible sound but a KNOWING in your heart.</u>

<!-- p15 -->
There was a time when His word came so clear and firm that it seemed as if it were audible. I answered Him out loud, it was that real. But there was no one there but me and the Lord, so I cannot say for sure whether He spoke to me in the same way we speak to each other.

<!-- p16 -->
Once, when Jesus was being baptized in the Jordan River, the Father said: “This is My Son, in whom I am well pleased,” but some who were present there said it sounded like thunder. So I suppose He could have spoken to me audibly as well, but for now, I want you to understand that when I say that God spoke to me, it is more a KNOWING in my heart than a voice in my ear.

<!-- p17 -->
2.  <u>This KNOWING is not formed by the reasoning of your mind; rather it is a complete thought or understanding.</u>

<!-- p18 -->
The Lord has never spoken anything to me that would put me in confusion. He has never said anything to me that left me wondering ‘why.’ It is always a KNOWING with complete UNDERSTANDING. God is not the author of confusion (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:33</span>).

<!-- p19 -->
3.  <u>When He speaks, He speaks in complete thoughts.</u>

<!-- p20 -->
He never leaves us to fill in the blanks on our own. In the blink of an eye, God can fill your heart with so much knowledge that you could preach for a lifetime. One touch of God can fill you with a fire that will keep you burning forever. He is a perfect communicator.

<!-- p21 -->
4.  <u>When God speaks to us, He never drives. He leads.</u>

<!-- p22 -->
He is not a harsh taskmaster; He is a shepherd. Herdsmen drive, but shepherds lead. Jesus is the great Shepherd of the sheep, and we are the sheep of His pasture.

<!-- p23 -->
5.  <u>When He speaks to His children, He never condemns; He convicts.</u>

<!-- p24 -->
His children are those who are born again (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:3</span>, 5) through the confession of their faith in the shed blood of **Jesus** and in His finished work on Calvary. Who would want to listen to someone who is always condemning us for our mistakes? I believe that many of **God’s** children fear that **God** will speak to them, because they have been told that **God** will condemn them for this or that. See <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 3:19-20</span>. What does it say about who condemns or accuses us? It is our soul or heart that condemns the child of **God**, but **God the Holy Spirit** knows everything and is greater than our hearts. He is there as our Comforter. Read the following Scriptures, and they will help you understand that the Holy Spirit convicts only sinners. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33-34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:33-34</span> teaches us that nothing can come between us and the love of **God**. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:16</span>, 26 teaches us that the Holy Spirit is our Comforter, not our accuser; our Counselor, not our punisher; our Intercessor and Helper, not our hinderer. Also read <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 9:31</span> and 15:28 to see how the Holy Spirit (the voice of **God**) helps, comforts, and strengthens us.

<!-- p25 -->
6.  <u>The voice of God can be tested so that you may know it is God, not your thoughts or the voice of familiar spirits.</u>

<!-- p26 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 4:1-3</span> instructs us to “test every spirit” so that we can make sure it is from God. When you feel that you are hearing from God, you have the right and responsibility to ask that voice one question (this is testing or proving the spirit.) The question to ask is: “Did Jesus Christ come in the flesh?” The one thing familiar spirits will never acknowledge is that Jesus came to earth in the flesh. He was fully man and fully God. No false spirit or religion will acknowledge that Jesus came to this earth in the flesh and that He has eternally redeemed humanity from all their sins. Test the voice you hear. You should also know that God will never lead you outside His written Word, the Bible. If you are hearing something that is not supported in the Bible, you are not hearing from God. Cast it away! Test the voice you hear to see whether it is from God.

<!-- p27 -->
7.  <u>The voice of God always glorifies Jesus Christ.</u>

<!-- p28 -->
The Holy Spirit is the voice of heaven. He is here on earth to work with us, and Jesus said that He will always speak what He hears, and He will always glorify Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13-14</span>). He is not here to glorify your ministry, your works, or church programs. All these things are good, and many of them are necessary for the work we do, but the Holy Spirit will speak and glorify the person of Jesus. If the voice you hear is glorifying you, then you are not hearing from God. Being glorified and being encouraged and strengthened are different things. The Holy Spirit will encourage and strengthen you every time He speaks to you, even when He is correcting you (guiding) with the Word. But glorying is when you believe that you are the great one. Without Jesus you are nobody—He is the one who is great, not us.

<!-- p29 -->
8.  <u>The voice of God will always reveal something to us.</u>

<!-- p30 -->
See <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:14</span>. He always takes what belongs to **Jesus** and reveals it to us. Thinking about the word “reveal,” the Amplified adds these words: declare, disclose, and transmit. **God** always discloses something to us after He declares it to us, and then through your faith, He transmits it to us. Also study <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 2:10</span> and 12.

<!-- p31 -->
9.  <u>The voice of God will always bring peace.</u>

<!-- p32 -->
Read <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:26</span> and 27, and <span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 3:14</span> and 15. His voice never confuses us. It is the voice that brings perfect peace. Inner peace exists because there is a certainty that gives us confidence before God, which turns into perfect peace. If the voice you are hearing is confusing you, then you are not hearing from God—turn it off. Jesus has promised us peace, not as the world gives, but as He gives to us through the comforting words and constant indwelling of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:27</span>).

<!-- p33 -->
10. <u>The voice of God always brings truth.</u>

<!-- p34 -->
Read <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:17</span>, 26; 16:13. In fact, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span> calls the Holy Spirit: “the Spirit of truth.” God cannot lie! He is truth and therefore will speak only truth. I have heard people say: “I can’t believe that what I’m hearing is from God. It’s too good to be true.” And perhaps it does seem too good to be true; therefore you must test the spirit with the Word of God to be sure it is true. Some people cannot accept it because what they are hearing is always positive, and they have been taught that God condemns us for wrongdoing. God will tell only the truth, and it will always be good news. The sad truth is that most people hear only bad news from the voice they are listening to and never expect good news, so when good news comes, they question it as possibly untrue. **Truth is always good news!** It is the **truth** that sets us free, so how could the **truth** be anything other than good news?

<!-- p35 -->
11. <u>The voice of **God** always takes us forward and upward.</u>

<!-- p36 -->
Read Deuteronomy 28:1-2, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="30" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 30:21</span>, and <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span>

<!-- p37 -->
God never takes you down! He never condemns you; He only lifts you up and strengthens you. When God speaks to you, He speaks from the perspective that everything the Word says about you has already been accomplished and accepted by you. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="33" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 33:3</span> says: “Call to Me, and I will answer you, and show you great and mighty things which you do not know.” Does that sound like putting you down or like darkness and despair? There is no darkness or despair in God, therefore He can never tell you such dark or gloomy things. For darkness and despair you will have to stick with the world’s news agencies.

<!-- p38 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="29" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p39 -->
> **<u>Jeremiah 29:11</u>** (NKJV)
>
> For I know the thoughts that I think toward you, says the Lord, thoughts of peace and not of evil, to give you a future and a hope.

<!-- p40 -->
</span>

<!-- p41 -->
Now, if God thinks good thoughts of peace, a future, and hope about us, then why would He speak any differently? The answer is, He wouldn’t, and He doesn’t! He only leads us upward and forward with great hope so that He can give us a hopeful future.

<!-- p42 -->
I hope this encourages you to “call on Him” and learn how **to listen to His voice**. He has great things to tell you, but before all His good things can do you good, He needs your attentively listening ear.
