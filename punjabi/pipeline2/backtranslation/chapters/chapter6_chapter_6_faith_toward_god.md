# Chapter 6 - Faith Toward God

<!-- p1 -->
Faith is a foundational concept in Christianity. In Christianity, “faith” is such a commonly used word that at times its meaning gets altered by subtle misconceptions and misunderstandings. But it is not something you can hold with your hands—it is formed in your heart as you learn to trust God. Faith is persuasion.

<!-- p2 -->
The author of Hebrews teaches us that we should have “faith toward God.”

### Definition of Faith

<!-- p10 -->
Faith is an aspect of Christianity that is often misunderstood and misrepresented. As the saying goes, “There’s a ditch on both sides of the road”! Because in modern Christianity the word “faith” is used so commonly without proper explanation, it can take on wrong definitions amplified by misconceptions. Faith is often portrayed either as a vague blanket statement of belief, or as an almost mystical power or “magic”—something you possess in a fixed, measurable, and limited quantity.

<!-- p11 -->
#### What Faith Is Not

<!-- p12 -->
Faith wrongly presented in an <u>abstract</u> way lacks any specific conviction or persuasion. With a little probing this often becomes evident because such a presentation of faith cannot be made specific. If you ask, ‘What do you believe?’ or ‘Why do you believe?’, a person with this understanding often cannot articulate much beyond ‘I believe.’ <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 3:15</span> says that we should be ready to give an answer concerning the hope within us.

<!-- p13 -->
Sometimes people think of faith as a magical power—but Scripture does not teach this. Faith is about a real relationship that stands on trust. In Scripture, faith is much more dynamic and relational—it is rooted in persuasion, trust, and confidence in God’s character and promises. It is not an impersonal force, but a participatory response to God that shapes thoughts, emotions, and actions.

<!-- p14 -->
Why is faith related to persuasion?

<!-- p15 -->
Our beliefs determine how we see and interact with the world around us. Faith shapes our perceptions and motivates our actions. Our minds are built this way—we live the life we believe is possible.

<!-- p16 -->
We act in line with what we see, understand, and consider possible. When we walk down a corridor, our brain recognizes the boundaries of the floor and the wall and automatically tells our feet where to step and what to avoid. This truth applies beyond just physically finding our way.

<!-- p17 -->
Suppose you believe it is impossible for you to become financially independent or to live a healthy and active lifestyle. Then your actions will follow that belief, and you will, in line with your belief, not do—or perhaps not even attempt—what you consider impossible.

<!-- p18 -->
If you believe that God is unloving and punitive in nature, that perception of Him will keep you from walking with Him as His child.

<!-- p19 -->
#### Faith is persuasion

<!-- p20 -->
So, what is faith? Persuasion! The Greek word ***pistis*** means persuasion, or the result of being persuaded. We are to have persuasion toward God. In English we might say faith (persuasion) ‘**in** God’. If you are married, hopefully you have faith ‘**in’** your spouse. You believe that they will do what they say, and you believe that they are sincere and real. This trust should extend beyond the things they have said, generally to them as a person.

<!-- p21 -->
Developing faith, persuasion, and trust in a relationship requires time and experience. Hopefully in life you have learned how to build a trustworthy relationship with another human being. The principles you have learned that help you increase trust in someone you know will likewise help you grow your faith in God. In a relationship, faith grows over time, in experience, with consistency, and through small and large actions that build your confidence about the other person’s character and nature.

<!-- p22 -->
‘Faith towards God’, is relational. Relationships are formed by two parties, not one. The good news about our faith in God is that the responsibility for His side of the relationship is God’s (made possible through Jesus’ atoning death and the outpouring of His Spirit at the new birth), and He also helps us on our side (through the work of the Comforter - the Holy Spirit). God is doing almost all the work; our part (as we will discuss later in this chapter) is to walk *with* Him in obedience.

<!-- p23 -->
### Jesus matures our faith

<!-- p24 -->
Jesus is the author and finisher of our faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 12:2</span>). Our faith is designed to continually grow and expand. We do not arrive at some point where we have enough ‘faith’; instead our faith grows. Think about a deep and personal relationship present in your life. As with any relationship we do not reach some particular milestone where now we have ‘known’ that person. We keep on knowing them, and increase our knowledge about them, and this refines, shapes, and grows our relationship.

<!-- p25 -->
How do we grow our faith in God? By continually experiencing Him through His countless revelations, manifestations, and attributes. Again, think about some deep personal relationship in your life. You increase the depth of the relationship by engaging with them in different ways, different formats, different mediums, different settings. You talk with them, send messages, perhaps write to them. You meet them in private and in groups. You talk about your values, and sometimes you even challenge one another. These are endless ways. Through these many expressions you come to know that person more deeply.

<!-- p26 -->
We all, through Jesus, should know God as our Redeemer. Consider the names of God (*see the appendix),* just because we have known God as Redeemer does not mean that we have yet experienced Him as Deliverer, Healer, Counselor, Lord.

<!-- p27 -->
If you are at a place where you have been persuaded (faith) to know God as your Redeemer, then building this relationship will give you such confidence in His character that you can know Him in other ways as well.

<!-- p28 -->
I am blessed that I have many very deep relationships in my life. One such relationship is with my wife. In our relationship, because of the level of trust (faith) we have in one another, my wife can tell me things she will do, which I have not yet seen happen, and I have the confidence to believe her because her character is proven.

<!-- p29 -->
This example shows how our deep relationship with God can be experienced with trust and assurance! Perhaps we have not yet fully experienced Him (in fact we will never fully experience Him in this life), but as we mature in our relationship, we can trust Him beyond the limit of what I personally know, and what I have already seen happen.

<!-- p30 -->
Jesus increases our faith with us through experience. One important way to experience Him, which we can and should do, is His Word.

<!-- p36 -->
When we put *persuasion* in place of the word *faith* then it reads like this:

<!-- p37 -->
*‘So persuasion comes by hearing and hearing by the word of God’*. This truth is naturally self-evident. One big way persuasion comes to someone is that they have been persuaded (convinced), and we are persuaded when someone makes an ‘argument’ - or gives a reasoned and clear explanation.

<!-- p38 -->
There are two Greek words for ‘Word’ in the Bible-**logos** and **rhema**. Understanding the difference shows us how faith grows.

<!-- p39 -->
**Logos**: Generally, used for the **concept, idea, reason, or broad expression of the Word of God**. It encompasses a universal, foundational truth or message and is often used to describe God’s eternal wisdom and Christ Himself as the Living Word.

<!-- p40 -->
**Rhema (G4487)**: emphasizes a **specific, spoken word or utterance**. It typically refers to a particular word or message given at a specific time, especially the spoken or expressed word that applies directly to a person's situation.

<!-- p41 -->
*Logos* is like the entire message of a book, whereas *rhema* is like a specific paragraph or sentence that speaks directly to someone at a particular moment. While *logos* is the collective truth of God, *rhema* is the moment when a part of that truth is spoken to a person in a personal and applied way. Together, they show the fullness of God’s communication: *logos* as the broad, foundational truths of God’s character and will, and *rhema* as the specific, applied word that brings this truth to life in a person’s life and circumstances.

<!-- p42 -->
### An Example of a Rhema Word in Action

<!-- p43 -->
God promises to give light and wisdom to all who ask. When He speaks to us, that utterance is a *rhema* word. Rhema words are from God and serve to confirm, lead, and guide us. They are not a replacement for the *logos* word, nor do they carry the authority of Scripture. *Rhema* words should be evaluated by the same standards as prophecy or the interpretation of tongues (see the chapter on the Holy Spirit for an in-depth study on this topic).

<!-- p44 -->
At many points in my career, I have prayed for divine guidance. A few years ago, I was thinking about starting to look for a new opportunity, and during a time of prayer, I heard God say to me, ‘*Just wait on Me; I have an opportunity for you.’* I was starting to look for a new job because I was not satisfied with my current role and felt discouraged. God’s directive, ‘*Wait on Me’* gave me peace and confidence in an otherwise restless season.

<!-- p45 -->
Three or four months passed; I kept working diligently and faithfully, maintaining a positive attitude. God would remind me, *‘Wait on Me’*, and then His promise was fulfilled. I received a promotion opportunity and a nice increase in salary.

<!-- p46 -->
I could have continued my job search; I could have quit. I could have started fresh somewhere else. No doubt that promotion would have gone to someone else! But because I sought the Lord’s counsel and believed His **rhema word**, I obeyed and received the benefit. God’s word is **living and personal**, and He promises to give us multifaceted and generous wisdom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:5</span>).

<!-- p47 -->
I have dozens, if not hundreds, of times when I prayed and asked God what to do, and He gave me a rhema word. Not every outcome was equally spectacular, but all of them brought a sense of peace and confidence that I was obeying God and that He would make everything right. Those were the words I needed at the time! Many times, when God brings a rhema word, it is from the logos. He brings a portion of Scripture to my remembrance, “*Do not fear, for I am with you!*” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="41" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 41:10</span>), “*By His stripes we are healed”* (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="53" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 53:5</span>), “*Do not grow weary in doing good!*” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:9</span>), and the examples go on and on!

<!-- p48 -->
Receiving rhema words not only strengthens our faith but also deepens our relationship with God, as we trust Him more fully in our everyday decisions. We will continue our study of learning to hear from God in the chapter on the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p49 -->
Our faith is also strengthened by what we see God do, but sometimes what we see in the natural world challenges what we hear from God. Reconciling the inconsistency between what we see and what we hear is a vital ministry of the Holy Spirit, who promises to lead us into all truth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span>).

### A Faith Greater Than What We Can See

<!-- p51 -->
After His resurrection, Jesus appeared to many of His disciples several times. Thomas (one of the Twelve) was not with the other disciples, so he missed the appearance of the risen Savior. Thomas believed in Jesus and trusted his fellow disciples, but he did not accept that Jesus had risen bodily; he insisted that until he personally experienced it, he could not actively believe it.

When Jesus appeared to His disciples and calmed them (I imagine that when someone comes through locked doors and appears among you, it is quite startling), He spoke to Thomas. He did not condemn or accuse Thomas or indict him by calling him part of the evil and adulterous generation that seeks a sign (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 16:4</span>) but revealed Himself to Thomas.

<!-- p60 -->
Jesus goes on to say, ‘*Blessed are those who have not seen and have believed*’. This is not Jesus encouraging “blind or ignorant faith” or rejecting the use of empirical evidence. Rather, He is saying that when we can believe without the need for empirical proof, we are even more blessed. Why? If our faith, our trust, were limited only to what we have empirically seen demonstrated, we could never have any confidence in the work yet to come. We would have great confidence in what has already happened, but we could not walk in God’s peace for our tomorrow and our future.

<!-- p61 -->
Using a relational example, how challenging would my relationship with my wife be if my attitude were constantly ‘<u>I’ll believe it when I see it!</u>’? Instead, a trust-based relationship says, ‘I know you are a trustworthy person.’

### Faith produces obedience and good works

<!-- p80 -->
Our trusting relationship with God is strengthened through obedience, resulting in good works! These works neither save us nor God's love-they are the natural result of a life rooted in Him.

<!-- p81 -->
Scripture shows the natural progression of faith. As we know God and experience His presence, we grow in our faith in Him. This faith moves us to act in ways that align with our faith, leading us to obey His commands. Our obedience produces good works, which bring a harvest of positive and beneficial outcomes.

<!-- p82 -->
Look again at this passage from Romans:

<!-- p83 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

Breaking this down in reverse order:

<!-- p87 -->
1. Hearing the word (rhema) of God produces faith (pistis)

<!-- p88 -->
2. Faith, which is a state of assurance or conviction, will produce the act of believing. Again, think of the person who believes that he can be debt-free and acts on it!

<!-- p90 -->
3.  The action of our faith is manifested in obedience.

<!-- p91 -->
The word for ‘obey’ is **hupakouō** (**hoop-ak-oo'-o**- G5219): **hupakouō** means *to listen under* (as a *subordinate*), that is, *to listen attentively*; by implication *to heed* or *to conform* to a command or authority: - to heed, to be obedient, to obey.

<!-- p92 -->
In a workplace environment, when a boss or supervisor is giving instructions, they are not “talking for the sake of talking,” but are giving directions they want their employees to follow.

<!-- p93 -->

The counselor will teach them the principles of financial freedom and help them create a budget. This counseling will produce (trust / faith) in them regarding what they must do to become debt-free. But trust is only the first step; to obtain the good fruit and blessing of being debt-free, the person must follow the principles learned through disciplined practice.

It is better to properly carry out what we ought to do, rather than having to offer a sacrifice to make up for what we did not do right. Thank God that when we stray from the path, miss the mark (sin), and need to turn back to God, grace is available from God.

<!-- p108 -->
As we build our faith in God, it naturally produces obedience in us. This obedience is for our benefit and produces a good harvest. Returning to my personal example in this chapter, if I had not obeyed the word God spoke to me to *wait for Him*, I would not have experienced that blessing of peace or progress in that season!
