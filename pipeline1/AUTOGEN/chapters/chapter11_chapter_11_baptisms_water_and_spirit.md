# Chapter 11 - Baptisms-Water and Spirit

<!-- p1 -->
Baptism in our modern age, separated from the Jewish roots, is typically only thought of in terms of water baptism, and the Holy Spirit baptism (Pentecost).


<!-- p2 -->
The book of Hebrews was written to a Jewish audience. The overwhelming majority of Christians in our modern age do not have Jewish roots or heritage. Without the Jewish roots and culture many of us do not have the cultural context for things that 1st century messianic Jews (those Jewish believers professing Jesus) would have shared. As we were not raised in the Jewish tradition we don’t typically know much about the feasts, fasts, prayers, washings, rites, and rituals that Jewish believers would know.


<!-- p3 -->
The word for baptisms, or washings, in the Greek is: **Baptismos** (bap-tis-mos’ - **G909**) - The act of dipping or washing. Refers to ceremonial washings or ritual cleansing. It can indicate immersion, purification rites, or washings practiced in Jewish and Christian contexts.


<!-- p4 -->
Baptisms is one of the foundational doctrines mentioned in Hebrews:



<!-- p5 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="6" data-verses="1-2" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">


<!-- p6 -->
> **<u>Hebrews 6:1-2</u>** (NKJV) ***1** Therefore, leaving the discussion of the elementary principles of Christ, let us go on to perfection, not laying again the foundation of repentance from dead works and of faith toward God, **2** of the doctrine of baptisms, of laying on of hands, of resurrection of the dead, and of eternal judgment.*

</span>



<!-- p8 -->
## Old Covenant Washings - Historical Context


<!-- p9 -->
In the Old Covenant, ceremonial washings played a central role in maintaining ritual purity and symbolizing holiness before God. These washings were prescribed in the Law of Moses and applied both to the priests who served in the Tabernacle and to the people of Israel. For example, priests were required to wash their hands and feet at the bronze basin before entering the Tabernacle or approaching the altar, symbolizing their readiness to serve in the presence of a holy God (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="30" data-verses="18-21" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Exodus 30:18-21</span>). Similarly, rituals for cleansing individuals from defilement, such as contact with a dead body or skin diseases, involved washing with water as a sign of restoration to purity (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="LEV" data-chapter="14" data-verses="8" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Leviticus 14:8</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="NUM" data-chapter="19" data-verses="17-19" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Numbers 19:17-19</span>). These acts served as outward demonstrations of an inward desire for holiness, emphasizing the importance of being set apart for God.


<!-- p10 -->
The ceremonial washings in the Old Testament required regular repetition to maintain ritual purity. Unlike permanent solutions, these rituals only provided temporary external cleansing, leaving the deeper issue of sin unresolved. The writer of Hebrews highlights this limitation, explaining that these practices were “symbolic for the present time,” unable to clear the conscience of the worshiper (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-verses="9-10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 9:9-10</span>). These washings acted as constant reminders of human imperfection and the need for something greater-an ultimate purification that could cleanse not just the body, but the heart and soul as well.


<!-- p11 -->
The ceremonial washings of the Old Covenant find their fulfillment in Christ, who provides complete and eternal cleansing through His sacrifice. Unlike the temporary rituals of the law, Christ’s blood purifies fully, cleansing not just the flesh but also the conscience (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-verses="13-14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 9:13-14</span>). Through His death and resurrection, Jesus accomplished what the Old Testament rituals could only foreshadow-true reconciliation with God. <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-verses="22" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 10:22</span> calls believers to “draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, having our hearts sprinkled clean from an evil conscience and our bodies washed with pure water.” This language ties Christ’s work to the imagery of Old Testament washings while showing its far superior efficacy. His sacrifice does not merely cover sin but removes it completely (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="1" data-verses="7" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 John 1:7</span>). In this way, the ceremonial washings served as a shadow, pointing to the deeper cleansing that only Christ could provide, fulfilling humanity’s need for holiness through divine intervention.


<!-- p12 -->
To reiterate this point, Christians are under no obligation to perform any ceremonial washing. Washings did not provide salvation under the old covenant and do not provide salvation in the new covenant. Ceremonial washing will not increase our standing with God, nor gain us favor. Paul strongly condemns the Judaizers of Galatia (read Galatians) who tried to mix in requirements of the Jewish context as necessary for salvation (e.g., circumcision). Salvation is by faith in Jesus alone!


<!-- p13 -->
## John’s Baptism


<!-- p14 -->
In the New Testament canon there are several baptisms mentioned. We’ve already discussed the baptisms and washings of the Jewish tradition.


<!-- p15 -->
John the Baptist’s ministry centered on preparing the way for the coming of Jesus Christ. His baptism, described as a baptism of repentance for the forgiveness of sins (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="1" data-verses="4" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Mark 1:4</span>), called people to confess their sins, turn their hearts back to God, and prepare for the arrival of the Messiah. It served as an outward sign of inward repentance, symbolizing a desire for spiritual cleansing and readiness for the kingdom of God. John’s baptism, however, was temporary and preparatory-it pointed forward to the greater work that Christ would accomplish. While John’s baptism focused on repentance, it did not provide complete forgiveness or spiritual regeneration, as it lacked the indwelling and empowerment of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-verses="11-12" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 3:11-12</span>). Instead, John openly declared that one greater than He was coming, who would baptize not only with water but with the Holy Spirit and fire (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="3" data-verses="16" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Luke 3:16</span>).


<!-- p16 -->
John’s baptism was fulfilled and surpassed through the death, burial, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. Christian baptism, instituted by Jesus and practiced by the apostles, signifies a deeper reality-union with Christ, death to sin, and resurrection to new life (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-verses="3-4" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 6:3-4</span>). Unlike John’s baptism, Christian baptism is directly connected to faith in Jesus’ atoning sacrifice and the indwelling of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-verses="38" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 2:38</span>). The arrival of the Holy Spirit at Pentecost marked the new covenant and the beginning of Spirit-empowered living, which John’s baptism could not provide. Because of this, John’s baptism is no longer practiced, as it has been replaced by the baptism commanded by Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-verses="19" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 28:19</span>), which incorporates repentance, faith, and the sealing of believers by the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-verses="13-14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>).


<!-- p17 -->
## Christ’s Baptism


<!-- p18 -->
Christ’s baptism set a powerful example for His followers and established the foundation for the Christian practice of water baptism. Though Jesus was without sin, He submitted to baptism by John to “fulfill all righteousness” (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-verses="15" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 3:15</span>). In doing so, He identified with humanity’s need for repentance and pointed forward to His own death, burial, and resurrection. At His baptism, the Holy Spirit descended like a dove, and the Father declared, “This is my beloved Son, with whom I am well pleased” (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-verses="16-17" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 3:16-17</span>). This moment marked the beginning of Jesus’ public ministry and revealed the unity of the Father, Son, and Spirit, foreshadowing the spiritual transformation believers would experience through faith in Christ.


<!-- p19 -->
Jesus later commanded His followers to be baptized, commissioning them to “go and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit” (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-verses="19" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 28:19</span>). Water baptism, as instituted by Christ, became an **act of obedience** and a **public declaration of faith** for believers. It represents their identification with Jesus’ death, burial, and resurrection (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-verses="3-4" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 6:3-4</span>). Going under the water symbolizes dying to sin, and coming up out of the water illustrates rising to new life in Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-verses="12" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Colossians 2:12</span>). Yet, it is crucial to clarify that this outward act is not the source of salvation but a **visible sign** of the **inner work of grace** already accomplished by the blood of Jesus.


<!-- p20 -->
Water baptism is a **symbol** of the cleansing power of Christ’s blood and the renewal of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-verses="26" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 5:26</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-verses="5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Titus 3:5</span>). It signifies repentance, faith, and a commitment to follow Jesus. It is an outward testimony of an inward transformation-a way for believers to publicly declare their allegiance to Christ and their participation in the body of Christ, the Church (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-verses="41" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 2:41</span>). While it is commanded by Jesus and modeled in the early Church, it does not **save** a person. Salvation is by grace through faith alone (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-verses="8-9" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>), and the true cleansing occurs spiritually through the blood of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-verses="5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Revelation 1:5</span>). Water baptism is a response to that salvation, an act of obedience rather than a requirement for it.


<!-- p21 -->
In the New Testament, water baptism is consistently tied to **repentance and faith** (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-verses="38" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 2:38</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-verses="16" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Mark 16:16</span>), both of which require a personal decision to follow Christ-something infants are not capable of making. Baptism symbolizes a believer’s deliberate identification with Christ’s death, burial, and resurrection (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-verses="3-4" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 6:3-4</span>), as well as their commitment to live in obedience to Him. Some denominations (e.g., Catholics, Lutherans) practice infant baptism as a covenantal sign akin to circumcision, however because infants cannot express personal faith or repentance, the practice of **infant baptism** does not align with the biblical model of **believer’s baptism**. Instead, many churches practice **dedication ceremonies** for infants, following the example of Hannah dedicating Samuel to the Lord (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1SA" data-chapter="1" data-verses="27-28" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Samuel 1:27-28</span>) and Mary and Joseph presenting Jesus at the temple (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="2" data-verses="22" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Luke 2:22</span>). Dedication is not a substitute for baptism but rather a commitment by parents to raise their child in the faith, teaching them God’s Word, and modeling a godly life until the child is old enough to make their own decision to follow Christ and be baptized.


<!-- p22 -->
Unlike the **ceremonial washings** of the Old Covenant, which required repeated rituals for purification, Christian baptism is a one-time event that reflects a completed work of grace. Old Testament washings symbolized external cleansing and foreshadowed the deeper spiritual cleansing that comes through Christ. In contrast, New Testament baptism symbolizes the inward renewal and rebirth accomplished by the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-verses="5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Titus 3:5</span>). It points to the believer’s participation in the new covenant, where cleansing from sin is permanent and based on Christ’s finished work.


<!-- p23 -->
Water baptism is closely tied to the **promise of the Holy Spirit**. In <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-verses="38" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 2:38</span>, Peter calls people to repent, be baptized, and receive the gift of the Holy Spirit. While water baptism publicly signifies the believer’s repentance and faith, the true baptism occurs inwardly when the Holy Spirit washes, regenerates, and seals the believer (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-verses="13" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 12:13</span>). This spiritual washing makes us members of Christ’s body and empowers us to live holy lives. The **Holy Spirit’s role** in baptism reminds us that salvation is not accomplished through rituals but through God’s transforming power.


<!-- p24 -->
It is important to distinguish between the **symbolic act of water baptism** and the spiritual reality of salvation through Christ’s blood. The Bible clearly teaches that salvation is based on faith in Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="10" data-verses="9-10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 10:9-10</span>) and not on works, including baptism (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-verses="8-9" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>). However, water baptism is an **act of obedience** that publicly identifies the believer with Christ and the Church. It demonstrates an outward commitment to live as a disciple of Jesus, but it does not add to or complete the work of salvation.


<!-- p25 -->
Christ’s water baptism set an example for believers to follow, demonstrating humility, obedience, and preparation for ministry. For Christians today, water baptism serves as a public declaration of faith, symbolizing death to sin and resurrection to new life. While it is a command of Jesus and an important step of obedience, it is not required for salvation. True cleansing occurs through faith in the blood of Jesus, and the empowerment of Christian living comes through the Holy Spirit’s work in the believer. Baptism unites us with Christ and His Church, reminding us of the inward transformation and renewal that God has already accomplished.


<!-- p26 -->
Having established water baptism as an outward sign of faith, we now turn to the baptism of the Holy Spirit, which empowers believers for ministry and service.


<!-- p27 -->
## Holy Spirit Baptism


<!-- p28 -->
The baptism in the Holy Spirit is a profound and transformative experience promised by God to empower believers for ministry, service, and holy living. Every believer receives the Holy Spirit when they’re born again-this is how we’re made spiritually alive. But later, we should also receive the baptism in the Holy Spirit, which empowers us for boldness, spiritual gifts, and effective ministry.


<!-- p29 -->
Water baptism is a public declaration of faith and symbolizes the cleansing of sin through Christ’s blood, Spirit baptism represents the empowering presence of God in the believer’s life. Together, these two modes of baptism form complementary aspects of the Christian walk-one as an outward sign of obedience and the other as an inward source of power and renewal. The baptism of the Holy Spirit is the Spirit upon us rather than within. At salvation all believers receive the Spirit within them, but when the Spirit of God comes upon them, they receive power and boldness for ministry.


<!-- p30 -->
The baptism in the Holy Spirit was foretold by John the Baptist, who declared, 



<!-- p31 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="fragment" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-verses="11" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">


<!-- p32 -->
> **<u>Matthew 3:11</u>** *I baptize you with water for repentance, but He who is coming after me… will baptize you with the Holy Spirit and fire”


<!-- p33 -->
</span>



<!-- p34 -->
This promise pointed to a greater work that Jesus would accomplish after His resurrection-sending the Holy Spirit to dwell within and empower His followers. Jesus confirmed this when He instructed His disciples to wait for the gift of the Spirit, explaining that they would receive power to be His witnesses (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-verses="8" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 1:8</span>). The fulfillment of this promise occurred on the day of Pentecost, when the disciples were filled with the Holy Spirit, evidenced by speaking in tongues, prophecy, and boldness to proclaim the gospel (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-verses="1-4" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 2:1-4</span>).


<!-- p35 -->
This outpouring marked the beginning of the **Church Age** and demonstrated that the Spirit’s power was now available to all believers. This promise wasn’t limited to the apostles but extends to all who believe, as Peter declared, *“The promise is for you and your children and for all who are far off-for all whom the Lord our God will call”* (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-verses="39" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 2:39</span>).


<!-- p36 -->
### Spirit Within vs Spirit Upon


<!-- p37 -->
When someone puts their faith in Jesus, the Holy Spirit gives them new life on the inside. That’s what it means to be born again (**regenerated**). At that moment of faith, the Holy Spirit indwells **WITHIN** the believer, making them spiritually alive and adopting them into the family of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-verses="5-6" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 3:5-6</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-verses="5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Titus 3:5</span>). This is the new birth or being born again, as Jesus described in John 3.


<!-- p38 -->
Scriptural Basis:


<!-- p39 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-verses="5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Titus 3:5</span>* - “He saved us through the washing of rebirth and renewal by the Holy Spirit.”


<!-- p40 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-verses="13-14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>* - The Holy Spirit seals believers as a **guarantee** of their inheritance.


<!-- p41 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-verses="9" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 8:9</span>* - “Anyone who does not have the Spirit of Christ does not belong to him.”


<!-- p42 -->
This work of regeneration is universal for all believers at salvation and establishes a permanent indwelling of the Spirit. It is not dependent on any outward action, such as water baptism, and guarantees salvation by grace through faith.


<!-- p43 -->
In contrast, the baptism in the Holy Spirit is a separate and subsequent experience that equips believers with power for service and witness (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-verses="8" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 1:8</span>). While regeneration deals with new life and identity, Spirit baptism focuses on empowerment **UPON** and the release of spiritual gifts. This baptism is often accompanied by signs and manifestations of Holy Spirit gifts.


<!-- p44 -->
Scriptural Basis:



<!-- p45 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-verses="8" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">


<!-- p46 -->
> **<u>Acts 1:8</u>** *(NKJV) But you shall receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you; and you shall be witnesses to Me in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the end of the earth.*


<!-- p47 -->
</span>



<!-- p48 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-verses="15-17" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 8:15-17</span>* - The Samaritans had believed and were baptized, but they later received the Holy Spirit through prayer and the laying on of hands.


<!-- p49 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-verses="6" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 19:6</span>* - Disciples in Ephesus, already believers, received the Holy Spirit when Paul laid hands on them.


<!-- p50 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit does not replace regeneration but builds upon it, equipping the believer for spiritual service and boldness in ministry. Being baptized in the Holy Spirit is a free gift to those who are saved, not a requirement to be saved. It is not a requirement that someone manifest a <u>specific</u> gift of the Holy Spirit to prove their ‘baptism’ in the Holy Spirit, but we should expect that the Holy Spirit baptism shows up the same way today as it did in the book of Acts, with signs and boldness accompanying! Some people err in thinking if a person doesn't speak in tongues that they are not saved, or that they do not have the Holy Spirit baptism. Not everyone will receive every gift or manifestation of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-verses="29-31" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 12:29-31</span>).

## How to Receive the Baptism of the Holy Spirit


<!-- p52 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit is described in Scripture as a gift that is promised to believers and is received by faith. It is separate from salvation and is primarily for empowerment and witness (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-verses="8" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 1:8</span>). The baptism of the Holy Spirit is a gift of empowerment for believers, promised by Jesus and fulfilled at Pentecost. It is received through repentance, faith, and asking God. While laying on of hands is often used as a method, it is ultimately God who baptizes with the Spirit.


<!-- p53 -->
Scripture presents speaking in tongues and prophecy as a common sign, but the purpose of this baptism is far greater-providing boldness, power, and spiritual gifts for ministry. Every believer is encouraged to seek this experience as part of their spiritual journey, trusting that God will equip them for His work.


<!-- p54 -->
The Bible outlines several core principles regarding how this baptism is received:


<!-- p55 -->

<!-- p56 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit is not something believers can earn-it is a free gift given by God. God wants to give good gifts to His children and is eager to give the Holy Spirit to those who ask with faith and a desire for empowerment.

<!-- p58 -->

<!-- p59 -->
</span>




<!-- p60 -->

<!-- p61 -->
> **<u>Acts 2:38-39</u>** (NKJV) **38** *Then Peter said to them, "Repent, and let every one of you be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins; and you shall receive the gift of the Holy Spirit. **39** For the promise is to you and to your children, and to all who are afar off, as many as the Lord our God will call."*


<!-- p62 -->

- **2. Ask, Seek, and Receive by Faith**


<!-- p64 -->
Scripture emphasizes that the baptism in the Holy Spirit is received through asking in prayer and faith-filled expectation. Faith, combined with a willingness to ask and receive, is the key to experiencing Spirit baptism. If you are not sure if God wants to give you the baptism of the Holy Spirit, ask Him about it, He is a good father! As He prompts you, you may feel a tangible presence of God in your body that you've never experienced before. It’s okay if you don’t! God is a big God, and He doesn't need to be boxed into one perfect formula. If you feel God tugging at your heart, yield to him!

### **3. Laying on of Hands (Optional)**


<!-- p71 -->
While some received the Holy Spirit spontaneously (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="10" data-verses="44" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 10:44</span>), others received through the **laying on of hands** by other believers. Laying on of hands is a biblical pattern, but not a requirement. The Holy Spirit ultimately responds to faith, not rituals.



<!-- p72 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-verses="17" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">


<!-- p73 -->

## The Gifts of the Holy Spirit


<!-- p79 -->
When the Holy Spirit was poured out, and people baptized in the Holy Spirit, oftentimes manifestations of the Holy Spirit accompanied it.


<!-- p80 -->
There are several lists of gifts in the New Testament:


<!-- p81 -->
- Motivational Gifts given by God: <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-verses="6-8" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 12:6-8</span>


<!-- p82 -->
  - Discussed in the chapter on the Church.


<!-- p83 -->
- Five-Fold Ministry Gifts given by Jesus: <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-verses="11-13" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 4:11-13</span>


<!-- p84 -->
  - Discussed in the chapter on the church.


<!-- p85 -->
- Manifestation Gifts given by the Holy Spirit: <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-verses="8-10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span>


<!-- p86 -->
  - Discussed here.


<!-- p87 -->
Each set of these gifts are given for different purposes, and operate differently, but they collectively point to the **diversity** and **unity** of gifts within the **Body of Christ**. Each gift has a unique role in fulfilling God’s purposes.


<!-- p88 -->
### Purpose & Operations of the Gifts


<!-- p89 -->
The primary purpose of the gifts of the Holy Spirit is to build up the Body of Christ and bring glory to God:


<!-- p90 -->
- **Edification** - Strengthening and encouraging believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-verses="3" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>).


<!-- p91 -->
- **Unity** - Promoting harmony and cooperation within the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-verses="12-13" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 4:12-13</span>).


<!-- p92 -->
- **Evangelism** - Demonstrating God’s power as a witness to unbelievers (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-verses="43" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 2:43</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-verses="6-7" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 8:6-7</span>).


<!-- p93 -->
- **Service** - Meeting the needs of others through acts of mercy and generosity (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-verses="6-8" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 12:6-8</span>).


<!-- p94 -->
These gifts are not for personal glory but for the common good (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-verses="7" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 12:7</span>). They function best when exercised in love and humility, reflecting the character of Christ (1 Corinthians 13).


<!-- p95 -->
Paul emphasized that spiritual gifts must be exercised decently and in order (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-verses="40" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 14:40</span>). This ensures that the use of gifts promotes unity, peace, and understanding, rather than confusion or division.


<!-- p96 -->
- Prophecies should be evaluated (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-verses="29" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 14:29</span>).


<!-- p97 -->
- When giving a message in tongues to the body, pray that it be interpreted (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-verses="27-28" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 14:27-28</span>).


<!-- p98 -->
- Gifts must always bring **edification** to the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-verses="12" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 14:12</span>).


<!-- p99 -->
This order does not quench the Spirit but ensures that the gifts operate in ways that **honor God** and **benefit others**.


<!-- p100 -->
The operation of spiritual gifts must always be grounded in love. Paul dedicates an entire chapter (1 Corinthians 13) to explaining that without love, even the greatest spiritual gifts are meaningless. Love provides the motivation and framework for using gifts in ways that build up others rather than tearing them down.


<!-- p101 -->
Additionally, the gifts operate **as the Spirit wills** (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-verses="11" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 12:11</span>). They cannot be manufactured or controlled by human effort-they are sovereignly given and empowered by the Holy Spirit, often in moments of need or ministry. <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="13" data-verses="8" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 13:8</span> says that eventually prophecy and tongues will cease. Theologically people who believe that the gifts of the Holy Spirit have ceased are known as ‘Cessationists’. <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="13" data-verses="10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 13:10</span> says “when the prefect \[Jesus\] comes, that wish is in part will be cause to cease”. As Jesus has not yet returned for His church, the gifts are still in operation on the earth.


<!-- p102 -->
It is crucial to recognize that the **gifts of the Spirit** should not be separated from the **fruit of the Spirit** (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-verses="22-23" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Galatians 5:22-23</span>). While gifts demonstrate power, the fruit reveals character-love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control.


<!-- p103 -->
- **Gifts without Fruit**: A believer can operate in gifts yet lack maturity or character, leading to pride, misuse, or harm.


<!-- p104 -->
- **Fruit without Gifts**: A believer may have godly character but lack the empowerment to minister effectively.


<!-- p105 -->
Both are necessary. Gifts equip believers for service. The fruit is a sign of their spiritual health (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="7" data-verses="16" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 7:16</span>) and provide a ‘pleasant taste’ to those who consume it.


<!-- p106 -->
### Explaining the 9 Holy Spirit Manifestation Gifts


<!-- p107 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-verses="8-10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span> lists the 9 Holy Spirit manifestation gifts:


<!-- p108 -->
1.  **Word of Wisdom** - Supernatural insight into God’s purposes or direction for a situation. It applies divine wisdom to complex problems or decisions (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-verses="10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 6:10</span>).


<!-- p109 -->
2.  **Word of Knowledge** - A supernatural revelation of information about a person, event, or circumstance that could not have been known naturally (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="4" data-verses="17-18" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 4:17-18</span>).


<!-- p110 -->
3.  **Faith** - A supernatural ability to trust God for the miraculous, even in impossible situations. This gift inspires boldness and confidence (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="3" data-verses="6-7" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 3:6-7</span>).


<!-- p111 -->
4.  **Gifts of Healing** - The supernatural power to heal physical, emotional, or spiritual illnesses and restore wholeness (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="3" data-verses="6-8" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 3:6-8</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="9" data-verses="17-18" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 9:17-18</span>).


<!-- p112 -->
5.  **Working of Miracles** - The ability to perform supernatural acts that defy natural laws, such as multiplying food or raising the dead (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="9" data-verses="40-41" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 9:40-41</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="2" data-verses="9-11" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 2:9-11</span>).


<!-- p113 -->
6.  **Prophecy** - Declaring God’s truth or will under the inspiration of the Holy Spirit. It is to be encouraging, edifying, and comforting (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-verses="3" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>).


<!-- p114 -->
7.  **Distinguishing (Discernment) Between Spirits** - The ability to discern whether a spiritual influence is from God, the enemy, or human origin (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-verses="16-18" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 16:16-18</span>).


<!-- p115 -->
8.  **Various Kinds of Tongues** - Speaking in languages unknown to the speaker, often as an act of worship, prayer, or a sign to unbelievers (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-verses="4" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 2:4</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-verses="22" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 14:22</span>).


<!-- p116 -->
9.  **Interpretation of Tongues** - The supernatural ability to interpret the message spoken in tongues so that the church may be edified (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-verses="27" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 14:27</span>).


<!-- p117 -->
Paul makes it clear that all gifts are valuable and should work together for the common good of the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-verses="7" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 12:7</span>). However, He also encourages believers to desire the greater gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-verses="31" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 12:31</span>). The greater gifts are often interpreted as those that most directly edify the church, such as prophecy (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-verses="1-5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 14:1-5</span>).


<!-- p118 -->
- Prophecy is especially emphasized because it strengthens, encourages, and comforts others (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-verses="3" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>). Paul instructs believers to earnestly desire this gift as it promotes clarity and understanding in the church.


<!-- p119 -->
- Gifts like tongues are highly valued for personal edification and prayer (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-verses="4" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 14:4</span>), but Paul teaches that when giving a message in tongues to the Church to pray for interpretation to ensure they build up the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-verses="27-28" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 14:27-28</span>).


<!-- p120 -->
While the Holy Spirit gives the gifts, we must yield and to submit to the Holy Spirit to use them. Like muscles and talents, when we practice yielding to the Holy Spirit to use His gifts we become more confident and reliable in the use of them.
