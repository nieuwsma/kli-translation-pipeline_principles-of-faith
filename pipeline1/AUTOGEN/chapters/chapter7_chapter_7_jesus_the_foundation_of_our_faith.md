# Chapter 7 - Jesus-The Foundation of Our Faith

<!-- p1 -->
Building on our study of Jesus as the Messiah, in this chapter we continue in looking at the life, person, and work of Jesus. We’ll see that Jesus is the Mediator of a new and better covenant-and that He perfectly fills the roles of Prophet, Priest, and King. It is in this New Covenant that we as believers have good standing with God and transition from a works-based covenant to a trust-based (faith-based) covenant. Finally, we will read and study the *Apostles Creed* as a way to round-out our study on Jesus.


<!-- p2 -->
## Mediator of the New Covenant


<!-- p3 -->
The concept of a mediator involves a go-between who bridges the gap between two parties, facilitating a relationship or agreement. In the Exodus story, Moses is the initial mediator of the covenant God established with His people. Moses received the law, offered the sacrifice (priest), called God’s people to obedience (prophet), and judged them (judge / king). In the remainder of the Old Testament after Moses, the mediating role was fulfilled through the role of the Prophet, the Levitical Priesthood, and the Judges and later Kings of the nation.


<!-- p4 -->
Old Testament Roles of Mediators:


<!-- p5 -->
1.  **Prophet:** Prophets were God’s messengers, sent to speak His heart to the people and call them back when they wandered. Moses, for example, served as a prophet who mediated the Old Covenant by receiving the Law from God and teaching it to Israel. Prophets also foretold the coming of the Messiah and the ultimate redemption of humanity. Other prominent examples of prophet include: Isaiah, Jeremiah, Elijah, Elisha, Samuel.


<!-- p6 -->
2.  **Levitical Priesthood:** Priests acted as mediators by offering sacrifices for the atonement of sins and interceding for the people before God. The High Priest, especially, played a vital role on the Day of Atonement, entering the Holy of Holies to sprinkle blood on the mercy seat, symbolizing reconciliation with God. The priest's ministry is to intercede for the people.


<!-- p7 -->
3.  **Judge/King:** Judges, and later Kings, were charged with ruling over God’s people, enforcing His laws, judging between the people, delivering them from their enemies, and leading them in righteousness. The book of Judges tells the stories of these leaders-people like Deborah, Samson, and Gideon-whom God raised up to rescue and guide Israel. Israel later had a line of kings, many wicked, some good (see 1st and 2nd Kings, 1st and 2nd Samuel, and 1<sup>st</sup> and 2<sup>nd</sup> Chronicles). Some prominent examples are Saul, David, and Solomon.


<!-- p8 -->
Worth noting is that all the Old Covenant mediators were fully man and not divine. As men they were able to sin, and many times were given to sin. Some of the mediators were sinful and drove people away from God and towards profane worship: false prophets, lying and corrupt priests, and wicked kings.


<!-- p9 -->
### Christ as the Mediator of the New Covenant


<!-- p10 -->
Jesus’ death, resurrection and ascension fulfil the Old Covenant, and establish the New Covenant.


<!-- p11 -->
The New Covenant is a transformative relationship between God and His people, established through the life, death, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. Unlike the Old Covenant, which was focused on external laws, the New Covenant emphasizes internal transformation and grace. It was inaugurated by Jesus at the Last Supper, where He declared that His blood would establish this covenant, replacing the repeated animal sacrifices of the Old Covenant with His once-for-all atonement (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="22" data-verses="20" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Luke 22:20</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-verses="12" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 9:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-verses="10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 10:10</span>). Through this sacrifice, the New Covenant provides complete forgiveness and cleansing from sin, fulfilling the requirement for a perfect and permanent reconciliation with God (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-verses="34" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Jeremiah 31:34</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-verses="12" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 8:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-verses="12-18" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 10:12-18</span>).


<!-- p12 -->
One of the central features of the New Covenant is its internal nature, as God writes His law on the hearts of His people, fostering a heart-level relationship with Him through the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-verses="33" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Jeremiah 31:33</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-verses="10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 8:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="3" data-verses="3" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">2 Corinthians 3:3</span>). This shift enables believers to live out God’s will not by mere adherence to external commands but through empowerment by the Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-verses="1-4" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 8:1-4</span>). The New Covenant also grants direct access to God, eliminating the need for an earthly priesthood. Jesus serves as the eternal High Priest, and believers are called a royal priesthood, able to confidently approach God through Christ’s sacrifice (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-verses="24-25" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 7:24-25</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-verses="19-20" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 10:19-20</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-verses="9" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Peter 2:9</span>).


<!-- p13 -->
Unlike the Old Covenant, which was exclusive to Israel, the New Covenant is universal, extending to all who believe in Christ, regardless of ethnicity or background (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-verses="16" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 3:16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="3" data-verses="28" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Galatians 3:28</span>). This covenant is based entirely on grace, offering salvation and forgiveness freely through faith in Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-verses="8-9" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>). It is not sustained by human effort but by the enabling power of the Holy Spirit, who transforms and empowers believers to live in obedience and reflect God’s character (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EZK" data-chapter="36" data-verses="26-27" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ezekiel 36:26-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-verses="9-16" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 8:9-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-verses="22-23" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Galatians 5:22-23</span>). Furthermore, the New Covenant is everlasting, surpassing the temporary and conditional nature of the Old Covenant (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JER" data-chapter="32" data-verses="40" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Jeremiah 32:40</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-verses="13" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 8:13</span>). It represents the ultimate fulfillment of God’s redemptive plan, reconciling humanity to Himself and enabling believers to live as His people under His rule for eternity.


<!-- p14 -->
Jesus is explicitly identified as the Mediator of the New Covenant in 


<!-- p15 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-verses="15" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">


<!-- p16 -->
> **<u>Hebrews 9:15</u>** (NKJV) - *For this reason Christ is the mediator of a new covenant, that those who are called may receive the promised eternal inheritance*.


<!-- p17 -->
</span>



<!-- p18 -->
Through His atoning death and resurrection, Christ inaugurates this new covenant, replacing the old system of sacrifices and external laws with a covenant written on the hearts of believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-verses="31-34" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Jeremiah 31:31-34</span>). He stands between God and humanity, not only interceding on behalf of His people but also providing the means of reconciliation through His own blood (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-verses="12" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 9:12</span>). Unlike the mediators of the Old Covenant, Jesus is sinless, eternal, and fully capable of fulfilling the divine requirements for justice and mercy, ensuring a lasting relationship between God and believers.


<!-- p19 -->
Jesus Fulfills and Perfects These Roles


<!-- p20 -->
1.  **Prophet:** Jesus is the ultimate Prophet, not merely a messenger of God’s word but the Word made flesh (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-verses="14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 1:14</span>). He reveals God’s character, will, and plan for salvation fully and perfectly (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="1" data-verses="1-2" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 1:1-2</span>). His teachings, miracles, and prophetic declarations fulfill the Old Testament prophecies and provide the foundation for the New Covenant.


<!-- p21 -->
2.  **Priest:** Jesus is the perfect High Priest, offering Himself as the once-for-all sacrifice for sin (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-verses="27" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 7:27</span>). Unlike the priests of the Old Covenant who had to repeatedly offer sacrifices for their own sins and those of the people, Jesus, being sinless, offered His own blood to secure eternal redemption (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-verses="14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 9:14</span>).


<!-- p22 -->
3.  **King & Judge:** Jesus is the eternal King, reigning in righteousness and justice. As the Son of David, He fulfills the promise of an everlasting kingdom (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="2SA" data-chapter="7" data-verses="16" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">2 Samuel 7:16</span>). His kingship is not merely earthly but cosmic, ruling over all creation and establishing peace through His victory over sin and death (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-verses="9-11" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Philippians 2:9-11</span>). Jesus will return as king and judge, and judge ‘both the living and the dead’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="10" data-verses="42" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 10:42</span>).


<!-- p23 -->
In Christ, the roles of Prophet, Priest, and King converge and are fulfilled perfectly. As the Mediator of the New Covenant, He bridges the gap between a holy God and sinful humanity, enabling believers to approach God with confidence (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="4" data-verses="16" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 4:16</span>) and ensuring the eternal inheritance promised in Him.

## The Apostles Creed


<!-- p25 -->
The *Apostles Creed* is not found in scripture, it was created and modified based on the earliest teachings of the church. A creed is a formal confession of beliefs, think of it like a pledge or oath combined with a statement of belief. Creeds are important because they are used to unify and separate those who believe in a particular way from those who do not. In the United States it is common at civil functions like city council meetings or legislative gatherings for the attendees to recite the Pledge of Allegiance. The pledge is used widely in the United States, but isn’t used in other countries, because the citizens of those countries do not owe allegiance to the United States. The pledge is not what makes a person ‘American’, but it does represent a significant essential belief for those who are citizens.


<!-- p26 -->
The first recognizable versions of the creed were written in the fourth century A.D. The creed has changed slightly over the centuries, and has slight variations based on the denomination. Not every Christian denomination uses the creed, but in large part it is widely accepted in some form across the whole of Christianity.


<!-- p27 -->
The Apostles Creed has been used as a teaching and catechism tool for centuries and communicates the most fundamental truths of the Christian faith, the base beliefs that must be acknowledged and held to be a Christian. Some denominations have a formal catechism process which is how a new believer joins that specific denomination and becomes indoctrinated into their beliefs.


<!-- p28 -->
A person does not need to fully understand these beliefs to come to God and receive salvation, salvation is by faith, not by a doctrinal test! However, in continuing in that faith we all should come to understand these fundamental truths.

## Brief Explanation of the Apostles Creed


<!-- p31 -->
The fundamental truths in the Apostles Creed are numerous and quite deep, a full explanation would certainly take many whole volumes to explain. While this chapter is about Jesus, many of the topics cross over into the other members of the Godhead. This will be an abbreviated explanation of the creed, focusing only on the most central themes. Several of these topics are studied in greater depth across the chapters of this book.


<!-- p32 -->
#### *I believe in God, the Father almighty, creator of heaven and earth.*


<!-- p33 -->
This affirms belief in God as the all-powerful, sovereign Creator and Father. The title "Father" emphasizes God’s loving relationship with His creation, particularly His children through faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-verses="12" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 1:12</span>). As the Creator, He spoke the universe into existence, bringing order out of chaos (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="1" data-verses="1-3" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Genesis 1:1-3</span>). His almighty power is displayed in the vastness of creation, while His fatherly care is seen in His ongoing sustenance and providence. This foundational statement establishes God as the origin and ruler of all life, deserving worship and trust.


<!-- p34 -->
#### *I believe in Jesus Christ, His only Son, our Lord.*


<!-- p35 -->
Jesus Christ is the unique Son of God, eternally begotten, not created, and sharing in the divine nature (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-verses="1-2" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 1:1-2</span>, 14). Jesus is the ‘Word’ since the beginning, and He became flesh and lived with us. As "Christ," He is the anointed Messiah, fulfilling the Old Testament promises of a Savior. The title "Lord" acknowledges His authority over all creation and His rightful position as ruler and King (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-verses="10-11" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Philippians 2:10-11</span>). He is not only Lord, but the Lord who saves (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-verses="31" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 16:31</span>).


<!-- p36 -->
Jesus is not **just** sent from God; He **is** part of God. Jesus abides in the Father and the Father abides in Jesus. The Gospel of John repeatedly establishes that Jesus and the Father are one (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-verses="30" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 10:30</span>), that Jesus did not act on His own accord (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="5" data-verses="19" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 5:19</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="12" data-verses="49-50" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 12:49-50</span>), and that everything He did was to glorify His Father (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="17" data-verses="4" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 17:4</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-verses="13" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 14:13</span>), and was done by the authority and power of His Father (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-verses="10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 14:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="8" data-verses="28-29" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 8:28-29</span>).


<!-- p37 -->
#### *He was conceived by the power of the Holy Spirit and born of the Virgin Mary.*


<!-- p38 -->
This affirms the miraculous conception of Jesus through the Holy Spirit, underscoring His divine origin. Born of the Virgin Mary, Jesus fulfills the prophecy of <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="7" data-verses="14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Isaiah 7:14</span>, which foretold the virgin birth of the Messiah. This event marks the incarnation, where the eternal Word of God took on human flesh (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-verses="14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 1:14</span>). By being both fully God and fully human, Jesus bridges the gap between God and humanity, enabling Him to be the perfect mediator and Savior.


<!-- p39 -->
Jesus was conceived by the seed of the Holy Spirit, and did not inherit a sinful nature that we, born of the seed of men, have. Jesus was in every way tempted and tested, but He was perfect and without sin. <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="4" data-verses="15" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 4:15</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="5" data-verses="21" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">2 Corinthians 5:21</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-verses="22" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Peter 2:22</span>.


<!-- p40 -->
#### *He suffered under Pontius Pilate, was crucified, died, and was buried.*


<!-- p41 -->
Jesus’ suffering and death were real, historical events that occurred under the Roman governor Pontius Pilate, grounding the Christian faith in verifiable history. His crucifixion was a fulfillment of Old Testament prophecies, such as <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="53" data-verses="5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Isaiah 53:5</span>, which speaks of the Suffering Servant who would bear the sins of many. Jesus’ death was sacrificial, taking the penalty for sin on behalf of humanity (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-verses="24" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Peter 2:24</span>). His burial confirms the reality of His death and prepares for the significance of the resurrection.


<!-- p42 -->
#### *He descended to the dead.*


<!-- p43 -->
After His death, Jesus descended to the realm of the dead, often referred to as *Hades* or *Sheol* in Scripture. This act demonstrates His full identification with humanity, even in death, and signifies His victory over sin, death, and the devil (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="3" data-verses="18-20" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Peter 3:18-20</span>). By descending, He proclaimed His triumph and the completion of His redemptive work.


<!-- p44 -->
#### *On the third day He rose again.*


<!-- p45 -->
The resurrection of Jesus is the cornerstone of our Christian faith, confirming His divinity and the truth of His teachings. By rising from the dead, He defeated sin and death, providing the assurance of eternal life for believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="15" data-verses="17-22" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 15:17-22</span>). The resurrection also fulfills Scripture, including Jesus’ own predictions (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-verses="21" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 16:21</span>). This miraculous event transformed the despair of the disciples into bold faith, forming the foundation of the early Church's witness.


<!-- p46 -->
#### *He ascended into heaven, and is seated at the right hand of the Father.*


<!-- p47 -->
Jesus’ ascension marks His return to the Father’s presence in glory. Seated at the father’s right hand (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-verses="20" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 1:20</span>), He assumes the role of our eternal High Priest, interceding for believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-verses="25" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 7:25</span>). His position of authority also signifies His exaltation as King, reigning over all creation and directing the Church’s mission (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-verses="22" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 1:22</span>) to do greater works (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-verses="12" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 14:12</span>) and fulfilling the great commission He gave to us (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-verses="18-20" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 28:18-20</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-verses="15-18" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Mark 16:15-18</span>). The ascension assures believers of His ongoing presence and power through the Holy Spirit.


<!-- p48 -->
#### *He will come again to judge the living and the dead.*


<!-- p49 -->
This points to the future return of Christ when He will bring final justice and restoration. Jesus will judge all people, rewarding the faithful and bringing justice to the unrepentant (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="4" data-verses="1" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">2 Timothy 4:1</span>). His return will fulfill God’s ultimate plan for creation, ushering in His eternal Kingdom of righteousness and peace. This hope encourages believers to live faithfully and expectantly, knowing their labor in the Lord is not in vain (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="15" data-verses="58" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 15:58</span>).


<!-- p50 -->
#### *I believe in the Holy Spirit.*


<!-- p51 -->
The Holy Spirit, the third person of the Trinity, is God’s presence and power active in the world today. He convicts of sin, regenerates hearts, and empowers believers to live holy lives (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-verses="8-13" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 16:8-13</span>). The Spirit unites Christians as one body, the Church, and equips them with spiritual gifts for service (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-verses="4-11" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 12:4-11</span>). He is the Comforter, Advocate, and Guide, dwelling within believers as a seal of their eternal inheritance (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-verses="13-14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>).


<!-- p52 -->
#### *The holy catholic Church, the communion of saints.*


<!-- p53 -->
The "holy catholic Church" refers to the universal body of Christ, comprising all believers across time and space. The Church is set apart for God’s purposes, called to reflect His holiness and share the gospel. The "communion of saints" emphasizes the unity of all Christians, living and dead, as members of Christ’s body (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-verses="4-6" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 4:4-6</span>). This fellowship transcends earthly divisions, demonstrating the shared hope and mission of believers.


<!-- p54 -->
Jesus is the head of the church. The church is referred to as both the bride of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-verses="25-27" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 5:25-27</span>) and the body of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-verses="18" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Colossians 1:18</span>).


<!-- p55 -->
#### *The forgiveness of sins.*


<!-- p56 -->
This highlights the heart of the gospel: through Jesus’ sacrificial death, God offers complete forgiveness for sins to all who repent and believe (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-verses="7" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 1:7</span>). This forgiveness restores the broken relationship between humanity and God, removing guilt and shame. It also calls believers to extend forgiveness to others, reflecting the grace they have received (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="6" data-verses="14-15" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 6:14-15</span>).


<!-- p57 -->
#### *The resurrection of the body.*


<!-- p58 -->
Like Jesus, we will experience a bodily resurrection at the end of time. This resurrection will transform mortal, perishable bodies into glorified, imperishable ones fit for eternal life (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="15" data-verses="42-44" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 15:42-44</span>). It underscores the Christian hope of restoration, not just for individuals, but for all creation, as God makes all things new (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="REV" data-chapter="21" data-verses="4-5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Revelation 21:4-5</span>).


<!-- p59 -->
#### *And the life everlasting. Amen.*


<!-- p60 -->
Eternal life with God is the ultimate promise of the Christian faith. This life begins now through a relationship with Christ and will be fully realized in the new heavens and new earth, where believers will dwell with God forever (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-verses="16" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 3:16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="REV" data-chapter="22" data-verses="1-5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Revelation 22:1-5</span>). This closing affirmation reflects the joy and confidence of salvation, echoing the trust and hope expressed throughout the Creed.


<!-- p61 -->
## Conclusion


<!-- p62 -->
In the foundational truths expressed in the Apostles Creed, we see Jesus’ life and work as central to God’s plan for humanity. Each statement in the Creed captures key aspects of our belief in God, and who Jesus is-His divinity, humanity, mission, and ultimate triumph. These truths point to His unique role as the Mediator of the New Covenant. As Mediator He invites us to approach God with confidence, trusting in His perfect sacrifice and ongoing intercession. It calls us to worship Him as the rightful King and to follow His teachings as the ultimate Prophet. Ultimately, the study of Jesus leads to a deeper relationship with Him, transforming us into people who live in the light of His grace and truth.


<!-- p63 -->
Understanding who Jesus is and what He has accomplished is the foundation of the Christian faith. The many names, titles, and roles of Jesus illuminate different facets of His character and mission, providing a fuller picture of His love and authority.


<!-- p64 -->
Like our study of God, the scriptures are full of various names referring to Jesus. Each name represents a specific aspect of His total character, ministry, and role. These names were given to us to reveal an aspect of Jesus.
