# Chapter 13 - Spiritual Authority

<!-- p1 -->
Jesus is the supreme and complete source of spiritual authority. As the triumphant Christ, He has final authority over all things in heaven and on earth.

<!-- p2 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p3 -->
> **<u>Matthew 28:18-20</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And Jesus came and spoke to them, saying: "All authority has been given to Me in heaven and on earth.
>
> Go therefore and make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit,
>
> teaching them to observe all things that I have commanded you; and behold, I am with you always, even to the end of the age." Amen.
>

<!-- p4 -->
</span>

<!-- p5 -->
In this chapter we will study the authority and power of Christ, which authorities and powers He has delegated to us (His church), and how to operate in the authority of the name of Jesus. We will also study angelic and demonic beings and examine their authority, power, and natures.

<!-- p6 -->
Authority means having the right to act. Power is the ability to carry it out. Both are essential in spiritual life. Authority is different from power. Let’s look at the following table that compares authority and power and consider some examples.

<!-- p7 -->
[See Table 7](../tables/table7.md)

<!-- p8 -->
Here are some examples to help understand the difference between power and authority.

<!-- p9 -->
Example 1: At work, an employee may have the talents and skills to carry out a sale of the company's assets (power). This power is lawful if he also has the associated permission (authority) from the owners of the company. Without this authority, selling the company's assets is theft.

<!-- p10 -->
Example 2: A police officer is given special training in weapons and hand-to-hand combat to learn how to apprehend and physically force a criminal (power). Police officers have been given the job responsibility, backed by laws (authority), of enforcing criminal statutes.

## The source of Jesus' authority

<!-- p74 -->
Now that we have accurately examined what authority is, we need to know who is the source of that authority. The authority of Jesus is both divine and delegated. It originates in God the Father, is affirmed by His death and resurrection, and operates in perfect harmony with the Father's will. Though absolute, His authority respects human free will, demonstrating God's love rather than forced control.

<!-- p75 -->
The authority of Jesus is rooted in His divine identity as the Son of God and is granted by the Father. The Father entrusted all authority to Christ, including judgment and rule over heaven and earth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 11:27</span>). The authority of Jesus reflects the unity and love within the Trinity, where the Son perfectly carries out the Father's plan (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="35" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:35</span>).

<!-- p76 -->
Although Jesus had inherent authority as the Son of God, His victory over sin, death, and hell was fully established through His death and resurrection. Christ's obedience in His sacrifice led to His exaltation (seated at the right hand of the Father), securing His authority over all creation. By conquering death, Christ obtained absolute dominion over life and eternity.

## Laying on of hands

<!-- p116 -->
The laying on of hands is a significant biblical practice used throughout both the Old and New Testaments to symbolize blessing, commissioning, healing, and impartation of authority or spiritual gifts. It is more than a simple physical act: it represents a spiritual connection and transfer under the direction of God's authority.

<!-- p117 -->
In the Old Testament, the laying on of hands is often associated with blessing and dedication. For example, Jacob blessed his grandsons, Ephraim and Manasseh, by laying his hands on them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="48" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 48:14</span>). Likewise, Moses laid hands on Joshua to commission him as his successor, imparting leadership authority and wisdom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="NUM" data-chapter="27" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Numbers 27:18-23</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="DEU" data-chapter="34" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Deuteronomy 34:9</span>). This act publicly signified God's choice and empowerment for the task ahead.

<!-- p118 -->
In the New Testament, the laying on of hands is closely tied to the ministry of Jesus and the early church. Jesus regularly laid His hands on the sick to heal them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 6:5</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 4:40</span>), demonstrating both compassion and authority over sickness. The apostles continued this practice, using it to impart the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:17</span>), spiritual gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 4:14</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 1:6</span>) and blessings to believers. It also played a role in the commissioning of leaders and missionaries for ministry (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 13:3</span>).

<!-- p119 -->
The laying on of hands symbolizes an act of faith and obedience to the Word of God. It is not a mechanical ritual, but a spiritual practice rooted in dependence on the Holy Spirit. For example, in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:6</span>, the apostles laid hands on those selected to serve as deacons, commending them to God's service and publicly recognizing their calling. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 19:6</span>, Paul laid hands on believers, and they received the Holy Spirit, showing its role in spiritual empowerment.

<!-- p120 -->
However, the Bible also gives warnings about the practice:

<!-- p121 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p122 -->
> **<u>1 Timothy 5:22</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Do not lay hands on anyone hastily, nor share in other people's sins; keep yourself pure.
>

<!-- p123 -->
</span>

<!-- p124 -->
This highlights the importance of discernment and spiritual maturity when appointing individuals to leadership positions. As we studied in the chapter on the church, leaders must be tested and approved to avoid promoting unqualified or immature believers who could cause harm to the Body of Christ.

<!-- p125 -->
Some additional guidelines for the laying on of hands:

<!-- p126 -->
1.  Contextually appropriate - the person on whom hands are laid should be a willing participant.

<!-- p127 -->
2.  Physically appropriate - the laying on of hands does not involve sexual or sensual touching. In practice, placing a hand on the shoulder, the hand, or the forehead are generally accepted places to touch (with permission) in the Western world.

<!-- p128 -->
3.  Culturally appropriate - in some cultures it is not appropriate for men and women to have contact unless they are related by blood or marriage.

<!-- p129 -->
The laying on of hands remains today a powerful expression of faith and obedience in the Church. It serves as a means of blessing, healing, sending, and equipping, always pointing to the authority of Christ and the work of the Holy Spirit. Whether praying for the sick, sending missionaries, or commissioning leaders, it is a reminder that believers do not operate in their own power, but under the authority of Jesus Christ and in alignment with His purposes.

<!-- p130 -->
Now that we have studied the authority of Jesus and the authority of the believer, we will talk about the other beings of the spiritual realm, angels and demons.

<!-- p131 -->
## Angels and Demons

<!-- p132 -->
### Angels

<!-- p133 -->
Angels are a significant part of God's creation, and they serve as His messengers and agents to carry out His divine purposes. The Bible reveals a spiritual realm inhabited by these beings, who are powerful, intelligent, and obedient to God's commands. Although they are often invisible, their presence and actions impact human history and spiritual warfare. Understanding their functions, classifications, and activities gives us a deeper understanding of God's plan and the invisible realities that surround us.

<!-- p134 -->
The term "angel" itself means "messenger," which underscores their primary function. Angels act in obedience to the will of God, whether delivering messages, providing protection, executing judgment, or participating in spiritual battles. They are not objects of worship, but serve as examples of obedience and reverence, always pointing to the glory of God. This chapter explores the nature, classifications, and roles of angels, shedding light on their importance in Scripture and in our lives today.

<!-- p135 -->
In Scripture, angels are seen as **messengers**, **servants**, and **warriors**. Angels frequently act as messengers, delivering important announcements, warnings, and encouragement. They reveal God's plans and provide guidance in times of need (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26-38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 1:26-38</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 2:13</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 1:1</span>). Angels are actively involved in protecting, strengthening, and delivering believers. They minister to God's people in times of distress and provide supernatural help (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="91" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalms 91:11</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 12:7-11</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 4:11</span>). Angels engage in spiritual warfare, combating demonic forces to advance the kingdom of God and defend believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="DAN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Daniel 10:13</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:7-9</span>).

<!-- p136 -->
#### The Nature of Angels

<!-- p137 -->
Angels are created beings, brought into existence by God before the foundation of the world. They are distinct from humans and possess unique characteristics that equip them for their heavenly functions. The Bible teaches that angels are spiritual beings, not subject to physical limitations, although they can take on visible forms when interacting with humans.

<!-- p138 -->
Angels are described as immortal, which means they do not die or age. They are innumerable, with vast armies filling the heavenly realms. Although they are often referred to with masculine pronouns, Scripture makes it clear that angels are sexless and do not marry or reproduce. These qualities emphasize their purpose as servants of God, entirely dedicated to His will and His commands. Angels possess remarkable traits that reflect their divine purpose. They are obedient, reverent, wise, and powerful. These qualities enable them to carry out God's will effectively and faithfully.

[See Table 8](../tables/table8.md)

<!-- p140 -->
Although the Bible highlights various roles and titles for angels, it does not provide a comprehensive classification system. However, some angels are identified by specific functions or roles:

<!-- p141 -->
- **The Angel of the Lord** - Often considered a preincarnate appearance of Christ, manifesting with divine authority. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="23" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 23:20-23</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JDG" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Judges 6:11-24</span>*)

<!-- p142 -->
- **Archangels** - High-ranking angels such as Michael, who leads armies, and Gabriel, who delivers divine messages. (*Jude 9; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:7</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 1:19-26</span>*)

<!-- p143 -->
- **Cherubim** - Guardians of the glory and the presence of God, as in Eden and the Ark of the Covenant. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 3:24</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 25:22</span>*)

<!-- p144 -->
- **Seraphim** - Worshipers of God, who continually declare His holiness. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 6:1-6</span>*)

<!-- p145 -->
- **Elect angels** - Faithful angels who resisted Satan’s rebellion and remain loyal to God. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:21</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 25:41</span>*)

<!-- p146 -->
These classifications emphasize the diversity and organization within the angelic realm, but their primary focus is always to fulfill God’s purposes.

<!-- p147 -->
#### Human interaction with angels

<!-- p148 -->
Human beings often encounter angels in Scripture, but we are never instructed to give them orders or to pray to them. Instead, angels act according to God’s direction. They minister to believers as **God’s servants** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 1:14</span>). Their interactions include protection, guidance, and deliverance, always pointing us to God’s power and sovereignty.

<!-- p149 -->
- **Warnings against the worship of angels**: Scripture forbids worshiping angels or seeking encounters with them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:18</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 22:8-9</span>).

<!-- p150 -->
- **Prayer to God, not to angels**: Believers should pray to God, who sends angels as He wills (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="91" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalms 91:11</span>).

<!-- p151 -->
- **Encouragement and confidence**: Angels remind us of God’s care, but our faith must rest fully in God, not in angels.

<!-- p152 -->
Angels are awe-inspiring beings created to serve God and minister to His people. Although their work is often invisible, Scripture offers glimpses of their activity, revealing their faithfulness, strength, and reverence. Angels remind us of God’s provision and protection, but they always point us to His glory. As believers, we are called to trust in God’s care, knowing that angels operate as His servants to accomplish His divine purposes.

<!-- p153 -->
### Demons

<!-- p154 -->
Just as the study of angels is necessary to understand the spiritual world, so is the study of demons. Scripture clearly describes their activity, their influence, and their defeat through Jesus Christ. Recognizing their role in spiritual warfare helps believers to stand firm in the faith without fear.

<!-- p155 -->
#### Origin and nature of demons

<!-- p156 -->
Demons are fallen angels who followed Satan’s rebellion against God. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:7-9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 25:41</span>).* Satan, formerly known as Lucifer (“light-bearer”), an exalted angel, tried to exalt himself above God and was cast out of heaven along with other rebellious angels. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 14:12-15</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EZK" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ezekiel 28:12-17</span>).*

<!-- p157 -->
Demons are spirits without physical bodies but can oppress and torment human beings and animals. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 5:11-13</span>).* They seek to deceive, tempt, oppress, and destroy. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 11:14</span>). Unlike God,* they have limits and are not omnipresent (everywhere), omniscient (all-knowing), omnipotent (almighty)*.* Their ultimate defeat is certain, and they will face eternal punishment. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 20:10</span>)*

<!-- p158 -->
Scripture describes the work and activity of the devil as:

<!-- p159 -->
- Opposes the work of God *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Thessalonians 2:18</span>)*

<!-- p160 -->
- Deceives the world *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 4:4</span>)*

<!-- p161 -->
- Tempts and accuses believers *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 22:31</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:10</span>)*

<!-- p162 -->
- Afflicts and harms *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JOB" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Job 1:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 13:16</span>)*

<!-- p163 -->
There are many names and descriptions for the devil, each describing a different aspect of his work.

<!-- p164 -->
[See Table 9](../tables/table9.md)

<!-- p165 -->
The Gospels and the Epistles further clarify the work of the enemy. Evil spirits, being disembodied, must largely do their work through willing and open vessels. God works through people; so does the devil. This oppression is often characterized by torment, self-harm, and destructive behaviors *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 8:27-29</span>).* His attacks cause fear, temptation, and discouragement *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 12:7</span>).* The most common source of his power (not authority) in a person's life is through the influence of lies and emotional strongholds *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 10:3-5</span>).*

<!-- p166 -->
It is important to note that not every lie that a person believes, illness in their body, or stronghold in their life is the direct result of demonic influence, but these places of wounding are places where the enemy can take advantage of us and keep us in a place of frustration and oppression.

<!-- p167 -->
#### The authority of Jesus over the works of the enemy

<!-- p168 -->
Do not be afraid, Jesus has complete and total authority over the works of the enemy. Jesus demonstrated power over demons during His ministry *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 1:34</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 5:1-20</span>).* The works of the enemy and Satan himself are defeated through the death and resurrection of Jesus *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:15</span>).*

<!-- p169 -->
As believers we have been given authority delegated by Jesus to cast out demons in His name:

<!-- p170 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p171 -->
> **<u>Mark 16:17</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And these signs will follow those who believe: In My name they will cast out demons; they will speak with new tongues;
>

<!-- p172 -->
</span>

<!-- p173 -->
Additionally, as heirs of salvation we have the authority to submit to God, resist the devil, and he will flee from us! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 4:7</span>).

<!-- p174 -->
To deal with an evil or unclean spirit the pattern expressed in Scripture is to take authority over it in the name of Jesus and command it to leave. The Holy Spirit promises to give us wisdom and discernment if we ask! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:5</span>). We do not need to turn it into a production, nor into a drama like in the movies, nor even talk to them, that is a trick of the devil to distract us. Satan's power is established and maintained only by the lies he can get us to believe.

<!-- p175 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="18" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p176 -->
> **<u>Matthew 18:18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> "Assuredly, I say to you, whatever you bind on earth will be bound in heaven, and whatever you loose on earth will be loosed in heaven.
>

<!-- p177 -->
</span>

<!-- p178 -->
We speak to them in the name of Jesus and command them to leave.

<!-- p179 -->
There are two main warnings in Scripture when dealing with the demonic:

<!-- p180 -->
1.  **Only those who have a relationship with Jesus have authority over the demonic**. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 19:11-20</span> speaks of the sons of a Jewish chief priest named Sceva. They tried to intervene in the life of someone who was oppressed and tormented by the devil, and the evil spirit refused to leave because they did not have a relationship with Jesus. The man who was oppressed pounced on them, stripped them, and beat them. Just as you should not impersonate a police officer, **¡do not impersonate someone who has a relationship with Jesus, instead have a relationship with Jesus**!

<!-- p181 -->
2.  **When an evil spirit leaves, it goes away for a time and will try to return**. If it finds the place where it had dwelt empty, it will come back worse. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="43-45" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 12:43-45</span>. When we minister to a person oppressed by the devil, that person needs to fill the void that that evil spirit occupied in their life. That means, at a minimum, establishing a personal relationship with Jesus and strengthening it through obedience to the Holy Spirit. Often, an evil or unclean spirit has power to operate in someone’s life through the decisions they have made, based on their beliefs. Anyone who is delivered from the devil needs to replace that old belief system and be transformed by the renewing of their mind (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:1-2</span>).

<!-- p182 -->
We serve an awesome and powerful God, and we are ‘more than conquerors’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="37" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:37</span>) over the defeated enemy. We have no need to fear our defeated enemy, because Jesus has given us His authority to crush his head. ¡The devil’s time is short, and he will finally be judged along with every principality and power on earth that rejects the rule of Christ!
