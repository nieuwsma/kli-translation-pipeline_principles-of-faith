# Chapter 9 - The Church &amp; Our Role in It

<!-- p1 -->
The church is the way God reveals Himself on the earth-through His people, doing His work. We are called to be His body, we are His hands and feet to carry out His work and purpose. Without our efforts the harvest will remain in the field and will go unharvested.

<!-- p2 -->
## Who Is the Church?

<!-- p3 -->
The church, ***Ekklesia** (eh-klee-SEE-ah - **G1577**),* is derived from two Greek words:

<!-- p4 -->
- **"Ek"**: "Out of."

<!-- p5 -->
- **"Kaleo"**: "To call."

<!-- p6 -->
Together, **Ekklesia** literally means "called out" or "assembly of the called out." In the city-states of ancient Greece, it referred to a gathering of citizens convened for a specific purpose, such as legislative or judicial meetings. It was a secular term denoting an assembly of people.

<!-- p7 -->
In the New Testament, it is used primarily to refer to the community of believers in Jesus Christ. It can denote:

<!-- p8 -->
- **The Local Church**: A specific gathering of believers in a particular place (e.g., the church in Corinth, Philippi, etc.).

<!-- p9 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p10 -->
> **<u>1 Corinthians 1:2</u>** (NKJV)
>
> To the church of God which is at Corinth, to those who are sanctified in Christ Jesus, called to be saints, with all who in every place call on the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, both theirs and ours:
>

<!-- p11 -->
</span>

<!-- p12 -->
- **The Universal Church**: The collective body of all believers across time and space.

<!-- p13 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p14 -->
> **<u>Matthew 16:18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And I also say to you that you are Peter, and on this rock I will build My church, and the gates of Hades shall not prevail against it.
>

<!-- p15 -->
</span>

<!-- p16 -->
The church is the people, not the building or the place where they gather. The Called Out Ones: Reflects the idea that Christians are called out of the world and set apart for God's purposes (e.g., <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:9</span>) for a specific mission and purpose (the Great Commission). Technically, the church is any gathering of two or more believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="18" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 18:20</span>). Typically, the church is organized into regular meetings, perhaps with an established place and time, but wherever two or more people gather in the name of Jesus, there is the church!

<!-- p17 -->
The church was established by Jesus and grew in the first century A.D. through the work of the disciples who followed Him. The church that we have and experience today in all its local bodies throughout the earth is the direct result of the original work of Christ and His apostles. As believers we must be committed to a local church for fellowship, provision, equipping for ministry, and to do good works!

<!-- p18 -->
To help us understand what the church is, the Scriptures give us powerful images that show what it is meant to be.

<!-- p19 -->
## Understanding the Church

<!-- p20 -->
There are several fundamental metaphorical images used to represent the attributes of the church:

<!-- p21 -->
1.  The Body

<!-- p22 -->
2.  The Bride

<!-- p23 -->
3.  The Temple

<!-- p24 -->
4.  The Family

<!-- p25 -->
5.  The Flock

<!-- p26 -->
6.  The Vine and the Branches

<!-- p27 -->
Each analogy highlights a unique aspect of the church, but together they reveal the church’s identity as a unified and diverse community under the leadership of Christ.

<!-- p28 -->
### The Body

<!-- p29 -->
The church is often described as **the Body of Christ**, emphasizing both its unity and its diversity. With Christ as the head, the church functions as a living organism where each believer plays an essential and potentially diverse role. Each member has unique gifts and contributes to the health and mission of the body according to his or her own function. This image highlights the interdependence of believers—specifically, that the body must be connected to function, and that our role is collaborative, not competitive. When each part works together, the body grows and thrives in unity and love (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:12-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:15-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 1:18</span>).

<!-- p30 -->
### The Bride

<!-- p31 -->
The church is described as **the Bride of Christ**; this reflects an intimate and loving relationship with Jesus. Just as a bride is loved and prepared for her husband, the church is sanctified and purified by Christ’s sacrificial love. This metaphor captures the devotion and faithfulness expected of the church, as well as the deep affection that Christ has for his people. The image also points to the ultimate union between Christ and the church, celebrated at the marriage supper of the Lamb, where the church is presented in glory without spot or blemish (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="25-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:25-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 19:7-9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="21" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 21:2</span>).

<!-- p32 -->
### The Temple

<!-- p33 -->
The church is described as **the Temple of God**, the dwelling place of His Spirit. Believers are described as living stones, being built together upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets, with Christ himself the cornerstone. This holy structure signifies the presence of God among His people and its role as a community of worship and prayer. The image of the temple underscores the importance of holiness and the church’s collective purpose to glorify God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 3:16-17</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:19-22</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:4-5</span>).

<!-- p34 -->
### The Family

<!-- p35 -->
The church is described as **the Family of God**, emphasizing relational unity and care among its members. Through the Spirit of adoption, believers are made children of God and members of His household. This family image highlights the love, sense of belonging, and shared inheritance that believers have in Christ. It also reflects the call to treat one another with compassion and humility as brothers and sisters in the family of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:19</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:15-17</span>). In addition, through the image of the family it is shown how the members of the church are to become mature, being taught and nourished by spiritual mothers and fathers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:1-2</span>), so that we may pass from being children drinking the pure milk of the Word to being mature adults able to eat the solid food of the Word (Hebrews 5).

<!-- p36 -->
### The Flock

<!-- p37 -->
The church is described as **the Flock**, sheep under the care of Jesus, the Good Shepherd. This image conveys the church’s dependence on Christ for guidance, sustenance, and protection. It also emphasizes the responsibility of church leaders to shepherd God’s flock faithfully as undershepherds, reflecting Christ’s example of love and care. The image of the flock speaks of trust, obedience, and the close relationship between the Great Shepherd and His sheep (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:14-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:2</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>).

<!-- p38 -->
### The Vine and the Branches

<!-- p39 -->
The church is described as **the Vine and the Branches**, illustrating the essential connection between believers and Christ. Jesus is the true vine, and believers are the branches who must abide in Him to bear fruit. This metaphor highlights dependence on Christ for spiritual vitality, growth, and productivity. Without Him, believers can do nothing, but by abiding in Him, they glorify God and fulfill their purpose. Jesus will prune so that more fruit may be borne in our lives (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:1-5</span>).

<!-- p40 -->
## Roles of the Church

<!-- p41 -->
The mission of the church, like the role of the Holy Spirit, can be understood through three dimensions: upward, inward, and outward, each reflecting a unique aspect of its calling in God’s plan. These dimensions guide the church in fulfilling its role as a worshiping, edifying, and missional community.

<!-- p42 -->
### Upward: Worship and Glorification of God

<!-- p43 -->
The church’s first purpose is to worship and glorify God. Corporate worship, where believers gather to exalt God together, is a visible reflection of His glory and a response to His greatness. As the psalmist declares: “*Worship the Lord in the beauty of holiness; tremble before Him, all the earth*” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="96" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 96:9</span>). Beyond corporate worship, the church is called to live lives of obedience and sacrifice, offering themselves as “living sacrifices” as an act of spiritual worship (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:1-2</span>). This upward focus keeps the church centered on the majesty of God and aligns its priorities with His will.

<!-- p44 -->
### Inward: Building up the body

<!-- p45 -->
The Great Commission commands us to ‘*teach them to obey’*, this mandate goes far beyond formal teaching methods like this book. Discipleship (addressed more fully in the chapter on discipleship) consists of shaping the follower into the likeness of the discipline, as a father raises his child. Good parents don’t give a five-year-old an encyclopedia and challenge them to figure it out! Rather they model, demonstrate, correct, guide, nurture, love, care, and shepherd.

<!-- p46 -->
Parents sacrifice for their children because they love them, because they are part of the same body, because they share the same blood. As members of the body and family of Christ, we share the same body, we share the same blood. Communion reminds us, through its symbolism, that we have been united to one another through the sacrifice of Christ. The act of communion should be a point of unity, reminding us that we all need redemption and that salvation comes only from God. In this unity we see that no part of the body is greater than another, except our head, Christ.

<!-- p47 -->
### Outward: Reaching the world

<!-- p48 -->
The most well-known part of the church’s mission reaches outward to bring the gospel to the world. Jesus’ Great Commission calls His followers to evangelize and make disciples of all nations (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19-20</span>). Empowered by the Holy Spirit, the church is to be His witnesses to the ends of the earth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>).

<!-- p49 -->
Jesus is the light of the world (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 8:12</span>). Light shines outward in all directions from the source. His light shines through its manifestations: the Word, His Spirit, and us, His body. We are the light of the world; in our lives we must radiate His light into our darkened worlds and demonstrate good works that glorify God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 5:14-16</span>). In practice, this means that the church is called to advocate for justice and mercy, both spiritual and physical. As James says, our pure worship is to care for the vulnerable, the orphans, the widows (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:27</span>).

<!-- p50 -->
## Church Leadership

<!-- p51 -->
Across Christendom there is a vast variety of denominations, gatherings, congregations, and assemblies. There are very few universal rules for church leadership, but the ones we do have are very significant:

<!-- p52 -->
1.  Jesus is the sole head of the Church. All serve Him (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 1:18</span>).

<!-- p53 -->
2.  There are high standards for those who lead; they are worthy of double honor, because they will receive double judgment (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:17</span>).

<!-- p54 -->
2.1  They must be mature, not conceited, responsible (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:1-13</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 1:5-9</span>).

<!-- p55 -->
2.2.  They must be servants of all (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42-45" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 10:42-45</span>).

<!-- p56 -->
2.3.  They must devote themselves to the Word and to prayer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:4</span>).

<!-- p57 -->
2.4.  They are responsible for equipping the body for the work of ministry.

<!-- p58 -->
3.  There are no greater or lesser members in the body. The Scriptures that support this are:

<!-- p59 -->
3.1.  There must be no partiality between rich and poor (James 2).

<!-- p60 -->
3.2. There is no division between Jew and Gentile, slave or free, male or female (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 3:27-28</span>).

<!-- p61 -->
The structure of the Church, beyond leadership rules, is important but much of it is situational and a matter of tradition rather than universally dictated. “<u>This is how we’ve always done it!</u>” is a popular claim; if you go back far enough, there was usually a good reason why they did it that way. This, in general, is neither good nor bad, but a matter of taste, needs, and availability.

<!-- p62 -->
A helpful example of tradition being the foundation of structure is family dinner time. The most important part of the family dinner is that everyone eats and that the food is nutritious. The variations are endless: what they eat, how they eat, how they season their food, etc. The various combinations are generally acceptable and largely a matter of taste and preference.

<!-- p63 -->
A church with 10,000 members has different needs and capacities than a church of 50. The pattern established in the book of Acts and throughout the New Testament canon was the model of **elders** and **deacons**. Although leadership structures may look different among churches and denominations, ultimately this model represents the minimally viable leadership structure for the church. Let’s take a look at these leadership offices and how they are selected.

<!-- p64 -->
### Elders (Bishops)

<!-- p65 -->
Role and responsibilities:

<!-- p66 -->
- **Spiritual leadership** - Provide oversight, guidance, and pastoral care for the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:2</span>).

<!-- p67 -->
- **Teaching and preaching** - Responsible for teaching sound doctrine and protecting against false teaching (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:17</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 1:9</span>).

<!-- p68 -->
- **Shepherd the flock** - Care for the spiritual needs of the congregation, modeling humility and service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:2-3</span>).

<!-- p69 -->
- **Protect the Church** - Serve as watchmen, defending the church from false doctrine and division (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29-31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:29-31</span>).

<!-- p70 -->
- **Equip the saints** - Train and equip believers for works of ministry and discipleship (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-12</span>).

<!-- p71 -->
Selection process:

<!-- p72 -->
- **Appointed in every church** - Elders were appointed by the apostles or their representatives, often through prayer and fasting (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 14:23</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 1:5</span>).

<!-- p73 -->
- **Confirmed by the Holy Spirit** - Their appointment was considered Spirit-led, recognizing God’s calling and gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>).

<!-- p74 -->
- **Tested and approved** - Candidates had to demonstrate maturity and faithfulness before being entrusted with leadership (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:10</span>). They cannot be newly converted! ((<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:6</span>)

<!-- p75 -->
### Deacons (Servants)

<!-- p76 -->
Role and responsibilities:

<!-- p77 -->
- **Practical service** - Focused on meeting physical needs in the church, freeing the elders to concentrate on prayer and teaching (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-4</span>).

<!-- p78 -->
- **Support ministry** - They help with hospitality, care for the poor, and administrative tasks (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:3</span>).

<!-- p79 -->
- **Promote unity** - They address issues of fairness and care to prevent division (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-7</span>).

<!-- p80 -->
- **Examples of faith** - They serve as models in humility, faith, and service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:13</span>).

<!-- p81 -->
Selection process (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-6</span>):

<!-- p82 -->
- **Recognized by the congregation** - Chosen according to their reputation and spiritual character.

<!-- p83 -->
- **Approved by the leaders** - The apostles reviewed and commissioned the chosen deacons through prayer and the laying on of hands.

<!-- p84 -->
- **Confirmed in service** - The deacons were first put to the test to demonstrate their faithfulness before being officially appointed (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:10</span>).

<!-- p85 -->
### Qualifications for leadership

<!-- p86 -->
#### Qualifications for both elders and deacons:

<!-- p87 -->
- Moral character - Both must be above reproach, dignified, and demonstrate self-control (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:2</span>, 8).

<!-- p88 -->
- Family life - Both must be faithful in marriage (“husband of one wife”) and manage their households well (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:4-5</span>, 12).

<!-- p89 -->
- Spiritual maturity - Both must be tested and approved before their appointment (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:6</span>, 10).

<!-- p90 -->
- Reputation - Both need a good reputation inside and outside the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:7</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
- Faith and integrity - Both must hold the faith with a clean conscience and live according to their beliefs (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:9</span>).

<!-- p92 -->
### Example structures

<!-- p93 -->
Using what we have learned so far about rules and structures for leadership, let’s look at two examples of church structure found in the New Testament, Jerusalem and Antioch.

<!-- p94 -->
### Church in Jerusalem

<!-- p95 -->
The church in Jerusalem was led primarily by the **apostles**, who focused on teaching, prayer, and spiritual oversight (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:42</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:4</span>). As the community grew, the need for an administrative structure became evident, particularly to address practical matters such as the care of widows and the distribution of resources. This led to the appointment of **deacons** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-7</span>). These individuals, chosen for their spiritual maturity and good reputation, took charge of practical ministries to allow the apostles to focus on their spiritual responsibilities. Over time, **elders** also emerged as leaders in the church in Jerusalem. For example, during the Jerusalem Council (Acts 15), both apostles and elders participated in decision-making, showing a collaborative leadership structure.

<!-- p96 -->
### Church in Antioch

<!-- p97 -->
The church in Antioch had a more decentralized and diverse leadership structure compared to Jerusalem. It was led by a group of **prophets and teachers**, including Barnabas, Saul (Paul), Simeon, Lucius, and Manaen (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 13:1</span>). The church in Antioch was also much more oriented toward reaching the Gentiles, whereas the church in Jerusalem more clearly reflected its Jewish roots. Although the New Testament does not explicitly mention deacons in Antioch, practical roles likely existed given the growing size of the church and its involvement in sending aid to Jerusalem (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29-30" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 11:29-30</span>). The church in Antioch became a model for commissioning missionaries, with leaders praying, fasting, and laying hands on Paul and Barnabas to send them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 13:2-3</span>). This demonstrates a structure that balanced spiritual oversight (prophets and teachers) with missional outreach and practical support.

<!-- p98 -->
Unlike Paul’s corrections to the Corinthians and Galatians, Scripture does not criticize the leadership structures of Jerusalem or Antioch. This suggests that these churches, under the guidance of the Holy Spirit, were free to adapt their gatherings to meet their unique needs and circumstances within the framework of sound doctrine and apostolic teaching (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:42</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>).

<!-- p99 -->
Throughout these passages certain terms are used that we have not yet defined and studied: *apostle, prophet, evangelist*. These gifts are part of the fivefold ministry that Jesus gave to the church.

<!-- p100 -->
### The gifts of the fivefold ministry

<!-- p101 -->
Jesus established five distinct ministry gifts, often referred to as the fivefold ministry gifts, to equip believers for service and build up the Body of Christ.

<!-- p102 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p103 -->
> **<u>Ephesians 4:11-13</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And He Himself gave some to be apostles, some prophets, some evangelists, and some pastors and teachers,
>
> for the equipping of the saints for the work of ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ,
>
> till we all come to the unity of the faith and of the knowledge of the Son of God, to a perfect man, to the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ;
>

<!-- p104 -->
</span>

<!-- p105 -->
The five gifts given to equip the saints for the work of ministry are:

<!-- p106 -->
1.  **Apostles** - Pioneers and visionaries who lay foundations, plant churches, and advance the Kingdom of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:20</span>). Apostles model courage and faith, demonstrating how to move into new territories and take spiritual initiative.

<!-- p107 -->
2.  **Prophets** - Messengers and guides who proclaim God’s truth, bringing encouragement, edification, and direction (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>). Prophets inspire faith, call others to align with the Word of God, and demonstrate dependence on hearing the voice of God.

<!-- p108 -->
3.  **Evangelists** - Proclaimers of the Gospel who focus on reaching the lost and moving others to action in sharing Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:5-8</span>). Evangelists teach disciples to share their faith effectively, model boldness in witnessing, and inspire compassion for the lost.

<!-- p109 -->
4.  **Pastors (Pastors)** - Caregivers and nurturers who protect, guide, and provide spiritual oversight for the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>). Pastors teach disciples the importance of community, caring for others, and accountability within the body of Christ.

<!-- p110 -->
5.  **Teachers** - Instructors and shapers who clarify doctrine and help believers grow in understanding and application of the Word of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 2:15</span>). Teachers equip disciples to study the Scriptures deeply, apply them in their daily lives, and teach others effectively.

<!-- p111 -->
## Leadership versus gifts

<!-- p112 -->
A commonly misunderstood aspect of leadership in the church is the separation between the authority and responsibility of leadership, and the specific gifts.

<!-- p113 -->
This confusion centers on our use of words like teacher, pastor, prophet, apostle, evangelist. These words are gifts. There is no office of ‘apostle’ in the New Testament church. There are people who do the work of an apostle, who have the gift or calling of apostle. Having the gift of apostle does not give them authority or responsibility.

<!-- p114 -->
I am a skilled musician; I have played saxophone for more than 20 years. I played throughout school and college and played semi-professionally for several summers while working with an evangelistic team. Despite having the skill, the gift, and the ability to be a saxophonist, I do not have the right to walk into your church and get on stage with the worship team.

<!-- p115 -->
In the same way, there are people with the gift of being a pastor/pastor, but that does not make them ‘THE PASTOR’ of the local church nor does it make them ‘your’ pastor. The fivefold ministry gifts do not lead the church; the leaders (elders and deacons) do. It is very common for leaders to have specific fivefold ministry gifts, but that gift enhances their leadership; it does not create it. Likewise, it is common for people still in development with fivefold ministry gifts to serve and aspire to leadership, but God cares first about character, then He cares about gifts. We are all to become mature, able to discern the word of God, filled with the Spirit, and doing good works.

<!-- p116 -->
Every leader and every gift must first submit to being a disciple, to being part of the body.

<!-- p117 -->
Jesus gave specific ministry gifts for the equipping of the saints for the work of ministry. These are different from the motivational gifts (addressed later in this chapter) or the manifestation gifts (addressed in the chapter on baptisms). As disciples grow, the fivefold ministry gifts equip them for ministry, while the elders and deacons provide oversight to ensure sound doctrine and practice.

<!-- p118 -->
## Your role in the church

<!-- p119 -->
So far in this chapter we have covered church leadership and the central analogies for the church. Let’s build on the specific analogy of *the body* and consider a marathon runner.

<!-- p120 -->
### You are part of the whole

<!-- p121 -->
The church, as the Body of Christ, depends on each member working together to fulfill its mission. Just as the body cannot function properly if one part is missing, the church needs every believer to participate actively, grow, and serve. Every member of the body has a very important role. Being part of the body means staying connected. A body can survive with diminished capacity without a member, but the member itself cannot live separated from the body. We must be an active part of the church and not become dismembered. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:24</span> says that we must gather together and not abandon the assembly.

<!-- p122 -->
To truly work and function together you need to have fellowship! You need to know others, love others, and, ideally, to like others! Build strong relationships and friendships and repair them when they are strained or injured. When a joint is injured or inflamed, you need to work to rest and/or reset it; so that the joint that is out of place does not end up breaking. A broken member that does not heal will eventually be separated, and you cannot be part of the body, by yourself.

<!-- p123 -->
We do not all have the same function. Some of us may share the Gospel from a stage, others around a table. But we are ALL called to be ministers of reconciliation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 5:18-19</span>). We have all been received into the priesthood of believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:9</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 1:6</span>). This means that we are all meant to be able to do the work of the ministry by the power of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p124 -->
Toes work with the feet, the feet with the ankles, and so on-each part contributing to the overall mission. Marathon runners must train if they want to be able to complete the race. You must be ready-not only learning, but actually practicing what you have learned. The church, like a marathon runner, thrives when each member plays its role in unison.

<!-- p125 -->
There are a great many people in your life whom only you can reach. The Pastor cannot be the only one trying to shepherd them, and the Evangelist cannot be the only one trying to reach them. God wants to use you to reach unbelievers by the power of His Holy Spirit. We all do the work of the ministry; the Holy Spirit helps us.

<!-- p126 -->
### You need training

<!-- p127 -->
Believers must train and equip themselves for their unique functions in the church, just as a runner strengthens specific muscles for the race. You will not succeed if you launch into a marathon and have not trained, and not all training is the same, you need to be properly prepared for the race. Jesus has given specific ministry gifts to equip believers, not to do all the work themselves, but to enable each member to serve effectively in their unique functions.

<!-- p128 -->
Training for a marathon will bring transformation. You need strength and you need to be transformed. It is our responsibility to submit to God and let Him transform us in renewal (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:1-2</span>). Transformation should lead you to maturity, enabling you to carry your own burdens and support others. You should be able to bear MUCH fruit. This will be covered extensively in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p129 -->
Training is hard, sometimes it is uncomfortable. Sometimes the transformation you need to go through you would prefer to skip. But if you want to achieve the goal, you need to remain on the narrow path. At times, you might need warning, comfort, or support-all offered with patience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Thessalonians 5:14</span>).

<!-- p130 -->
Every great marathon runner has a coach who trains them; you will need a coach to help you. Of course, you have the Holy Spirit, but He often relies on His coaches, the mature men and women of God, in the Body of Christ alongside whom you are called to work.

<!-- p131 -->
As individuals, we need to submit first of all to God and, second, in this context, to a body of believers. Submission in the church means honoring godly leadership (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 13:17</span>) and submitting to one another in humility (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:21</span>).

<!-- p132 -->
Submission can be difficult, especially when you have experienced broken trust or failed leadership before. This does not imply blind obedience or control, but rather reflects a spirit of reverence and cooperation. Worldly submission is demanded by those in authority and can be demeaning and lead to resentment. Biblical submission, rooted in humility and discernment, strengthens the church and fosters accountability. Be willing to listen to guidance and constructive feedback.

<!-- p133 -->
Now that we have laid a solid foundation - you a significant part of the body but you need training - let us move on to finding where you fit.

<!-- p134 -->
### Finding where you fit

<!-- p135 -->
Finding your role in the church involves seeking God's guidance, understanding your spiritual gifts, and actively participating in your church community. It is a journey of prayer, exploration, and growth, where you discover how God has uniquely equipped you to contribute to His kingdom. It is very important to point out that your role in the church will likely change over time and not be the same throughout your whole life.

<!-- p136 -->
Here is a practical approach to help you find your place, presented as a series of steps for clarity, but you will likely repeat the same step multiple times throughout your walk with God.

<!-- p137 -->
#### Step 1 - Seek God's guidance through prayer and the Word

<!-- p138 -->
Begin by **seeking God's guidance through prayer**. God is the supreme source of your purpose and calling (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="29" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 29:11</span>). Pray regularly for clarity about how you can serve in the church, asking Him to reveal your spiritual gifts and passions to you. Trust that He will direct your steps as you surrender your plans to His will (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 3:5-6</span>).

<!-- p139 -->
**Study the Word of God** to deepen your understanding of the mission of the church and your role within it. Key passages such as 1 Corinthians 12 and <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-12</span> highlight the spiritual gifts and the diversity of functions in the Body of Christ. Reflect on examples of people in Scripture who discovered and fulfilled their roles, such as Paul, Timothy, and Lydia.

<!-- p140 -->
#### Step 2 - Identify your gifts, passions, and skills

<!-- p141 -->
Identifying your **spiritual gifts** is an essential step. It may be helpful to take a spiritual gifts assessment. These gifts are given by God to build up the church. There are several kinds of gifts listed in the New Testament:

<!-- p142 -->
- **Motivational gifts** given by God: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:6-8</span>

<!-- p143 -->
- They are discussed after this list.

<!-- p144 -->
- Fivefold ministry gifts given by Jesus: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-13</span>

<!-- p145 -->
- They are discussed earlier in this chapter.

<!-- p146 -->
- Manifestation gifts given by the Holy Spirit: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span>

<!-- p147 -->
- They are discussed in the chapter on baptism.

<!-- p148 -->
The **motivational gifts** reflect how a believer is predisposed to think, feel, and act. They motivate believers to serve in specific ways that build up the church. Each gift complements the others, ensuring that the church functions as a united body with diverse roles (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:4-5</span>). They operate as grace-given abilities (Greek: ***charis** - khar'-ece - **G5463 -** an unearned gift*), which means they are given by God, not earned or learned. Although the gifts come from God, a person who has a gift can become more competent in its use.

<!-- p149 -->
The motivational gifts are:

<!-- p150 -->
1.  **Prophecy** - A God-given inclination to boldly declare truth, often with a focus on conviction and righteousness, guiding others to align with the Word of God.\*

<!-- p151 -->
2.  **Service (Helps)** - Meets practical needs through acts of service and assistance to support others.

<!-- p152 -->
3.  **Teaching** - Explains and clarifies truth, helping others understand and apply the Word of God.

<!-- p153 -->
4.  **Exhortation (Encouragement)** - Motivates, encourages, and comforts others to inspire growth and perseverance.

<!-- p154 -->
5.  **Giving** - Provides resources generously to meet needs and advance the work of God without seeking recognition.

<!-- p155 -->
6.  **Leadership (Administration)** - Organizes and directs others to achieve goals with vision and efficiency.

<!-- p156 -->
7.  **Mercy** - Shows compassion and care to comfort and support those who are suffering or in need.

<!-- p157 -->
\* Prophecy as used in the context of Romans is understood as different from prophecy as a manifestation gift of the Holy Spirit (1 Corinthians). The Greek word used is the same in both Scriptures, but the context is different. For a deeper study of the difference, I recommend reading:

<!-- p158 -->
- *The Gift of Prophecy* by Kenneth E. Hagin ([www.rhema.org](http://www.rhema.org))

<!-- p159 -->
<!-- -->

<!-- p160 -->
- *The Gifts and Ministries of the Holy Spirit* by Lester Sumrall

<!-- p161 -->
As you identify your gifts, consider your **passions and abilities**. God often calls us to serve in areas we are passionate about or in which we are skilled (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 31:2-6</span>). Reflect on what excites you about serving God and others, and think about how your professional skills, hobbies, and life experiences could contribute to ministry.

<!-- p162 -->
Seek the guidance of **leaders and mentors** who can provide wisdom and insight (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 15:22</span>). Meet with a pastor or ministry leader to talk about your interests and ask for honest feedback on where you might fit best. Pray about the feedback you receive. Consider joining a discipleship group to receive training and ongoing support.

<!-- p163 -->
#### Step 3 - Be faithful now

<!-- p164 -->
The parable of the talents (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-30" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 25:14-30</span>) reminds us that we need to be faithful in little to be trustworthy in much (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 16:10</span>).

<!-- p165 -->
**Get involved and serve** in various ministries to explore where God is calling you. Start small by offering your time as a volunteer in areas of need within your church and in the ministries you support. For example:

<!-- p166 -->
- Join the hospitality team, the children's ministry, or outreach programs. Consider leading a discipleship-oriented group for new believers on this book *Principles of Faith,* using the group discussion questions and the activities at the end of each chapter to facilitate dialogue.

<!-- p167 -->
- Stay open to stepping out of your comfort zone to discover new opportunities.

<!-- p168 -->
- Notice where God works through you and where you feel most effective.

<!-- p169 -->
Practice giving. We can give to the church in many important ways, and we are instructed to do so to fulfill the mission.

<!-- p170 -->
- Time - we can give our time and volunteer. When we give our time, we use our efforts and our talents. This can also be a great way to learn new skills and increase existing ones.

<!-- p171 -->
- Resources - we can give of our resources. Today, the most common is probably to give financially to the church and to ministries (not just to the “institution,” but to those in need in the church). Giving money is helpful, because the church can use it to buy necessary resources, but people have given their physical resources beyond money; for example, extra houses, clothing, cars, food, building materials, tools, etc. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="32-35" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 4:32-35</span> we see an example of the early believers giving freely, sharing the proceeds from the sale of extra land and houses and giving so that ‘no one was in need’.

<!-- p172 -->
Working alongside others in ministry can reveal your strengths and deepen your understanding of your role. Participate in small groups or church activities to foster connections. Listen to the burdens and needs of those around you and consider how you can support them. Building relationships within the church helps you connect with others and understand the needs of the community.

<!-- p173 -->
#### Step 4 - Get help!

<!-- p174 -->
Jesus said that in life we would have tribulation! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:33</span>) We are encouraged not to carry the weight of our burdens alone, but to cast them on God, who cares for us! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:7</span>)

<!-- p175 -->
Sometimes on our journey we may get hurt. Marathon runners, even when they do “everything right,” can push their bodies to the point of injury, or they can trip and fall.

<!-- p176 -->
Not all ‘falling’ is related to sin, but some of it is; not all weakness is ‘our fault’; sometimes others will sin against us. Regardless of the cause of our weakness or injury, we must be restored and healed.

<!-- p177 -->
There is great freedom in seeking inner healing and restoration. We are called to cover the vulnerable parts of the body (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:23</span>) and to restore those caught in any trespass (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:1</span>).

<!-- p178 -->
There is much to say on this topic, and we explore the topics of healing as an overview in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p179 -->
#### Step 5 - Be patient

<!-- p180 -->
Pay attention to confirmation from God and from others. Often, God confirms your calling through fruit, joy, and the affirmation of those around you. Reflect on where your efforts have borne fruit and listen to feedback about the impact of the ministry. This can help you recognize where you are most effective in serving the Body of Christ.

<!-- p181 -->
Be patient and trust God’s timing. Discovering your role can take time and involve trial and error (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 1:6</span>). Trust that God will guide you as you remain faithful and open to his direction. During this time, commit to growth and training by attending workshops, Bible studies, and other resources that equip you for service. Do this with the church; get involved with the Body of Christ to discover your gifts.

<!-- p182 -->
Remember, the church is not a building or an institution; it is a living, beating body made up of people like you. By taking up your role, you contribute to its mission and glorify Christ as its head.

<!-- p193 -->
