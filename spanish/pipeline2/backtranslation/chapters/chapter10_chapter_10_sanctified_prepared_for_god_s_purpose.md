# Chapter 10 - Sanctified-Prepared for the purpose of God

<!-- p1 -->
In the previous two chapters we have talked about several important topics. We explored the role and ministry of the Holy Spirit, as well as the role and ministry of the church.

<!-- p2 -->
We have a part to play in the church, and although we know that we need training, we also need purification. To be truly prepared for the purposes of God we must be sanctified.

<!-- p3 -->
The definition of **sanctification** is to purify something for a special purpose. This is closely related to the term **consecration**, which means to set something apart for a special purpose.

<!-- p4 -->
Imagine a master sculptor preparing to work with a large block of marble. The master imagines what this raw and shapeless marble can be transformed into through his careful craft. He has a very specific design in mind, orders his assistants to set this block of marble apart for a special design (**consecration**). As he refines and works the marble, adding texture, depth, and shape, he purifies it (**sanctifies**) for that special design. The act of dedicating the marble for his special project was quick, while the process of shaping the marble to its final design requires considerable patience.

<!-- p5 -->
We were set apart, consecrated to God at salvation. By the act of purifying our souls with the help of the Spirit we are transformed. This process of purification is what sanctifies us.

<!-- p6 -->
When we received Jesus through salvation, we were born again. We were justified, our sins were forgiven. Our spirit was regenerated, we came to life in Christ. We were adopted into the family of God and became the literal temple of the Holy Spirit. Such a wonderful transformation that the Scriptures say: “*there is joy in the presence of the angels of God over one sinner who repents.” (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 15:10</span>).

<!-- p7 -->
Receiving Jesus for salvation (**justification**) saves our spirit, cleanses us and makes us presentable before God, able to receive His Holy Spirit. However, in spite of such great salvation **our souls** (our mind, will, and emotions) still **need work**!

<!-- p8 -->
Was the work of salvation incomplete? ¡No! The work of salvation is effective, but it must be continued in the life of the believer through the renewal produced by the Holy Spirit. This process of renewal and transformation is the work of **sanctification.**

<!-- p9 -->
We know there is ongoing work to be done after conversion because our painful memories were not resolved, many of our beliefs remained the same and many of our habits or rituals did not disappear. Furthermore, conversion did not separate our minds from their fleshly (carnal) nature.

<!-- p10 -->
The word ‘carnal’ comes from the Latin root meaning ‘flesh’ or ‘meat’. The carnal nature is within each of us from birth; like the instincts of an animal, it exists to help us survive. The way the carnal nature works is by being self-centered, self-oriented, egocentric. Children are natural examples of a carnal nature. Despite their innocence, their nature focuses mainly on their desires, longings, needs; and ‘watch out!’ if you do not satisfy each one of their expectations.

<!-- p11 -->
Part of growing up is maturing beyond our childish ways, but when we are pressured we very easily revert to a pattern of thinking and living oriented around the self. As James says:

<!-- p12 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p13 -->
> **<u>James 4:1</u>** (NKJV)
>
> ¿Where do wars and fights come from among you? ¿Is it not from your passions that war in your members?
>

<!-- p14 -->
</span>

<!-- p15 -->
Paul explains this carnal nature in more detail in his epistle to the Romans (chapters 6 - 8). He says that this carnal nature keeps him doing what he hates and leads him to do evil. He describes this inner conflict as a war between his man-made nature and his God-made spirit.

<!-- p16 -->
This conflict between the flesh and the spirit exists in each of us, and it is our job to do something about it. If we do not confront our carnal nature, our souls will remain entangled in patterns of spiritual decay, even though our spirits have been redeemed.

<!-- p17 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

> So then, those who are in the flesh cannot please God.

<!-- p20 -->
These words of Paul are for us both a warning and a promise. Although we have been born again, we still have a carnal mind that needs to be brought into submission to the knowledge of Christ. By doing so, we walk in the life and peace of God.

<!-- p21 -->
Take heart, there is hope for us! We must walk according to the Spirit and be led by the Spirit of God. By doing so, we will not walk according to the flesh.

<!-- p22 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="1,13,14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

>

>
> For if you live according to the flesh you will die; but if by the Spirit you put to death the deeds of the body, you will live.
>
> For as many as are led by the Spirit of God, these are sons of God.

<!-- p24 -->
</span>

<!-- p25 -->
How do we learn to walk in the Spirit? Is it *only a conscious choice*? We learn to walk in the Spirit in the same way we learn to walk in the natural. We are motivated to move and grow, so we struggle, wrestle, crawl, and stagger until we can stand on our own two feet. Our natural parents cannot walk for us, but they can support us, encourage us, help us, and equip us in our walking.

<!-- p26 -->
Likewise, God cannot live our faith for us. He will teach us, but we must put in the effort. As Paul said to the Philippians:

<!-- p27 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="12-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p28 -->
> **<u>Philippians 2:12-13</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Therefore, my beloved, as you have always obeyed, not only in my presence, but now much more in my absence, work out your own salvation with fear and trembling;
>
> for it is God who works in you both to will and to do for His good pleasure.

<!-- p29 -->

<!-- p30 -->
We must work out our own salvation. That does not mean that we climb onto a wooden cross and are physically crucified, but we do have to crucify our carnal nature (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:24</span>). God promises that He will work in us and help us, but we must make the decision to carry this out, and we must stand firm in it.

<!-- p31 -->
It is true to say that ‘we’ are dealing with our carnal nature, but it is also true to say that ‘God’ is dealing with it. The work of the cross (justification) was hard for Jesus, He had to sacrifice His life for ours, but easy for us, it is a free gift. The work of the Spirit (sanctification) is easy for God, He walks in perfect truth and is willing and eager to remain with us, but it is hard for us, because we must crucify our old nature, and our nature is not very interested in being crucified.

<!-- p32 -->
This process is collaborative. God helps, succors, guides, directs, and corrects. We listen, obey, follow, and respond. The result is growth and transformation. This is the pruning that Jesus said God would do in us. Jesus is the vine, His Father the vinedresser, we are the branches:

<!-- p33 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p36 -->
Pruning is incredibly healthy for a plant. Recalling our chapter on repentance, we are called to turn away from dead works (those works that are no longer bearing fruit) and turn to God. The result of turning away from the dead places in our lives, and of turning to God, empowers the great gardener of our lives to prune branches, improve growth, and produce greater spiritual fruit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:2</span>).

<!-- p37 -->
Although pruning can feel painful, it reflects God’s loving care as He molds us as vessels for His purpose (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 12:11</span>). Some may feel a sense of loss, even fear, as they go through the pruning process. However, God is tender, and He will not even break a bruised reed (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="42" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 42:3</span>). Vines are pruned during their season of rest to minimize the plant’s stress. The gardener who tends the growth will trim the old, woody growth that no longer produces fruit as before; this allows the vine to direct energy to the branches with greater potential for good fruit.

<!-- p38 -->
Like a gardener who trims branches to direct growth, God may allow seasons of correction and discipline to refine our faith and character, but His work is patient and kind. God prunes those who abide in Him. Our focus should be more on abiding in Christ, and less on the work of pruning.

In the following section I will describe each of the memory systems, their purpose and function, some practical steps to renew this aspect of your mind, as well as resources for ongoing practice and study.

### **Memory systems and how to renew them**

<!-- p62 -->
#### **1. Explicit memory (declarative memory)**

<!-- p63 -->
**Purpose:** Stores facts, events, and experiences that we can consciously recall. It is divided into two types-**semantic** and **episodic**.

<!-- p64 -->
**Semantic memory - Facts and knowledge.** Semantic memory is where we store facts, definitions, and general knowledge-things like historical dates, Bible verses, and practical information. It helps us predict outcomes, make decisions, and avoid danger (e.g., recognizing poisonous plants). Although it gives us stability and order, it can also lead to rigidity-an overreliance on rules instead of adapting to unexpected situations.

<!-- p65 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p66 -->
- **Study Scripture** to shape our worldview (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="119" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="105" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 119:105</span>).

<!-- p67 -->
- **Memorize truths** and doctrines to keep you firm in the faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 3:16</span>).

<!-- p68 -->
- **Read Christian books** such as *"Renewing the Mind" by Casey Treat* or *"Battlefield of the Mind" by Joyce Meyer* to develop godly thought patterns.

<!-- p69 -->
**Episodic memory - Personal experiences.** Episodic memory stores our personal experiences, linking them to specific times and places-such as a wedding day or a traumatic event. These memories provide context, helping us learn from the past and build relationships. However, episodic memory is prone to emotional bias and distortion. Past traumas can create fearful associations, making it difficult to move forward.

<!-- p70 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p71 -->
- **Reflect and confess** to bring hidden pain into God’s light (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 1:9</span>).

<!-- p72 -->
- **Reframe past experiences** through the lens of God’s grace (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:28</span>).

<!-- p73 -->
- **Seek inner healing** through prayer and counseling (*“Your Pain is Showing” by Larry F. Low, "The Bondage Breaker" by Neil T. Anderson*, *"Emotionally Healthy Spirituality" by Peter Scazzero*).

<!-- p74 -->
#### **2. Implicit memory (nondeclarative memory)**

<!-- p75 -->
**Purpose:** Unconscious memory used for habits, skills, and emotional associations. It operates automatically, helping us respond quickly to situations. It is divided into **procedural memory** and **emotional conditioning**.

<!-- p76 -->
**Procedural memory - Skills and habits.** Procedural memory allows us to perform tasks without thinking-like driving, typing, or playing an instrument. It is efficient, saving us mental energy and time. However, bad habits and negative patterns can also become deeply ingrained and difficult to break.

<!-- p77 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p78 -->
- **Practice spiritual disciplines** such as prayer and fasting to form godly habits (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 5:14</span>).

<!-- p79 -->
- **Train yourself in godliness** through repetition and obedience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 4:7</span>).

<!-- p80 -->
- **Read books like** *"Atomic Habits" by James Clear* (practical) and *"Spiritual Disciplines for the Christian Life" by Donald Whitney* (biblical) to establish habits that honor God.

<!-- p81 -->
**Emotional conditioning - Emotional associations.** Emotional conditioning links sensory cues (like smells or sounds) with feelings, triggering responses before we consciously process them. This system protects us, but it can also cause irrational fears or emotional reactions when past experiences are negative.

<!-- p82 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p83 -->
- **Pray for healing** from past wounds (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="41" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 41:10</span>).

<!-- p84 -->
- **Practice forgiveness** and let go of bitterness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-32" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:31-32</span>).

<!-- p85 -->
- **Replace fear with God's promises** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 1:7</span>).

<!-- p86 -->
- **Seek guidance in books like** *“Your Pain is Showing” by Larry F. Low, "The Bondage Breaker" by Neil T. Anderson*, “*Soul Care" by Rob Reimer* to overcome emotional triggers.

<!-- p87 -->
#### **3. Working memory (short-term memory)**

<!-- p88 -->
**Purpose:** Provides temporary storage for problem-solving and decision-making. It helps us focus, briefly retain information, and process tasks-like remembering instructions or making quick decisions. Although it is powerful, it is also limited, prone to distraction, and easily overloaded, especially under stress.

<!-- p89 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p90 -->
- **Focus and meditate** on the Word of God daily (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 1:2</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
- **Renew your thoughts** through intentional thinking (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:2</span>).

<!-- p92 -->
- **Practice mindfulness** and simplify mental clutter (*"Habits of Grace" by David Mathis*).

<!-- p93 -->
- **Read** *"The Celebration of Discipline" by Richard Foster* to sharpen focus through spiritual disciplines.

<!-- p94 -->
## Conclusion

<!-- p95 -->
The renewal of the mind is not a quick solution-it is a gradual process of transformation that is lifelong. Each memory system plays a role in shaping how we think, feel, and act. By working intentionally with the Holy Spirit, applying Scripture, and developing godly habits, we can train our minds to align with God's truth. Whether addressing past wounds, learning new habits, or staying focused in the moment, God has provided both **spiritual tools** and **practical wisdom** to help us walk in renewal and freedom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 10:5</span>).

<!-- p96 -->
Our world is full of problems, tribulation, and trials. Through the disciplined renewal of the mind, our souls are transformed, and we can live a life of freedom for God, filled with the fruit of His Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-25</span>).
