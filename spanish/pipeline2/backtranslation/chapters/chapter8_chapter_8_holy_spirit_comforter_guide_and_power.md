# Chapter 8 - Holy Spirit-Comforter, Guide and Power

<!-- p1 -->
The Holy Spirit is not a new spirit, but is of God and has been with God from the beginning. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 1:2</span> the Spirit of God was moving over the waters during creation. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="NEH" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Nehemiah 9:20</span>, the Scriptures say that it was the Spirit who instructed the people in the wilderness. It was by this same Holy Spirit that God anointed kings, judges, and prophets in the Old Testament. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JOL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Joel 2:28-29</span> it is promised that the Spirit will be poured out on all humanity. It was by this same Holy Spirit that God raised Jesus from the dead, and He now dwells in us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:11</span>). There is only one Holy Spirit, and He has always been the same. The way He works with people has seemed different in different eras, but His heart has never changed.

<!-- p2 -->
Jesus gave His disciples, and all of us, the powerful promise of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p3 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p4 -->
> **<u>John 14:16-18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And I will ask the Father, and He will give you another Helper, to be with you forever—
>
> the Spirit of truth, whom the world cannot receive, because it neither sees Him nor knows Him; but you know Him, for He dwells with you and will be in you.
>
> I will not leave you orphans; I will come to you.
>

<!-- p5 -->
</span>

<!-- p6 -->
While Jesus walked the earth in His physical body, He was limited as we are; His body needed the same things our bodies need: rest, water, food, sleep, etc. This limitation affected not only Jesus, but also His followers, since He could only be in one place at a time. Often, when Jesus traveled, a great crowd thronged Him; they tried to call out to Him and touch Him. Jesus often needed to spend time with His heavenly Father to recharge and pray.

<!-- p7 -->
For the Father’s work to be multiplied, Jesus needed to raise up disciples and have them continue this work. Jesus knew that a crucial aspect of His ministry on earth depended on His connecting with and remaining with His Father. In fact, Jesus said that He could do nothing unless His Father did it through Him and showed Him to do so (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 5:19</span>). God, in His wisdom, sent His Holy Spirit to dwell within us, to guide and direct us, enabling us to do the Father’s work.

<!-- p8 -->
## When was the Holy Spirit sent to all believers?

<!-- p9 -->
Jesus said:

<!-- p10 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p11 -->
> **<u>John 16:7</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Nevertheless, I tell you the truth. It is to your advantage that I go away; for if I do not go away, the Helper will not come to you; but if I go, I will send Him to you.
>

<!-- p12 -->
</span>

<!-- p13 -->
After Jesus was crucified, His death made it possible for the Spirit of God to dwell within us. Until that moment, the tangible Spirit of God was mostly contained behind a thick veil in the temple. From time to time, select individuals were chosen by God and anointed (consecrated and set apart for a unique service), and they received the Holy Spirit upon them. God’s anointing and the Spirit upon them was special, temporary, and conditional. David prayed after his sin with Bathsheba, ‘*Do not take Your Holy Spirit from me!*’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="51" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 51:11</span>), because David had seen the Spirit depart from King Saul due to his acts of disobedience.

<!-- p14 -->
We (humanity) could not receive the Holy Spirit within us because of our sinful nature, but when Jesus dealt with the problem of sin through His sacrifice, He opened a way by faith for us to become children and heirs of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:17</span>).

<!-- p15 -->
In John 20, after His resurrection, Jesus appears to His disciples in the middle of a locked room and breathes on them saying, ‘*Receive the Holy Spirit*’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 20:22</span>). Theology is somewhat debated regarding what precisely occurred at that moment, but most Pentecostal interpretations see this as the disciples receiving the Spirit of God within them, signifying the new birth, being born again. However, this was not when they received the Spirit of God upon them, empowering them with boldness for ministry. This was when they received the Holy Spirit within them, enabling them to abide and have fellowship with God. This will be discussed in greater depth later in this chapter, as well as in the chapters on sanctification and baptism.

<!-- p16 -->
Later, at the conclusion of the 40-day period between the resurrection and the ascension (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:3</span>), Jesus gives the promise of the Holy Spirit:

<!-- p17 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="4-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p18 -->
> **<u>Acts 1:4-9</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And being assembled together with them, He commanded them not to depart from Jerusalem, but to wait for the Promise of the Father, "which," He said, "you have heard from Me;
>
> for John truly baptized with water, but you shall be baptized with the Holy Spirit not many days from now."
>
> Therefore, when they had come together, they asked Him, saying, "Lord, will You at this time restore the kingdom to Israel?"
>
> And He said to them, "It is not for you to know times or seasons which the Father has put in His own authority.
>
> But you shall receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you; and you shall be My witnesses in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the end of the earth."
>
> Now when He had spoken these things, while they watched, He was taken up, and a cloud received Him out of their sight.
>

<!-- p19 -->
</span>

<!-- p20 -->
The disciples were to wait for the promise of the outpoured Spirit, and that this would empower them to be witnesses to the ends of the earth. The Holy Spirit is integral to God’s plan for humanity and is essential for us as believers, to complete our mission, and for the world.

<!-- p21 -->
## Knowing the Holy Spirit

<!-- p22 -->
The Holy Spirit is, above all, of and proceeding from God. The Scriptures refer to the Spirit as the Spirit of God, or the Spirit of the Lord, or the Spirit of Christ; although these highlight different aspects, they are all one and the same. The Holy Spirit, being fully God, is also eternal and is holy (blameless, innocent, pure, not profane). Some translations refer to the Holy Spirit as the Holy Ghost.

<!-- p23 -->
The word for spirit in Greek is **Pneuma** (**NYOO-mah** - **G4151**), meaning breath or wind. The Hebrew word for spirit means the same. Breath is significant since we constantly need it to live. It was the breath of God that blew into Adam to give him life; likewise, it was the breath of Jesus that he exhaled for the last time before dying on the cross. Without breath there is no life. Breath, like the wind, can be felt and experienced, but it is not as easily observed as other ‘elements’ water, fire, earth.

<!-- p24 -->
There are several other fundamental adjectives to describe the Holy Spirit. Each of these adjectives is used together with ‘the Spirit of’, which means that these properties are vital to the Spirit’s definition. For example, the Spirit of Truth or the Spirit of Life.

<!-- p25 -->
The Spirit of:

<!-- p26 -->
- **Truth** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:17</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span> Refers to the Spirit’s role of guiding believers into all truth, revealing God’s will and the reality of Christ.

<!-- p27 -->
- **Life** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:2</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:8</span> Highlights the Spirit’s role of imparting spiritual and eternal life to believers, freeing them from the law of sin and death.

<!-- p28 -->
- **Grace** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:29</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ZEC" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Zechariah 12:10</span> Reflects the Spirit’s work in dispensing God’s favor, forgiveness, and unmerited kindness to humanity.

<!-- p29 -->
- **Glory** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 4:14</span> Indicates the Spirit’s role in revealing and manifesting the glory of God, particularly in times of suffering and persecution.

<!-- p30 -->
- **Adoption** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:15</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 4:5</span> Refers to the Spirit’s work in making believers children of God, granting them the status of sons and daughters.

<!-- p31 -->
- **Holiness** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 1:4</span> Points to the sanctifying work of the Spirit, setting believers apart for God’s purposes.

<!-- p32 -->
- **Power** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 1:7</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span> Describes the Spirit’s empowering of believers for boldness, ministry, and the ability to live out their faith effectively.

<!-- p33 -->
In all these attributes, the Holy Spirit is also properly recognized as the Helper or Comforter. Jesus refers to the Holy Spirit as the coming helper, he uses the Greek word ***Paraklētos** (*pah-RAH-klay-tos - **G3875**), which means advocate, helper, called to be alongside.

<!-- p34 -->
An advocate speaks on behalf of another, like a defense attorney. They intercede and help the person who has been entrusted to them.

<!-- p35 -->
The helper helps. It helps to empower the work, but its job is primarily to support the work. The helper is not solely responsible for carrying out the work, but comes alongside to help bring it about.

<!-- p36 -->
The Holy Spirit has several distinct purposes and functions in His ministry. His work is inward (in the hearts and lives of believers), upward (in service and acknowledgment of God) and outward (to our mission field, the world).

<!-- p37 -->
## Roles of the Holy Spirit-Inward, Upward, Outward

<!-- p38 -->
### Inward-The Holy Spirit unites us with Jesus

<!-- p39 -->
Before Jesus performed miracles or began his public ministry, he was baptized by John the Baptist and the Spirit of God descended upon him, and a voice from heaven proclaimed, ‘This is my beloved Son in whom I am well pleased’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:17</span>).

<!-- p40 -->
When we accept Jesus by faith, we receive salvation and the seal of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p41 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p42 -->
> **<u>Ephesians 1:13-14</u>** (NKJV)
>
> In Him you also trusted, after hearing the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation; and in Him you also, having believed, were sealed with the Holy Spirit of promise,
>
> who is the guarantee of our inheritance until the redemption of the purchased possession, to the praise of His glory.
>

<!-- p43 -->
</span>

<!-- p44 -->
This seal is not metaphorical but literal. It serves as a sign of our redemption and is a foretaste of the full inheritance we receive as children of God. Romans 8 describes that, through the new birth, we receive in our mortal bodies the very Spirit of God who raised Christ from the dead and we become the literal temple of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 6:16</span>).

<!-- p45 -->
This reception of the Spirit of God transforms us and causes rivers of living water to flow from within us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 7:38</span>). This renewal is immediate and complete, but it continues throughout our entire lives. The river of the Holy Spirit should never cease to flow in our lives!

<!-- p46 -->
Through salvation and the indwelling of the Holy Spirit we can abide in Christ and He in us. The Spirit of God no longer remains outside, but can dwell deeply with us. We can walk in fellowship and harmony with God from now and into eternal life.

<!-- p47 -->
There is much more to say about the inner work of the Holy Spirit and His role in our sanctification, but that will be addressed in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p48 -->
### Upward-The Holy Spirit bears witness to Jesus

<!-- p49 -->
The Holy Spirit comes from God and is of God. Just as Jesus said He did nothing unless the Father showed Him (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 5:19</span>), the Holy Spirit likewise does nothing unless He is directed by the Father. Since the Holy Spirit is the Helper, He does not act on His own, but works in perfect harmony with the Father.

<!-- p50 -->
The Spirit bears witness of Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:26</span>), glorifies Jesus, and announces and declares what Jesus has for us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:14</span>).

<!-- p51 -->
The Holy Spirit will bear witness to Jesus, glorify Him, teach and bring to our remembrance the teaching of Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:26</span>), and guide us into all truth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span>). Jesus told His disciples that He had much more to teach them, but that they could not bear it yet, and that the Holy Spirit would guide them into all truth. This is another nuanced point in theology. These teachings that Jesus needed His disciples to know were communicated in several ways. Jesus taught them in person both before His crucifixion, and after the resurrection and before His ascension.

<!-- p52 -->
Jesus also promised that the Spirit of Truth, who was poured out on them on the day of Pentecost, would guide them into all truth through divine revelation. Is there still divine revelation today? **Certainly yes!** The gifts of the Holy Spirit, including prophecy and words of knowledge and wisdom, have not yet ceased! However, no prophecy or word from a spirit is of private interpretation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2PE" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Peter 1:20</span>). This is crucial. Some people will mistakenly claim that they are hearing the Holy Spirit, but their actions and words do not bear witness to Jesus. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 4:3</span> warns against any spirit that does not testify of Jesus, saying that such a spirit is of the antichrist. This bears repeating: if any spirit deviates from the teaching of Christ it is not the Holy Spirit, but a profane one!

<!-- p53 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p54 -->
> **<u>1 John 4:1</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Beloved, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits, whether they are of God; because many false prophets have gone out into the world.
>

<!-- p55 -->
</span>

<!-- p56 -->
Throughout Scripture, we see examples of claims made in error, often by false prophets or by well-meaning people who spoke against the will of God. For example, Jeremiah warned against prophets who spoke visions from their own minds, proclaiming peace while destruction was approaching (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="23" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 23:16-17</span>). Similarly, in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 16:16-18</span>, the words of a slave girl about Paul and Silas were accurate but inspired by a demonic spirit, not by the Holy Spirit. These examples remind us that even words that seem true can originate from unholy sources.

<!-- p57 -->
Every claim of ‘God told me!’ or a ‘new’ teaching must be measured against the biblical tests of truth. To discern correctly, we must ask ourselves:

<!-- p58 -->
- Does this align with Scripture?

<!-- p59 -->
- Does this exalt Jesus Christ?

<!-- p60 -->
- Does this produce godly fruit?

<!-- p61 -->
By applying these tests, we can distinguish the true guidance of the Holy Spirit from errors or deception.

<!-- p62 -->
God customarily provides us with ample &amp; abundant wisdom and direction (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:5</span> &amp; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="33" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 33:3</span>). God's divine wisdom will not violate Scripture, but it will go beyond the scope of Scripture. His *entolē* *(*commandments*)* are immediate prescriptions that must be obeyed, they are rhema words (as addressed in the chapter on faith).

<!-- p63 -->
It is likely that this wisdom, understanding, and direction are not word-for-word in Scripture. As an example, the Scriptures give great general direction on what to look for in a spouse, but they do not give specific prescriptions about the exact person. God does promise to direct our path, to guide us into all truth, and we can take comfort in knowing that this wisdom and guidance from the Holy Spirit will never violate any teaching of Scripture.

<!-- p64 -->
### Outward-The Holy Spirit Empowers the Mission

<!-- p65 -->
Jesus gave us His mission, the Great Commission:

<!-- p66 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p67 -->
> **<u>Matthew 28:18-20</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And Jesus came and spoke to them, saying, "All authority has been given to Me in heaven and on earth.
>
> Go therefore and make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit,
>
> teaching them to observe all things that I have commanded you; and lo, I am with you always, even to the end of the age." Amen.
>

<!-- p68 -->
</span>

<!-- p69 -->
The Holy Spirit has an integral part in the mission, convicting the world of sin, and of righteousness, and of judgment:

<!-- p70 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="8-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p71 -->
> **<u>John 16:8-11</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And when He has come, He will convict the world of sin, and of righteousness, and of judgment:
>
> of sin, because they do not believe in Me;
>
> of righteousness, because I go to My Father and you see Me no more;
>
> of judgment, because the ruler of this world is judged.
>

<!-- p72 -->
</span>

<!-- p73 -->
Going deeper, the Spirit brings conviction of these three truths:

<!-- p74 -->
1.  **Conviction of sin**: Revealing the reality of human rebellion against God, calling people's attention to the depravity of their own lives.

<!-- p75 -->
2.  **Conviction of righteousness**: Demonstrating Jesus' perfect fulfillment of the Father's will, and that Jesus' sacrifice provided the one atonement necessary for the forgiveness of sins.

<!-- p76 -->
3.  **Conviction of judgment**: Affirming the defeat of Satan, the certainty of God's justice, and that all will stand before a judgment for the work and deeds of their life.

<!-- p77 -->
The Holy Spirit also empowers the mission. The Holy Spirit gives believers the power to be bold witnesses in the face of persecution to share the gospel. Boldness does not mean stridency. It means having courage despite fear. Power is strength, fortitude, and ability. The Holy Spirit gives the strength and the ability to be a witness of the Gospel in spite of threats and fear of punishment.

<!-- p78 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p79 -->
> **<u>Acts 1:8</u>** (NKJV)
>
> But you shall receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you; and you shall be witnesses to Me in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the end of the earth."

<!-- p80 -->
</span>

<!-- p81 -->
The entire Book of Acts is full of stories and accounts that follow this general cycle:

<!-- p82 -->
1.  **Prayer**: Believers ask for courage and God's intervention.

<!-- p83 -->
2.  **Empowerment**: The Holy Spirit fills them with courage, power, and guidance.

<!-- p84 -->
3.  **Proclamation**: They boldly proclaim the gospel, often with signs, wonders, and miracles.

<!-- p85 -->
4.  **Opposition**: Resistance arises, leading to persecution or challenges. At times believers were martyred, other times imprisoned and/or beaten.

<!-- p86 -->
5.  **Renewal**: The church returns to prayer, seeking fresh empowerment, and the cycle continues.

<!-- p87 -->
The New Testament church has largely grown in the face of determined opposition. Around the world, the church suffers violence at the hands of oppressors and evil rulers intent on suppressing the knowledge of the truth of Jesus.

<!-- p88 -->
God knew this opposition would occur; Jesus prophesied about it:

<!-- p89 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p90 -->
> **<u>Luke 12:11-12</u>** (NKJV)
>
> "When they bring you to the synagogues, before magistrates and authorities, do not worry about how or what you should answer, or what you should say.
>
> For the Holy Spirit will teach you in that very hour what you ought to say."
>

<!-- p91 -->
</span>

<!-- p92 -->
Do not fear! Jesus promised us that:

<!-- p93 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p94 -->
> **<u>Matthew 16:18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And I also say to you that you are Peter, and on this rock I will build My church, and the gates of Hades shall not prevail against it.
>

<!-- p95 -->
</span>

<!-- p96 -->
This is an astonishing and comforting promise. Gates are fixed defensive structures. When Jesus says that the *gates of hell will not prevail*, he is painting the picture of a conquering army that breaks down the gates and captures the city!

<!-- p97 -->
The Holy Spirit brings supernatural courage to proclaim the testimony of the Gospel in the face of persecution. The result of this empowerment can be described, but is better experienced. It is a supernatural (more-than-human) courage that motivates believers to share. This boldness in preaching is often accompanied by supernatural acts of demonstration of the Spirit and of power (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 2:1-5</span>). People are healed physically and emotionally, the dead are raised, those tormented by evil spirits are set free, the hard-hearted turn and repent.

<!-- p98 -->
Jesus said that we would do these works and even greater ones! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:12</span>). These collective works are supernatural, divine, and desperately needed in a world that is falling apart, miserable, full of pain, waiting for the mature sons of God to be clearly revealed! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:22-23</span>)

<!-- p99 -->
The instrument chosen by Jesus for change on the earth is his church, empowered, full of courage, and led by the Holy Spirit.

## Building a Relationship with the Holy Spirit

<!-- p101 -->
Every relationship takes time and experience to develop deeply. The Holy Spirit is our advocate, our comforter, our helper, and our guide. He empowers us and sanctifies us.

<!-- p102 -->
Begin to think of the Holy Spirit as you would think of a person. In our relationship, both of us can work to build it up or do things that weaken it. He can be ignored, He can be quenched, He can be blasphemed, or He can be listened to, honored, and obeyed.

<!-- p103 -->
Do not ignore the voice of the Holy Spirit. He is always speaking to us and to the church. If you do not yet know how to discern the voice of the Holy Spirit, that takes time, willingness, and effort (like all healthy and beneficial relationships). But do not worry, you already have within you all the necessary parts to begin developing a personal relationship with the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p104 -->
The fastest and most reliable ways to build an effective relationship with the Holy Spirit are

<!-- p105 -->
1.  Cry out to Him individually in prayer, pause and wait for Him to speak.

<!-- p106 -->
2.  Cry out to Him as part of a **group** of people who know Him (the church), pause and wait for Him to speak.

<!-- p107 -->
I use the phrase ‘cry out’ to mean, literally, with your mouth, or however it is you speak (with your hands if you cannot speak out loud with your mouth). Why speak? A few reasons:

<!-- p108 -->
1.  We are speaking spirits, made in the image of God. God speaks, and so do we.

<!-- p109 -->
2.  We use speech as the primary way to communicate. You can also write what you want to say (keep a journal) and say it out loud.

<!-- p110 -->
3.  Speaking is an intentional act and indicates to the party you are speaking with that you would like to engage in a conversation.

<!-- p111 -->
#### What should you say?

<!-- p112 -->
If you have never spoken with God, try introducing yourself. Sure, it will feel a little weird, but that discomfort is okay and is temporary. In addition to introducing yourself, tell God that you want to talk with Him and that you want Him to answer you.

<!-- p113 -->
Consider asking Him a question. It doesn’t have to be a deep theological question; it can start simple. Can You show me Your love for me? Why do I have a certain feeling? What would You like to tell me?

<!-- p114 -->
#### And then what?

<!-- p115 -->
Well, in all effective relationships you talk a little, then pause and listen. God will speak.

<!-- p116 -->
God speaks in many ways, but He wants to do so in a personal and internal way. It probably will not be an audible voice (as if your best friend said your name out loud), but an inner voice that speaks to your heart and mind. Elijah refers to this as the ‘still small voice’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1KI" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Kings 19:11-13</span>).

<!-- p117 -->
The most common ways I have personally seen God speak to people, and I have been blessed to help dozens of people learn to speak with God directly, are some combination of the following:

<!-- p118 -->
- A certainty - the person who is speaking with God receives an immediate understanding that helps them.

<!-- p119 -->
- An image - also known in the Scriptures as a vision or a dream (a vision is when you are awake, a dream is when you are asleep), God will give you an image in your mind. Maybe it is a memory, or a place you have been or that you know? Explore the image, what do you see? What do you feel as a result? What is happening?

<!-- p120 -->
- An inner voice - like an audible voice, but only you can hear it, and that speaks to your heart and your mind.

<!-- p121 -->
#### What will God say?

<!-- p122 -->
I'm not sure! But that's where the fun is. I can say that He is NOT the spirit of fear, but of perfect love, and that He will never violate the teaching of Scripture. For example, God will never tell you to have an extramarital affair.

<!-- p123 -->
What now?

<!-- p124 -->
Keep the dialogue going! It really is that simple.

<!-- p125 -->
Some good books that I have read and will personally recommend to help you learn to talk with God are:

<!-- p126 -->
- *Genuine Christianity* by Larry Low ([<u>www.larrylow.com</u>](http://www.larrylow.com))

<!-- p127 -->
- *Good Morning, Holy Spirit* by Benny Hinn ([<u>www.bennyhinn.org</u>](http://www.bennyhinn.org))

<!-- p128 -->
### Why build a relationship?

<!-- p129 -->
The Holy Spirit is the Helper. The Helper helps us! The work goes much more smoothly if we have a shared language and a relationship with Him. Can you imagine trying to work with someone at your job if you had no relationship, didn't speak the same language, and couldn't communicate?!

<!-- p130 -->
We serve a God who wants to communicate with us. Jesus said that His sheep know His voice (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:27</span>), we already have built into us the natural abilities we need, but we may lack the expertise that comes from experience.

<!-- p131 -->
Speaking from personal experience, a personal and active relationship with the Holy Spirit is one of the best things about being a Christian. His presence brings us peace, comfort, and direction, but only if you know how to seek Him, listen to Him, and obey Him.

<!-- p132 -->
Develop a sensitivity to the Holy Spirit. Like many married people over time, you can communicate with your spouse in a unique way and sometimes with just a glance. The glance alone tells you everything you need to know about what just happened and what is going to happen. Build that kind of sensitivity and familiarity with the Holy Spirit. Include Him in your whole life, this is a wonderful part of your relationship with Him that will bring much depth and richness to your walk of faith.

<!-- p133 -->
The Holy Spirit has come to lead and guide us, to empower us to be the people of God, and to see the people of the Earth radically transformed, set free from the kingdom of darkness to the kingdom of light. Knowing Him, building trust with Him is essential for everyday life and for fulfilling the work that Jesus has for us to do.

<!-- p134 -->
Our discussion of the work of the Holy Spirit does not end in this chapter. His work is discussed extensively throughout the remaining chapters of this book, especially the chapters on sanctification and baptism.
