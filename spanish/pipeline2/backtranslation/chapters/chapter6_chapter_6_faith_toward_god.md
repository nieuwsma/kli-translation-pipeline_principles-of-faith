# Chapter 6 - Faith toward God

<!-- p1 -->
Faith is a fundamental concept in Christianity. Faith is a word so commonly used in Christianity that sometimes the meaning of the word gets supplanted by subtle misconceptions and misunderstanding. But it is not something you can hold in your hands-it’s something formed in your heart as you come to trust God. Persuasion is belief.

<!-- p2 -->
The author of Hebrews instructs us that we must have ‘faith toward God’.

### Defining faith

<!-- p10 -->
Faith is a commonly misunderstood and misrepresented aspect of Christianity. As the saying goes, “there’s a ditch on both sides of the road”! Because the word faith is used so commonly without due explanation in our modern Christianity, it can come to take on erroneous definitions amplified by misunderstanding. Faith is often presented as a generalized statement of abstracted belief or as an almost mystical power or “magic,” something you possess in a specifically measured and limited amount.

<!-- p11 -->
#### What faith is not

<!-- p12 -->
Faith misrepresented <u>in the abstract</u> has no specific conviction or persuasion. This often becomes evident with a little probing, as this representation of faith cannot be made concrete. If you ask, ‘what do you believe’ or ‘why do you believe’, the person with this understanding of faith often cannot clarify beyond ‘I believe’. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 3:15</span> says that we must be ready to give an answer for the hope that is in us.

<!-- p13 -->
Sometimes people think of faith as a magic power-but that is not what Scripture teaches. In Scripture, faith is much more dynamic and relational-it is rooted in persuasion, trust, and confidence in God’s character and promises. It is not an impersonal force but a committed response to God that shapes thoughts, feelings, and actions.

<!-- p14 -->
Why is belief relevant to persuasion?

<!-- p15 -->
Our beliefs shape the way we see and interact with the world around us. Belief shapes perception and precipitates our actions. That’s how our minds are set up-we live according to what we believe is possible.

<!-- p16 -->
We act according to what we perceive, understand, and believe possible. When we walk down a hallway, our brain detects the boundaries of the floor and the wall and automatically tells our feet where we can step and what to avoid. This truth goes beyond our physical navigation of the world around us.

<!-- p17 -->
Suppose you believe that it is impossible for you to become financially independent or to live a healthy and active lifestyle. In that case your actions will follow that belief, and you, true to that belief, will not do or likely even attempt what you believe is impossible.

<!-- p18 -->
If you believe that God has an unloving and punitive character, that perception of Him will keep you from walking with Him as His child.

<!-- p19 -->
#### Faith is persuasion

<!-- p20 -->
Then, what is faith? Persuasion! The Greek word ***pistis*** means persuasion, or the result of having been persuaded. We are to have persuasion toward God. In English we might say faith (persuasion) ‘**in** God’. If you are married, hopefully you have faith ‘**in’** your spouse. You believe that they will do what they said they would do and you believe they are authentic and genuine. This trust should extend beyond what they have said, to extend to them in general.

<!-- p21 -->
Developing faith, persuasion, and trust in a relationship takes time and experience. Hopefully, in life you have learned how to develop a trusting relationship with another person. The principles you have learned that enable you to increase your trust in someone you know will also enable you to increase your faith in God. Faith in a relationship is increased over time, in experience, with consistency, and through small and large actions that strengthen your trust in the other person’s character and nature.

<!-- p22 -->
‘Faith toward God’, is relational. Relationships are built between two parties, not one. The good news about our faith in God is that God is responsible for His side of the relationship (made possible by the atoning death of Jesus and the fullness of His Spirit in the new birth), and He helps us on our side as well (by the work of the Comforter - the Holy Spirit). God is doing almost all the work, our part (as we will talk about later in this chapter) is to walk *WITH* Him in obedience.

<!-- p23 -->
### Jesus matures our faith

<!-- p24 -->
Jesus is the author and finisher of our faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 12:2</span>). Our faith is designed to grow and expand continually. We do not arrive at a single point at which we have enough ‘faith’; rather, our faith increases. Consider a deep and personal relationship you have in your life today. As in any relationship, we do not arrive at a specific milestone at which we now ‘know’ the person. We keep getting to know them and increasing our knowledge of them, and this refines, shapes, and grows our relationship.

<!-- p25 -->
How do we grow our faith in God? By continuing to experience Him through His many revelations, manifestations, and attributes. Again, think of a deep personal relationship in your life. You increase the depth of the relationship by engaging with that person in different ways, in different formats, in different media, in different environments. You talk with them, you send them messages, perhaps you write to them. You see them individually and in group settings. You talk about what you value and perhaps challenge one another. The numerous ways go on to infinity. Through these many expressions you come to know the person more deeply.

<!-- p26 -->
We should all come to know God, through Jesus, as our savior. Think about the names of God (*see the appendix),* the fact that we know God as savior does not mean that we have already experienced Him as deliverer, healer, counselor, Lord.

<!-- p27 -->
If you are at a point where you have been persuaded (faith) to know God as your savior, building this relationship will give you the confidence in His character to know Him in other ways.

<!-- p28 -->
I am blessed to have several very deep relationships in my life. One of those relationships is with my wife. In our relationship, because of the level of trust (faith) we have in one another, my wife can tell me things she will do, which I have not yet seen manifest, and I have confidence to believe her because of her proven character.

<!-- p29 -->
This example is how our deep relationship with God can be experienced, with confidence and assurance! We may not yet have fully experienced it (in fact, we will never fully experience it in this life), but as we mature in our relationship, we can trust Him beyond the limit of what I personally know and what I have already seen happen.

<!-- p30 -->
Jesus increases our faith through experience with Him. A significant way in which we can and should experience Him is through His word.

<!-- p32 -->
Consider this passage from Romans:

<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

> **<u>Romans 10:16-17</u>** (NKJV)
>
> But they have not all obeyed the gospel. For Isaiah says: "LORD, WHO HAS BELIEVED OUR MESSAGE?"
>
> So then faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God.
>

<!-- p35 -->
</span>

<!-- p36 -->
When we replace the word *faith* with *persuasion* it says:

<!-- p37 -->
*‘So then persuasion comes by hearing, and hearing by the Word of God’*. This truth is naturally self-evident. An important way in which someone comes to have persuasion is having been persuaded (convinced), and we are convinced when someone presents an ‘argument’-or provides a reasoned and articulated explanation.

<!-- p38 -->
There are two Greek words for ‘Word’ in the Bible-**logos** and **rhema**. Understanding the difference helps us see how faith grows.

<!-- p39 -->
**Logos**: generally, it refers to the **broader concept, idea, reason, or expression of the word of God**. It encompasses a universal and fundamental truth or message, and is often used to describe the eternal wisdom of God and Christ Himself as the living Word.

<!-- p40 -->
**Rhema (G4487)**: emphasizes a **specific, spoken word or statement**. It generally refers to a particular word or message given at a specific moment, especially a spoken or revealed word that applies directly to a person’s situation.

<!-- p41 -->
*Logos* is like the overall message of a book, whereas *rhema* is like a particular passage or phrase that speaks directly to someone at a given moment. While *logos* is the overarching truth of God, *rhema* is when part of that truth is spoken to an individual in a personal and applicable way. Together, they illustrate the fullness of God’s communication: *logos* as the broad, foundational truths of God’s character and will, and *rhema* as the specific, applied word that brings that truth to life in a person’s life and situation.

<!-- p42 -->
### Example of a Rhema Word in Action

<!-- p43 -->
God promises revelation and wisdom to all who ask. When He speaks to us, that statement is a *rhema* word. *Rhema* words come from God and serve to confirm, guide, and direct us. They are not a replacement for the *logos* word, nor do they have the authority of Scripture. *Rhema* words should be judged by the same standard as prophecy or the interpretation of tongues (see the chapter on the Holy Spirit for an in-depth study of this topic).

<!-- p44 -->
At various points in my career, I have prayed for divine guidance. Several years ago, I was contemplating the process of beginning to look for a new opportunity and, in my prayer time, I heard God say to me, ‘*Just wait on Me, I have an opportunity for you.’* I was beginning to look for a new job because I was not satisfied with my current position and felt frustrated. God’s directional word ‘*wait on Me’* helped me to have peace and confidence in a restless season.

<!-- p45 -->
Three or four months passed, I kept working hard and faithfully, maintaining a positive attitude. God reminded me, *‘wait on Me’*, then His promise occurred. I had the opportunity to be promoted and received a generous pay raise.

<!-- p46 -->
I could have continued my job search; I could have left. I could have started somewhere else. No doubt someone else would have gotten that promotion! But because I sought the Lord’s counsel and was persuaded by His **rhema word**, I obeyed and received the benefit. God’s words are **living and personal**, and He promises to give us abundant and generous wisdom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:5</span>).

<!-- p47 -->
I have dozens, if not hundreds, of times when I have prayed and asked God what to do and He has given me a rhema word. Not all had the same spectacular outcome, but all brought a sense of peace and confidence that I was obeying God and that He would work things out. They were the words I needed for the moment! Many times, when God brings a rhema word, it is from the logos. He will bring to my remembrance a passage of Scripture, “¡Do not fear, for I am with you!” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="41" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 41:10</span>), “By His stripes we are healed” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="53" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 53:5</span>), “¡Let us not grow weary while doing good!” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:9</span>), the examples remain numerous!

<!-- p48 -->
Receiving a rhema word not only strengthens our faith, but also deepens our relationship with God, as we trust Him more fully in our daily decisions. We will continue our study of learning to hear from God in the chapter on the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p49 -->
Our faith is also strengthened by what we see God do, but sometimes what we see in the natural world challenges what we hear from God. Reconciling the inconsistency between what we see and what we hear is an important ministry of the Holy Spirit, who promises to guide us into all truth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span>).

### A Faith Greater Than What We Can See

<!-- p51 -->
After His resurrection, Jesus appeared multiple times to many of His disciples. Thomas (one of the twelve) had not been with the other disciples, so he missed the appearance of the risen Savior. Thomas believed in Jesus and trusted his fellow disciples, but he did not believe that Jesus had risen in bodily form; he insisted that he could not actively believe it until he experienced it personally.

When Jesus appeared to His disciples and calmed them (I imagine it is quite alarming when someone appears in your midst, having entered through closed doors), He spoke with Thomas. He did not criticize or blame Thomas nor accuse him as part of the evil and perverse generation that seeks a sign (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 16:4</span>) but rather He revealed Himself to Thomas.

<!-- p60 -->
Jesus goes on to say: ‘*blessed are those who have not seen and have believ*ed’. This is not Jesus encouraging a ‘blind or ignorant faith’ nor rejecting the use of empirical evidence. But He is saying that we are more blessed when we can believe without the need for empirical proof. Why? If our faith, our trust, were limited purely to what we had seen demonstrated empirically, we could never have any confidence in future work. We would have great confidence in what had been accomplished, but we would not walk in the peace of God for our tomorrows and for our future.

<!-- p61 -->
Using a relational example, how challenging would my relationship with my wife be if my attitude were consistently ‘<u>¡I’ll believe it when I see it!</u>’? Instead, a relationship based on trust says ‘I know you are a trustworthy person’.

### Faith produces obedience and good works

<!-- p80 -->
Our trusting relationship with God is strengthened through obedience, whose outcome brings good works! These works do not save us or earn us God’s love-they are the natural result of a life rooted in Him.

<!-- p81 -->
Scripture presents a natural progression of faith. As we come to know God and experience His presence, we grow in our belief in Him. This belief compels us to act in ways that align with our faith, leading us to obey His commandments. Our obedience produces good works, which yield a harvest of positive and beneficial results.

<!-- p82 -->
Consider again this passage from Romans:

<!-- p83 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

2.  Faith, which is a state of belief or persuasion, will produce the action or verb of believing.  Again, consider that the person who believes they can be debt-free acts on it!

<!-- p90 -->
3.  Our act of believing is demonstrated in obedience.

<!-- p91 -->
The word for ‘obeyed’ is **hupakouō** (**hoop-ak-oo'-o**- G5219): **hupakouō** means to hear under (as a subordinate), that is, to listen attentively; by implication to heed or conform to a command or authority: - give ear, be obedient to, obey.

<!-- p92 -->
In a work environment, when the boss or supervisor is giving instructions, they are not “talking just to talk”, but they are giving directions that they expect their employees to follow.

<!-- p93 -->
When we truly listen to God, that should lead us to action: to trust in Him and obey. As the Scriptures say in:

The advisor will teach them about the principles of financial freedom and help them to develop a budget. This counseling (trust / faith) in what they need to do to be debt-free. But trust is only the first step, the person must obey the principles they have learned through disciplined practice to receive the good work and the blessing of being debt-free.

The time it takes for the harvest to be received, or the measure of that harvest, varies, but it will produce a good harvest. (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 8:22</span>).

It is better to rightly follow what we ought to do than to have to make a sacrifice to make up for what we did not do correctly. Fortunately, there is grace from God when we get off the path, miss the mark (sin), and need to turn back to God.

<!-- p108 -->
As we build our faith in God, it will naturally produce obedience in us. This obedience is for our benefit and produces a good harvest. Returning to my personal example in this chapter, I would not have experienced peace in that season, nor the blessing of a promotion if I had not obeyed God's word to me to *wait on Him*.

### Practical steps to grow your faith

<!-- p153 -->
Walking with God is relational, but there are skills to learn if you want good relationships. In earthly relationships, the skills that produce strong and secure relationships are based on:

<!-- p154 -->
- Open and free dialogue and communication

<!-- p155 -->
- Healthy conflict resolution

<!-- p156 -->
- Commitment

<!-- p157 -->
- Consistency

<!-- p158 -->
You must show up consistently, reveal (communicate) yourself (our thoughts and emotions), receive the other person (listen), and walk with them if you want a strong and vibrant relationship! All of these skills apply to our relationship with God.

<!-- p159 -->
Beyond basic relational skills, there are specific practices and disciplines that will strengthen your relationship with God:

<!-- p160 -->
- **Build and maintain a life of prayer; listen and communicate with God.** Regular, sincere prayer is essential for a close relationship with God. Through prayer, you can share your concerns, joys, and gratitude, seeking His guidance and strength. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 4:6-7</span>*) Being sensitive to the voice of the Holy Spirit helps you discern God's will, find wisdom in decisions, and grow in spiritual maturity. Take time for silent reflection in order to listen for His direction. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:14</span>*)

<!-- p161 -->
- **Set aside time for worship.** Worship is a powerful way to draw near to God, expressing love and reverence. Through praise, singing, or silent reflection, worship deepens your awareness of His presence. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="100" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 100:2-4</span>*)

<!-- p162 -->
- **Read, study, and meditate on Scripture.** God's Word reveals His character, His promises, and His instructions for life. Devoting time to the study of Scripture helps you grow in faith, understanding, and spiritual resilience. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>*)

<!-- p163 -->
- **Obey His commandments.** Obedience to God's instructions demonstrates your love, respect, and trust in Him. Walking in obedience aligns your life with His purpose and brings blessings. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:15</span>*)

<!-- p164 -->
- **Actively attend a local church and serve.** Participating in a church community offers spiritual support, encouragement, and opportunities to serve others. Being involved helps build a sense of belonging and strengthens your commitment to God's mission. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:24-25</span>*)

<!-- p165 -->
- **Build relationships with other believers.** Fellowship with other Christians provides accountability, encouragement, and shared wisdom. These relationships enrich your walk of faith and help you grow through mutual support. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="27" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 27:17</span>*)

<!-- p166 -->
Our ‘faith toward God’ is not a one-time event for salvation, but an ongoing practice and relationship that we are meant to cultivate throughout our lives. The benefits of a trusting and fruitful relationship are incredibly numerous, but we must do the good work of fostering that trust and growth in order to see it thrive.
