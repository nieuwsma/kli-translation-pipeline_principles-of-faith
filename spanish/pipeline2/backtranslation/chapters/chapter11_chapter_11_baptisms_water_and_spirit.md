# Chapter 11 - Baptisms-Water and Spirit

<!-- p1 -->
Baptism in our modern era, separated from Jewish roots, is usually thought of only in terms of water baptism and the baptism of the Holy Spirit (Pentecost).

<!-- p2 -->
The book of Hebrews was written for a Jewish audience. The vast majority of Christians in our modern era have neither Jewish roots nor heritage. Without Jewish roots and culture, many of us lack the cultural context for things that first-century Messianic Jews (those Jewish believers who professed Jesus) would share. Since we were not raised in the Jewish tradition, we generally do not know much about the feasts, fasts, prayers, washings, rites, and rituals that Jewish believers would know.

<!-- p3 -->
The word for baptisms, or washings, in Greek is: **Baptismos** (bap-tis-mos’ - **G909**) - The act of immersing or washing. It refers to ceremonial washings or ritual purification. It can indicate immersion, rites of purification, or washings practiced in Jewish and Christian contexts.

<!-- p4 -->
Baptisms are one of the foundational doctrines mentioned in Hebrews:

<!-- p5 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p6 -->
> **<u>Hebrews 6:1-2</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Therefore, leaving the discussion of the elementary principles of Christ, let us go on to perfection, not laying again the foundation of repentance from dead works and of faith toward God,

<!-- p8 -->
## Washings of the Old Covenant - Historical Context

<!-- p9 -->
In the Old Covenant, ceremonial washings played a central role in maintaining ritual purity and symbolizing holiness before God. These washings were prescribed in the Law of Moses and applied both to the priests who served in the Tabernacle and to the people of Israel. For example, the priests were required to wash their hands and feet at the bronze laver before entering the Tabernacle or approaching the altar, symbolizing their readiness to serve in the presence of a holy God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="30" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 30:18-21</span>). Similarly, the rituals for cleansing people from impurity, such as contact with a corpse or skin diseases, involved washing with water as a sign of restoration to purity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LEV" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Leviticus 14:8</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="NUM" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Numbers 19:17-19</span>). These acts served as outward demonstrations of an inward desire for holiness, emphasizing the importance of being set apart for God.

<!-- p10 -->
Ceremonial washings in the Old Testament required regular repetition to maintain ritual purity. Unlike permanent solutions, these rituals only provided temporary external cleansing, leaving unresolved the deeper problem of sin. The author of Hebrews highlights this limitation, explaining that these practices were “symbolic for the present time,” unable to cleanse the worshiper’s conscience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:9-10</span>). These washings acted as constant reminders of human imperfection and the need for something greater-a definitive purification that could cleanse not only the body, but also the heart and soul.

<!-- p11 -->
The ceremonial washings of the Old Covenant find their fulfillment in Christ, who provides a complete and eternal cleansing through His sacrifice. Unlike the temporary rituals of the law, the blood of Christ fully purifies, cleansing not only the flesh but also the conscience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:13-14</span>). Through His death and resurrection, Jesus accomplished what the Old Testament rituals could only foreshadow: true reconciliation with God. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:22</span> calls believers to “let us draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, having our hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience, and our bodies washed with pure water.” This language links Christ’s work to the imagery of Old Testament washings while showing their far superior effectiveness. His sacrifice does not simply cover sin, but removes it completely (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 1:7</span>). In this way, the ceremonial washings served as a shadow, pointing to the deeper cleansing that only Christ could provide, fulfilling humanity’s need for holiness through divine intervention.

<!-- p12 -->
To reiterate this point, Christians are not obligated to perform any ceremonial washing. The washings did not provide salvation under the old covenant and do not provide salvation in the new covenant. Ceremonial washing will not increase our standing before God, nor win us favor. Paul strongly condemns the Judaizers of Galatia (read Galatians) who attempted to mix requirements from the Jewish context as necessary for salvation (e.g., circumcision). ¡Salvation is by faith in Jesus alone!

<!-- p13 -->
## The baptism of John

<!-- p14 -->
Several baptisms are mentioned in the New Testament canon. We have already spoken about the baptisms and washings of the Jewish tradition.

<!-- p15 -->
The ministry of John the Baptist focused on preparing the way for the coming of Jesus Christ. His baptism, described as a baptism of repentance for the forgiveness of sins (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 1:4</span>), called people to confess their sins, turn their hearts to God, and prepare for the arrival of the Messiah. It served as an external sign of internal repentance, symbolizing a desire for spiritual cleansing and readiness for the kingdom of God. However, John’s baptism was temporary and preparatory—it pointed to the greater work that Christ would fulfill. While John’s baptism focused on repentance, it did not provide complete forgiveness or spiritual regeneration, since it lacked the indwelling and empowerment of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:11-12</span>). Instead, John openly declared that one greater than he was coming, who would baptize not only with water but with the Holy Spirit and fire (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 3:16</span>).

<!-- p16 -->
John’s baptism was fulfilled and surpassed through the death, burial, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. Christian baptism, instituted by Jesus and practiced by the apostles, signifies a deeper reality-union with Christ, death to sin, and resurrection to new life (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 6:3-4</span>). Unlike John’s baptism, Christian baptism is directly related to faith in Jesus’ atoning sacrifice and the indwelling of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:38</span>). The arrival of the Holy Spirit at Pentecost marked the new covenant and the beginning of a Spirit-empowered life, something John’s baptism could not provide. Therefore, John’s baptism is no longer practiced, as it has been replaced by the baptism ordained by Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19</span)), which incorporates repentance, faith, and the sealing of believers by the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>).

<!-- p17 -->
## The baptism of Christ

<!-- p18 -->
The baptism of Christ established a powerful example for His followers and laid the foundation for the Christian practice of water baptism. Although Jesus had no sin, He submitted to John’s baptism to “fulfill all righteousness” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:15</span>). In doing so, He identified with humanity’s need for repentance and pointed toward His own death, burial, and resurrection. At His baptism, the Holy Spirit descended like a dove, and the Father declared: “This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:16-17</span>). This moment marked the beginning of Jesus’ public ministry and revealed the unity of the Father, the Son, and the Spirit, foreshadowing the spiritual transformation that believers would experience by faith in Christ.

<!-- p19 -->
Later, Jesus commanded His followers to be baptized, commissioning them to “go therefore and make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19</span>). Water baptism, as instituted by Christ, became an **act of obedience** and a **public declaration of faith** for believers. It represents their identification with Jesus’ death, burial, and resurrection (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 6:3-4</span>). Being immersed under the water symbolizes dying to sin, and coming up out of the water illustrates being raised to new life in Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:12</span>). However, it is crucial to clarify that this external act is not the source of salvation, but a **visible sign** of the **internal work of grace** already accomplished by the blood of Jesus.

<!-- p20 -->
Water baptism is a **symbol** of the cleansing power of the blood of Christ and of the renewal of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:26</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>). It signifies repentance, faith, and a commitment to follow Jesus. It is an external testimony of an internal transformation-a way for believers to publicly declare their allegiance to Christ and their participation in the body of Christ, the Church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:41</span>). Although it is a command of Jesus and was modeled in the early Church, it does not **save** a person. Salvation is by grace through faith alone (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>), and true cleansing occurs spiritually through the blood of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 1:5</span>). Water baptism is a response to that salvation, an act of obedience rather than a requirement for it.

<!-- p21 -->
In the New Testament, water baptism is consistently linked to **repentance and faith** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:38</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 16:16</span>), both of which require a personal decision to follow Christ-something infants are not able to make. Baptism symbolizes the believer’s deliberate identification with the death, burial, and resurrection of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 6:3-4</span>), as well as their commitment to live in obedience to Him. Some denominations (e.g., Catholics, Lutherans) practice infant baptism as a covenant sign similar to circumcision; however, since infants cannot express personal faith or repentance, the practice of **infant baptism** does not align with the biblical model of **believers’ baptism**. Instead, many churches practice **dedication ceremonies** for infants, following the example of Hannah dedicating Samuel to the Lord (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1SA" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Samuel 1:27-28</span>) and of Mary and Joseph presenting Jesus in the temple (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 2:22</span>). Dedication is not a substitute for baptism, but a commitment by parents to raise their child in the faith, teaching the Word of God and modeling a godly life until the child is old enough to make their own decision to follow Christ and be baptized.

<!-- p22 -->
Unlike the **ceremonial ablutions** of the Old Covenant, which required repeated rituals for purification, Christian baptism is a one-time event that reflects a work of grace already completed. The Old Testament ablutions symbolized external cleansing and foreshadowed the deeper spiritual cleansing that comes through Christ. In contrast, New Testament baptism symbolizes the inner renewal and new birth accomplished by the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>). It points to the believer’s participation in the new covenant, where cleansing from sin is permanent and is based on the finished work of Christ.

<!-- p23 -->
Water baptism is closely tied to the **promise of the Holy Spirit**. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:38</span>, Peter calls people to repent, be baptized, and receive the gift of the Holy Spirit. While water baptism publicly signifies the believer’s repentance and faith, the true baptism occurs inwardly when the Holy Spirit washes, regenerates, and seals the believer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:13</span>). This spiritual washing makes us members of the body of Christ and enables us to live holy lives. The **role of the Holy Spirit** in baptism reminds us that salvation is not achieved by rituals, but by God’s transforming power.

<!-- p24 -->
It is important to distinguish between the **symbolic act of water baptism** and the spiritual reality of salvation by the blood of Christ. The Bible clearly teaches that salvation is based on faith in Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 10:9-10</span>) and not on works, including baptism (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>). However, water baptism is an **act of obedience** that publicly identifies the believer with Christ and with the Church. It demonstrates an outward commitment to live as a disciple of Jesus, but it does not add to or complete the work of salvation.

<!-- p25 -->
Christ’s water baptism set an example for believers to follow, demonstrating humility, obedience, and readiness for ministry. For Christians today, water baptism serves as a public declaration of faith, symbolizing death to sin and resurrection to a new life. Although it is a command of Jesus and an important step of obedience, it is not required for salvation. True cleansing occurs through faith in the blood of Jesus, and empowerment for the Christian life comes through the work of the Holy Spirit in the believer. Baptism unites us with Christ and with His Church, reminding us of the internal transformation and renewal that God has already accomplished.

<!-- p26 -->
Having established water baptism as an external sign of faith, we now turn to the baptism of the Holy Spirit, which empowers believers for ministry and service.

<!-- p27 -->
## Baptism in the Holy Spirit

<!-- p28 -->
The baptism in the Holy Spirit is a profound and transformative experience promised by God to empower believers for ministry, service, and holy living. Every believer receives the Holy Spirit when they are born again-that is how we are made spiritually alive. But afterward, we must also receive the baptism in the Holy Spirit, which empowers us with boldness, spiritual gifts, and an effective ministry.

<!-- p29 -->
Water baptism is a public declaration of faith and symbolizes the cleansing of sin by the blood of Christ, whereas Spirit baptism represents the empowering presence of God in the believer’s life. Together, these two modes of baptism form complementary aspects of the Christian life-one as an external sign of obedience and the other as an internal source of power and renewal. The baptism of the Holy Spirit is the Spirit upon us more than within us. In salvation all believers receive the Spirit within them, but when the Spirit of God comes upon them, they receive power and boldness for ministry.

<!-- p30 -->
The baptism in the Holy Spirit was foretold by John the Baptist, who declared,

<!-- p31 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p32 -->
> **<u>Matthew 3:11</u>** (NKJV)
>
> I indeed baptize you with water unto repentance, but He who is coming after me is mightier than I, whose sandals I am not worthy to carry. He will baptize you with the Holy Spirit and fire.
>

<!-- p33 -->
</span>

<!-- p34 -->
This promise pointed to a greater work that Jesus would carry out after his resurrection-to send the Holy Spirit to dwell within his followers and empower them. Jesus confirmed this when he instructed his disciples to wait for the gift of the Spirit, explaining that they would receive power to be his witnesses (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>). The fulfillment of this promise occurred on the Day of Pentecost, when the disciples were filled with the Holy Spirit, evidenced by speaking in tongues, prophecy, and boldness to proclaim the gospel (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:1-4</span>).

<!-- p35 -->
This outpouring marked the beginning of the **Church Age** and demonstrated that the power of the Spirit was now available to all believers. This promise was not limited to the apostles, but extends to all who believe, as Peter declared, *“The promise is for you and your children and for all who are far off-for all whom the Lord our God will call”* (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="39" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:39</span>).

<!-- p36 -->
### Spirit within vs Spirit upon

<!-- p37 -->
When someone puts their faith in Jesus, the Holy Spirit gives them new life on the inside. That is what it means to be born again (**regenerated**). At that moment of faith, the Holy Spirit dwells **WITHIN** the believer, making them spiritually alive and adopting them into the family of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:5-6</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>). This is the new birth, or being born again, as Jesus described in John 3.

<!-- p38 -->
Biblical basis:

<!-- p39 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>* - “He saved us through the washing of regeneration and renewal by the Holy Spirit.”

<!-- p40 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>* - The Holy Spirit seals believers as a **guarantee** of their inheritance.

<!-- p41 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:9</span>* - “Whoever does not have the Spirit of Christ does not belong to him.”

<!-- p42 -->
This work of regeneration is universal for all believers at the moment of salvation and establishes a permanent dwelling of the Spirit. It does not depend on any external action, such as water baptism, and it guarantees salvation by grace through faith.

<!-- p43 -->
In contrast, the baptism in the Holy Spirit is a distinct and subsequent experience that empowers believers with power for service and witness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>). Whereas regeneration deals with new life and identity, the baptism in the Spirit focuses on empowerment UPON and the release of spiritual gifts. This baptism is often accompanied by signs and manifestations of the gifts of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p44 -->
Biblical foundation:

<!-- p45 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p46 -->
> **<u>Acts 1:8</u>** (NKJV)
>
> But you will receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you; and you will be my witnesses in Jerusalem, in all Judea and Samaria, and to the ends of the earth."
>

<!-- p47 -->
</span>

<!-- p48 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:15-17</span>* - The Samaritans had believed and were baptized, but later received the Holy Spirit through prayer and the laying on of hands.

<!-- p49 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 19:6</span>* - Disciples in Ephesus, already believers, received the Holy Spirit when Paul laid hands on them.

<!-- p50 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit does not replace regeneration but rather builds upon it, equipping the believer for spiritual service and boldness in ministry. Being baptized in the Holy Spirit is a free gift for those who are saved, not a requirement to be saved. It is not a requirement that someone manifest a <u>specific gift</u> of the Holy Spirit to prove their ‘baptism’ in the Holy Spirit, but we should expect the baptism of the Holy Spirit to be manifested today in the same way as in the book of Acts, ¡with signs and boldness accompanying! Some people err in thinking that if a person does not speak in tongues they are not saved, or that they do not have the baptism of the Holy Spirit. Not everyone will receive every gift or manifestation of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29-31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:29-31</span>).

<!-- p51 -->
## How to receive the baptism of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p52 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit is described in Scripture as a gift promised to believers and received by faith. It is distinct from salvation and is primarily for empowerment and witness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>). The baptism of the Holy Spirit is a gift of empowerment for believers, promised by Jesus and fulfilled at Pentecost. It is received through repentance, faith, and asking God. Though the laying on of hands is often used as a method, ultimately it is God who baptizes with the Spirit.

<!-- p53 -->
Scripture presents speaking in tongues and prophecy as a common sign, but the purpose of this baptism is much greater-providing boldness, power, and spiritual gifts for ministry. Every believer is encouraged to seek this experience as part of their spiritual journey, trusting that God will equip them for His work.

<!-- p54 -->
The Bible outlines several core principles on how this baptism is received:

<!-- p55 -->
### **1. The baptism of the Holy Spirit is a free gift from God**

<!-- p56 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit is not something believers can earn - it is a free gift given by God. God wants to give good gifts to His children and is eager to give the Holy Spirit to those who ask Him with faith and a desire for empowerment.

<!-- p57 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p58 -->
> **<u>Luke 11:13</u>** (NKJV)
>
> For if you, being evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, ¿how much more will your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to those who ask Him!"
>

<!-- p59 -->
</span>

<!-- p60 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="38-39" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p61 -->
> **<u>Acts 2:38-39</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Then Peter said to them, "Repent, and let every one of you be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of sins; and you shall receive the gift of the Holy Spirit.
>

###  **2. Ask, seek, and receive by faith**

<!-- p64 -->
Scripture emphasizes that the baptism in the Holy Spirit is received by asking in prayer and with faith-filled expectation. Faith, combined with the willingness to ask and receive, is the key to experiencing the baptism in the Spirit. If you're not sure whether God wants to give you the baptism of the Holy Spirit, ask Him about that, He is a good father! As He prompts you, you may feel a tangible presence of God in your body that you have never experienced before. It's okay if you don't! God is a great God, and He doesn't need to be boxed into a perfect formula. If you feel God pulling on your heart, surrender to Him!

## The gifts of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p79 -->
When the Holy Spirit was poured out, and people were baptized in the Holy Spirit, they were often accompanied by manifestations of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p80 -->
There are several lists of gifts in the New Testament:

<!-- p81 -->
- Motivational gifts given by God: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:6-8</span>

<!-- p82 -->
- Addressed in the chapter on the Church.

<!-- p83 -->
- Fivefold ministry gifts given by Jesus: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-13</span>

<!-- p84 -->
- Addressed in the chapter on the church.

<!-- p85 -->
- Manifestation gifts given by the Holy Spirit: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span>

<!-- p86 -->
- Addressed here.

<!-- p87 -->
Each set of these gifts is given for different purposes and operates in distinct ways, but together they point to the diversity and unity of gifts within the Body of Christ. Each gift has a unique role in fulfilling God’s purposes.

<!-- p88 -->
### Purpose &amp; Operations of the gifts

<!-- p89 -->
The primary purpose of the gifts of the Holy Spirit is to build up the Body of Christ and bring glory to God:

<!-- p90 -->
- Edification - Strengthening and encouraging believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
- Unity - Promoting harmony and cooperation within the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:12-13</span>).

<!-- p92 -->
- Evangelism - Demonstrating the power of God as a testimony to unbelievers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="43" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:43</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:6-7</span>).

<!-- p93 -->
- Service - Meeting the needs of others through acts of mercy and generosity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:6-8</span>).

<!-- p94 -->
These gifts are not for personal glory but for the common good (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:7</span>). They function best when exercised with love and humility, reflecting the character of Christ (1 Corinthians 13).

<!-- p95 -->
Paul emphasized that spiritual gifts should be exercised decently and in order (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:40</span>). This ensures that the use of the gifts promotes unity, peace, and understanding, rather than confusion or division.

<!-- p96 -->
- Prophecies must be evaluated (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:29</span>).

<!-- p97 -->
- When a message in tongues is given to the body, pray that it may be interpreted (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:27-28</span>).

<!-- p98 -->
- Gifts must always bring edification to the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:12</span>).

<!-- p99 -->
This order does not quench the Spirit, but ensures that the gifts operate in ways that honor God and benefit others.

<!-- p100 -->
The operation of spiritual gifts must always be grounded in love. Paul devotes an entire chapter (1 Corinthians 13) to explaining that without love, even the greatest spiritual gifts are meaningless. Love provides the motivation and the framework to use the gifts in ways that build others up rather than destroy them.

<!-- p101 -->
Furthermore, the gifts operate according to the will of the Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:11</span>). They cannot be manufactured or controlled by human effort-they are sovereignly given and empowered by the Holy Spirit, often at moments of need or ministry. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 13:8</span> says that eventually prophecy and tongues will cease. Theologically, people who believe that the gifts of the Holy Spirit have ceased are known as ‘Cessationists’. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 13:10</span> says “when the prefect \[Jesus\] comes, that desire which is in part will be caused to cease”. Since Jesus has not yet returned for His church, the gifts are still in operation on the earth.

<!-- p102 -->
It is crucial to recognize that the gifts of the Spirit must not be separated from the fruit of the Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-23</span>). While the gifts demonstrate power, the fruit reveals character: love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control.

<!-- p103 -->
- **Gifts without Fruit**: A believer may operate in gifts and still lack maturity or character, leading to pride, misuse, or harm.

<!-- p104 -->
- **Fruit without Gifts**: A believer may have godly character but lack the empowerment to minister effectively.

<!-- p105 -->
Both are necessary. The gifts equip believers for service. The fruit is a sign of their spiritual health (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 7:16</span>) and provide a ‘pleasant flavor’ to those who consume it.

<!-- p106 -->
### Explaining the 9 manifestation gifts of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p107 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span> lists the 9 manifestation gifts of the Holy Spirit:

<!-- p108 -->
1.  **Word of Wisdom** - Supernatural insight into God’s purposes or direction for a situation. Applies divine wisdom to complex problems or decisions (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:10</span>).

<!-- p109 -->
2.  **Word of Knowledge** - A supernatural revelation of information about a person, event, or circumstance that could not have been known naturally (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 4:17-18</span>).

<!-- p110 -->
3.  **Faith** - A supernatural ability to trust God for the miraculous, even in impossible situations. This gift inspires courage and confidence (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 3:6-7</span>).

<!-- p111 -->
4.  **Gifts of Healing** - The supernatural power to heal physical, emotional, or spiritual ailments and restore wholeness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 3:6-8</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 9:17-18</span>).

<!-- p112 -->
5.  **Working of Miracles** - The ability to perform supernatural acts that defy natural laws, such as multiplying food or raising the dead (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40-41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 9:40-41</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 2:9-11</span>).

<!-- p113 -->
6.  **Prophecy** - Declaring God’s truth or will under the inspiration of the Holy Spirit. It should be encouraging, edifying, and comforting (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>).

<!-- p114 -->
7.  **Distinguishing (Discernment) Between Spirits** - The ability to discern whether a spiritual influence comes from God, the enemy, or human origin (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 16:16-18</span>).

<!-- p115 -->
8.  **Various Kinds of Tongues** - Speaking in languages unknown to the speaker, often as an act of worship, prayer, or a sign to unbelievers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:4</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:22</span>).

<!-- p116 -->
9.  **Interpretation of Tongues** - The supernatural ability to interpret the message spoken in tongues so that the church may be edified (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:27</span>).

<!-- p117 -->
Paul makes it clear that all the gifts are valuable and must work together for the common good of the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:7</span>). However, He also encourages believers to desire the greater gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:31</span>). The greater gifts are often interpreted as those that more directly edify the church, such as prophecy (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:1-5</span>).

<!-- p118 -->
- Prophecy is especially emphasized because it strengthens, encourages, and comforts others (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>). Paul instructs believers to earnestly desire this gift, as it promotes clarity and understanding in the church.

<!-- p119 -->
- Gifts such as tongues are highly valued for personal edification and prayer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:4</span>), but Paul teaches that when giving a message in tongues to the Church one should pray for interpretation to ensure that they edify the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:27-28</span>).

<!-- p120 -->
Although the Holy Spirit gives the gifts, we must yield and submit to the Holy Spirit to use them. As with muscles and talents, when we practice surrendering to the Holy Spirit to use His gifts, we become more confident and reliable in their use.
