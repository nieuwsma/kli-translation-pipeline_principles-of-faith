# Chapter 11 - Baptisms - Water and the Spirit

<!-- p1 -->
Baptism in our modern era, separated from its Jewish roots, is commonly thought of only in terms of water baptism, and the baptism of the Holy Spirit (Pentecost).

<!-- p2 -->
The book of Hebrews was written for a Jewish audience. A large number of Christians in our modern era have neither Jewish roots nor heritage. Without Jewish roots and culture many of us lack the cultural context for things that first-century Messianic Jews (those Jewish believers who confess Jesus) would have shared. Since we were not raised in Jewish traditions, we generally do not know much about the feasts, fasting, prayer, washings, customs, and procedures that Jewish believers would have known.

<!-- p3 -->
The Greek word for baptisms, or washings, is: **Baptismos** (bap-tis-mos’ - **G909**) - The act of immersion or washing. It refers to ceremonial washings or ritual purification. It can denote immersion, purification procedures, or washings performed in a Jewish and Christian context.

<!-- p4 -->
Baptisms are among the foundational doctrines mentioned in Hebrews:

<!-- p5 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="Hebrews" data-chapter="6" data-verses="1-2" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p6 -->
> **<u>Hebrews 6:1-2</u>** (NKJV) ***1** Therefore, leaving the discussion of the elementary teachings of Christ, let us go on to perfection, not laying again the foundation of repentance from dead works and of faith toward God, **2** of the doctrine of baptisms, of laying on of hands, of resurrection of the dead, and of eternal judgment.*

<!-- p7 -->
</span>

<!-- p8 -->
## Old Testament Washings - Historical Context

<!-- p9 -->
In the Old Testament, ritual washings played a central role in maintaining ceremonial purity and signifying holiness before God. These washings were commanded in the Law of Moses and concerned both the priests who served in the Tabernacle and the people of Israel. For example, the priests were required to wash their hands and feet at the bronze basin before entering the tent or approaching the altar, signifying their readiness to minister before a holy God (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Exodus" data-chapter="30" data-verses="18-21" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Exodus 30:18-21</span>). Likewise, procedures for cleansing someone from impurity, such as contact with a corpse or skin diseases, involved washing with water as a sign of restoration to cleanliness (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Leviticus" data-chapter="14" data-verses="8" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Leviticus 14:8</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Numbers" data-chapter="19" data-verses="17-19" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Numbers 19:17-19</span>). These acts were outward demonstrations of an inward desire for holiness, emphasizing the importance of being set apart for God.

<!-- p10 -->
The ritual washings in the Old Testament had to be repeated regularly to maintain ceremonial purity. Unlike a permanent solution, these procedures provided only temporary external cleansing, leaving the deeper issue of sin unresolved. The writer of Hebrews underscores this limitation, explaining that these practices were “a symbol for the present time,” unable to cleanse the conscience of the worshiper (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Hebrews" data-chapter="9" data-verses="9-10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 9:9-10</span>). These washings were constant reminders of human insufficiency and the need for something greater—the ultimate cleansing that could purify not only the body but also the heart and spirit.

<!-- p11 -->
The Old Testament ritual washings find their fulfillment in Christ, who provides complete and eternal cleansing through His sacrifice. Unlike the temporary procedures of the law, the blood of Christ cleanses perfectly, purifying not only the body but also the conscience (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Hebrews" data-chapter="9" data-verses="13-14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 9:13-14</span>). Through His death and resurrection, Jesus accomplished what the Old Testament rites could only foreshadow—the true reconciliation with God. <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Hebrews" data-chapter="10" data-verses="22" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 10:22</span> calls believers to “draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, having our hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience and our bodies washed with pure water.” This language ties the work of Christ to the imagery of Old Testament washings while highlighting its vastly superior efficacy. His sacrifice does not merely cover sin but removes it entirely (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 John" data-chapter="1" data-verses="7" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 John 1:7</span>). In this way, the ritual washings were shadows, pointing to the deeper inward cleansing that Christ alone would provide, fulfilling humanity’s need for holiness through God’s intervention.

<!-- p12 -->
To underscore this point, Christians are under no obligation to perform any ceremonial washings. Washings did not provide salvation under the old covenant and do not provide salvation in the new covenant. Ceremonial washing will not elevate our standing before God, nor will it grant us favor. Paul strongly condemns the Judaizers of Galatia (see Galatians) who attempted to mix requirements from the Jewish context as necessary for salvation (e.g., circumcision). Salvation is by faith in Jesus alone!

<!-- p13 -->
## The Baptism of John

<!-- p14 -->
Within the New Testament canon there are several baptisms mentioned. We have already discussed the baptisms and washings of Jewish culture.

<!-- p15 -->
The ministry of John the Baptist centered on preparing the way for the coming of Jesus Christ. His baptism, described as a baptism of repentance for the forgiveness of sins (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Mark" data-chapter="1" data-verses="4" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Mark 1:4</span>), called people to confess their sins, turn their hearts back to God, and prepare for the arrival of the Messiah. It served as an outward sign of inward repentance, signifying a desire for spiritual cleansing and readiness for the kingdom of God. However, John’s baptism was temporary and preparatory—it pointed to the greater work that Christ would accomplish. Although John’s baptism emphasized repentance, it did not provide complete forgiveness or spiritual rebirth, because it lacked the indwelling and empowerment of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Matthew" data-chapter="3" data-verses="11-12" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 3:11-12</span>). Instead, John openly declared that One greater than he was coming, who would baptize not only with water but with the Holy Spirit and fire (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Luke" data-chapter="3" data-verses="16" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Luke 3:16</span>).

<!-- p16 -->
John’s baptism was fulfilled and superseded through the death, burial, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. Christian baptism, instituted by Jesus and carried out by the apostles, signifies a deeper inward reality—union with Christ, death to sin, and resurrection to new life (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Romans" data-chapter="6" data-verses="3-4" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 6:3-4</span>). Unlike John’s baptism, Christian baptism is directly linked to faith in Jesus’ atoning sacrifice and the indwelling of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="2" data-verses="38" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 2:38</span>). The arrival of the Holy Spirit on the Day of Pentecost marked the new covenant and the beginning of living by the power of the Spirit—something John’s baptism could not impart. For this reason, John’s baptism is no longer practiced, as it was replaced by the baptism commanded by Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Matthew" data-chapter="28" data-verses="19" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 28:19</span>), which encompasses repentance, faith, and the sealing of believers by the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Ephesians" data-chapter="1" data-verses="13-14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>).

<!-- p17 -->
## The Baptism of Christ

<!-- p18 -->
The baptism of Christ set a powerful example for His followers and established the foundation for the Christian practice of water baptism. Though Jesus had no sin, He submitted to be baptized by John in order to “fulfill all righteousness” (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Matthew" data-chapter="3" data-verses="15" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 3:15</span>). In doing so, He identified with humanity’s need for repentance and pointed forward to His own death, burial, and resurrection. At His baptism, the Holy Spirit descended like a dove, and the Father declared, “This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased” (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Matthew" data-chapter="3" data-verses="16-17" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 3:16-17</span>). That moment marked the beginning of Jesus’ public ministry and revealed the unity of the Father, the Son, and the Spirit, hinting at the spiritual transformation believers would experience through faith in Christ.

<!-- p19 -->
Later Jesus commanded His followers to be baptized, sending them to “go therefore and make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit” (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Matthew" data-chapter="28" data-verses="19" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 28:19</span>). Water baptism, as instituted by Christ, became an **act of obedience** and a **public declaration of faith** for believers. It shows their identification with the death, burial, and resurrection of Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Romans" data-chapter="6" data-verses="3-4" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 6:3-4</span>). Immersion under the water signifies death to sin, and rising from the water represents resurrection to new life in Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Colossians" data-chapter="2" data-verses="12" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Colossians 2:12</span>). However, it is important to note that this outward act is not the source of salvation but is a **visible sign** of the **inward work of grace** already accomplished by the blood of Jesus.

<!-- p20 -->
Water baptism is a **sign** of the purifying power of the blood of Christ and the renewal of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Ephesians" data-chapter="5" data-verses="26" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 5:26</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Titus" data-chapter="3" data-verses="5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Titus 3:5</span>). It signifies repentance, faith, and a commitment to follow Jesus. It is an outward testimony of inward change—a way for believers to publicly declare their allegiance to Christ and their participation in the body of Christ, the Church (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="2" data-verses="41" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 2:41</span>). Although it was commanded by Jesus and demonstrated in the early Church, it does not **save** a person. Salvation is by grace through faith alone (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Ephesians" data-chapter="2" data-verses="8-9" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>), and true cleansing takes place spiritually through the blood of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Revelation" data-chapter="1" data-verses="5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Revelation 1:5</span>). Water baptism is a response to that salvation, an act of obedience rather than a condition for obtaining it.

<!-- p21 -->
In the New Testament, water baptism is firmly connected to **repentance and faith** (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="2" data-verses="38" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 2:38</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Mark" data-chapter="16" data-verses="16" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Mark 16:16</span>), both of which require a personal decision to follow Christ—something infants are not able to do. Baptism signifies the believer’s deliberate identification with Christ’s death, burial, and resurrection (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Romans" data-chapter="6" data-verses="3-4" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 6:3-4</span>), along with his or her commitment to live in obedience to Him. Some denominations (for example, Catholics, Lutherans) practice infant baptism as a covenant sign analogous to circumcision; however, since infants cannot demonstrate personal faith or repentance, the practice of **infant baptism** does not align with the biblical pattern of **believers’ baptism**. Instead, many churches hold **child dedication ceremonies** following the example of Hannah dedicating Samuel to the Lord (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Samuel" data-chapter="1" data-verses="27-28" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Samuel 1:27-28</span>) and Mary and Joseph presenting Jesus at the temple (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Luke" data-chapter="2" data-verses="22" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Luke 2:22</span>). Dedication is not a substitute for baptism but a promise by parents to raise their child in the faith, teaching the Word of God and modeling a life of godliness until the child is old enough to make his or her own decision to follow Christ and be baptized.

<!-- p22 -->
Unlike the ritual washings of the Old Covenant, which required repeated procedures for purification, Christian baptism is a one-time event that displays the completed work of grace. The Old Covenant washings signified external cleansing and foreshadowed the internal spiritual cleansing that would come through Christ. By contrast, New Testament baptism signifies the inward renewal and rebirth accomplished by the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Titus" data-chapter="3" data-verses="5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Titus 3:5</span>). It points to the believer’s participation in the new covenant, in which the cleansing of sin is permanent and rests upon the finished work of Christ.

<!-- p23 -->
Water baptism is closely linked to the **promise of the Holy Spirit**. In <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="2" data-verses="38" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 2:38</span>, Peter calls people to repent, be baptized, and receive the gift of the Holy Spirit. While water baptism publicly signifies the believer’s repentance and faith, true baptism happens inwardly when the Holy Spirit washes, regenerates, and seals the believer (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Corinthians" data-chapter="12" data-verses="13" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 12:13</span>). This spiritual washing makes us members of the body of Christ and enables us to live holy lives. The role of the **Holy Spirit** in baptism reminds us that salvation is not accomplished through rituals but through the transforming power of God.

<!-- p24 -->
It is important to distinguish between the **symbolic act of water baptism** and the spiritual reality of salvation through the blood of Christ. The Bible clearly teaches that salvation depends on faith in Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Romans" data-chapter="10" data-verses="9-10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 10:9-10</span>) and not works, including baptism (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Ephesians" data-chapter="2" data-verses="8-9" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>). However, water baptism is an **act of obedience** that publicly identifies the believer with Christ and the Church. It displays an outward pledge to live as a disciple of Jesus, but it neither adds to nor completes the work of salvation.

<!-- p25 -->
Christ’s water baptism set an example for believers to follow, demonstrating humility, obedience, and readiness for ministry. For Christians today, water baptism serves as a public declaration of faith, representing death to sin and resurrection to new life. Although it is commanded by Jesus and an important step of obedience, it is not a requirement for salvation. True cleansing takes place through faith in the blood of Jesus, and the empowerment for the Christian life comes from the work of the Holy Spirit within the believer. Baptism unites us with Christ and His Church, reminding us of the inward transformation and renewal that God has already accomplished.

<!-- p26 -->
Having established water baptism as an outward sign of faith, we now turn to the baptism of the Holy Spirit, which empowers believers for ministry and service.

<!-- p27 -->
## The Baptism of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p28 -->
Baptism in the Holy Spirit is a profound and transformative experience promised by God to empower believers for ministry, service, and holy living. Every believer receives the Holy Spirit when born again—this is how we are made spiritually alive. But afterward, we should also receive baptism in the Holy Spirit, which gives us boldness, spiritual gifts, and effective ministry.

<!-- p29 -->
Water baptism is a public announcement of faith and signifies the cleansing of sin through the blood of Christ; the baptism of the Spirit displays God’s empowering presence in the believer’s life. Together, these two kinds of baptism form complementary facets of the Christian walk—one as an outward sign of obedience and the other as an inward source of power and renewal. The baptism of the Holy Spirit is the Spirit upon us rather than within us. At salvation all believers receive the Spirit within them, but when the Spirit of God comes upon them, they receive power and boldness for ministry.

<!-- p30 -->
The baptism in the Holy Spirit was foretold by John the Baptist, who said,

<!-- p31 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="fragment" data-book="Matthew" data-chapter="3" data-verses="11" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p32 -->
> **<u>Matthew 3:11</u>** *I baptize you with water for repentance, but He who comes after me… will baptize you with the Holy Spirit and fire”*

<!-- p33 -->
</span>

<!-- p34 -->
This promise pointed to the greater work that Jesus would accomplish after His resurrection—sending the Holy Spirit to dwell within and empower His followers. Jesus confirmed this when He directed His disciples to wait for the gift of the Spirit, explaining that they would receive power to be His witnesses (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="1" data-verses="8" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 1:8</span>). The fulfillment of this promise occurred on the Day of Pentecost, when the disciples were filled with the Holy Spirit, evidenced by speaking in tongues, prophesying, and boldness to proclaim the gospel (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="2" data-verses="1-4" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 2:1-4</span>).

<!-- p35 -->
This outpouring inaugurated the beginning of the **Church Age** and showed that the power of the Spirit is now available to all believers. This promise was not limited to the apostles but extends to all who believe, as Peter declared, “This promise is for you and your children and for all who are far off—for all whom the Lord our God will call” (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="2" data-verses="39" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 2:39</span>).

<!-- p36 -->
### The Spirit Within versus the Spirit Upon

<!-- p37 -->
When a person places his or her faith in Jesus, the Holy Spirit gives new life within. That is what it means to be born again (**regeneration**). At that moment of faith, the Holy Spirit dwells **WITHIN** the believer, making him or her spiritually alive and welcoming him or her into the family of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="John" data-chapter="3" data-verses="5-6" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 3:5-6</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Titus" data-chapter="3" data-verses="5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Titus 3:5</span>). This is regeneration or being born again, as Jesus explained in John 3.

<!-- p38 -->
Scriptural foundation:

<!-- p39 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Titus" data-chapter="3" data-verses="5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Titus 3:5</span>* - “He saved us through the washing of regeneration and renewing by the Holy Spirit.”

<!-- p40 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Ephesians" data-chapter="1" data-verses="13-14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>* - The Holy Spirit seals believers as the **guarantee** of their inheritance.

<!-- p41 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Romans" data-chapter="8" data-verses="9" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 8:9</span>* - "Anyone who does not have the Spirit of Christ is not His."

<!-- p42 -->
This work of regeneration is universal for all believers at the time of salvation and establishes the permanent indwelling of the Spirit. It does not depend on any external act, such as water baptism, and it secures salvation by grace through faith.

<!-- p43 -->
By contrast, baptism in the Holy Spirit is a distinct and subsequent event that empowers believers with power for ministry and witness (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="1" data-verses="8" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 1:8</span>). While regeneration concerns new life and identity, the baptism of the Spirit focuses on empowerment **UPON** and the impartation of spiritual gifts. This baptism is often accompanied by signs and manifestations of the gifts of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p44 -->
Scriptural Basis:

<!-- p45 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="1" data-verses="8" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p46 -->
> **<u>Acts 1:8</u>** *(NKJV) But you shall receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you; and you shall be My witnesses in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the end of the earth.*

<!-- p47 -->
</span>

<!-- p48 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="8" data-verses="15-17" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 8:15-17</span>* - The Samaritans had believed and been baptized, but later received the Holy Spirit through prayer and the laying on of hands.

<!-- p49 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="19" data-verses="6" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 19:6</span>* - The disciples in Ephesus, who were already believers, received the Holy Spirit when Paul laid his hands on them.

<!-- p50 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit does not alter regeneration but builds upon it, empowering the believer for spiritual ministry and boldness in service. Being baptized in the Holy Spirit is a free gift for those who are saved, not a requirement for being saved. It is not necessary for a person to display a <u>specific</u> gift of the Holy Spirit to validate their ‘baptism’ in the Holy Spirit, but we should expect that the baptism of the Holy Spirit will appear today in much the same way as it did in the book of Acts, accompanied by signs and boldness! Some people err by thinking that if one does not speak in tongues then they are not saved, or that they do not have the baptism of the Holy Spirit. Not everyone will receive every gift or manifestation of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Corinthians" data-chapter="12" data-verses="29-31" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 12:29-31</span>).

<!-- p51 -->
## How to Receive the Baptism of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p52 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit is described in Scripture as a promised gift for believers and is received by faith. It is distinct from salvation and is primarily for empowerment and witness (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="1" data-verses="8" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 1:8</span>). The baptism of the Holy Spirit is a gift of empowerment for believers, promised by Jesus and fulfilled on the day of Pentecost. It is received through repentance, faith, and asking God. Although the laying on of hands is often used as a means, ultimately it is God who baptizes with the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p53 -->
Scripture presents speaking in tongues and prophecy as common signs, but the purpose of this baptism is greater - to provide boldness, power, and spiritual gifts for ministry. Every believer is encouraged to seek this experience as part of their spiritual journey, believing that God will empower them for His work.

<!-- p54 -->
The Bible outlines several foundational principles regarding how this baptism is received:

<!-- p55 -->
### **1. The Baptism of the Holy Spirit is a Free Gift from God**

<!-- p56 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit is not something believers can earn—it is a free gift given by God. God wants to give His children good gifts and is ready to give the Holy Spirit to those who ask in faith with a desire to be empowered.

<!-- p57 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="Luke" data-chapter="11" data-verses="13" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p58 -->
> **<u>Luke 11:13</u>** (NKJV) *If you then, being evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to those who ask Him!"*

<!-- p59 -->
</span>

<!-- p60 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="2" data-verses="38-39" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p61 -->
> **<u>Acts 2:38-39</u>** (NKJV) **38** *Then Peter said to them, "Repent, and let every one of you be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins; and you shall receive the gift of the Holy Spirit. **39** For the promise is to you and to your children, and to all who are afar off, as many as the Lord our God will call."*

<!-- p62 -->
</span>

<!-- p63 -->
- **2. Pray, Seek, and Receive by Faith**

<!-- p64 -->
Scripture emphasizes that baptism in the Holy Spirit is received by asking in prayer and with faith-filled expectation. Faith, combined with a readiness to ask and receive, is the key to experiencing the baptism of the Spirit. If you are unsure whether God wants to give you the baptism in the Holy Spirit, ask Him about it, He is a good Father! As He stirs you, you may feel a tangible sense of God’s presence in your body that you have never experienced before. It’s okay if you don’t feel that! God is a great God and does not need to be confined to a single perfect formula. If you feel God drawing your heart, surrender to Him!

<!-- p65 -->
- **<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="8" data-verses="15-17" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 8:15-17</span>** - Peter and John prayed and **laid hands** on the believers, and they **received the Holy Spirit**.

<!-- p66 -->
- **<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="10" data-verses="44-46" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 10:44-46</span>** - Cornelius and his household **received the Holy Spirit** while Peter was preaching-without hands being laid on them-showing that ultimately it is **God who baptizes**.

<!-- p67 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="fragment" data-book="Galatians" data-chapter="3" data-verses="2" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p68 -->
> **<u>Galatians 3:2</u>** (NKJV) *… Did you receive the Spirit by the works of the law, or by the hearing of faith?*

<!-- p69 -->
</span>

<!-- p70 -->
### **3. Laying On of Hands (Optional)**

<!-- p71 -->
While some received the Holy Spirit suddenly (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="10" data-verses="44" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 10:44</span>), others received through the laying on of hands by other believers. Laying on of hands is a biblical practice, but not a requirement. Ultimately the Holy Spirit responds to faith, not religious procedures.

<!-- p72 -->

## Gifts of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p79 -->
When the Holy Spirit was poured out, and people were baptized in the Holy Spirit, the manifestation of the Holy Spirit often accompanied it.

<!-- p80 -->
There are several lists of gifts in the New Testament:

<!-- p81 -->
- Motivational gifts given by God: <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Romans" data-chapter="12" data-verses="6-8" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 12:6-8</span>

<!-- p82 -->
- Discussed in the chapter about the Church.

<!-- p83 -->
- Fivefold Ministry gifts given by Jesus: <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Ephesians" data-chapter="4" data-verses="11-13" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 4:11-13</span>

<!-- p84 -->
- Discussed in the chapter about the church.

<!-- p85 -->
- Manifestation gifts given by the Holy Spirit: <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Corinthians" data-chapter="12" data-verses="8-10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span>

<!-- p86 -->
- Discussed here.

<!-- p87 -->
Each set of these gifts is given for different purposes and operates in different ways, but together they display the diversity and unity of the gifts within the **Body of Christ**. Each gift has a unique role in fulfilling God's purposes.

<!-- p88 -->
### Purpose & Operation of the Gifts

<!-- p89 -->
The primary purpose of the gifts of the Holy Spirit is to build up the Body of Christ and bring glory to God:

<!-- p90 -->
- **Edification** - Strengthening and encouraging believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Corinthians" data-chapter="14" data-verses="3" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
- **Unity** - Promoting harmony and cooperation within the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Ephesians" data-chapter="4" data-verses="12-13" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 4:12-13</span>).

<!-- p92 -->
- **Evangelism** - Manifesting God's power as a witness to unbelievers (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="2" data-verses="43" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 2:43</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="8" data-verses="6-7" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 8:6-7</span>).

<!-- p93 -->
- **Service** - Meeting the needs of others through acts of mercy and generosity (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Romans" data-chapter="12" data-verses="6-8" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 12:6-8</span>).

<!-- p94 -->
These gifts are not for personal glory but for the benefit of all (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Corinthians" data-chapter="12" data-verses="7" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 12:7</span>). They operate best when used with love and humility, reflecting the character of Christ (1 Corinthians 13).

<!-- p95 -->
Paul emphasized that spiritual gifts must be used with respect and in an orderly manner (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Corinthians" data-chapter="14" data-verses="40" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 14:40</span>). This ensures that the use of the gifts promotes unity, peace, and understanding, rather than confusion or division.

<!-- p96 -->
- Prophecy should be tested (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Corinthians" data-chapter="14" data-verses="29" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 14:29</span>).

<!-- p97 -->
- When you give a message in tongues to the body, pray that it may be interpreted (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Corinthians" data-chapter="14" data-verses="27-28" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 14:27-28</span>).

<!-- p98 -->
- The gifts must always bring **edification** to the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Corinthians" data-chapter="14" data-verses="12" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 14:12</span>).

<!-- p99 -->
This order does not quench the Spirit but ensures that the gifts operate in ways that honor God and benefit others.

<!-- p100 -->
The exercise of spiritual gifts must always be rooted in love. Paul devotes an entire chapter (1 Corinthians 13) to explain that without love, even the greatest spiritual gifts are meaningless. Love provides the motivation and framework for using the gifts in ways that build others up rather than tear them down.

<!-- p101 -->
Moreover, the gifts operate **as the Spirit wills** (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Corinthians" data-chapter="12" data-verses="11" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 12:11</span>). They cannot be manufactured or controlled by human effort—they are given in sovereignty and empowered by the Holy Spirit, often in times of need or ministry. <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Corinthians" data-chapter="13" data-verses="8" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 13:8</span> says that ultimately prophecy and speaking in tongues will cease. Theologically, people who believe that the gifts of the Holy Spirit have ceased are known as ‘Cessationists’. <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Corinthians" data-chapter="13" data-verses="10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 13:10</span> says “when that which is perfect \[Jesus\] has come, that which is in part will cease”. Since Jesus has not yet returned for His Church, the gifts are still in operation in the world.

<!-- p102 -->
It is important to recognize that **the gifts of the Spirit** should not be separated from **the fruit of the Spirit** (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Galatians" data-chapter="5" data-verses="22-23" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Galatians 5:22-23</span>). While the gifts demonstrate power, the fruit reveals character—love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control.

<!-- p103 -->
- **Gifts without Fruit**: A believer may operate in gifts but lack maturity or character, which can lead to pride, misuse, or harm.

<!-- p104 -->
- **Fruit without Gifts**: A believer may have godly character but lack the empowerment to minister effectively.

<!-- p105 -->
Both are needed. The gifts empower believers for ministry. The fruit is a sign of their spiritual health (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Matthew" data-chapter="7" data-verses="16" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 7:16</span>) and provides a ‘good taste’ to those who taste it.

<!-- p106 -->
### Explaining the 9 Manifestation Gifts of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p107 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Corinthians" data-chapter="12" data-verses="8-10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span> lists the 9 Manifestation Gifts of the Holy Spirit:

<!-- p108 -->
1.  **Word of Wisdom** - A miraculous insight into God’s purposes or direction for a particular situation. It applies Divine wisdom to problems or difficult decisions (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="6" data-verses="10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 6:10</span>).

<!-- p109 -->
2.  **Word of Knowledge** - A miraculous revelation of information about a person, event, or situation that could not be known naturally (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="John" data-chapter="4" data-verses="17-18" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 4:17-18</span>).

<!-- p110 -->
3.  **Faith** - The miraculous ability to trust God for miraculous things, even in impossible circumstances. This gift stirs boldness and confidence (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="3" data-verses="6-7" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 3:6-7</span>).

<!-- p111 -->
4.  **Gifts of Healings** - The miraculous power to heal physical, emotional, or spiritual illnesses and restore wholeness (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="3" data-verses="6-8" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 3:6-8</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="9" data-verses="17-18" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 9:17-18</span>).

<!-- p112 -->
5.  **Working of Miracles** - The ability to perform miraculous acts that defy natural laws, such as multiplying food or raising the dead (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="9" data-verses="40-41" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 9:40-41</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="John" data-chapter="2" data-verses="9-11" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 2:9-11</span>).

<!-- p113 -->
6.  **Prophecy** - Declaring the truth or will of God under the inspiration of the Holy Spirit. It should be encouraging, edifying, and comforting (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Corinthians" data-chapter="14" data-verses="3" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>).

<!-- p114 -->
7.  **Discerning (Distinguishing) of Spirits** - The ability to determine whether a spiritual influence is from God, the enemy, or a human source (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="16" data-verses="16-18" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 16:16-18</span>).

<!-- p115 -->
8.  **Different Kinds of Tongues** - Speaking in languages unknown to the speaker, often as an act of worship, prayer, or a sign to unbelievers (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="2" data-verses="4" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 2:4</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Corinthians" data-chapter="14" data-verses="22" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 14:22</span>).

<!-- p116 -->
9.  **Interpretation of Tongues** - The miraculous ability to interpret a message spoken in tongues so that the church may be edified (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Corinthians" data-chapter="14" data-verses="27" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 14:27</span>).

<!-- p117 -->
Paul makes it clear that all the gifts are valuable and should work together for the common good of the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Corinthians" data-chapter="12" data-verses="7" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 12:7</span>). However, he also urges believers to desire the greater gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Corinthians" data-chapter="12" data-verses="31" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 12:31</span>). The greater gifts are often interpreted as those that build up the church more directly, such as prophecy (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Corinthians" data-chapter="14" data-verses="1-5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 14:1-5</span>).

<!-- p118 -->
- Prophecy is strongly emphasized because it strengthens, encourages, and comforts others (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Corinthians" data-chapter="14" data-verses="3" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>). Paul instructs believers to earnestly desire this gift since it brings clarity and understanding in the church.

<!-- p119 -->
- Gifts such as speaking in tongues are highly valued for personal edification and prayer (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Corinthians" data-chapter="14" data-verses="4" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 14:4</span>), but Paul teaches that when delivering a message in tongues to the Church they should ask for interpretation to ensure the church is edified (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Corinthians" data-chapter="14" data-verses="27-28" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 14:27-28</span>).

<!-- p120 -->
Although it is the Holy Spirit who gives the gifts, we must humble ourselves and surrender to the Holy Spirit so that He may use them through us. As with muscles and talents, as we practice yielding to the Holy Spirit to use His gifts, we become more confident and reliable in using them.
