# Chapter 6 - Faith Toward God

<!-- p1 -->
Faith is a foundational concept in Christianity. The word faith is used a great deal in Christianity, and so sometimes the meaning of the word gets replaced by slippery, wrong notions and a distorted understanding. But it is not something you can grasp with your hand-it is formed in your heart when you trust in God. Conviction is belief.

<!-- p2 -->
The author of Hebrews instructs us to have ‘faith toward God’.

### Defining Faith

<!-- p10 -->
Faith is often misunderstood and misrepresented within Christianity. As the proverb says, “there is a ditch on both sides of the road”! Because the word faith is used so much without being properly explained in our modern Christianity, it can take on increasingly wrong definitions as it is misunderstood. Faith is often portrayed as making a general, undefined statement of belief or as looking like some kind of force or “magic,” something you have a particular, measurable, limited amount of.

<!-- p11 -->
#### What Faith Is Not

<!-- p12 -->
Faith misrepresented as <u>general and undefined</u> has no specific belief or conviction. This often shows up when a little is asked, since this representation of faith cannot specify anything concrete. If you ask, ‘what do you believe’ or ‘what do you believe in,’ the person with this understanding of faith often cannot clarify anything beyond ‘I believe.’ <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 3:15</span> says we are to be ready to give the answer for the hope within us.

<!-- p13 -->
Sometimes people think faith is a magical power-but that is not what Scripture teaches. Faith is about a real relationship built on trust. In Scripture, faith is far more active and relational-it is grounded in conviction, trust, and confidence in the character and promises of God. It is not an independent personal force, but an active response directed toward God that shapes thoughts, feelings, and actions.

<!-- p14 -->
What does belief have to do with conviction?

<!-- p15 -->
Belief shapes how we see and interact with the world around us. Belief shapes perspective and drives our actions. That is how our minds work-we live according to what we believe is possible.

<!-- p16 -->
We move toward what we see, understand, and believe is possible. As we walk down a hallway, our minds perceive the boundaries of the floor and the wall and tell our feet directly where we can step, and what to avoid. That truth goes beyond the physical guidance of the world around us.

<!-- p17 -->
What if you believe that it is not possible for you to become financially self-sufficient or to live a healthy, active life. Then your actions will follow that belief, and you, like the belief, will not do-or are not even likely to try-what you believe is not possible.

<!-- p18 -->
If you believe that God has an unloving, punitive character, that view of Him will keep you from approaching Him as His child.

<!-- p19 -->
#### Faith is Conviction

<!-- p20 -->
Now, what is faith? Persuasion! The Greek word ***pistis*** means persuasion, or the result of having been persuaded. We must have persuasion toward God. In English it would be said faith (persuasion) ‘**in** God’. If you are married, we hope that you have faith ‘**in’** your spouse. You believe that they will do what they said they will do, and you believe that they are honest and true. This confidence must go beyond what they have said, and extend to them as a whole.

<!-- p21 -->
Building faith, persuasion, and trust within a relationship takes time and experience. Hopefully in life you have learned how to cultivate a trustworthy relationship that you have with another person. The principles you have learned that allow you to increase the trust you have in someone you know also allow you to increase the faith you have in God. Relational faith grows over time, through experience, consistency, and small and large actions that build your confidence in the other person's character and attributes.

<!-- p22 -->
‘Faith directed toward God’ is relational. Relationships are built by two sides, not just one. Our understanding about the faith we have in God is that God is responsible for His side of the relationship (made possible by the atoning death of Jesus, and by filling us with His Spirit at new birth), and He is also helping us on our side (through the work of the Comforter—the Holy Spirit). God does most of the work; our part (as we will discuss later in this chapter) is to keep in step with Him by obeying *LA*.

### Jesus Matures Our Faith

<!-- p24 -->
Jesus is the author and finisher of our faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 12:2</span>). Our faith is intended to continually grow and expand. We do not reach a single point where we have enough ‘faith’; rather, our faith increases. Consider a deep personal relationship that you have in your life today. Like any relationship, we do not arrive at a particular point where we now ‘know’ the person. We continue to get to know them and increase our knowledge of them, which refines, shapes, and grows our relationship.

<!-- p25 -->
How do we grow in the faith we have in God? By continuing to encounter Him across His many revelations, manifestations, and attributes. Again, consider a deep personal relationship in your life. You deepen the relationship by interacting in different ways, formats, media, and settings. You talk, you send messages, you might even write to them. You see them one-on-one, as well as in a group. You talk about what you value, and you may challenge each other. The ways are many and virtually limitless. Through these many expressions you come to know the person more deeply.

<!-- p26 -->
We all must come to know God, through Jesus, as our Savior. Remember the names of God (*see appendix),* only because we know God as Savior does not mean we are already experiencing Him as Deliverer, Healer, Counselor, Lord.

<!-- p27 -->
If you are at a place where you have been persuaded (faith) to know God as your Savior, building this relationship will give you confidence in His character so that you may come to know Him in other ways.

<!-- p28 -->
I am blessed to have several very deep relationships in my life. One of those relationships is the one between my wife and me. Because of our relationship, due to the level of trust (faith) we have in each other, my wife can tell me things she will do before I have yet seen them come to pass, and I have confidence to believe because of her proven character.

<!-- p29 -->
This example is how our deep relationship with God can be experienced—with confidence and trust! We may not yet fully experience Him (indeed, we will never completely experience Him in this life), but as we mature in our relationship, we can trust Him beyond the bounds of what I personally know and what I have previously seen happen.

<!-- p30 -->
Jesus increases our faith through experiencing Him. An important way we can and need to experience Him is His Word.

<!-- p36 -->
When we replace the word *faith* and substitute *persuasion* it reads like this:

<!-- p37 -->
*‘Then persuasion comes from hearing, and hearing comes by the Word of God’*. This truth shines quite naturally. A great way for a person to have persuasion is to be persuaded (to be convinced), and we become convinced when someone makes an ‘argument’—or provides a reasoned, well-articulated explanation.

<!-- p38 -->
There are two Greek words for ‘Word’ in the Bible—**logos** and **rhema**. Understanding the difference helps us see how faith grows.

<!-- p39 -->
**Logos**: generally, it refers to the **idea, concept, reason, or broad revelation of the Word of God**. It encompasses universal or foundational truth or message and is often used to describe the eternal wisdom of God and Christ Himself as the living Word.

<!-- p40 -->
**Rhema (G4487)**: it emphasizes a **specific word that is spoken or utterance**. Typically, it refers to a specific word or message given at a set time, especially a word spoken or revealed that directly pertains to a person’s situation.

<!-- p41 -->
*Logos* is like the message of an entire book, whereas *rhema* is like a specific section or sentence that speaks directly to a person at a given time. While *logos* is God’s general truth, *rhema* is when a part of that truth is spoken to a person in a personal and applicable way. Together, they show the completeness of God’s communication: *logos* as the broad truths foundational to the character and will of God, and *rhema* as the specific, applied word that makes that truth come alive in a person’s life and situation.

<!-- p42 -->
### An Example of a Rhema Word in Action

<!-- p43 -->
God has promised revelation and wisdom to anyone who asks. When He speaks to us, that speech is a *rhema* word. Rhema words come from God and serve to confirm us, guide us, and direct us. They do not replace the *logos* word, nor do they carry the authority of Scripture. *Rhema* words should be judged by the same standard as prophecy or interpretation of tongues (see the chapter on the Holy Spirit for a more detailed study on this topic).

<!-- p44 -->
Many times throughout my career, I have prayed for divine guidance. A few years ago, I was considering starting to look for a new opportunity, and in my prayer time I heard God say, ‘*Just wait for Me; I have an opportunity for you.*’ I had begun to look for a new job because I wasn’t satisfied with my current role and I felt discouraged. God’s directive word, ‘*wait for Me*’, helped me find peace and confidence during another turbulent season.

<!-- p45 -->
Three or four months passed; I continued to work hard and faithfully, keeping my attitude positive. God would remind me, *‘wait for Me’*, and then His promise was fulfilled. I was given an opportunity for a promotion, and I received a generous raise.

<!-- p46 -->
I could have continued the job search; I could have left. I could have started somewhere new. Without a doubt someone else would have received that promotion! But because I sought the Lord’s counsel and was convinced by **His rhema word**, I obeyed, and I received the benefit. God’s words are **living and personal**, and He has promised to give us abundant, generous wisdom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:5</span>).

<!-- p47 -->
Dozens, if not hundreds, of times I have prayed and asked God what I should do, and He has given me a rhema word. Not every one had an astonishing outcome, but all of them produced a sense of peace and confidence that I was obeying God and that He would work everything out. They were the words I needed at that time! Often, when God brings a rhema word, it comes from the logos. He brings a portion of Scripture back to my remembrance, “*Do not fear, for I am with you!*” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="41" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 41:10</span>), “*By His stripes we are healed*” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="53" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 53:5</span>), “*Do not grow weary in doing good!*” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:9</span>), and many more examples continue!

<!-- p48 -->
Receiving a rhema word not only strengthens our faith, it also deepens our relationship with God, as we fully trust Him with daily decisions. We will continue our learning about how to hear from God in the chapter on the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p49 -->
Our faith is also strengthened by what we see God do; however, sometimes what we observe in the natural world challenges what we hear from God. Reconciling the misalignment between what we see and what we hear is an important ministry of the Holy Spirit, who promises to guide us into all truth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span>).

### Faith Greater Than What We See

<!-- p51 -->
After His resurrection, Jesus revealed Himself many times to His disciples. Thomas (one of the twelve) was not with the other disciples, and thus he missed the appearance of the risen Savior. Thomas believed in Jesus, and he trusted his fellow disciples, yet he did not believe that Jesus had physically risen; he insisted he could not actively believe until he personally experienced it.

<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="24-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

When Jesus revealed Himself to His disciples and settled them (I imagine it would be quite startling when someone appears in your midst after coming through locked doors), He spoke to Thomas. He did not rebuke or accuse Thomas, nor did He charge him with being part of the wicked and perverse generation that seeks a sign (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 16:4</span>), but He revealed Himself to Thomas.

<!-- p60 -->
Jesus went on to say, ‘*blessed are those who have not seen and have believed*.’ This is not Jesus encouraging a ‘blind or ignorant faith’ or rejecting the use of visible evidence. Rather, He is saying that we are more blessed when we can believe without needing visible proof. Why is that? If our faith, our confidence, were confined only to what has been visibly shown to us, we would never have confidence in what is to come in the future. We would trust what has been done greatly, but we would not walk in the peace of God regarding our tomorrows and our future.

<!-- p61 -->
If we use a relationship example, how difficult would my relationship with my wife be if my posture were consistently, ‘<u>I will believe when I see!</u>’? Instead, a trust-based relationship says, ‘I know you are someone who can be trusted.’

3.  The action of our faith is shown by obedience.

<!-- p91 -->

<!-- p92 -->
In a workplace, when an officer or supervisor gives instructions, they are not “just talking,” but giving directions they want their employees to follow.

<!-- p93 -->
When we truly listen to God, it should lead us to action—to trust Him and obey. As Scripture says:

<!-- p94 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

The counselor will teach the principles of financial freedom and help them build a budget. This counseling will create (confidence / faith) about what they need to do to get out of debt. But confidence is only the first step; the person must obey the principles they learned by practicing them consistently to obtain the good result and the blessing of becoming debt-free.

As we build our faith in God, obedience will naturally arise in us. This obedience is to our benefit, and it produces a good harvest. Returning to my personal example in this chapter, I would not have had the peace at that time, nor the blessing of the promotion, if I had not obeyed the word of God to me to *wait on Him*!

### Practical Steps to Grow Your Faith

<!-- p153 -->
Walking with God is relational, but there are skills to be learned if you want healthy relationships. In relationships in the world, the skills that produce strong and secure connections are built on:

<!-- p154 -->
- Open and free dialogue and communication

<!-- p155 -->
- Resolving conflicts in a healthy way

<!-- p156 -->
- Commitment

<!-- p157 -->
- Consistency

<!-- p158 -->
You must show up consistently, express yourself (communication) (our thoughts and feelings), receive the other person (listen), and follow through, if you want a strong, vibrant relationship! All these skills apply to our relationship with God.

<!-- p159 -->
Beyond the basic relationship skills, there are particular practices and disciplines that will strengthen your relationship with God:

<!-- p160 -->
- **Build and Maintain a Prayer Life, Listen and Communicate with God.** Consistent, heartfelt prayer is essential for a close relationship with God. Through prayer, you can share your concerns, joys, and gratitude, seeking His guidance and strength. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 4:6-7</span>*) Being attentive to the voice of the Holy Spirit helps you discern God’s will, gain wisdom when making decisions, and grow in spiritual maturity. Take time for quiet, still reflection to hear His direction. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:14</span>*)

<!-- p161 -->
- **Make Time for Worship.** Worship is a powerful way to draw near to God, expressing love and reverence. Through praise, song, or quiet reflection, worship deepens your awareness of His presence. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="100" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalms 100:2-4</span>*)

<!-- p162 -->
- **Read, Study, and Meditate on Holy Scripture.** God’s Word reveals His character, His promises, and His guidance for life. Devoting time to studying Scripture helps you grow in faith, understanding, and spiritual resilience. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>*)

<!-- p163 -->
- **Obey His Commands.** Obeying God’s instructions shows your love, respect, and trust in Him. Walking in obedience aligns your life with His purpose and brings blessings. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:15</span>*)

<!-- p164 -->
- **Actively Participate in a Local Church and Serve.** Belonging to a church community provides spiritual support, encouragement, and opportunities to serve others. Participation helps build a sense of belonging and strengthens your commitment to God’s mission. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:24-25</span>*)

<!-- p165 -->
- **Build Relationships with Other Believers.** Fellowship with other Christians brings accountability, encouragement, and shared wisdom. Those relationships nurture your faith journey and help you grow through mutual support. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="27" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 27:17</span>*)

<!-- p166 -->
‘Our faith in God’ is not a one-time event of salvation but a practice and an ongoing relationship that we are meant to cultivate throughout our lives. The benefits of a trusting, fruitful relationship are many, but we must do the good work of nurturing that trust and growth in order to see it flourish.
