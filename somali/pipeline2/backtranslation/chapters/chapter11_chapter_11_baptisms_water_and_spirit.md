# Chapter 11 - Baptisms - Water and Spirit

<!-- p1 -->
Baptism in the modern era, separated from Jewish roots, is often thought of only as water baptism and the baptism of the Holy Spirit (Pentecost).

<!-- p2 -->
The book of Hebrews was written to a Jewish audience. The vast majority of modern-day Christians have no Jewish roots or heritage. Lacking Jewish roots and culture, many of us do not have the cultural understanding that first-century Jewish Christians (Jews who confess Jesus) would have shared. Since we did not grow up in Jewish culture, we typically know little about the feasts, fasts, prayers, washings, regulations, and customs that Jewish believers were familiar with.

<!-- p3 -->
The Greek word for baptisms, or washings, is: **Baptismos** (bap-tis-mos’ - **G909**) - The act of immersing or washing. It refers to ceremonial washings or ritual purification. It can denote immersion, purification rites, or washings practiced in Jewish and Christian contexts.

<!-- p4 -->
Baptisms are one of the foundational matters mentioned in Hebrews:

<!-- p5 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p6 -->
> **<u>Hebrews 6:1-2</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Therefore, leaving the discussion of the elementary principles of Christ, let us go on to perfection, not laying again the foundation of repentance from dead works and of faith toward God,

## Old Testament Washings - Historical Meaning

<!-- p9 -->
In the Old Testament, ceremonial washings played a central role in maintaining ritual purity and marking sanctification before God. These washings were commanded in the Law of Moses and were applied both to the priests who served in the Tent of Meeting and to the people of Israel. For example, the priests were required to wash their hands and feet at the bronze basin before entering the Tent or approaching the altar, signifying their readiness to serve in the presence of a holy God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="30" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 30:18-21</span>). Likewise, purification rites for those who had become unclean, such as touching a corpse or skin diseases, involved washing with water as a sign of the restoration of purity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LEV" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Leviticus 14:8</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="NUM" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Numbers 19:17-19</span>). These actions were outward expressions reflecting an inward desire for holiness, underscoring the importance of being set apart for God.

<!-- p10 -->
Old Testament ceremonial washings required continual repetition to maintain ritual purity. Unlike permanent solutions, these rites provided only temporary, external cleansing, leaving the deeper issue of sin unresolved. The author of Hebrews highlights this limitation, explaining that these things were “a symbol for the present time,” unable to cleanse the worshiper’s conscience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:9-10</span>). These washings were a continual reminder of human imperfection and the need for something greater—a final cleansing that could purify not just the body but also the heart and soul.

<!-- p11 -->
The ceremonial washings of the Old Testament found their completion in Christ, who provides full and everlasting cleansing through His sacrifice. Unlike the temporary rites of the law, the blood of Christ fully purifies, cleansing not only the body but also the conscience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:13-14</span>). Through His death and resurrection, Jesus fulfilled what the Old Testament rites could only foreshadow—true reconciliation with God. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:22</span> urges believers to “draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, having our hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience and our bodies washed with pure water.” This language ties Christ’s work to the imagery of Old Testament washings, highlighting its far superior effectiveness. His sacrifice does not merely cover sin; it removes it entirely (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 1:7</span>). Thus, the ceremonial washings served as a shadow, pointing to the deeper cleansing that only Christ can provide, fulfilling humanity’s need for holiness through divine intervention.

<!-- p12 -->
To reinforce this point, Christians are under no obligation to perform any ceremonial washings. Washings did not grant salvation under the Old Covenant, nor do they under the New. Ceremonial washing does not increase our standing before God, nor earn us His favor. Paul strongly rebukes the Judaizers in Galatia (read Galatians) who tried to add requirements from Jewish practices deemed necessary for salvation (e.g., circumcision). Salvation is by faith in Jesus alone!

<!-- p13 -->
## The Baptism of John

<!-- p14 -->
The New Testament writings mention several baptisms. We have already discussed the baptisms and washings of Jewish practice.

<!-- p15 -->
The ministry of John the Baptist centered on preparing the way for the coming of Jesus Christ. His baptism, described as a baptism of repentance for the forgiveness of sins (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 1:4</span>), called people to confess their sins, turn their hearts to God, and ready themselves for the arrival of the Messiah. It served as an outward sign reflecting inward repentance, marking a desire for spiritual cleansing and readiness for the kingdom of God. However, John’s baptism was transitional and preparatory—it pointed toward the greater work that Christ would accomplish. While John’s baptism emphasized repentance, it did not provide complete forgiveness or spiritual rebirth, since it did not include the indwelling and empowerment of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:11-12</span>). Instead, John clearly declared that One greater than he was coming, who would baptize not only with water but with the Holy Spirit and fire (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 3:16</span>).

<!-- p16 -->
John’s baptism was fulfilled and superseded through the death, burial, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. Christian baptism, instituted by Jesus and practiced by the apostles, signifies a deeper reality—union with Christ, death to sin, and resurrection to new life (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 6:3-4</span>). Unlike John’s baptism, Christian baptism is directly connected to faith in the atoning sacrifice of Jesus and the indwelling of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:38</span>). The coming of the Holy Spirit at Pentecost marked the New Covenant and the beginning of Spirit-empowered life, which John’s baptism could not impart. For this reason, John’s baptism is no longer practiced, for it was replaced by the baptism commanded by Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19</span>), which encompasses repentance, faith, and the sealing of believers by the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>).

<!-- p17 -->
## The Baptism of Christ

<!-- p18 -->
The baptism of Christ set a powerful example for His followers and established the foundation for the Christian practice of water baptism. Though Jesus was without sin, He humbled Himself to be baptized by John in order to “fulfill all righteousness” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:15</span>). In doing so, He identified with humanity’s need for repentance and pointed to His death, burial, and resurrection. At His baptism, the Holy Spirit descended like a dove, and the Father declared, “This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:16-17</span>). This moment marked the beginning of Jesus’ public ministry and revealed the unity of the Father, the Son, and the Spirit, foreshadowing the spiritual transformation believers would experience through faith in Christ.

<!-- p19 -->
Jesus then commanded His followers to baptize, sending them to “go and make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19</span>). Water baptism, as instituted by Christ, became an **act of obedience** and a **public confession of faith** for believers. It represents identifying with the death, burial, and resurrection of Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 6:3-4</span>). Going under the water symbolizes dying to sin, and coming up portrays rising to new life in Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:12</span>). However, it is essential to clarify that this outward act is not the source of salvation; it is a **visible sign** of the **inward work of grace** already accomplished by the blood of Jesus.

<!-- p20 -->
Water baptism is a **sign** that demonstrates the cleansing power of the blood of Christ and the renewal of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:26</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>). It signifies repentance, faith, and a commitment to follow Jesus. It is an outward testimony of the inward change—a way for believers to openly declare their allegiance to Christ and participation in the body of Christ, the Church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:41</span>). Although commanded by Jesus and practiced by the early Church, it does not **save** anyone. Salvation is by grace alone through faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>), and true cleansing occurs spiritually through the blood of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 1:5</span>). Water baptism is a response to that salvation, an act of obedience, not a condition for it.

<!-- p21 -->
In the New Covenant, water baptism is consistently connected to **repentance and faith** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:38</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 16:16</span>), both of which require a personal decision to follow Christ—something infants are unable to do. Baptism symbolizes the believer’s deliberate identification with Christ’s death, burial, and resurrection (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 6:3-4</span>), as well as their commitment to live in obedience to Him. Some groups (e.g., Catholics, Lutherans) practice infant baptism, viewing it as a covenant sign similar to circumcision; however, since infants cannot express personal faith or repentance, the practice of **infant baptism** does not align with the biblical pattern of **believer’s baptism**. Instead, many churches hold **dedication ceremonies** for children, following the example of Hannah dedicating Samuel to the Lord (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1SA" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Samuel 1:27-28</span>) and Mary and Joseph presenting Jesus in the temple (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 2:22</span>). Dedication is not a replacement for baptism but a commitment by parents to raise their child in the faith, teaching God’s Word and modeling a God-fearing life until the child is old enough to make his or her own decision to follow Christ and be baptized.

<!-- p22 -->
Unlike the **ceremonial washings** of the Old Covenant, which required repeated rituals for purification, Christian baptism is a one-time event that reflects a completed work of grace. The Old Covenant washings symbolized external cleansing and foreshadowed the deeper spiritual cleansing that comes from Christ. By contrast, New Covenant baptism signifies the inward renewal and second birth accomplished by the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>). It points to the believer’s participation in the new covenant, where the cleansing of sin is lasting and founded on Christ’s finished work.

<!-- p23 -->
Water baptism is closely connected to the **promise of the Holy Spirit**. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:38</span>, Peter called the people to repent, be baptized, and receive the gift of the Holy Spirit. While water baptism publicly expresses the believer’s repentance and faith, the true baptism happens within when the Holy Spirit washes, regenerates, and seals the believer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:13</span>). This spiritual washing makes us members of the body of Christ and empowers us to live a holy life. The **role of the Holy Spirit** in baptism reminds us that salvation is not obtained through rituals but through God’s transforming power.

<!-- p24 -->
It is important to distinguish **the symbolic act of water baptism** from the spiritual reality of salvation through the blood of Christ. The Bible clearly teaches that salvation is based on faith in Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 10:9-10</span>) and not on works, including baptism (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>). Nevertheless, water baptism is an **act of obedience** that publicly identifies the believer with Christ and the Church. It shows an outward commitment to live as Jesus’ disciple, but it neither adds to nor completes the work of salvation.

<!-- p25 -->
Christ’s water baptism set an example for believers to follow, demonstrating humility, obedience, and readiness for service. For Christians today, water baptism serves as a public proclamation of faith, symbolizing death to sin and resurrection to new life. Although it is Jesus’ command and an important step of obedience, it is not a requirement for salvation. True cleansing comes through faith in the blood of Jesus, and the power to live a Christian life comes from the work of the Holy Spirit in the believer. Baptism unites us with Christ and His Church, reminding us of the inner transformation and renewal God has already accomplished.

<!-- p26 -->
Having established that water baptism is an outward sign of faith, we now turn to the baptism of the Holy Spirit, which empowers believers for service and ministry.

<!-- p27 -->
## Baptism of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p28 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit is a profound, transformative experience that God has promised to empower believers for service, ministry, and holy living. Every believer receives the Holy Spirit at new birth—that is how we become spiritually alive. But afterward, we should also receive the baptism of the Holy Spirit, which empowers us with boldness, spiritual gifts, and effective ministry.

<!-- p29 -->
Water baptism is a public proclamation of faith that symbolizes the cleansing of sin through the blood of Christ, while Spirit baptism represents God’s empowering presence in the believer’s life. Together, the two forms of baptism constitute complementary aspects of the Christian walk- one an outward sign of obedience and the other the inward source of power and renewal. The baptism of the Holy Spirit is the Spirit being UPON us rather than within us. At salvation all believers receive the Spirit dwelling within, but when the Spirit of God comes upon them, they receive power and boldness for ministry.

<!-- p30 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit was foretold by John the Baptist, who said,

<!-- p31 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p32 -->
> **<u>Matthew 3:11</u>** (NKJV)
>
> I indeed baptize you with water unto repentance, but He who comes after me is mightier than I, whose sandals I am not worthy to carry. He will baptize you with the Holy Spirit and fire.
>

<!-- p33 -->
</span>

<!-- p34 -->
This promise pointed to a greater work Jesus would do after His resurrection- the sending of the Holy Spirit to dwell within and empower His followers. Jesus affirmed this when He commanded His disciples to wait for the gift of the Spirit, explaining that they would receive power to be His witnesses (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>). Believers received the fulfillment of this promise on the Day of Pentecost, when they were filled with the Holy Spirit, evidenced by speaking in tongues, prophesying, and bold proclamation of the gospel (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:1-4</span>).

<!-- p35 -->
This outpouring marked the beginning of the **Church Age** and demonstrated that the Spirit’s power is now available to all believers. This promise was not limited to the apostles but extends to all who believe, as Peter declared, *“The promise is for you and your children and for all who are far off—for all whom the Lord our God will call”* (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="39" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:39</span>).

<!-- p36 -->
### The Spirit Within vs the Spirit Upon

<!-- p37 -->
When a person places his or her faith in Jesus, the Holy Spirit gives new life within. That is what is meant by being born again (**regeneration**). At that moment of faith, the Holy Spirit dwells **WITHIN** the believer, making him or her spiritually alive and adopting him or her into the family of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:5-6</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>). This is the new birth or being born again, as Jesus explained in John 3.

<!-- p38 -->
Scriptural basis:

<!-- p39 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>* - “He saved us by the washing of regeneration and renewing of the Holy Spirit.”

<!-- p40 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>* - The Holy Spirit seals believers as the **guarantee** of their inheritance.

<!-- p41 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:9</span>* - “He who does not have the Spirit of Christ does not belong to Him.”

<!-- p42 -->
This work of new birth is common to all believers at salvation, and it establishes the continual indwelling of the Spirit. It is not dependent on any external act, such as water baptism, and it guarantees salvation by grace through faith.

<!-- p43 -->
On the other hand, the baptism of the Holy Spirit is a distinct subsequent experience that equips believers with power for service and witness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>). Whereas the new birth speaks of new life and identity, the baptism in the Spirit focuses on empowerment **UPON** and the release of spiritual gifts. This baptism is often accompanied by signs and manifestations of the gifts of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p44 -->
Scriptural foundation:

<!-- p45 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p46 -->
> **<u>Acts 1:8</u>** (NKJV)
>
> But you shall receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you; and you shall be witnesses to Me in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the end of the earth."
>

<!-- p47 -->
</span>

<!-- p48 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:15-17</span>* - The Samaritans believed and were baptized, but they later received the Holy Spirit through prayer and the laying on of hands.

<!-- p49 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 19:6</span>* - The disciples in Ephesus, who were already believers, received the Holy Spirit when Paul laid hands on them.

<!-- p50 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit does not replace the new birth but builds upon it, equipping the believer for spiritual ministry and boldness in service. To be baptized in the Holy Spirit is a free gift given to those who are saved, not a condition for salvation. It is not a requirement that a person manifest <u>a particular gift</u> of the Holy Spirit to prove his ‘baptism’ in the Holy Spirit, but we should expect the baptism of the Holy Spirit to appear today as it did in the book of Acts, accompanied by signs and boldness! Some are mistaken in thinking that if a person does not speak in tongues, he is not saved, or does not have the baptism of the Holy Spirit. Not everyone will receive every gift or manifestation of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29-31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:29-31</span>).

<!-- p51 -->
## How to Receive the Baptism of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p52 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit is described in Scripture as a gift promised to believers and received by faith. It is distinct from salvation, and is primarily intended for empowerment and witness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>). The baptism of the Holy Spirit is an empowering gift for believers, promised by Jesus and fulfilled at Pentecost. It is received through repentance, faith, and asking God. Although the laying on of hands is often a method used, ultimately God is the one who baptizes in the Spirit.

<!-- p53 -->
Scripture presents speaking in tongues and prophecy as a common sign, but the purpose of this baptism is much greater—providing boldness, power, and spiritual gifts for service. Every believer is encouraged to seek this experience as part of his spiritual journey, trusting that God will equip him for His work.

<!-- p54 -->
The Bible presents several core principles regarding how this baptism is received:

<!-- p55 -->
### **1. The Baptism of the Holy Spirit is a Free Gift from God**

<!-- p56 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit is not something believers can earn—it is a free gift that God gives. God wants to give good gifts to His children, and He is very eager to give the Holy Spirit to those who ask in faith and desire empowerment.

<!-- p57 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p58 -->
> **<u>Luke 11:13</u>** (NKJV)
>
> If you then, being evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to those who ask Him!"
>

<!-- p59 -->
</span>

<!-- p60 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="38-39" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p61 -->
> **<u>Acts 2:38-39</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Then Peter said to them, "Repent, and let every one of you be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins; and you shall receive the gift of the Holy Spirit.

> For the promise is to you and to your children, and to all who are afar off, as many as the Lord our God will call."

<!-- p63 -->
###  **2. Ask, Seek, and Receive by Faith**

<!-- p64 -->
Scripture emphasizes that the baptism of the Holy Spirit is received through asking in prayer and expectant faith. Faith, together with a readiness to ask and to receive, is the key to experiencing the baptism of the Spirit. If you are not sure that God wants to give you the baptism of the Holy Spirit, ask Him about it—He is a good Father! When He stirs you, you may feel in your body a tangible presence of God that you have not experienced before. If you do not feel that, that's okay! God is great, and He is not confined to a single formula. If you feel God drawing your heart, submit to Him!

<!-- p65 -->
- **<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:15-17</span>** - Peter and John prayed and **laid hands** on the believers, and then they **received the Holy Spirit**.

<!-- p66 -->
- **<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="44-46" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 10:44-46</span>** - Cornelius and his household **received the Holy Spirit** while Peter was preaching-without hands being laid on them-showing that ultimately **God is the One who baptizes**.

<!-- p78 -->
## The Gifts of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p79 -->
When the Holy Spirit was poured out, and people were baptized in the Holy Spirit, manifestations of the Holy Spirit often accompanied it.

<!-- p80 -->
There are several lists of gifts in the New Testament:

<!-- p81 -->
- The motivational gifts that God gives: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:6-8</span>

<!-- p82 -->
- Discussed in the chapter on the Church.

<!-- p83 -->
- The five-fold ministry gifts that Jesus gave: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-13</span>

<!-- p84 -->
- Discussed in the chapter on the church.

<!-- p85 -->
- The manifestation gifts given by the Holy Spirit: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span>

<!-- p86 -->
- Discussed here.

<!-- p87 -->
Each of these collections of gifts is given for different purposes, and they function in different ways, but together they point to the **diversity** and **unity** of the gifts within the **Body of Christ**. Each gift has a unique role in accomplishing God's purposes.

<!-- p88 -->
### Purposes &amp; Operations of the Gifts

<!-- p89 -->
The primary purpose of the gifts of the Holy Spirit is to build up the Body of Christ and to bring glory to God:

<!-- p90 -->
- **Edification** - Strengthening and encouraging believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
- **Unity** - Promoting harmony and cooperation within the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:12-13</span>).

<!-- p92 -->
- **Evangelism** - Demonstrating God's power as a witness to unbelievers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="43" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:43</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:6-7</span>).

<!-- p93 -->
- **Service** - Responding to the needs of others through acts of mercy and generosity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:6-8</span>).

<!-- p94 -->
These gifts are not for personal glory; they are for the common good (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:7</span>). They function best when practiced in love and humility, reflecting the character of Christ (1 Corinthians 13).

<!-- p95 -->
Paul emphasized that the spiritual gifts should be exercised decently and in order (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:40</span>). This ensures that the use of the gifts promotes unity, peace, and understanding, rather than creating confusion or division.

<!-- p96 -->
- Prophecies should be weighed (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:29</span>).

<!-- p97 -->
- When a message in tongues is delivered to the body, pray that it may be interpreted (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:27-28</span>).

<!-- p98 -->
- The gifts must always result in the **edification** of the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:12</span>).

<!-- p99 -->
This order does not quench the Spirit; it ensures that the gifts operate in ways that honor God and benefit others.

<!-- p100 -->
The operation of the spiritual gifts must always be grounded in love. Paul devoted an entire chapter (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 13</span>) to explain that without love, even the greatest spiritual gifts are meaningless. Love provides the motivation and framework for using the gifts so that they build people up rather than tear them down.

<!-- p101 -->
Furthermore, the gifts operate **as the Spirit wills** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:11</span>). They cannot be produced or managed by human effort—they are independently given and empowered by the Holy Spirit, often at times of need or service. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 13:8</span> says that eventually prophecy and tongues will cease. Theologically, those who believe that the gifts of the Holy Spirit have ceased are called ‘Cessationists’. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 13:10</span> says, “when the perfect [Jesus] comes, that which is in part will be done away.” Since Jesus has not yet returned for His church, the gifts are still operating on the earth.

<!-- p102 -->
It is very important to recognize that **the gifts of the Spirit** are not to be confused with **the fruit of the Spirit** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-23</span>). While the gifts display power, the fruit display character—love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control.

<!-- p103 -->
- **Gifts without Fruit**: A believer may operate in gifts yet lack maturity or character, which can lead to pride, misuse, or harm.

<!-- p104 -->
- **Fruit without Gifts**: A believer may have God-pleasing character yet lack the empowerment to serve effectively.

<!-- p105 -->
Both are needed. The gifts equip believers for service. The fruit are a sign of their spiritual health (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 7:16</span>) and give a ‘sweet taste’ to those who partake.

<!-- p106 -->
### Explanation of the 9 Manifestation Gifts of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p107 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span> lists the 9 manifestation gifts of the Holy Spirit:

<!-- p108 -->
1.  **Word of Wisdom** - A supernatural insight into God’s purposes or the direction for a situation. It applies divine wisdom to problems or difficult decisions (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:10</span>).

<!-- p109 -->
2.  **Word of Knowledge** - A supernatural revelation that provides information about a person, event, or circumstances that could not be known naturally (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 4:17-18</span>).

<!-- p110 -->
3.  **Faith** - A supernatural ability to trust God for miracles even in impossible situations. This gift inspires boldness and confidence (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 3:6-7</span>).

<!-- p111 -->
4.  **Gifts of Healings** - A supernatural ability to heal physical, emotional, or spiritual illnesses and restore wholeness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 3:6-8</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 9:17-18</span>).

<!-- p112 -->
5.  **Working of Miracles** - The ability to perform supernatural acts that defy natural laws, such as multiplying food or raising the dead (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40-41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 9:40-41</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 2:9-11</span>).

<!-- p113 -->
6.  **Prophecy** - Declaring God’s truth or will as prompted by the Holy Spirit. It should be for encouragement, edification, and comfort (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>).

<!-- p114 -->
7.  **Discerning (Discernment) of Spirits** - The ability to recognize whether a spiritual influence is from God, the enemy, or human origin (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 16:16-18</span>).

<!-- p115 -->
8.  **Different Kinds of Tongues** - Speaking in languages unknown to the speaker, often as worship, prayer, or a sign intended for unbelievers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:4</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:22</span>).

<!-- p116 -->
9.  **Interpretation of Tongues** - A supernatural ability to interpret the message spoken in tongues so that the church may be edified (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:27</span>).

<!-- p117 -->
Paul clearly showed that all the gifts are valuable and should work together for the common good of the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:7</span>). However, he also encourages believers to desire the greater gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:31</span>). The greater gifts are often understood to be those that directly edify the church, such as prophecy (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:1-5</span>).

<!-- p118 -->
- Prophecy is especially emphasized because it strengthens, encourages, and comforts others (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>). Paul instructs believers to earnestly desire this gift since it promotes clarity and understanding within the church.

<!-- p119 -->
- Gifts such as tongues are highly valued for personal edification and prayer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:4</span>), but Paul teaches that when a message in tongues is given to the Church one should pray for its interpretation to ensure that it edifies the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:27-28</span>).

<!-- p120 -->
Although the Holy Spirit gives the gifts, we still need to yield and submit to the Holy Spirit so that He may use us. Like muscles and talent, as we practice obeying the Holy Spirit so that He may use His gifts, we become more confident and dependable in their use.
