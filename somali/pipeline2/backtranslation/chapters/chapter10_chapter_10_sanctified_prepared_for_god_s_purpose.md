# Chapter 10 - Sanctified-Prepared for God's Purpose

<!-- p1 -->
In the previous two chapters we discussed several important topics. We explored the role and ministry of the Holy Spirit, as well as the role and ministry of the church.

<!-- p2 -->
We are part of the church, and although we know that we need training, we also need cleansing. To be truly prepared for God's purposes we must be sanctified.

<!-- p3 -->
The definition of **sanctification** is to cleanse something for a specific purpose. This is closely related to the word **setting apart** which means to place something for a specific purpose.

<!-- p4 -->
Consider a master sculptor preparing to work on a large block of marble. The master envisions what this raw, unshaped marble can be transformed into through his careful craftsmanship. He has a very specific design in mind, and he orders his assistants to set this piece of marble in a place reserved for that specific design (**setting apart**). As he further refines and works the marble, adding surface detail, depth, and form, he purifies it (**sanctifies it**) for that specific design. The act of dedicating the marble to his special project was instantaneous, whereas the process of shaping the marble to its final design requires great patience.

<!-- p5 -->
When we received salvation we were set apart, and God consecrated us. As our souls are cleansed, and in cooperation with the Spirit, we are transformed. This process of cleansing is the one that sanctifies us.

<!-- p6 -->
When we received Jesus as salvation, we were born again. We were justified, and our sins were forgiven. Our spirit was made alive, and we began to live in Christ. We were adopted into God's family, and we became the true temple of the Holy Spirit. Concerning such a great and astonishing change the Scripture says, “*there is joy in the presence of the angels of God over one sinner who repents.” (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 15:10</span>).

<!-- p7 -->
Receiving Jesus as salvation (**justification**) saves our spirit, cleanses us, and makes us presentable before God, able to receive His Holy Spirit. However, despite such a great salvation, **our souls** (our mind, will, and emotions) still **need work**!

<!-- p8 -->
Was the work of salvation incomplete? No! The work of salvation is effective, but it must be continued in the believer’s life as renewal brought by the Holy Spirit goes on. This process of renewal and transformation is the work of **sanctification.**

<!-- p9 -->
We know there is ongoing work after being saved, because our painful memories were not resolved, many beliefs remained the same, and many habits or patterns did not disappear. Moreover, salvation did not separate our mind from its carnal nature.

<!-- p10 -->
The word ‘carnal’ comes from a Latin root meaning ‘body’ or ‘flesh.’ The carnal nature is in each one of us from birth; like animal instinct, it exists to help us survive. The way this carnal nature works is by being self-directed, self-oriented, and self-centered. Children are natural examples of the fleshly nature. Although they are innocent, their disposition is often directed toward their wants, desires, and needs; and ‘watch out!’ if you don’t meet every one they expect.

<!-- p11 -->
Part of maturity is that we outgrow our childish ways, but when we are pressured we are very prone to revert to self-centered patterns of thought and living. As James said:

<!-- p12 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p13 -->
> **<u>James 4:1</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Where do wars and fights come from among you? Do they not come from your desires for pleasure that wage war in your members?
>

<!-- p14 -->
</span>

<!-- p15 -->
Paul explained this carnal nature in detail in his letter to the Romans (chapters 6 - 8). He said this carnal nature continues to drive him to do what he hates, and it causes him to do evil. He describes this inner struggle as a war between his human nature and his God-created spirit.

<!-- p16 -->
The clash between the flesh and the spirit is in each one of us, and it is our job to deal with it. If we do not face our carnal nature, our souls will continue to be confined to corrupt spiritual patterns, even though our spirit has been redeemed.

<!-- p17 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p18 -->
> **<u>Romans 8:6-8</u>** (NKJV)
>
> For to be carnally minded is death, but to be spiritually minded is life and peace.
>
> Because the carnal mind is enmity against God; for it is not subject to the law of God, nor indeed can be.
>
> So then, those who are in the flesh cannot please God.
>

<!-- p19 -->
</span>

<!-- p20 -->
These words of Paul are both a warning and a promise to us. Although we have been born again, we still have a carnal mind that needs to be brought under the knowledge of Christ. When we do so, we walk in the life and peace of God.

<!-- p21 -->
Take heart, there is hope for us! We must walk in the Spirit and let the Spirit of God lead us. When we do so, we will not follow the flesh.

<!-- p22 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="1,13,14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p23 -->
> **<u>Romans 8:1,13,14</u>** (NKJV)
>
> There is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus, who do not walk according to the flesh, but according to the Spirit.
>
> For if you live according to the flesh you will die; but if by the Spirit you put to death the deeds of the body, you will live.
>
> For as many as are led by the Spirit of God, these are the sons of God.
>

<!-- p24 -->
</span>

<!-- p25 -->
How do we learn to walk in the Spirit? Is it only a conscious choice? We learn to walk in the Spirit the way we learn natural walking. We are encouraged to move and to grow, so we struggle, strive, crawl, toddle, until we can stand on our feet. Our natural parents cannot walk for us, but they can support us, encourage us, help us, and empower our walk.

<!-- p26 -->
Likewise, God cannot walk our faith on our behalf. He will teach us, but we are the ones who must put in the effort. As Paul said to the Philippians:

<!-- p27 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="12-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p28 -->
> **<u>Philippians 2:12-13</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Therefore, my beloved, as you have always obeyed, not only in my presence but now much more in my absence, work out your own salvation with fear and trembling;
>
> for it is God who works in you both to will and to do for His good pleasure.
>

<!-- p29 -->
</span>

<!-- p30 -->
We must work out our own salvation. That does not mean that we climb onto a wooden cross to be physically nailed, but that we crucify our fleshly nature (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:24</span>). God has promised that He will work in us and help us, but we must decide to walk in this, and we must persevere.

<!-- p31 -->
It is true to say that ‘we’ deal with our fleshly nature, but it is also true to say that ‘God’ deals with it. The work of the cross (justification) was hard for Jesus—He gave His life for ours—but for us it is easy; it is a free gift. The work of the Spirit (sanctification) is easy for God—He walks in perfect truth and is ready and willing to dwell with us—but for us it is hard, because we must crucify our former nature, and our nature has no interest in being crucified.

<!-- p32 -->
This process is a partnership. God helps, assists, guides, directs, and corrects. We listen, obey, follow, and respond. The result is growth and transformation. This is the pruning that Jesus said God will do in us. Jesus is the vine, His Father is the vinedresser, and we are the branches:

<!-- p33 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p34 -->
> **<u>John 15:1-4</u>** (NKJV)
>
> "I am the true vine, and My Father is the vinedresser.
>
> Every branch in Me that does not bear fruit He takes away; and every branch that bears fruit He prunes, that it may bear more fruit.
>
> You are already clean because of the word which I have spoken to you.
>
> Abide in Me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself unless it abides in the vine, neither can you, unless you abide in Me.
>

<!-- p35 -->
</span>

<!-- p36 -->
Pruning is very healthy for a plant. Returning to our chapter on repentance, we are called to turn away from dead works (which are works that no longer bear fruit) and to turn to God. The result of turning away from the dead places in our lives, and turning to God, enables the great vinedresser of our lives to prune the branches, improve growth, and produce even more spiritual fruit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:2</span>).

<!-- p37 -->
Although pruning may seem painful, it nevertheless demonstrates the loving care of God as He makes us into vessels for His purpose (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 12:11</span>). Some may feel loss, even fear, as they go through the process of pruning. However, God is merciful; He will not break even a bruised reed (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="42" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 42:3</span>). Grapevines are pruned during their dormant season to reduce stress on the plant. The vinedresser who tends the growth cuts away the aged, woody growth that no longer bears fruit as before; this allows the vine to direct energy to the branches most likely to produce good fruit.

<!-- p38 -->
Just as the vinedresser prunes the branches to guide growth, God may allow seasons of correction and discipline to refine our faith and character, but His work is patient and merciful. God prunes those who abide in Him. Our focus should be more on abiding in Christ and less on the work of pruning.

<!-- p39 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 12:11</span> reminds us that pruning “*it produces the peaceful fruit of righteousness*” in those whom it trains.

<!-- p40 -->
How does righteousness train us? By transforming us through the renewal of our minds.

In the next section, I will explain each of the memory systems, their purposes and functions, practical steps to renew this part of your mind, as well as resources for ongoing training and education.

<!-- p61 -->
### **Memory Systems and How to Renew Them**

<!-- p62 -->
#### **1. Explicit Memory (Declarative Memory)**

<!-- p63 -->
**Purpose:** It stores facts, events, and experiences we can consciously recall. It is divided into two types-**semantic** and **episodic** memory.

<!-- p64 -->
**Semantic Memory - Facts and Knowledge.** Semantic memory is where we store facts, definitions, and general knowledge—things like historical dates, Bible verses, and practical information. It helps us predict outcomes, make decisions, and avoid danger (for example, recognizing poisonous plants). Although it gives us stability and order, it can also cause rigidity—an excessive dependence on rules instead of adapting to unexpected situations.

<!-- p65 -->
**How to Renew It:**

<!-- p66 -->
- **Study Scripture** to shape our worldview (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="119" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="105" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalms 119:105</span>).

<!-- p67 -->
- **Memorize truths** and beliefs to strengthen your faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 3:16</span>).

<!-- p68 -->
- **Read Christian books** such as *"Renewing the Mind" by Casey Treat* or *"Battlefield of the Mind" by Joyce Meyer* to develop God-pleasing thought patterns.

<!-- p69 -->
**Episodic Memory - Personal Experiences.** Episodic memory stores our personal experiences by linking them to specific times and places-such as the wedding day or a traumatic event. These memories provide meaning and context, and they help us learn from the past and build relationships. However, episodic memory is prone to emotional bias and distortion. Past wounds can create fearful associations, making it harder to move forward.

<!-- p70 -->
**How to Renew It:**

<!-- p71 -->
- **Reflect and confess** to bring hidden pain into God’s light (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 1:9</span>).

<!-- p72 -->
- **Reframe past experiences** by viewing them through the lens of God’s grace (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:28</span>).

<!-- p73 -->
- **Seek inner healing** through prayer and counseling (*“Your Pain is Showing” by Larry F. Low, "The Bondage Breaker" by Neil T. Anderson*, *"Emotionally Healthy Spirituality" by Peter Scazzero*).

<!-- p74 -->
#### **2. Implicit Memory (Non-Declarative Memory)**

<!-- p75 -->
**Purpose:** Unconscious memory used for habits, skills, and emotional associations. It operates automatically, helping us respond quickly to situations. It is divided into **procedural memory** and **emotional conditioning**.

<!-- p76 -->
**Procedural Memory - Skills and Habits.** Procedural memory allows us to perform tasks without thinking—such as driving, writing, or playing a musical instrument. It is efficient and saves our brain’s energy and time. However, bad habits and negative patterns can also become deeply ingrained and hard to break.

<!-- p77 -->
**How to Renew It:**

<!-- p78 -->
- **Practice spiritual disciplines** such as prayer and fasting to build God-pleasing habits (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 5:14</span>).

<!-- p79 -->
- **Train in godliness** by using repetition and obedience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 4:7</span>).

<!-- p80 -->
- **Read books like** *"Atomic Habits" by James Clear* (practical) and *"Spiritual Disciplines for the Christian Life" by Donald Whitney* (Bible-based) to build habits that honor God.

<!-- p81 -->
**Emotional Conditioning - Emotional Associations.** Emotional conditioning ties sensory cues (such as smells or sounds) to emotions and triggers reactions before we consciously process them. This system protects us but can also cause irrational fear or emotional reactions when past experiences have been negative.

<!-- p82 -->
**How to Renew:**

<!-- p83 -->
- **Pray for healing** of past wounds (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="41" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 41:10</span>).

<!-- p84 -->
- **Practice forgiveness** and let go of bitterness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-32" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:31-32</span>).

<!-- p85 -->
- **Replace fear with God's promises** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 1:7</span>).

<!-- p86 -->
- **Seek guidance from books like** *“Your Pain is Showing” by Larry F. Low, "The Bondage Breaker" by Neil T. Anderson*, “*Soul Care" by Rob Reimer* to overcome emotional triggers.

<!-- p87 -->
#### **3. Working Memory (Short-term Memory)**

<!-- p88 -->
**Purpose:** It provides temporary storage for problem-solving and decision-making. It helps us focus, briefly retain information, and carry out tasks—such as remembering instructions or making quick decisions. Although it is powerful, it is limited and susceptible to distraction, and can easily become overloaded, especially when under pressure.

<!-- p89 -->
**How to Renew:**

<!-- p90 -->
- **Focus on and meditate on** God's Word daily (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalms 1:2</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
- **Renew your thoughts** by thinking intentionally (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:2</span>).

<!-- p92 -->
- **Practice mindfulness** and simplify mental clutter (*"Habits of Grace" by David Mathis*).

<!-- p93 -->
- **Read** *"The Celebration of Discipline" by Richard Foster* to further sharpen your focus using spiritual disciplines.

<!-- p94 -->
## Conclusion

<!-- p95 -->
Mental renewal is not a quick fix—it is a gradual, lifelong process of change. Each of the memory systems plays a role in shaping how we think, feel, and act. By intentionally working with the Holy Spirit, applying Scripture, and developing God-honoring habits, we can train our minds to align with God's truth. Whether we are dealing with past wounds, learning new habits, or focusing on the moment, God has provided both **spiritual tools** and **practical wisdom** to help us walk in renewal and freedom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 10:5</span>).

<!-- p96 -->
Our world is full of trouble, suffering, and trials. By renewing the mind with discipline, our souls are transformed, and we can live a life of freedom for God, full of the fruit of His Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-25</span>).
