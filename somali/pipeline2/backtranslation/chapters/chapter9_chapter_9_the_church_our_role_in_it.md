<!-- p0 -->
# Chapter 9 - The Church &amp; Our Role Within It

<!-- p1 -->
The Church is how God reveals Himself on earth—by having His people do His work. We are called to be His body; we are His hands and feet to carry out His work and purpose. If our effort diminishes, the harvest will remain in the field and will not be gathered.

<!-- p2 -->
## What is the Church?

<!-- p3 -->
The Church, ***Ekklesia** (eh-klee-SEE-ah - **G1577**),* comes from two Greek words:

<!-- p4 -->
- **"Ek"**: "Come out."

<!-- p5 -->
- **"Kaleo"**: "To call."

<!-- p6 -->
Together, **Ekklesia** literally means "those who are called" or "a called assembly." In the ancient Greek city-state, it referred to a gathering of citizens called for a specific purpose, such as legislative or judicial meetings. It was a civic term denoting an assembly of people.

<!-- p7 -->
In the New Testament, it is most often used to refer to the community of believers in Jesus Christ. It can signify:

<!-- p8 -->
- **The Local Church**: A particular gathering of believers in a specific place (e.g., the church at Corinth, Philippi, etc.).

<!-- p9 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p10 -->
> **<u>1 Corinthians 1:2</u>** (NKJV)
>
> To the church of God which is at Corinth, to those who are sanctified in Christ Jesus, called to be saints, with all who in every place call on the name of Jesus Christ our Lord, both theirs and ours:
>

<!-- p11 -->
</span>

<!-- p12 -->
- **The Universal Church**: The corporate body of all believers at all times and in every place.

<!-- p13 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p14 -->
> **<u>Matthew 16:18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And I also say to you that you are Peter, and on this rock I will build My church, and the gates of Hades shall not prevail against it.
>

<!-- p15 -->
</span>

<!-- p16 -->
The Church is the people, not the building or the place where they meet. **The Called-Out Ones**: It reflects the idea that Christians are called out of the world and set apart for God’s purposes (e.g., <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:9</span>) for a specific task and purpose (the Great Commission). Technically, the church is two or more believers meeting together (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="18" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 18:20</span>). Typically, the church organizes for regular meetings, which may have a set place and time, but wherever two or more gather in the name of Jesus, there the church is!

<!-- p17 -->
The Church was founded by Jesus, and it continued to grow in the 1st century AD through the work of His followers. The church we are part of and meet with today—in all its local bodies throughout the world—is a direct result of the original work of Christ and His apostles. As believers, we should be devoted to a local church to receive fellowship, care, equipping for ministry, and to do good works!

<!-- p18 -->
To help us understand what the church is, Scripture gives us powerful images that show what it is meant to be.

<!-- p19 -->
## Understanding the Church

<!-- p20 -->
There are several basic metaphorical images used to illustrate the characteristics of the church:

<!-- p21 -->
1.  The Body

<!-- p22 -->
2.  The Bride

<!-- p23 -->
3.  The Temple

<!-- p24 -->
4.  The Family

<!-- p25 -->
5.  The Flock

<!-- p26 -->
6.  The Vine and Its Branches

<!-- p27 -->
Each metaphor portrays a particular aspect of the church, yet taken together, they reveal the church’s identity as a unified and yet diverse community under the leadership of Christ.

<!-- p28 -->
### The Body

<!-- p29 -->
The church is often described as the **Body of Christ**, which emphasizes both its unity and its diversity. With Christ as the head, the church functions as a living body in which each believer plays an essential role that may differ. Each member is uniquely gifted and contributes to the health and work of the body according to his or her function. This image highlights the interdependence of believers, namely that the body must be joined together to function, and that our work is cooperative rather than competitive. When each part works together, the body grows and thrives in unity and love (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:12-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:15-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 1:18</span>).

<!-- p30 -->
### The Bride

<!-- p31 -->
The church is described as the **Bride of Christ**, which reflects the close, loving relationship it has with Jesus. Like a bride who is honored and prepared for her husband, the church is sanctified and cleansed by the self-sacrificial love of Christ. This metaphor captures the devotion and faithfulness expected of the church, as well as the deep love Christ has for his people. This image also points to the final union between Christ and the church, celebrated at the marriage supper of the Lamb, where the church is presented in glory, without spot or blemish (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="25-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:25-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 19:7-9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="21" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 21:2</span>).

<!-- p32 -->
### The Temple

<!-- p33 -->
The church is described as the **Temple of God**, the dwelling place of His Spirit. Believers are depicted as living stones being built together upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets, with Christ himself as the cornerstone. This holy structure symbolizes God’s presence among his people and their role as a community of worship and prayer. The temple imagery underscores the importance of holiness and the shared purpose of the church in glorifying God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 3:16-17</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:19-22</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:4-5</span>).

<!-- p34 -->
### The Family

<!-- p35 -->
The church is described as the **Family of God**, which emphasizes the relational unity and care among its members. Through the Spirit of adoption, believers are made children of God and members of his household. This family imagery expresses the love, belonging, and shared inheritance that believers have in Christ. It also reflects the call to treat one another with compassion and humility as siblings in the family of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:19</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:15-17</span>). Furthermore, through the family imagery it shows how members of the church become mature, being taught and nourished by spiritual mothers and fathers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:1-2</span>), so that we move from infants drinking the pure milk of the Word to mature adults able to eat the solid meat of the Word (Hebrews 5).

<!-- p36 -->
### The Flock

<!-- p37 -->
The church is depicted as the **Flock**, the sheep under the care of Jesus, the Good Shepherd. This image conveys the church’s dependence on Christ for guidance, nourishment, and protection. It also underscores the responsibility of church leaders to shepherd the flock of God faithfully as under-shepherds, imitating Christ’s example of love and care. The flock imagery speaks of trust, obedience, and the close relationship between the Great Shepherd and his sheep (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:14-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:2</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>).

<!-- p38 -->
### The Vine and Its Branches

<!-- p39 -->
The church is described as the **Vine and its Branches**, which depicts the indispensable relationship between believers and Christ. Jesus is the true vine, and believers are the branches who must remain in him in order to bear fruit. This metaphor shows dependence on Christ for spiritual vitality, growth, and fruitfulness. Apart from him, believers can do nothing, but by abiding in him they glorify God and fulfill their purpose. Jesus will prune in order to produce more fruit in our lives (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:1-5</span>).

<!-- p40 -->
## The Role of the Church

<!-- p41 -->
The mission of the church, like the role of the Holy Spirit, can be understood in three directions: upward, inward, and outward, each reflecting a particular aspect of its calling in God's plan. These directions guide the church as it fulfills its role as a worshiping, edifying, and missional community.

<!-- p42 -->
### Upward: Worship and Praise of God

<!-- p43 -->
The primary purpose of the church is to worship and praise God. Corporate worship, where believers gather to magnify God together, is a visible reflection of His glory and a response to His greatness. As the psalmist says, “*Worship the Lord in the beauty of holiness; tremble before Him, all the earth*” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="96" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalms 96:9</span>). Beyond corporate worship, the church is called to live in obedience and sacrifice, offering themselves as “living sacrifices” as an act of spiritual worship (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:1-2</span>). This upward focus keeps the church centered on the honor of God and aligns it with prioritizing His will.

<!-- p44 -->
### Inward: Building up the Body

<!-- p45 -->
The Great Commission commands us to ‘*teach them to observe’*, a mandate that goes far beyond formal methods of instruction like this book. Disciple-making (discussed in detail in the chapter on discipleship) is shaping a follower to conform to the discipline, as a parent raises their child. A good parent does not give a five-year-old an encyclopedia and tell them to figure it out! Rather, they model, demonstrate, correct, guide, nurture, love, care, and shepherd.

<!-- p46 -->
Parents devote themselves to their children because they love them, because they belong to the same body, because they share the same blood. As members of the body and family of Christ, we share the same body, and we share the same blood. Communion reminds us symbolically that we have been knit together with one another through the sacrifice of Christ. The act of communion should be a place of unity, reminding us that we all need redemption, and that salvation comes from God alone. Within this unity we see that there is no member of the body greater than another except our head, Christ.

<!-- p47 -->
### Outward: Reaching the World

<!-- p48 -->
The most well-known part of the church’s mission reaches outward to bring the gospel to the world. The Great Commission of Jesus calls His followers to preach and make disciples of all nations (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19-20</span>). Empowered by the Holy Spirit, the church is to be His witness to the ends of the earth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>).

<!-- p49 -->
Jesus is the light of the world (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 8:12</span>). Light shines in every direction as it radiates from its source. His light is kindled in His expressions: His Word, His Spirit, and us, His body. We are the light of the world; our lives should shine with His light into our darkened world and display the good works that glorify God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 5:14-16</span>). Practically, this means the church is called to advocate for justice and mercy, both spiritual and physical. As James says, our pure religion is the care of the vulnerable, the orphans, and widows (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:27</span>).

<!-- p50 -->
## Church Leadership

<!-- p51 -->
Across the Christian world there are many kinds of denominations, gatherings, assemblies, and councils. There are very few universal rules for church leadership, but the rules we do have are very important:

<!-- p52 -->
1.  Jesus is the only head of the Church. Everyone serves Him (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 1:18</span>).

<!-- p53 -->
2.  There are higher standards for those who lead, they deserve double honor, for they will receive double judgment (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:17</span>).

<!-- p54 -->
2.1  They must be mature, not proud, responsible (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:1-13</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 1:5-9</span>).

<!-- p55 -->
2.2.  They must be servants of all (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42-45" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 10:42-45</span>).

<!-- p56 -->
2.3.  They must devote themselves to the Word and prayer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:4</span>).

<!-- p57 -->
2.4.  They are responsible for equipping the body for the work of ministry.

<!-- p58 -->
3.  There are no greater or lesser members of the body. Scriptures that support this are:

<!-- p59 -->
3.1.  There must be no favoritism toward either rich or poor (James 2).

<!-- p60 -->
3.2. There is no division between Jew and Gentile, slave or free, male or female (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 3:27-28</span>).

<!-- p61 -->
The structure of the church, beyond the rules of leadership, is important, but most of it is situational and a matter of culture rather than something universally commanded. “<u>That’s how we’ve always done it!</u>” is a common saying; if you go back far enough, there was often a good reason they did it that way. This is generally neither good nor bad; it is a matter of taste, need, and availability.

<!-- p62 -->
A helpful example showing that culture underlies structure is the time of family dinner. The most important part of family dinner time is that everyone eats and the food is nourishing. The variations are endless: what they eat, how they eat, how they season their food, etc. The different combinations are generally acceptable, and are mostly a matter of taste and preference.

<!-- p63 -->
A church with 10,000 members has needs and capacities different from those of a church of 50. The established pattern found in the book of Acts and the New Testament canon was the model of **elders** and **deacons**. Although the leadership structure may vary from church to church and denomination to denomination, this pattern ultimately represents the minimum workable leadership model for the church. Let’s look at these leadership offices and how they are chosen.

<!-- p64 -->
### Elders (Overseers)

<!-- p65 -->
Role and Responsibilities:

<!-- p66 -->
- **Spiritual Leadership** - Provide oversight, guidance, and pastoral care for the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:2</span>).

<!-- p67 -->
- **Teaching and Preaching** - Responsible for teaching sound doctrine and guarding against false teaching (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:17</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 1:9</span>).

<!-- p68 -->
- **Shepherding the Flock** - Care for the spiritual needs of the congregation, setting an example of humility and service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:2-3</span>).

<!-- p69 -->
- **Protecting the Church** - Serve as watchmen, defending the church against false doctrine and division (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29-31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:29-31</span>).

<!-- p70 -->
- **Equipping the Saints** - Train and equip believers for works of service and discipleship (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-12</span>).

<!-- p71 -->
Selection Process:

<!-- p72 -->
- **Appointed in Each Church** - Elders were appointed by the apostles or their delegates, often with prayer and fasting (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 14:23</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 1:5</span>).

<!-- p73 -->
- **Confirmed by the Holy Spirit** - Their appointment was seen as Spirit-led, recognizing God’s calling and gifting (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>).

<!-- p74 -->
- **Testing and Validation** - Candidates must demonstrate maturity and faithfulness before being entrusted with leadership (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:10</span>). They are not allowed to be recent converts! ((<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:6</span>)

<!-- p75 -->
### Deacons (Servants)

<!-- p76 -->
Role and Responsibilities:

<!-- p77 -->
- **Practical Service** - Focus on meeting the physical needs of the church, freeing the elders to focus on prayer and teaching (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-4</span>).

<!-- p78 -->
- **Support of Ministry** - Assist with hospitality, care for the poor, and administrative tasks (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:3</span>).

<!-- p79 -->
- **Fostering Unity** - Address matters of justice and care to prevent division (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-7</span>).

<!-- p80 -->
- **Examples of Faith** - They serve as models of humility, faith, and service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:13</span>).

<!-- p81 -->
Selection Process (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-6</span>):

<!-- p82 -->
- **Recognized by the congregation** - They are chosen based on their reputation and spiritual character.

<!-- p83 -->
- **Approved by the leaders** - The apostles reviewed and appointed the selected deacons by prayer and the laying on of hands.

<!-- p84 -->
- **Confirmed in Service** - Deacons are first tested to prove their faithfulness before being formally appointed (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:10</span>).

<!-- p85 -->
### Leadership Qualifications

<!-- p86 -->
#### Qualifications for both Elders and Deacons:

<!-- p87 -->
- Character - Both must be above reproach, dignified, and display self-control (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:2</span>, 8).

<!-- p88 -->
- Family Life - Both must be faithful in marriage (“husband of one wife”) and manage their households well (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:4-5</span>, 12).

<!-- p89 -->
- Spiritual Maturity - Both must be tested and proven before appointment (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:6</span>, 10).

<!-- p90 -->
- Reputation - Both need a good reputation inside and outside the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:7</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
- Faith and Integrity - Both must hold the faith with a clear conscience and practice what they believe (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:9</span>).

<!-- p92 -->
### Structural Examples

<!-- p93 -->
Using what we have learned so far about leadership principles and structures, let’s look at two examples of church structure found in the New Testament, Jerusalem and Antioch.

<!-- p94 -->
### The Church in Jerusalem

<!-- p95 -->
The church in Jerusalem was led primarily by the **apostles**, who focused on teaching, prayer, and spiritual oversight (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:42</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:4</span>). As the community grew, the need for administrative structure became evident, especially to address practical matters such as caring for widows and distributing resources. This led to the appointment of **deacons** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-7</span>). These individuals, chosen for their spiritual maturity and good reputation, managed practical ministries so that the apostles could devote themselves to their spiritual responsibilities. Over time, **elders** also emerged as leaders in the Jerusalem church. For example, during the Jerusalem Council (Acts 15), both the apostles and the elders participated in decision-making, demonstrating a shared leadership model.

<!-- p96 -->
### The Church in Antioch

<!-- p97 -->
The church in Antioch had a less centralized and more diverse leadership structure compared to Jerusalem. It was led by a group of **prophets and teachers**, including Barnabas, Saul (Paul), Simeon, Lucius, and Manaen (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 13:1</span>). The Antioch church was also heavily engaged in outreach to the Gentiles, while the Jerusalem church reflected its Jewish roots. Although the New Testament does not explicitly mention deacons in Antioch, it is likely that practical ministries existed as the church grew and was involved in sending relief to Jerusalem (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29-30" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 11:29-30</span>). The Antioch church became a model for sending missionaries, with its leaders praying, fasting, and laying hands on Paul and Barnabas to commission them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 13:2-3</span>). This demonstrates a structure that balances spiritual oversight (prophets and teachers) with evangelistic outreach and practical support.

<!-- p98 -->
Unlike Paul’s corrections addressed to the Corinthians and the Galatians, Scripture does not criticize the leadership structures of Jerusalem or Antioch. This indicates that these churches, under the guidance of the Holy Spirit, were free to arrange their gatherings to meet their specific needs and circumstances, within the framework of sound doctrine and the apostles’ teaching (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:42</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>).

<!-- p99 -->
Throughout these sections certain specific terms have been used that we have not yet defined or studied: apostle, prophet, evangelist. These gifts are part of the fivefold ministry that Jesus gave to the church.

<!-- p100 -->
### The Fivefold Ministry Gifts

<!-- p101 -->
Jesus established five distinct ministry gifts, commonly known as the fivefold ministry gifts, to equip believers for service and to build up the Body of Christ.

<!-- p102 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p103 -->
> **<u>Ephesians 4:11-13</u>** (NKJV)
>
> He Himself gave some to be apostles, some prophets, some evangelists, and some pastors (shepherds) and teachers,
>
> for the equipping of the saints for the work of ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ,
>
> till we all come to the unity of the faith and of the knowledge of the Son of God, to a perfect man, to the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ;
>

<!-- p104 -->
</span>

<!-- p105 -->
The five gifts given to equip the saints for the work of ministry are:

<!-- p106 -->
1.  **Apostles** - Pioneers and visionaries who lay foundations, plant churches, and further advance the Kingdom of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:20</span>). Apostles model courage and faith, demonstrating how to step into new territories and take spiritual initiative.

<!-- p107 -->
2.  **Prophets** - Messengers and guides who proclaim God’s truth, bringing encouragement, edification, and direction (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>). Prophets stir up faith and call people to align with the Word of God, modeling dependence on hearing the Voice of God.

<!-- p108 -->
3.  **Evangelists** - Proclaimers of the Gospel who focus on reaching the lost and mobilize others to respond so that they bear witness to Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:5-8</span>). Evangelists teach disciples how to share their faith effectively, model boldness in witness, and ignite compassion for the lost.

<!-- p109 -->
4.  **Pastors (Shepherds)** - Caregivers and nurturers who protect, guide, and provide spiritual oversight for the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>). Pastors teach disciples the importance of community, caring for others, and accountability within the Body of Christ.

<!-- p110 -->
5.  **Teachers** - Instructors and trainers who clarify doctrine and help believers grow in understanding and applying the Word of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 2:15</span>). Teachers equip disciples to study Scripture deeply, apply it to their daily lives, and teach others effectively.

<!-- p111 -->
## Leadership and Giftedness

<!-- p112 -->
A commonly misunderstood point about church leadership is distinguishing between the authority and responsibility of leadership and particular gifts.

<!-- p113 -->
This mistake is rooted in how we use words like teacher, pastor, prophet, apostle, evangelist. These terms are gifts. There is no **office** of ‘apostle’ in the New Testament church. There are people who do the work of an apostle, who have the gift or calling of an apostle. Possessing an apostolic gift does not confer authority or responsibility.

<!-- p114 -->
I am a trained musician; I have played the saxophone for more than 20 years. I played in both school and college, and at certain times in the summer I played semi-professionally working with an evangelistic team. Although I have the skill, gift, and ability to be a saxophonist, I do not have the right to come to your church and stand on the platform with the worship team.

<!-- p115 -->
Likewise, there are people gifted as pastors/shepherds, but that does not make them the ‘PASTOR’ of the local church or ‘your’ pastor. The fivefold ministry gifts do not lead the church; leaders (elders and deacons) lead. It is very common for leaders to have particular gifts among the fivefold, but those gifts strengthen their leadership; they do not create it. Likewise, it is normal for people who are still developing and who have fivefold gifts to serve and aspire to leadership, but God first cares about character, then gifts. We should all be mature, able to rightly divide the Word of God, full of the Spirit, and doing good works.

<!-- p116 -->
Every leader and every gift must first submit to being a disciple, a member of the body.

<!-- p117 -->
Jesus gave specific ministry gifts to equip the saints for the work of ministry. These are different from the motivational gifts (to be discussed later in this chapter) or the manifestation gifts (to be discussed in the chapter on baptisms). As disciples grow, the fivefold ministry gifts equip for ministry, while elders and deacons provide oversight to ensure sound doctrine and right practice.

<!-- p118 -->
## Your Role in the Church

<!-- p119 -->
Up to this point in the chapter we have discussed church leadership and the central, foundational examples for the church. Let us build on the specific example of the *body* and consider a marathon runner.

<!-- p120 -->
### You Are Part of the Whole

<!-- p121 -->
The church, since it is the Body of Christ, depends on every member working together to accomplish its purpose. Just as the body cannot function properly if one member is missing, so too the church needs every believer to participate actively, grow, and serve. Every member of the body has a very important role. Being part of the body means remaining connected. The body can continue to live, though weakened, without a member, but the member itself cannot live when cut off from the body. We must be an active part of the church and not be disconnected. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:24</span> says that we should come together and not neglect the assembling.

<!-- p122 -->
To truly fit together and function you need brotherhood! You must know others, love others, and get the best from others! Build strong relationships and friendships, and repair them when they become strained or injured. When a joint is injured or inflamed, you must work to rest it or reset it; so that a dislocated joint does not break! A broken member that does not heal is eventually amputated, and you cannot be a part of the body by yourself.

<!-- p123 -->
We do not all have the same task. Some of us can share the Gospel from a stage, others beside a dinner table. But ALL OF US are called to be ministers of reconciliation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 5:18-19</span>). We have all been placed into the priesthood of believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:9</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 1:6</span>). This means that we all can do the work of ministry, empowered by the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p124 -->
The toes work with the feet, and the feet with the ankles, and so on - each part contributes to the overall mission. Marathon runners must train if they want to finish the race. You must be prepared—not only learning, but actually putting into practice what you have learned. The church, like a marathon runner, grows when each member carries out its role in a unified way.

<!-- p125 -->
There are many people in your life whom only you can reach. The pastor cannot be the only one trying to care for and shepherd, and the evangelist cannot be the only one trying to reach people. God wants to use you to reach those who do not believe, empowered by His Holy Spirit. We all do the work of ministry; the Holy Spirit helps us.

<!-- p126 -->
### You Need Training

<!-- p127 -->
Believers must train and equip themselves for their specific roles in the church, like a runner strengthens particular muscles for the race. You will not succeed if you jump into a marathon without training, and not all training is the same; you need to prepare adequately for the race. Jesus gave specific ministry gifts to equip believers, not so that they would do all the work themselves, but to strengthen each member to serve effectively in their particular role.

<!-- p128 -->
Marathon training brings transformation. You need strength, and you need to be transformed. Our responsibility is to submit ourselves to God and allow Him to change us through renewal (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:1-2</span>). The change should lead you to maturity, enabling you to carry your load and support others. You must be able to bear MUCH fruit. This will be discussed at length in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p129 -->
Training is hard, and at times uncomfortable. Sometimes the change you need to go through you would prefer to skip. But if you want to reach the goal, you must stay on the narrow path. At times, you may need admonition, comfort, or support—all delivered with patience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Thessalonians 5:14</span>).

<!-- p130 -->
Every great marathon runner has a coach to train them, and you will need a coach to help you. Of course, you have the Holy Spirit, but often He relies on His coaches, the mature men and women of God in the Body of Christ, whom you are called to work with.

<!-- p131 -->
As individuals, we must first submit ourselves to God, and then, in this context, to a body of believers. Submission in the church means honoring divine leadership (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 13:17</span>) and submitting to one another in mutual submission (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:21</span>).

<!-- p132 -->
Submission can be difficult, especially when you have previously experienced broken trust or failed leadership. This does not mean blind obedience or control, but it reflects a spirit of respect and cooperation. Worldly submission is demanded by those in power, and can be demeaning and foster resentment. Biblical submission, linked with mutual submission and understanding, strengthens the church and fosters accountability. Be willing to listen to guidance and constructive feedback.

<!-- p133 -->
Now that we have laid a solid foundation - you are a meaningful part of the body but you need training - let us move on to finding your fit.

<!-- p134 -->
### Finding Your Fit

<!-- p135 -->
Finding your role in the church involves seeking God’s guidance, understanding your spiritual gifts, and actively participating in your church community. It is a journey of prayer, exploration, and growth, in which you discover how God has uniquely equipped you to contribute to His kingdom. It is very important to recognize that your role in the church is likely to change over time and is unlikely to remain the same for your whole life.

<!-- p136 -->
Here is a practical approach to help you find where you fit, presented in sequential steps for clarity, though you may repeat the same step many times as you walk with God.

<!-- p137 -->
#### Step 1 - Seek God’s Guidance Through Prayer and the Word

<!-- p138 -->
Begin by seeking God’s guidance in prayer. God is the ultimate source of your purpose and calling (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="29" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 29:11</span>). Pray consistently to gain clarity about how you can serve the church, asking Him to reveal your spiritual gifts and passions. Trust that He will direct your steps as you submit your plans to His will (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 3:5-6</span>).

<!-- p139 -->
Study God’s Word to deepen your understanding of the church’s mission and your role within it. Key passages such as 1 Corinthians 12 and <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-12</span> highlight the spiritual gifts and the diversity of roles in the Body of Christ. Reflect on examples of individuals in Scripture who found and fulfilled their roles, such as Paul, Timothy, and Lydia.

<!-- p140 -->
#### Step 2 - Identify Your Gifts, Interests, and Skills

<!-- p141 -->
Recognizing your **spiritual gifts** is an essential step. You may find that taking a spiritual gifts assessment is helpful. These gifts are given by God to build up the church. There are several categories of gifts listed in the New Testament:

<!-- p142 -->
- The **Motivational Gifts** that God gives: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:6-8</span>

<!-- p143 -->
- These will be discussed after this list.

<!-- p144 -->
- The Fivefold Ministry Gifts that Jesus gave: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-13</span>

<!-- p145 -->
- These were discussed earlier in this chapter.

<!-- p146 -->
- The Manifestation Gifts that the Holy Spirit gives: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span>

<!-- p147 -->
- These were discussed in the chapter on baptism.

<!-- p148 -->
The **motivational gifts** reflect how a believer is wired to think, feel, and act. They motivate believers to serve in specific ways that build up the church. Each gift complements the others, ensuring that the church functions as a unified body with diverse roles (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:4-5</span>). They operate as grace-endowed abilities (Greek: ***charis** - khar'-ece - **G5463 -** an unearned gift*) which means they come from God and are not earned or learned. Although the gift comes from God, the gifted person can further develop skill in its use.

<!-- p149 -->
The motivational gifts are:

<!-- p150 -->
1.  **Prophecy** - A God-given inclination to boldly declare truth, often focusing on confession and righteousness, guiding others to align with the Word of God.\*

<!-- p151 -->
2.  **Service (Helps)** - Addresses practical needs through service and assistance to support others.

<!-- p152 -->
3.  **Teaching** - Explains and clarifies truth, helping others understand and apply the Word of God.

<!-- p153 -->
4.  **Exhortation (Encouragement)** - Stirs, encourages, and comforts others so they may grow and persevere.

<!-- p154 -->
5.  **Giving** - Generously provides resources to meet needs and advance God’s work without seeking recognition.

<!-- p155 -->
6.  **Leadership (Administration)** - Organizes and guides others to achieve goals with vision and efficiency.

<!-- p156 -->
7.  **Mercy** - Shows compassion and care to comfort and support those who are hurting or in need.

<!-- p157 -->
\* Prophecy in the Romans sense is understood to be different from prophecy as a manifestation gift of the Holy Spirit (1 Corinthians). The Greek word used is the same in both passages, but the context differs. For a deeper study of the distinction, I recommend reading:

<!-- p158 -->
- *The Gift of Prophecy* by Kenneth E. Hagin ([www.rhema.org](http://www.rhema.org))

<!-- p159 -->
<!-- -->

<!-- p160 -->
- *Gifts and Ministries of the Holy Spirit* by Lester Sumrall

<!-- p161 -->
When identifying your gift, consider your **interests and skills**. God often calls us to serve in areas where we are interested or skilled (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 31:2-6</span>). Consider what excites you about serving God and others, and think about how your job skills, hobbies, and life experience can contribute to ministry.

<!-- p162 -->
Seek guidance from **leaders and mentors** who can provide wisdom and insight (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 15:22</span>). Meet with a pastor or ministry leader to discuss your interests and ask for honest feedback on where you might fit best. Pray over the feedback you receive. Consider joining a discipleship group to gain ongoing training and support.

<!-- p163 -->
#### Step 3 - Be Faithful Now

<!-- p164 -->
The parable of the talents (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-30" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 25:14-30</span>) reminds us to be faithful in small things so that we may be entrusted with greater things (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 16:10</span>).

<!-- p165 -->
**Get involved and serve** in various ministries to discover where God is calling you. Start small by volunteering in areas of need within your church and the ministries it supports. For example:

<!-- p166 -->
- Join the hospitality team, children’s ministry, or outreach programs. Consider leading a discipleship-focused group for new believers on this book, *Principles of Faith,* using the group discussion questions and end-of-chapter activities to facilitate conversation.

<!-- p167 -->
- Be open to stepping out of your comfort zone to discover new opportunities.

<!-- p168 -->
- Pay attention to where God is working in you and where you find yourself most effective.

<!-- p169 -->
Practice giving. We can contribute to the church in many meaningful ways, and we are commanded to do so to accomplish the mission.

<!-- p170 -->
- Time - we can give our time and volunteer. When we give our time, we are using our efforts and gifts. This can also be a great way to learn new skills and enhance existing ones.

<!-- p171 -->
- Resources - we can give our resources. Today this most often means financial giving to the church and ministries (not only the “organization”, but also those in need within the church). Monetary giving is helpful, because the church can purchase needed resources, but people have also given physical resources beyond money, for example extra homes, clothing, cars, food, building materials, equipment, etc. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="32-35" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 4:32-35</span> we see an example of the early believers giving freely, sharing proceeds from the sale of land and extra houses and giving so that ‘no one was in need’.

<!-- p172 -->
Working with others within ministry can reveal your strengths and deepen your understanding of your role. Participate in small groups or church activities to build connections. Listen to the burdens and needs of those around you and consider how you can support them. Building relationships within the church helps you connect with others and understand the needs of the community.

<!-- p173 -->
#### Step 4 - Seek Help!

<!-- p174 -->
Jesus said that in life we will face trouble! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:33</span>) We are encouraged not to carry the weight of our burdens alone but to cast them on God who cares for us! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:7</span>)

<!-- p175 -->
Sometimes in our journey we can be injured. Marathon runners, even when they are doing “everything right,” can put strain on their bodies that causes injury, or they can stumble and fall.

<!-- p176 -->
Not all ‘falls’ are related to sin, though some are; not all weakness is ‘our fault,’ and sometimes others sin against us. Regardless of the cause of our weakness or injury, we need to be restored and healed.

<!-- p177 -->
There is great freedom in seeking inner healing and restoration. We are called to cover the weaker parts of the body (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:23</span>) and to restore those caught in transgressions (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:1</span>).

<!-- p178 -->
Much can be said on this topic, and we broadly explore healing themes in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p179 -->
#### Step 5 - Be Patient

<!-- p180 -->
Pay attention to affirmation that comes from God and others. Often, God confirms your calling through fruitfulness, joy, and affirmation from those around you. Reflect on where your efforts have borne fruit and listen to feedback about the impact of your ministry. This can help you recognize where you are most effective in serving the Body of Christ.

<!-- p181 -->
Be patient and trust God’s timing. Discovering your role can take time and may involve trial and error (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 1:6</span>). Trust that God will guide you as you remain faithful and open to His leading. During this time, commit to growth and training by attending workshops, Bible studies, and other resources that equip you for ministry. Do this alongside the church, engaging with the Body of Christ to discern your gifts.

<!-- p182 -->
Remember, the church is not a building or an institution; it is a living, breathing body made up of people like you. By taking up your role, you contribute to its mission and glorify Christ, who is its head.
