# Chapter 7 - Jesus-The Foundation of Our Faith

<!-- p1 -->
Building further on our study about Jesus as the Messiah, in this chapter we will further examine the life, person, and work of Jesus. We will see that Jesus is the Mediator of a better new covenant-that He perfectly fulfills the roles of Prophet, Priest, and King. Within this New Covenant we, as believers, have a right standing before God, and we move from a works-based covenant to a trust-based (faith-based) covenant. Finally, we will read and study the *Apostles' Creed* to complete our study about Jesus.

<!-- p2 -->
## The Mediator of the New Covenant

<!-- p3 -->
The idea of a mediator refers to someone who stands between two parties, bridging the gap and facilitating relationship or agreement. In the narrative of the Exodus, Moses was the first mediator of the covenant God made with His people. Moses received the law, offered sacrifices (priest), called the people to obey God (prophet), and would judge (judge/king). In the remainder of the Old Covenant after Moses, the mediating role was carried out through the roles of the Prophets, the Levitical priesthood, and the Judges and later the kings of the nation.

<!-- p4 -->
The Mediators’ Roles in the Old Covenant:

<!-- p5 -->
1.  **Prophet:** The prophets were the messengers of God, sent to declare His heart to the people and to call them to return when they strayed. Moses, for example, served as a prophet mediating the Old Covenant by receiving God’s Law and teaching it to Israel. The prophets also foretold the coming of the Messiah and the ultimate redemption of humanity. Other notable examples of prophets include: Isaiah, Jeremiah, Elijah, Elisha, Samuel.

<!-- p6 -->
2.  **The Levitical Priesthood:** The priests served as mediators by offering sacrifices of atonement for sins and by interceding for the people before God. The High Priest, in particular, played a crucial role on the Day of Atonement, entering the Holy of Holies to sprinkle blood on the mercy seat, which symbolized reconciliation with God. The priest’s task is to intercede for the people.

<!-- p7 -->
3.  **Judge/King:** The Judges, and later the Kings, were charged with ruling the people of God, enforcing His laws, adjudicating the people, delivering them from their enemies, and leading in righteousness. The book of Judges recounts the stories of these leaders—people like Deborah, Samson, and Gideon—whom God raised up to save and guide Israel. Israel later had a line of kings, most of them bad, some good (see Kings 1<sup>st</sup> and 2<sup>nd</sup>, Samuel 1<sup>st</sup> and 2<sup>nd</sup>, and Chronicles 1<sup>st</sup> and 2<sup>nd</sup>). Notable examples include Saul, David, and Solomon.

<!-- p8 -->
It is worth noting that all the mediators of the Old Covenant were fully human, not divine. Since they are human, they could sin, and often did fall into sin. Some of the mediators were sinful and led the people away from God into false worship: false prophets, deceitful and corrupt priests, and wicked kings.

<!-- p9 -->
### The Messiah as the Mediator of the New Covenant

<!-- p10 -->
The death, resurrection, and ascension of Jesus fulfilled the Old Covenant and established the New Covenant.

<!-- p11 -->
The New Covenant is a life-transforming relationship between God and His people, established through the life, death, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. Unlike the Old Covenant, which emphasized external regulations, the New Covenant emphasizes inner transformation and grace. It began when Jesus declared at the Last Supper that His blood would inaugurate this covenant, replacing the repeated animal sacrifices of the Old Covenant with His once-for-all atonement (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 22:20</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:10</span>). Through this sacrifice, the New Covenant offers complete forgiveness and cleansing of sin, meeting the requirement for a true and lasting reconciliation with God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 31:34</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:12-18</span>).

<!-- p12 -->
One of the central features of the New Covenant is its internal character, as God writes His law on the hearts of His people, fostering a heart-level relationship with Him through the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 31:33</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 3:3</span>). This transformation empowers believers to live in God’s will, not merely by adhering to external commands, but by the Spirit’s enabling (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:1-4</span>). The New Covenant also grants direct access to God, removing the need for an earthly priesthood. Jesus serves as the eternal High Priest, and believers are called a royal priesthood, able to draw near to God with confidence because of Christ’s sacrifice (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 7:24-25</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:19-20</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:9</span>).

<!-- p13 -->
Unlike the Old Covenant, which was particular to Israel, the New Covenant is universal, extending to all who believe in Christ, regardless of ethnicity or background (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 3:28</span>). This covenant is entirely rooted in grace, freely offering salvation and forgiveness through faith in Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>). It is not upheld by human effort but by the empowering of the Holy Spirit, who transforms and strengthens believers to live in obedience and to reflect the character of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EZK" data-chapter="36" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ezekiel 36:26-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:9-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-23</span>). Furthermore, the New Covenant is everlasting, surpassing the temporary and conditional nature of the Old Covenant (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="32" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 32:40</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:13</span>). It represents the ultimate fulfillment of God’s plan of redemption, reconciling humanity to Himself and enabling believers to live forever as His people under His rule.

<!-- p14 -->
Jesus is explicitly identified as the Mediator of the New Covenant in

<!-- p15 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p16 -->
> **<u>Hebrews 9:15</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And for this reason He is the Mediator of the new covenant, by means of death, for the redemption of the transgressions under the first covenant, that those who are called may receive the promise of the eternal inheritance.
>

<!-- p17 -->
</span>

<!-- p18 -->
Through His atoning death and resurrection, the Messiah inaugurated this new covenant, replacing the old system of sacrifices and external regulations with a covenant written on the hearts of believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 31:31-34</span>). He stands between God and humanity, not only making intercession for His people but also providing the means of reconciliation through His blood (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:12</span>). Unlike the mediators of the Old Covenant, Jesus is sinless, eternal, and fully qualified to meet the divine requirements of justice and mercy, guaranteeing a lasting relationship between God and believers.

<!-- p19 -->
Jesus Fulfills and Perfects These Roles

<!-- p20 -->
1.  **Prophet:** Jesus is the supreme Prophet; he is not only a messenger of the word of God but the Word made flesh (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:14</span>). He fully and perfectly reveals the character of God, His will, and His plan of salvation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 1:1-2</span>). His teaching, miracles, and prophetic declarations fulfill the prophets of the Old Testament and lay the foundation for the New Testament.

<!-- p21 -->
2.  **Priest:** Jesus is the perfect High Priest, having offered himself as a once-for-all sacrifice for sin (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 7:27</span>). Unlike the priests of the Old Testament who were compelled to offer sacrifices repeatedly for their own sins and for those of the people, Jesus, since he was sinless, offered his blood to secure eternal redemption (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:14</span>).

<!-- p22 -->
3.  **King &amp; Judge:** Jesus is the eternal King, ruling with righteousness and justice. As the Son of David, he fulfills the promise of an everlasting kingdom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2SA" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Samuel 7:16</span>). His kingship is not merely earthly but cosmic, reigning over all creation and establishing peace through his victory over sin and death (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 2:9-11</span>). Jesus will return as King and Judge, to judge ‘the living and the dead’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 10:42</span>).

<!-- p23 -->
In Christ, the offices of Prophet, Priest, and King converge and are perfectly fulfilled. As the Mediator of the New Covenant, he bridges the gap between a holy God and sinful humanity, enabling believers to draw near to God with confidence (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 4:16</span>) while securing the eternal inheritance promised in him.

## The Apostles’ Creed

<!-- p25 -->
*The Apostles’ Creed* is not found in Scripture; it was formed and revised based on the early teaching of the church. A creed is an official confession of belief; think of it as a pledge or oath attached to a statement of faith. Creeds are important because they are used to unite or distinguish those who specifically believe from those who do not. In the United States it is common in civic occasions such as city council meetings or legislative sessions for participants to recite the Pledge of Allegiance. The pledge is widely used in the United States, but it is not used in other countries, because the citizens of those countries do not have allegiance (allegiance) to the United States. The pledge is not what makes one ‘American,’ but it represents important, foundational beliefs held by citizens.

<!-- p26 -->
The earliest recognizable forms of the creed were written in the fourth century A.D. The creed has changed slightly over the centuries, and there are minor variations based on denomination. Not all Christian denominations use the creed, yet in general it is widely accepted in some form across all of Christianity.

<!-- p27 -->
The Apostles’ Creed has for centuries been used as a teaching and catechetical tool, conveying the most basic truths of the Christian faith, the foundational beliefs recognized and held in order to be a Christian. Some denominations have a formal catechism process, which is how a new believer joins that particular denomination and is taught its doctrines.

<!-- p28 -->
No one needs to fully understand these beliefs to come to God and receive salvation; salvation comes by faith, not a creed exam! However, in continuing in that faith, we all must learn and understand these basic truths.

## A Brief Explanation of the Apostles’ Creed

<!-- p31 -->
The foundational truths in the Apostles’ Creed are numerous and very deep; a full explanation would certainly take several volumes. Although this chapter is about Jesus, many topics intersect with the other persons of the Godhead. This will be a brief explanation of the creed, focusing only on the central topics. Many of these matters are studied in greater depth in the chapters of this book.

<!-- p32 -->
#### *I believe in God, the Father Almighty, Maker of heaven and earth.*

<!-- p33 -->
This affirms belief in God as the sovereign Creator and Father who is all-powerful. The title "Father" emphasizes the loving relationship God has with his creation, especially his children by faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:12</span>). As Creator, he created the universe by his word, bringing order out of chaos (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 1:1-3</span>). His Almighty power is seen in the vastness of creation, while his fatherly care is seen in his continual sustaining and ordering of it. This foundational clause places God as both the source and the ruler of all life, worthy of worship and trust.

<!-- p34 -->
#### *I believe in Jesus Christ, his only Son, our Lord.*

<!-- p35 -->
Jesus Christ is the unique Son of God, eternally begotten, not created, sharing the divine nature (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:1-2</span>, 14). Jesus is the ‘Word’ from the beginning, and he became flesh and dwelt among us. As the "Christ," he is the Anointed One, the Messiah, fulfilling the Old Testament promises concerning the Savior. The title "Lord" acknowledges his authority over all creation and his rightful role as ruler and King (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 2:10-11</span>). He is not only Lord, but the Lord who saves (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 16:31</span>).

<!-- p36 -->
Jesus was not merely sent by God; he is part of God. Jesus is in the Father, and the Father is in Jesus. The Gospel of John repeatedly makes clear that Jesus and the Father are one (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="30" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:30</span}), that Jesus does nothing of himself (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 5:19</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="49-50" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 12:49-50</span>), and that everything he did was to glorify his Father (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="17" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 17:4</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:13</span>), and was done under his Father’s command and authority (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 8:28-29</span>).

<!-- p37 -->
#### *He was conceived by the power of the Holy Spirit and born of the Virgin Mary.*

<!-- p38 -->
This affirms the miraculous conception of Jesus by the Holy Spirit and emphasizes his divine origin. Being born of the Virgin Mary, Jesus fulfills the prophecy of <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 7:14</span>, which foretold the virgin birth of the Messiah. This event marks the incarnation, where the eternal Word of God took on human flesh (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:14</span>). Being fully God and fully man, Jesus bridges the gap between God and humanity, enabling him to be the perfect Mediator and Savior.

<!-- p39 -->
Jesus was conceived by the seed of the Holy Spirit, and he did not inherit the sinful nature that holds us, who were born of the seed of men. Jesus was tempted and tested in every way, but he was perfect and without sin. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 4:15</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 5:21</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:22</span>.

<!-- p40 -->
#### *He suffered under Pontius Pilate, was crucified, died, and was buried.*

<!-- p41 -->
The suffering and death of Jesus were real historical events that took place during the time of the Roman governor Pontius Pilate, which grounds the Christian faith in verifiable history. His crucifixion was the fulfillment of the prophets of the Old Testament, such as <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="53" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 53:5</span>, which speaks of the Suffering Servant who would bear the sins of many. The death of Jesus was sacrificial, as he took upon himself the penalty of sin for the sake of the people (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:24</span>). His burial confirms the reality of his death and lays the groundwork for the significance of the resurrection.

<!-- p42 -->
#### *He descended to the dead.*

<!-- p43 -->
After his death, Jesus descended to the abode of the dead, which Scripture often calls Hades or Sheol. This act demonstrates his full identification with humanity, even in death, and it affirms his victory over sin, death, and the devil (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 3:18-20</span>). By descending he proclaimed his victory and the completion of his work of redemption.

<!-- p44 -->
#### *On the third day he rose again.*

<!-- p45 -->
The resurrection of Jesus is the cornerstone of our Christian faith, and it confirms his divinity and the truth of his teaching. By rising from the dead, he conquered sin and death and gave believers the assurance of eternal life (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 15:17-22</span>). The resurrection also fulfills Scripture, including the prophecy Jesus himself spoke (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 16:21</span>). This miraculous event transformed his disciples’ despair into courageous faith, laying the foundation for the testimony of the early Church.

<!-- p46 -->
#### *He ascended into heaven, and sits at the right hand of the Father.*

<!-- p47 -->
His ascension marks his return to the Father’s presence in glory. By sitting at the right hand of the Father (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:20</span>), he assumes the office of our eternal High Priest, interceding for believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 7:25</span>). His position of authority also reveals his exaltation as King, ruling over all creation and directing the work of the Church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:22</span>) so that it may do greater works (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:12</span>) and fulfill the great commission he gave us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:18-20</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 16:15-18</span>). The ascension assures believers of his ongoing presence and power through the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p48 -->
#### *He will come again to judge the living and the dead.*

<!-- p49 -->
This points to the future return of Christ, when he will bring final justice and restoration. Jesus will judge all people, rewarding the faithful and executing justice upon those who have not repented (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 4:1</span>). His return fulfills God’s ultimate plan for creation, ushering in his everlasting Kingdom of righteousness and peace. This hope encourages believers to live with faithfulness and expectation, knowing that their labor in the Lord is not in vain (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="58" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 15:58</span>).

<!-- p50 -->
#### *I believe in the Holy Spirit.*

<!-- p51 -->
The Holy Spirit, the third Person of the Trinity, is the presence and power of God at work in the world today. He convicts of sin, regenerates hearts, and strengthens believers to live holy lives (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:8-13</span>). The Spirit unites Christians as one body—the Church—and equips them with spiritual gifts for service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:4-11</span>). He is the Helper, Advocate, and Guide, dwelling within believers as the seal of their eternal inheritance (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>).

<!-- p52 -->
#### *The holy universal Church, the communion of saints.*

<!-- p53 -->
The phrase "the holy universal Church" refers to the global body of Christ, composed of all believers in every time and place. The Church is devoted to God’s purposes and is called to display his holiness and share the Gospel. The phrase "the communion of saints" emphasizes the unity of all Christians, both the living and the dead, as members of the body of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:4-6</span>). This fellowship transcends worldly divisions, displaying the shared hope and mission of believers.

<!-- p54 -->
Jesus is the head of the Church. The Church is called both the Bride of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="25-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:25-27</span>) and the body of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 1:18</span>).

<!-- p55 -->
#### *The forgiveness of sins.*

<!-- p56 -->
This highlights the heart of the Gospel: because of Jesus’ sacrificial death, God offers full forgiveness of sins to all who repent and believe (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:7</span>). This forgiveness restores the broken relationship between humanity and God, removing guilt and shame. It also calls believers to extend forgiveness to others, reflecting the grace they have received (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 6:14-15</span>).

<!-- p57 -->
#### *The resurrection of the body.*

<!-- p58 -->
Like Jesus, at the end of time we will experience a bodily resurrection. This resurrection will transform mortal, perishable bodies into glorious, imperishable ones, fit for eternal life (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42-44" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 15:42-44</span>). It emphasizes the Christian hope of restoration—not only of individuals, but of all creation—as God makes all things new (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="21" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 21:4-5</span>).

<!-- p59 -->
#### *And life everlasting. Amen.*

<!-- p60 -->
Eternal life with God is the highest promise of the Christian faith. This life begins now through a relationship with Christ and will be fully realized in the new heavens and the new earth, where believers will dwell with God forever (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 22:1-5</span>). This concluding confession reflects the joy and assurance of salvation, echoing the confidence and hope expressed throughout the Confession.

<!-- p61 -->
## Conclusion

<!-- p62 -->
In the foundational truths expressed in the Apostles’ Creed, we see that the life and work of Jesus are central to God’s plan for humanity. Every statement in the Creed addresses key aspects of our faith in God and who Jesus is—his divinity, his humanity, his mission, and his final victory. These truths point to his unique role as the Mediator of the New Covenant. As Mediator, he invites us to draw near to God, trusting in his perfect sacrifice and his ongoing intercession. It calls us to worship him as the righteous King and to follow his teaching as the supreme Prophet. Ultimately, learning of Jesus leads to a deeper relationship with him, transforming us to become a people who live in the light of his grace and truth.

<!-- p63 -->
Understanding who Jesus is and what he has done is the foundation of the Christian faith. The many names, titles, and roles of Jesus illuminate different facets of his character and work, providing a fuller picture of his love and his sovereign power.

<!-- p64 -->
As with our study of God, Scripture is filled with various names referring to Jesus. Each name represents a particular aspect of his overall character, ministry, and role. These names are given to us to reveal some facet of Jesus. For your personal study a reference table has been included in the Appendix: *Table 12 - The Names of Jesus.* Read these names and study the Scripture references.
