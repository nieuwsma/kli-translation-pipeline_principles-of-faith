# Chapter 2 - God-The Everlasting Creator and Loving Father

<!-- p1 -->
It is essential that we build a strong foundation of knowledge about who God is. We can understand by learning:

<!-- p2 -->
- His will, his purposes, and his actions,

<!-- p3 -->
- His nature, his character, and his attributes,

<!-- p4 -->
- What is said about him and what he says about himself.

<!-- p5 -->
Scripture specifically reveals the attributes of God, with each part deepening our understanding of the unchanging, eternal God.

<!-- p6 -->
To sum up God in a single attribute is a difficult task, if not impossible, for he is far too vast and multifaceted to be simplified. Although we can, and will, make great efforts to explain, describe, and portray God, ultimately, like art and music, he must be encountered to be understood.

<!-- p7 -->
When a person listens to music, they describe it by expressing how it moves them-how it sounds within, beyond merely the notes and melodies. A melody can carry warmth or strength, with rhythm beating like a heartbeat or crescendos creating feelings of exhilaration or longing. Music can soothe like a gentle wave or rouse like a storm, lifting the listener’s mood, grounding their thoughts, or transporting them somewhere else entirely. This experience is far more than a sequence of sounds; it is the soul of the music that remains, captured in how it shapes the listener’s emotions and personal journey.

<!-- p8 -->
Similarly, a person viewing a painting looks beyond the colors or brushstrokes, exploring what those elements reflect. A landscape can evoke a serene escape, while abstract art can stir curiosity or personal reflection. Certain shades can bring warmth, while composition can create a sense of calm, chaos, or beauty. The painting becomes a living experience, prompting a shift in perspective or a profound emotional response that words alone cannot capture. Thus, music and art become more than their physical form-they become encounters that touch something fundamental, transforming the listener or viewer in deeply personal ways.

## Exploring God through the Old Testament

<!-- p10 -->
### Genesis: The God Who Dwells with His Creation

<!-- p11 -->
A central theme in God’s story is his desire to dwell with his creation. This is clearly seen in the narratives in Genesis. Within its fifty chapters and genealogies beginning with Adam (the first man) to Joseph (who is the last major figure in the book of Genesis, the last significant leader of God’s people before Moses), there is a span of approximately ~2,400 years. In these stories we see the constancy of God’s goodness despite human evil.

<!-- p12 -->
In Genesis, God establishes relationships with chosen individuals.

<!-- p13 -->
1.  **Adam and Eve** - The first humans, created by God, and placed in the Garden of Eden. God walked with them and spoke to them, giving instructions and building a mutual relationship of love and obedience. However, that relationship was affected by their disobedience when they ate from the Tree of the Knowledge of Good and Evil (Genesis 1-3).

<!-- p14 -->
2.  **Cain** - Although Cain ultimately committed the murder of his brother Abel, God spoke to him directly, warning him of the danger of sin and giving him guidance before the killing. Even after Cain’s punishment, God set a mark for him to protect him from harm (Genesis 4).

<!-- p15 -->
3.  **Noah** - God formed a special relationship with Noah, a righteous man, at a time when corruption was very widespread. God commanded Noah to build an ark to save his family and the animals from the coming flood. Afterwards, God made a covenant with Noah, promising not to destroy the earth again with a flood, and set the rainbow as the sign of that covenant (Genesis 6-9).

<!-- p16 -->
4.  **Abraham (Abram)** - One of the most important relationships in Genesis, God called Abram to leave his homeland and promised to make him the father of a great nation. God made a covenant with Abram, promising land, offspring, and blessings for all nations through him. His faith and obedience become central themes, and his name was changed to Abraham as part of the covenant (Genesis 12, 15, 17, 22).

<!-- p17 -->
5.  **Hagar** - An Egyptian slave who served Sarah, Hagar encountered God in the wilderness after being mistreated. God comforted her, promised a future for her son Ishmael, and instructed her to return to Abraham and Sarah. Hagar gave God the name “El Roi” (“The God who sees me”), recognizing his care (Genesis 16).

<!-- p18 -->
6.  **Isaac** - The son of Abraham and Sarah, Isaac inherits his father’s covenant promises. God confirms these promises directly to Isaac, including the promise of offspring and blessings. God also guided Isaac during a famine, protecting him and strengthening the covenant relationship (Genesis 26).

<!-- p19 -->
7.  **Rebekah** - Although her relationship with God was indirect, God answered Rebekah’s prayer regarding her difficult pregnancy, revealing that her twins, Jacob and Esau, would become two nations with different destinies (Genesis 25).
