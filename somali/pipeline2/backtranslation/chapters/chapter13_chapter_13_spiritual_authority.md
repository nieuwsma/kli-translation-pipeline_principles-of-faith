# Chapter 13 - Spiritual Authority

<!-- p1 -->
Jesus is the highest and complete source of spiritual authority. As the victorious Messiah, He has ultimate authority over all things in heaven and on earth.

<!-- p2 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p3 -->
> **<u>Matthew 28:18-20</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Then Jesus came and spoke to them, saying, "All authority has been given to Me in heaven and on earth.
>
> Go therefore and make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit,
>
> teaching them to observe all things that I have commanded you; and behold, I am with you always, even to the end of the age." Amen.
>

<!-- p4 -->
</span>

<!-- p5 -->
In this chapter we will learn about the authority and power of Christ, the authorities and powers He has delegated to us (His church), and how to operate in the authority of the name of Jesus. We will also learn about angelic and demonic beings, and we will look at their authority, their power, and their characteristics.

<!-- p6 -->
Authority means having the right to act. Power is the ability to carry it out. Both are essential in the spiritual life. Authority is different from power. Let us look at the following table that compares authority &amp; power and consider a few examples.

<!-- p7 -->
[See Table 7](../tables/table7.md)

<!-- p8 -->
Here are a few examples to understand the difference between power and authority.

<!-- p9 -->
Example 1: At work, an employee may have the talent and skills to carry out the sale of the company's assets (power). This power is lawful only if they also have the relevant authorization (authority) from the owners of the company. Without this authority, the sale of the company's assets is theft.

<!-- p10 -->
Example 2: A police officer is given specialized training in firearms and hand-to-hand combat to learn how to apprehend a criminal and to use physical force (power). Police officers are given job responsibility supported by the laws (authority) to enforce the penal codes.

## The Source of Jesus' Power

<!-- p74 -->
Now that we have properly examined what power is, we need to know who the source of that power is. Jesus’ power is divine and delegated. It comes from God the Father, is confirmed by His death and resurrection, and operates perfectly in accordance with the will of the Father. Although it is absolute, His power respects human free will, revealing God’s love rather than a rule by force.

<!-- p75 -->
Jesus’ power is rooted in His divine identity as the Son of God, and it is given by the Father. The Father has entrusted Christ with all authority, including the rule and dominion of heaven and earth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 11:27</span>). Jesus’ power reflects the unity and love within the Trinity, wherein the Son perfectly carries out the Father’s plan (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="35" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:35</span>).

<!-- p76 -->
Although Jesus possessed inherent power as the Son of God, His victory over sin, death, and hell was fully accomplished and confirmed through His death and resurrection. Christ’s obedience in His sacrifice led to His exaltation (seated at the right hand of the Father), securing His authority over all creation. By overcoming death, Christ obtained absolute dominion over life and eternity.

## Laying On of Hands

<!-- p116 -->
The laying on of hands is an important biblical practice used in both the Old and New Testaments to signify blessing, appointment/sending, healing, and the impartation of power or spiritual gifts. It is more than a mere physical act—it represents a spiritual connection and transmission under the authority of God.

<!-- p117 -->
In the Old Testament, the laying on of hands was often associated with blessing and consecration/dedication. For example, Jacob blessed his grandchildren, Ephraim and Manasseh, by laying his hands on them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="48" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 48:14</span>). Likewise, Moses laid his hands on Joshua to appoint his successor, imparting leadership authority and wisdom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="NUM" data-chapter="27" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Numbers 27:18-23</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="DEU" data-chapter="34" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Deuteronomy 34:9</span>). That act clearly demonstrated God’s choice and empowerment for the task ahead.

<!-- p118 -->
In the New Testament, the laying on of hands is closely tied to the ministry of Jesus and the early church. Jesus regularly laid His hands on the sick to heal them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 6:5</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 4:40</span>), demonstrating compassion and power over sickness and disease. The apostles continued this practice, using it for the impartation of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:17</span>), spiritual gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 4:14</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 1:6</span>), and blessings directed to believers. It also played a role in appointing leaders and missionaries for the work of ministry (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 13:3</span>).

<!-- p119 -->
The laying on of hands is a sign of an act of faith and obedience to the Word of God. It is not a mechanical ritual, but a spiritual practice tied to reliance on the Holy Spirit. For example, in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:6</span>, the apostles laid hands on those chosen to serve as deacons, dedicating them to God’s service and clearly recognizing their calling. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 19:6</span>, Paul laid his hands on believers, and they received the Holy Spirit, showing his role in spiritual strengthening.

<!-- p120 -->
However, the Holy Bible also provides warnings about this practice:

<!-- p121 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p122 -->
> **<u>1 Timothy 5:22</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Do not be hasty in the laying on of hands, nor share in other people's sins; keep yourself pure.
>

<!-- p123 -->
</span>

<!-- p124 -->
This highlights the importance of discernment and spiritual maturity when appointing individuals to leadership roles. As we learned in the chapter on the church, leaders must be tested and confirmed to avoid promoting believers who are unqualified or still immature, who could harm the Body of Christ.

<!-- p125 -->
Further guidelines about the laying on of hands:

<!-- p126 -->
1.  Contextually appropriate - the person on whom hands are being laid should voluntarily consent.

<!-- p127 -->
2.  Physically appropriate - the laying on of hands does not involve sexual or arousing touch. Culturally, placing the hand on the shoulder, hand, or forehead are generally among the acceptable areas (with consent) in the Western world.

<!-- p128 -->
3.  Culturally appropriate - in some cultures, it is not appropriate for men and women to touch unless they are blood relatives or married.

<!-- p129 -->
The laying on of hands remains a powerful expression of faith and obedience in the Church today. It serves as a means of blessing, healing, commissioning, and empowerment, and always points to the power of Christ and the work of the Holy Spirit. Whether praying for the sick, sending missionaries, or appointing leaders, it is a reminder that believers do not operate in their own power but under the authority of Jesus Christ and in alignment with His purposes.

<!-- p130 -->
Now that we have learned about the authority of Jesus and the authority of the believer, we will discuss other beings in the spiritual realm, angels and demons.

<!-- p131 -->
## Angels and Demons

<!-- p132 -->
### Angels

<!-- p133 -->
Angels are an important part of God’s creation, serving as messengers and agents carrying out His divine purposes. The Holy Bible reveals a spiritual realm inhabited by these beings, who are powerful, intelligent, and obedient to God’s commands. Although many angels are unseen, their presence and actions influence human history and spiritual warfare. Understanding their role, hierarchy, and functions gives us deep insight into God’s plan and the unseen realities that surround us.

<!-- p134 -->
The word “angel” itself means “messenger,” which highlights their primary task. Angels operate in obedience to the will of God, whether delivering messages, providing protection, executing judgment, or engaging in spiritual battles. They are not to be worshiped, but serve as examples of obedience and reverence, always pointing to the glory of God. This chapter explores the nature, hierarchy, and roles of angels, highlighting their importance in Scripture and in our lives today.

<!-- p135 -->
Within the Scriptures angels are seen as **messengers**, **servants**, and **warriors**. Angels often act as messengers, delivering important announcements, warnings, and encouragement. They reveal God’s plans and provide guidance when needed (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26-38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 1:26-38</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 2:13</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 1:1</span>). Angels actively participate in protecting, strengthening, and delivering believers. They minister to God’s people in times of distress and provide help beyond the natural (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="91" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalms 91:11</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 12:7-11</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 4:11</span>). Angels are engaged in spiritual warfare, fighting against the demonic forces of evil to advance the kingdom of God and defend believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="DAN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Daniel 10:13</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:7-9</span>).

<!-- p136 -->
#### The Nature of Angels

<!-- p137 -->
Angels are created beings, whom God brought into existence before the foundation of the world. They are distinct from humans, possessing unique attributes that equip them for their roles in heaven. The Holy Bible teaches that angels are spiritual beings not bound by physical limitations, yet at times they can take on a visible form when interacting with people.

<!-- p138 -->
Angels are described as immortal, which means they do not die or age. They are innumerable, and vast hosts fill the heavenly places. Although they are often referred to with masculine terms, Scripture clearly defines that angels are sexless and do not marry or reproduce. These qualities emphasize their purpose as servants of God, wholly devoted to His will and commands. Angels possess remarkable characteristics that reflect their divine purpose. They are obedient, reverent, wise, and powerful. These traits equip them to carry out God’s will effectively and faithfully.

[See Table 8](../tables/table8.md)

<!-- p140 -->
Although the Bible highlights several roles and titles of angels, it does not provide a comprehensive classification system. However, some angels are identified by specific roles or functions:

<!-- p141 -->
- **The Angel of the LORD** - Often regarded as a pre-incarnate appearance of Christ, appearing with divine authority. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="23" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 23:20-23</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JDG" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Judges 6:11-24</span>*)

<!-- p142 -->
- **Archangels** - High-ranking angels such as Michael, who leads armies, and Gabriel, who delivers divine messages. (*Jude 9; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:7</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 1:19-26</span>*)

<!-- p143 -->
- **Cherubim** - Guardians of the glory and presence of God, such as in the Garden of Eden and the Ark of the Covenant. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 3:24</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 25:22</span>*)

<!-- p144 -->
- **Seraphim** - Those who worship God, continually proclaiming His holiness. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 6:1-6</span>*)

<!-- p145 -->
- **Elect Angels** - Faithful angels who resisted Satan’s rebellion and remain loyal to God. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:21</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 25:41</span>*)

<!-- p146 -->
These classifications highlight the diversity and order within the angelic realm, yet their primary focus is always the fulfillment of God’s purposes.

<!-- p147 -->
#### Human Interaction with Angels

<!-- p148 -->
People often encounter angels in Scripture, but we are never commanded to order them or pray to them. Rather, angels act as God directs. They serve believers as **servants of God** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 1:14</span>). Their interactions include protection, guidance, and deliverance; they always point us to the power and supremacy of God.

<!-- p149 -->
- **Warnings against worshiping angels**: Scripture forbids the worship of angels or seeking encounters with them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:18</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 22:8-9</span>).

<!-- p150 -->
- **Pray to God, not to angels**: Believers should pray to God, who sends angels as He wills (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="91" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalms 91:11</span>).

<!-- p151 -->
- **Encouragement and Confidence**: Angels remind us of God’s care, but our faith must rest fully in God, not in angels.

<!-- p152 -->
Angels are awe-inspiring creatures created to serve God and minister to His people. Although their work is often unseen, Scripture shows us glimpses of their activities, revealing their faithfulness, strength, and reverence. Angels remind us of God’s care and protection, yet they always return the focus to His praise. As believers, we are called to trust in God’s care, knowing that angels serve as His agents to accomplish His divine purposes.

<!-- p153 -->
### Demons

<!-- p154 -->
Just as the study of angels is essential to understanding the spiritual world, so too is the study of demons important. Scripture clearly explains their activities, influence, and defeat through Jesus Christ. Recognizing their role in spiritual warfare helps believers stand firm in faith without fear.

<!-- p155 -->
#### The Origin and Nature of Demons

<!-- p156 -->
Demons are the fallen angels who followed Satan’s rebellion against God. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:7-9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 25:41</span>).* Satan, originally known as Lucifer (“bearer of light”), was an exalted angel who sought to exalt himself above God and was cast out of heaven along with the other rebellious angels. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 14:12-15</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EZK" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ezekiel 28:12-17</span>).*

<!-- p157 -->
Demons are spirits without physical bodies, yet they can oppress and torment people and animals. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 5:11-13</span>).* They seek to deceive, tempt, oppress, and destroy. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 11:14</span>). Unlike God,* they have limits, and are not omnipresent (present everywhere), omniscient (all-knowing), omnipotent (all-powerful)*.* Their ultimate defeat is certain, and they will face eternal punishment. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 20:10</span>)*

<!-- p158 -->
Scripture describes the work and activity of Satan as follows:

<!-- p159 -->
- He hinders the work of God *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Thessalonians 2:18</span>)*

<!-- p160 -->
- He deceives the world *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 4:4</span>)*

<!-- p161 -->
- He tempts and accuses believers *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 22:31</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:10</span>)*

<!-- p162 -->
- He afflicts and harms *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JOB" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Job 1:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 13:16</span>)*

<!-- p163 -->
Satan has been given many names and attributes, each of which points to an aspect of his work.

<!-- p164 -->
[See Table 9](../tables/table9.md)

<!-- p165 -->
The Gospels and the epistles further detail the enemy’s work. Evil spirits, since they do not have bodies, often carry out their work through people who are willing and open. God works through people, and so does Satan. This oppression is often characterized by torment, self-harm, and destructive behaviors *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 8:27-29</span>).* Their attacks produce fear, temptation, and discouragement *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 12:7</span>).* The primary way their power (not authority) takes hold in a person’s life is through the influence of lies and emotional strongholds *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 10:3-5</span>).*

<!-- p166 -->
It is important to know that not every lie a person believes, every physical ailment, or every stronghold in one’s life is a direct result of demonic influence, but these areas of injury are places the enemy can exploit to keep us in a state of confusion and oppression.

<!-- p167 -->
#### The power of Jesus over the works of the enemy

<!-- p168 -->
Do not be afraid; Jesus has full and complete authority over the works of the enemy. Jesus demonstrated authority over demons during his ministry *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 1:34</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 5:1-20</span>).* The works of the enemy, and Satan himself, were broken through the death and resurrection of Jesus *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:15</span>).*

<!-- p169 -->
As believers we have been given authority delegated by Jesus to cast out demons in his name:

<!-- p170 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p171 -->
> **<u>Mark 16:17</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And these signs will follow those who believe: In My name they will cast out demons; they will speak with new tongues;
>

<!-- p172 -->
</span>

<!-- p173 -->
Furthermore, as heirs of salvation we have the authority to submit to God, resist the devil, and he will flee from us! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 4:7</span>).

<!-- p174 -->
To deal with an evil or unclean spirit, the pattern Scripture shows is to take authority in the name of Jesus and command it to leave. The Holy Spirit has promised to give us wisdom and discernment if we ask! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:5</span>). We do not need to make it a spectacle, or a show like in the movies, or even talk to them; that is a deception Satan uses to distract us. Satan’s power is built and maintained only by the lies he can persuade us to believe.

<!-- p175 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="18" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p176 -->
> **<u>Matthew 18:18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> "Assuredly, I say to you, whatever you bind on earth will be bound in heaven, and whatever you loose on earth will be loosed in heaven.
>

<!-- p177 -->
</span>

<!-- p178 -->
We speak to them in the name of Jesus, commanding them to leave.

<!-- p179 -->
There are two major warnings that Scripture gives when dealing with demons:

<!-- p180 -->
1.  **Only those who have a relationship with Jesus have authority over demons**. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 19:11-20</span> recounts the sons of a Jewish high priest named Sceva. They tried to intervene in the life of a person whom the devil had oppressed and tormented, but the evil spirit refused to leave because they had no relationship with Jesus. The man who was oppressed leaped on them, stripped off their clothes, and beat them. Just as you wouldn’t pretend to be a police officer, **do not pretend to be someone who has a relationship with Jesus; instead, have a relationship with Jesus**!

<!-- p181 -->
2.  **When an evil spirit goes out, it leaves for a short time and tries to return**. If it finds the place it formerly inhabited empty, it will come back worse. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="43-45" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 12:43-45</span>. When we minister to someone oppressed by the devil, that person needs to fill the vacancy the evil spirit held in their life. At the very least, that means establishing a personal relationship with Jesus and strengthening obedience to the Holy Spirit. Many times an evil or unclean spirit gains power to operate in a person’s life through the choices they have made, based on their beliefs. Anyone delivered from the devil needs to replace their former belief system and be transformed by the renewing of their mind (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:1-2</span>).

<!-- p182 -->
We serve a marvelous and powerful God, and we are ‘more than conquerors’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="37" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:37</span>) over a defeated enemy. We do not need to fear our defeated enemy, for Jesus has given us His authority so that we may crush his head! The devil’s time is short, and in the end he will be judged along with every principality and power on earth that opposes the reign of Christ.
