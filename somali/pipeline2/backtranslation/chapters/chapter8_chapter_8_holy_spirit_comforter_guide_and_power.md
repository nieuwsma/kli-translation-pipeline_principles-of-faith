# Chapter 8 - The Holy Spirit-Comforter, Guide, and Power

<!-- p1 -->
The Holy Spirit is not a new spirit but is from God, and He has been with God since the beginning. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 1:2</span> the Spirit of God was hovering over the waters during creation. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="NEH" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Nehemiah 9:20</span>, Scripture says that the Spirit was the one who taught the people in the wilderness. It was the same Holy Spirit whom God anointed upon the kings, judges, and prophets of the Old Testament. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JOL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Joel 2:28-29</span> it is promised that the Spirit will be poured out on all people. The same Holy Spirit is the one by whom God raised Jesus from the dead, and now dwells within us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:11</span>). There is only one Holy Spirit, and He has always been the same. The way He has worked with people has at times differed, but His heart has never changed.

<!-- p2 -->
Jesus gave His disciples, and all of us, the strong promise of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p3 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p4 -->
> **<u>John 14:16-18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> I will pray the Father, and He will give you another Helper, that He may abide with you forever—
>
> the Spirit of truth, whom the world cannot receive, because it neither sees Him nor knows Him

<!-- p20 -->
The disciples were instructed to wait for the promise of the Spirit that would be poured out, so that they might be empowered to be witnesses to the ends of the earth. The Holy Spirit is an important part of God's plan for humanity, and is indispensable for us as believers, so that we may complete our mission, and likewise for the world.

<!-- p21 -->
## Studying the Holy Spirit

<!-- p22 -->
The Holy Spirit, first and foremost, comes from God, and is God. Scripture calls the Spirit the Spirit of God, or the Spirit of the Lord, or the Spirit of Christ; although these reveal different aspects, they are the same. The Holy Spirit, being fully God, is also eternal and holy (blameless, innocent, pure, without evil). Some translations call the Holy Spirit the Holy Ghost.

<!-- p23 -->
The Greek word for spirit is **Pneuma** (**NYOO-mah** - **G4151**), which means breath, or wind. The Hebrew word for spirit is similar. Breath is important since we need a constant supply of it to live. God breathed into Adam to give him life; Jesus also breathed his last breath before he died on the cross. Without breath there is no life. Breath, like the wind, can be felt and experienced but is not easily seen like other 'elements' such as water, fire, earth.

<!-- p24 -->
There are several other fundamental attributes used to describe the Holy Spirit. Each of these attributes is used along with 'Spirit of', which means these attributes are essential to defining the Spirit. For example, *Spirit of Truth* or *Spirit of Life*.

<!-- p25 -->
The Spirit of:

<!-- p26 -->
- **Truth** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:17</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span> It refers to the Spirit’s role in guiding believers into all truth, revealing the will of God and the reality of Christ.

<!-- p27 -->
- **Life** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:2</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:8</span> It highlights the Spirit’s role in imparting spiritual and eternal life to believers, freeing them from the law of sin and death.

<!-- p28 -->
- **Grace/Mercy** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:29</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ZEC" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Zechariah 12:10</span> It reflects the Spirit’s work in dispensing God’s grace, forgiveness, and unmerited mercy extended to humanity.

<!-- p29 -->
- **Glory** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 4:14</span> It points to the Spirit’s role in revealing and manifesting the glory of God, especially in times of suffering and persecution.

<!-- p30 -->
- **Adoption (Sonship)** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:15</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 4:5</span> It refers to the Spirit’s work in making believers children of God, granting them the status of sons and daughters.

<!-- p31 -->
- **Holiness** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 1:4</span> It refers to the sanctifying work of the Spirit, setting believers apart for God’s purposes.

<!-- p32 -->
- **Power** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 1:7</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span> It describes the Spirit’s empowerment of believers to have boldness, service, and the ability to live out their faith effectively.

<!-- p33 -->
All these attributes of the Holy Spirit are rightly recognized in a Helper or Encourager. Jesus calls the Holy Spirit the coming helper, and uses the Greek word ***Paraklētos** (*pah-RAH-klay-tos - **G3875**), which means advocate, helper, one called alongside.

<!-- p34 -->
An advocate speaks on behalf of another, like a defense attorney. They intercede and assist the one they are charged to help.

<!-- p35 -->
A helper lends a hand. They help enable the work, but their primary role is to support the task. The helper is not solely responsible for doing the work, but comes alongside to help see it accomplished.

<!-- p36 -->
The Holy Spirit has several distinct purposes and roles within his ministry. His work is inward (in the hearts and lives of believers), upward (in service and the acknowledgment of God), and outward (our mission field, the world).

<!-- p37 -->
## The Roles of the Holy Spirit-Inward, Upward, Outward

<!-- p38 -->
### Inward-The Holy Spirit unites us to Jesus

<!-- p39 -->
Before Jesus performed any miracles or began his public ministry, he was baptized by John the Baptist, the Spirit of God descended upon him, and a voice from heaven declared, ‘This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:17</span>).

<!-- p40 -->
When we accept Jesus by faith, we receive salvation and the seal of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p41 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p42 -->
> **<u>Ephesians 1:13-14</u>** (NKJV)
>
> In Him you also trusted, after you heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation; in Him also, when you believed, you were sealed with the Holy Spirit of promise,
>
> who is the guarantee of our inheritance until the redemption of the purchased possession, to the praise of His glory.
>

<!-- p43 -->
</span>

<!-- p44 -->
This seal is not imaginary but is real. It serves as the sign of our redemption and is the down payment of the full inheritance that we receive as children of God. Romans 8 explains that in the new birth we receive the very Spirit of God who raised Christ from the dead—in our mortal bodies—and that we become the true temple of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 6:16</span>).

<!-- p45 -->
Receiving the Spirit of God transforms us and causes rivers of living water to flow from us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 7:38</span>). This renewal is immediate and complete, but it continues throughout our whole life. The river of the Holy Spirit must never be cut off from our lives!

<!-- p46 -->
With the salvation and the indwelling of the Holy Spirit, we can abide in Christ and He can abide in us. The Spirit of God is not confined outside; He can dwell with us deeply. We can walk in fellowship and harmony with God from now into eternal life.

<!-- p47 -->
Much more could be said about the inner work of the Holy Spirit and His role in our sanctification, but that will be discussed in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p48 -->
### Furthermore—the Holy Spirit bears witness to Jesus

<!-- p49 -->
The Holy Spirit comes from God and is of God. Just as Jesus said He did nothing except what the Father showed Him (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 5:19</span>), likewise the Holy Spirit does nothing unless directed by the Father. Since the Holy Spirit is the Helper, He does not work alone but works perfectly and in harmony with the Father.

<!-- p50 -->
The Spirit bears witness to Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:26</span>), He brings praise to Jesus, and He declares and makes known what Jesus has for us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:14</span>).

<!-- p51 -->
The Holy Spirit will bear witness to Jesus, He will glorify Him, He will teach, and He will remind us of the teachings of Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:26</span>), and He will guide us into all truth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span>). Jesus told His disciples that He had much to teach them, but that they could not yet bear it; however, the Holy Spirit would guide them into all truth. This is another noteworthy point theologically. The things Jesus needed His disciples to know were conveyed in several ways. Jesus taught directly prior to His crucifixion, and also after the resurrection and before His ascension.

<!-- p52 -->
Jesus also promised that the Spirit of Truth, poured out upon them on the Day of Pentecost, would guide into all truth by divine revelation. Are there still divine revelations today? **Of course, yes!** The gifts of the Holy Spirit, including prophecy and words of knowledge and wisdom, have not ceased! However, no prophecy or word that comes from a spirit is a matter of private interpretation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2PE" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Peter 1:20</span>). This is very important. Some wrongly claim they are listening to the Holy Spirit, yet their actions and words do not bear witness to Jesus. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 4:3</span> warns against any spirit that does not bear witness to Jesus, saying that such a spirit is of the antichrist. This must be reiterated: if any spirit departs from the teachings of Christ, it is not the Holy Spirit but an unclean one!

<!-- p53 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p54 -->
> **<u>1 John 4:1</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Beloved, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits, whether they are of God; for many false prophets have gone out into the world.
>

<!-- p55 -->
</span>

<!-- p56 -->
Throughout Scripture, we see examples of false claims, often from false prophets or well-meaning people whose words run contrary to the will of God. For example, Jeremiah warned against prophets who spoke visions from their own minds, declaring peace when destruction was impending (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="23" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 23:16-17</span>). Likewise, in (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 16:16-18</span>), the words that a slave girl spoke about Paul and Silas were true, but they were inspired by a demonic spirit, not the Holy Spirit. These examples remind us that even words that may sound true can come from non-divine sources.

<!-- p57 -->
Every claim of ‘God told me!’ or a ‘new’ teaching must be measured by the tests of biblical truth. To discern accurately, we must ask:

<!-- p58 -->
- Does this align with Scripture?

<!-- p59 -->
- Does this glorify Jesus Christ?

<!-- p60 -->
- Does this produce divine fruit?

<!-- p61 -->
When we use these tests, we can distinguish the true guidance of the Holy Spirit from error or deception.

<!-- p62 -->
God customarily gives us wisdom &amp; much broad guidance (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:5</span> &amp; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="33" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 33:3</span>). God’s divine wisdom will never contradict Scripture, but it can go beyond the scope of Scripture. *entolē* *(*commands*)* are immediate instructions to be obeyed; they are *rhema* words (as discussed in the chapter on faith).

<!-- p63 -->
This wisdom, understanding, and guidance are often not in Scripture word-for-word. For example, Scripture provides good general guidance about what to look for in a spouse, but it does not give specific instructions about the right person. God has promised to guide our path, to lead us into all truth, and we can be confident that any wisdom and guidance from the Holy Spirit will never contradict the teaching of Scripture.

The Holy Spirit also empowers the mission. The Holy Spirit gives believers power to bear witness boldly under persecution so that they share the Gospel. Boldness does not mean shouting. It means having courage despite fear. Power is strength, might, and ability. The Holy Spirit provides the strength and ability to be a Gospel witness despite threats and fear of punishment.

<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

This preaching boldness is often accompanied by supernatural acts that demonstrate the Spirit and power (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 2:1-5</span>). (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:22-23</span>)

The instrument Jesus has chosen for the transformation of the earth is His church, and the Holy Spirit empowers, emboldens, and leads it.

## Building a Relationship with the Holy Spirit

<!-- p101 -->
Every relationship needs time and experience to deepen. The Holy Spirit is our defender, our comforter, our helper, and our guide. He strengthens us and sanctifies us.

<!-- p102 -->
Begin to think of the Holy Spirit as a person. In our relationship we can work together to build it, or we can do things that weaken our relationship. He can be ignored, quenched, grieved, or He can be listened to, honored, and obeyed.

<!-- p103 -->
Do not ignore the voice of the Holy Spirit. He is always speaking to us and to the church. If you do not yet know how to discern the voice of the Holy Spirit, it requires time, willingness, and effort (like all healthy, beneficial relationships). But don’t worry, you already have all the necessary components to begin developing a personal relationship with the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p104 -->
The quickest and most reliable ways to build an effective relationship with the Holy Spirit are to

<!-- p105 -->
1.  Call upon Him in a **personal** prayer, pause and wait for Him to speak.

<!-- p106 -->
2.  Call upon Him as part of a **group** of people who know Him (the church), pause, and wait for Him to speak.

<!-- p107 -->
By the word ‘call’ I mean literally, with your mouth, or however you speak (with your hands if you cannot speak out loud). Why speak? Several reasons:

<!-- p108 -->
1.  We are speaking spirits made in the image of God. God speaks, and so do we.

<!-- p109 -->
2.  We use speech as the primary means of communication. You can also write what you want to say (a journal) and read it aloud.

<!-- p110 -->
3.  Speaking is a deliberate act, and it shows the One you are speaking to that you want to have a conversation.

<!-- p111 -->
#### What should you say?

<!-- p112 -->
If you have never spoken with God, try introducing yourself. Yes, it will feel a little strange, but that discomfort is okay and temporary. As you introduce yourself, tell God that you want to talk with Him, and that you want Him to answer you as well.

<!-- p113 -->
Consider asking Him a question. It does not have to be a deep, theological question; it can be simple. Can you show me the love you have for me? Why do I have a particular feeling? What would you like to say to me?

<!-- p114 -->
#### So what’s next?

<!-- p115 -->
Well, in all effective relationships you speak a little, then pause and listen. God will speak.

<!-- p116 -->
God speaks in many ways, but He wants to do so personally, internally. Often it is not an audible voice (as if your closest friend said your name out loud) but an inner voice that speaks to your heart and your mind. Elijah calls this ‘a still, small voice’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1KI" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Kings 19:11-13</span>).

<!-- p117 -->
The most common ways I have personally seen God speaking to people—and I have been fortunate to help dozens of people learn how to speak directly with God—are a combination of some of the following:

<!-- p118 -->
- Insight - the person God is speaking to receives a sudden understanding that helps.

<!-- p119 -->
- Picture - also known in Scripture as a vision or a dream (a vision is when you are awake, a dream is when you are asleep), God will give you a picture in your mind. It might be a memory, or a place you have been or that you know? Explore the picture; what do you see? What do you feel as a result? What is happening?

<!-- p120 -->
- Inner voice - like an audible voice, but only you hear it, and it is spoken to your heart and your mind.

<!-- p121 -->
#### What will God say?

<!-- p122 -->
I’m not sure! But that’s the exciting part. I can say that He is NOT the Spirit of fear, but He is the Spirit of perfect love, and He will never violate the teaching of Scripture. For example, God will never tell you to enter into a relationship outside of marriage.

<!-- p123 -->
What’s next?

<!-- p124 -->
Keep the conversation going! It really is that simple.

### Why build a relationship?

<!-- p129 -->
The Holy Spirit is the Helper. The Helper helps us! Work goes more smoothly if we share a language and have a connection with Him. Can you imagine trying to work with a coworker without a relationship, not speaking the same language, and being unable to communicate?!

<!-- p130 -->
We serve a God who wants to communicate with us. Jesus said His sheep know His voice (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:27</span>), the natural abilities we need are already built into us, but we may be lacking in skill and experience.

<!-- p131 -->
Speaking from personal experience, an active personal relationship with the Holy Spirit is one of the best things about being a Christian. His presence brings us peace, comfort, and guidance, but only if you know how to seek, listen to, and obey Him.

<!-- p132 -->
Develop a sensitive awareness with the Holy Spirit. Like many married couples over time, you can communicate with your spouse in a special way, sometimes even with just a glance. That single look can tell you everything you need to know about what has happened and what will happen. Build that kind of sensitivity and familiarity with the Holy Spirit. Include Him in all of your life; this is a beautiful part of your relationship with Him that will give your walk of faith greater depth and richness.

<!-- p133 -->
The Holy Spirit came to lead and guide us, to empower us to become the people of God, and so that people on the earth would be seen as deeply transformed, rescued from the kingdom of darkness and brought into the kingdom of light. Getting to know Him, and building trust with Him, is essential for daily life and for carrying out the work Jesus has for us.

<!-- p134 -->
Our discussion about the work of the Holy Spirit does not end in this chapter. His work is discussed at length in other chapters of this book, especially the chapters that speak about sanctification and baptism.

## Personal Reflection Questions

<!-- p136 -->
1.  How have you encountered the Holy Spirit as your Comforter, your Guide, or your source of power? Which role fits the season of your life right now?

<!-- p137 -->
2.  Reflect on the difference between the Holy Spirit “within” (internal work) and “upon” (external work). How does this distinction affect your understanding of His work in your life?

<!-- p138 -->
3.  What steps can you take to cultivate a deeper relationship with the Holy Spirit and to recognize His voice more clearly?

<!-- p139 -->
4.  We are called to rely on the strength of the Holy Spirit so that we can step out boldly and carry out the task God has given us. What specific assignment and focus has God given you?

<!-- p140 -->
5.  The Holy Spirit leads us into truth and discernment. Are there areas of your life where you need clarity or wisdom? How can you invite Him to guide you?

## Group Discussion Questions (20-30 Minutes)

1.  **Icebreaker Question:** “When you think about the Holy Spirit, what is the first word or image that comes to mind? Why?”

<!-- p143 -->
2.  **Scripture Exploration:** Read <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:26</span>. How does the Holy Spirit’s role as Teacher and Helper encourage you in your faith journey?

<!-- p144 -->
3.  **Holy Spirit Within vs. Upon:** Discuss the difference between the Holy Spirit who is “within” for transformation and “upon” for empowerment. Why are both aspects essential for a believer?

<!-- p145 -->

<!-- p146 -->
5.  **Reflection**: Think of a time you felt the Holy Spirit empowered you to take action. How did that experience affect your faith and the people around you?
