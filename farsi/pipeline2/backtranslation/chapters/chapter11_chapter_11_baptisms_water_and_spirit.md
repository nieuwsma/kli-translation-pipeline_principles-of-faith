# Chapter 11 - Baptisms-Water and Spirit

<!-- p1 -->
Baptism in our day, apart from its Jewish roots, is usually considered only as water baptism and the baptism of the Holy Spirit (Pentecost).

<!-- p2 -->
The Book of Hebrews was written for a Jewish audience. The vast majority of Christians in our time have no Jewish roots or heritage. Without Jewish roots and culture, many of us lack the cultural background for things that first-century Messianic Jews (those Jewish believers who confessed Jesus) commonly knew. Since we were not raised in the Jewish tradition, we are usually not very aware of the feasts, fasts, prayers, washings, rites, and ceremonies that Jewish believers knew.

<!-- p3 -->
The word related to “baptisms” or “washings” in Greek is: **Baptismos** (bap-tis-mos’ - **G909**) - the act of immersing or washing. It refers to ceremonial washings or ritual purification. It can denote immersion, purification rites, or washings performed in Jewish and Christian contexts.

<!-- p4 -->
Baptisms are one of the foundational doctrines mentioned in Hebrews:

<!-- p5 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p6 -->
> **<u>Hebrews 6:1-2</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Therefore, leaving the discussion of the elementary principles of Christ, let us go on to perfection, not laying again the foundation of repentance from dead works and of faith toward God,

<!-- p8 -->
## Old Testament Washings - Historical Background

<!-- p9 -->
In the Old Testament, ceremonial washings played a central role in maintaining ritual purity and symbolizing holiness in the presence of God. These washings were prescribed in the Law of Moses and applied both to the priests who served in the tabernacle and to the people of Israel. For example, the priests were required to wash their hands and feet in the bronze basin before entering the tent of meeting or approaching the altar, which symbolized their readiness to serve in the presence of the holy God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="30" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 30:18-21</span>). Likewise, purification rites for those affected by uncleanness, such as contact with a corpse or skin diseases, involved washing with water as a sign of returning to purity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LEV" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Leviticus 14:8</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="NUM" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Numbers 19:17-19</span>). These actions were outward displays of an inward desire for holiness and emphasized the importance of being set apart for God.

<!-- p10 -->
Ceremonial washings in the Old Testament required regular repetition to maintain ritual purity. Rather than being a permanent solution, these rites provided only temporary external cleanliness and did not address the deeper issue of sin. The author of Hebrews points to this limitation, explaining that these practices were “symbolic for the present time” and were not able to cleanse the worshiper’s conscience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:9-10</span>). These washings continually reminded people of human deficiency and the need for something greater—an ultimate cleansing that would purify not only the body but also the heart and soul.

<!-- p11 -->
The ceremonial washings of the Old Testament are fulfilled in Christ, who through his sacrifice provides complete and everlasting cleansing. Unlike the temporary rites of the Law, the blood of Christ fully purifies and washes not only the body but also the conscience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:13-14</span>). Through his death and resurrection, Jesus accomplished what the Old Testament rites only foreshadowed—true reconciliation with God. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:22</span> calls believers to “draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, having our hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience and our bodies washed with pure water.” This language links Christ’s work to the imagery of Old Testament washings while showing its far superior effectiveness. His sacrifice not only covers sin but removes it entirely (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 1:7</span>). Thus, the ceremonial washings were a shadow pointing to the deeper cleansing that only Christ could provide, meeting humanity’s need for holiness through divine intervention.

<!-- p12 -->
To emphasize this point, Christians are under no obligation to perform any ceremonial washing. The washings in the Old Testament did not save, and they do not save in the New Testament either. Ceremonial washing neither elevates our standing before God nor brings us favor. Paul sharply rebukes the Judaizers of Galatia (read Galatians) who tried to blend Jewish-background requirements as necessary conditions for salvation (such as circumcision). Salvation is only through faith in Jesus!

<!-- p13 -->
## John’s Baptism

<!-- p14 -->
Several baptisms are mentioned in the New Testament canon. We have already examined the baptisms and washings of the Jewish tradition.

<!-- p15 -->
John the Baptist’s ministry focused on preparing the way for the coming of Jesus Christ. His baptism, described as a baptism of repentance for the forgiveness of sins (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 1:4</span>), called people to confess their sins, turn their hearts back to God, and prepare for the coming of the Messiah. This baptism was an outward sign of inward repentance, symbolizing the desire for spiritual cleansing and readiness for the kingdom of God. But John’s baptism was temporary and preparatory—he pointed to a greater work that the Messiah would do. While John’s baptism emphasized repentance, it did not provide full forgiveness or spiritual new birth, because it lacked the indwelling and empowering of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:11-12</span>). Instead, John openly declared that someone greater than he was coming who would baptize not only with water but with the Holy Spirit and fire (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 3:16</span>).

<!-- p16 -->
John’s baptism was fulfilled and surpassed through the death, burial, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. Christian baptism, instituted by Jesus and carried out by the apostles, signifies a deeper reality—union with Christ, death to sin, and resurrection to new life (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 6:3-4</span>). Unlike John’s baptism, Christian baptism is directly connected to faith in Jesus’ atoning sacrifice and the indwelling of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:38</span>). The coming of the Holy Spirit on the day of Pentecost marked the New Covenant and the beginning of Spirit-empowered life—something John’s baptism could not provide. Therefore, John’s baptism is no longer practiced, because it has been replaced by the baptism that Christ commanded (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19</span>), a baptism that encompasses repentance, faith, and the sealing of believers by the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>).

<!-- p17 -->
## The Baptism of Christ

<!-- p18 -->
The baptism of Christ left a powerful pattern for his followers and laid the foundation for the Christian practice of water baptism. Although Jesus was sinless, he submitted to be baptized by John to “fulfill all righteousness” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:15</span>). By doing this, he identified himself with humanity’s need for repentance and pointed to his own death, burial, and resurrection. At his baptism, the Holy Spirit descended like a dove, and the Father declared, “This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:16-17</span>). This moment marked the beginning of Jesus’ public ministry and revealed the unity of the Father, the Son, and the Spirit, foreshadowing the spiritual transformation believers would experience through faith in Christ.

<!-- p19 -->
Jesus later commanded his followers to be baptized and commissioned them to “go and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19</span>). Water baptism, instituted by Christ, became an **act of obedience** and a **public profession of faith** for believers. This act displays their identification with the death, burial, and resurrection of Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 6:3-4</span>). Immersion in water symbolizes dying to sin, and coming up out of the water pictures rising to new life in Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:12</span>). However, it is important to clarify that this outward act is not the source of salvation, but rather a **visible sign** of the **inward work of grace** already accomplished by the blood of Jesus.

<!-- p20 -->
Water baptism is a **symbol** of the cleansing power of the blood of Christ and the renewal of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:26</span>؛ <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>). This baptism implies repentance, faith, and a commitment to follow Jesus. It is an outward testimony of inward transformation-a way for believers to publicly declare their allegiance to Christ and their participation in the body of Christ, that is, the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:41</span>). Although this baptism is commanded by Jesus and follows the pattern of the early church, it does not bring about **salvation**. Salvation is by grace alone and through faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>), and true cleansing takes place spiritually through the blood of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 1:5</span>). Water baptism is a response to that salvation; an act of obedience, not a condition for it.

<!-- p21 -->
In the New Testament, water baptism is consistently tied to **repentance and faith** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:38</span>؛ <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 16:16</span>), both of which require a personal decision to follow Christ—something infants are not able to make. Baptism symbolizes the believer’s conscious identification with the death, burial, and resurrection of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 6:3-4</span>), as well as his commitment to live in obedience to Him. Some denominations (such as Catholics and Lutherans) practice infant baptism as a covenant sign similar to circumcision, but since infants cannot express personal faith or repentance, the practice of **infant baptism** is not consistent with the biblical pattern of **believer’s baptism**. Instead, many churches hold **dedication ceremonies** for infants, following the example of Hannah, who dedicated Samuel to the Lord (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1SA" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Samuel 1:27-28</span>), and also Mary and Joseph, who presented Jesus at the temple (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 2:22</span>). Dedication is not a substitute for baptism, but rather a covenant and commitment of the parents to raise their child in the faith, to teach the Word of God to the child, and to model a godly life until the child reaches an age at which he or she can make a personal decision to follow Christ and be baptized.

<!-- p22 -->
Unlike the **ceremonial washings** of the Old Covenant that required repeated rites for purification, Christian baptism is a one-time event that reflects the finished work of grace. The washings of the Old Covenant symbolized outward purity and foreshadowed the deeper spiritual cleansing that comes through Christ. In contrast, New Covenant baptism symbolizes inner renewal and new birth accomplished by the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>). This baptism points to the believer’s participation in the New Covenant, where cleansing from sin is permanent and founded on the finished work of Christ.

<!-- p23 -->
Water baptism is closely linked to the **promise of the Holy Spirit**. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:38</span>, Peter calls the people to repent, be baptized, and receive the gift of the Holy Spirit. Although water baptism publicly displays the believer’s repentance and faith, the true baptism happens within, when the Holy Spirit washes, gives new birth, and seals the believer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:13</span>). This spiritual washing makes us members of the body of Christ and empowers us for holy living. The **role of the Holy Spirit** in baptism reminds us that salvation is not obtained by rituals, but is accomplished through the transforming power of God.

<!-- p24 -->
It is important to distinguish between the **symbolic act of water baptism** and the spiritual reality of salvation through the blood of Christ. Scripture clearly teaches that salvation is based on faith in Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 10:9-10</span>) and not on works, including baptism (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>). Nevertheless, water baptism is **an act of obedience** that publicly identifies the believer with Christ and the church. This act shows an outward commitment to live as a disciple of Jesus, but it adds nothing to the work of salvation and does not complete it.

<!-- p25 -->
Christ’s water baptism set an example for believers to follow and demonstrated humility, obedience, and readiness for service. For Christians today, water baptism is a public declaration of faith that symbolizes dying to sin and rising to new life. Although it is commanded by Jesus and is an important step of obedience, it is not a condition for salvation. True cleansing is obtained through faith in the blood of Jesus, and empowerment for the Christian life comes from the work of the Holy Spirit in the believer. Baptism unites us with Christ and His church and reminds us of the inner transformation and renewal that God has already accomplished.

<!-- p26 -->
After water baptism has been established as an outward sign of faith, we now turn to the baptism of the Holy Spirit, which empowers believers for service and ministry.

<!-- p27 -->
## Baptism of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p28 -->
Baptism in the Holy Spirit is a profound and transformative experience that God has promised to empower believers for service, ministry, and holy living. Every believer receives the Holy Spirit when they are born again-this is what makes us spiritually alive. But later, we should also receive baptism in the Holy Spirit; a baptism that empowers us for boldness, spiritual gifts, and effective ministry.

<!-- p29 -->
Water baptism is a public declaration of faith and a symbol of cleansing from sin by the blood of Christ; the baptism of the Spirit represents the empowering presence of God in the believer’s life. Together, these two kinds of baptism shape complementary dimensions of the Christian walk-one as an outward sign of obedience and the other as an inward source of power and renewal. Baptism in the Holy Spirit means the Spirit being upon us, not merely within us. In salvation, all believers receive the Spirit within, but when the Spirit of God comes upon them, they receive power and boldness for service.

<!-- p30 -->
Baptism in the Holy Spirit was prophesied by John the Baptist; he said,

<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p32 -->
> **<u>Matthew 3:11</u>** (NKJV)
>
> I baptize you with water for repentance, but the one who comes after me is mightier than I, whose sandals I am not worthy to carry. He will baptize you with the Holy Spirit and fire.
>

<!-- p33 -->
</span>

<!-- p34 -->
This promise pointed to a greater work that Jesus would do after His resurrection—sending the Holy Spirit to dwell within His followers and empower them. Jesus affirmed this when He told His disciples to wait for the gift of the Spirit and explained that they would receive power to be His witnesses (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>). The fulfillment of this promise took place on the Day of Pentecost; when the disciples were filled with the Holy Spirit, and speaking in tongues, prophecy, and boldness in proclaiming the gospel were among the results (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:1-4</span>).

<!-- p35 -->
This outpouring marked the beginning of the **Church Age** and revealed that the power of the Spirit is now available to all believers. This promise was not limited to the apostles, but, as Peter declared, extends to all who believe; *«This promise is for you and your children and for all who are far off-for everyone whom the Lord our God calls»* (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="39" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:39</span>).

<!-- p36 -->
### The Spirit within versus the Spirit upon us

<!-- p37 -->
When someone places their faith in Jesus, the Holy Spirit gives them new life within. This is what being **born again** means. At that very moment of faith, the Holy Spirit comes to dwell within the believer, makes them spiritually alive, and receives them into the family of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:5-6</span>؛ <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>). This is the new birth or being born again, as Jesus described in John 3.

<!-- p38 -->
Biblical basis:

<!-- p39 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>* - «He saved us, not according to works that we had done in righteousness, but because of the washing of regeneration and renewal of the Holy Spirit.»

<!-- p40 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>* - The Holy Spirit seals believers as the **guarantee** of their inheritance.

<!-- p41 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:9</span>* - "Anyone who does not have the Spirit of Christ is not His."

<!-- p42 -->
This act of new birth is common to all believers at the time of salvation and establishes the permanent indwelling of the Spirit. It is not dependent on any outward act, such as water baptism, and guarantees salvation, which is by grace and through faith.

<!-- p43 -->
In contrast, baptism in the Holy Spirit is a separate, subsequent experience that equips believers with power for service and witness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>). While the new birth deals with a new life and identity, Spirit baptism focuses on empowerment **upon** and the release of spiritual gifts. This baptism is often accompanied by signs and manifestations of the gifts of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p44 -->
Biblical basis:

<!-- p45 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p46 -->
> **<u>Acts 1:8</u>** (NKJV)
>
> But when the Holy Spirit comes upon you, you will receive power؛ and in Jerusalem and in all Judea and Samaria and to the ends of the earth, you will be My witnesses."
>

<!-- p47 -->
</span>

<!-- p48 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:15-17</span>* - The Samaritans had believed and been baptized, but later received the Holy Spirit through prayer and the laying on of hands.

<!-- p49 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 19:6</span>* - The disciples in Ephesus, who were already believers, received the Holy Spirit when Paul laid his hands on them.

<!-- p50 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit does not replace the new birth, but builds upon it and equips the believer for spiritual ministry and boldness in service. Baptism in the Holy Spirit is a free gift for the saved, not a condition for salvation. It is not necessary for someone to manifest a <u>specific</u> gift of the Holy Spirit to prove their «baptism» in the Holy Spirit, but we should expect the baptism of the Holy Spirit to appear today just as it did in the Book of Acts, with signs and accompanied by boldness! Some mistakenly think that if someone does not speak in tongues, they are not saved or do not have the baptism of the Holy Spirit. Not everyone will receive all the gifts or all the manifestations of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29-31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:29-31</span>).

<!-- p51 -->
## How to Receive the Baptism of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p52 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit is described in Scripture as a gift promised to believers and received by faith. This is distinct from salvation and is primarily for empowerment and witness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>). The baptism of the Holy Spirit is an empowering gift for believers that was promised by Jesus and fulfilled at Pentecost. This gift is received through repentance, faith, and asking God. Although the laying on of hands is often used as a means, ultimately it is God who baptizes with the Spirit.

<!-- p53 -->
Scripture presents speaking in tongues and prophecy as a common sign, but the purpose of this baptism is far broader—providing boldness, power, and spiritual gifts for ministry. Every believer is encouraged to seek this experience as part of their spiritual journey, trusting that God will equip them for His work.

<!-- p54 -->
Scripture outlines several core principles regarding how to receive this baptism:

<!-- p55 -->
### **1. The baptism of the Holy Spirit is a free gift from God**

<!-- p56 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit is not something believers can earn—this is a free gift given by God. God wants to give good gifts to His children and is eager to give the Holy Spirit to those who ask with faith and a desire for empowerment.

<!-- p57 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p58 -->
> **<u>Luke 11:13</u>** (NKJV)
>
> So if you, being evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will the heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to those who ask Him!"
>

<!-- p60 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="38-39" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p61 -->
> **<u>Acts 2:38-39</u>** (NKJV)
>

###  **2. Ask, seek, and receive by faith**

<!-- p64 -->
The Bible emphasizes that baptism in the Holy Spirit is received through asking in prayer and faith-filled expectation. Faith, together with a readiness to ask and receive, is the key to experiencing the baptism of the Spirit. If you are not sure that God wants to give you the baptism of the Holy Spirit, ask Him about it; He is a good Father! When He stirs you, you may feel a tangible presence of God in your body that you have never experienced before. If you do not feel it, that is fine! God is great, and we do not need to confine Him to a perfect formula. If you feel God drawing your heart, surrender to Him!

## Gifts of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p79 -->
When the Holy Spirit was poured out, and people were baptized in the Holy Spirit, manifestations of the Holy Spirit often accompanied it.

<!-- p80 -->
In the New Testament there are several lists of gifts:

<!-- p81 -->
- Motivational gifts given by God: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:6-8</span>

<!-- p82 -->
- They are discussed in the chapter on the church.

<!-- p83 -->
- The fivefold ministry gifts given by Jesus: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-13</span>

<!-- p84 -->
- They are discussed in the chapter on the church.

<!-- p85 -->
- The manifestation gifts given by the Holy Spirit: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span>

<!-- p86 -->
- They are examined here.

<!-- p87 -->
Each of these sets of gifts is given for different purposes and operates in different ways, but together they point to the **diversity** and **unity** of gifts in the **body of Christ**. Each gift has a unique role in fulfilling God's purposes.

<!-- p88 -->
### Purpose and functions of the gifts

<!-- p89 -->
The primary purpose of the gifts of the Holy Spirit is the edification of the body of Christ and bringing glory to God:

<!-- p90 -->
- **Edification** - strengthening and encouraging believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
- **Unity** - promoting harmony and cooperation within the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:12-13</span>).

<!-- p92 -->
- **Evangelism** - demonstrating God's power as a testimony to unbelievers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="43" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:43</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:6-7</span>).

<!-- p93 -->
- **Service** - meeting the needs of others through acts of mercy and generosity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:6-8</span>).

<!-- p94 -->
These gifts are not for personal glory, but for the common good (1 Corinthians 12:7). They function best when exercised with love and humility and reflect the character of Christ (1 Corinthians 13).

<!-- p95 -->
Paul emphasized that spiritual gifts should be exercised decently and in order (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:40</span>). This ensures that the use of the gifts results in unity, peace, and understanding rather than confusion or division.

<!-- p96 -->
- Prophecies should be evaluated (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:29</span>).

<!-- p97 -->
- When a message in tongues is presented to the body, pray that it may be interpreted (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:27-28</span>).

<!-- p98 -->
- Gifts must always result in the **edification** of the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:12</span>).

<!-- p99 -->
This order does not quench the Spirit, but ensures that the gifts operate in a way that **honors God** and **benefits others**.

<!-- p100 -->
The operation of spiritual gifts must always be grounded in love. Paul devotes an entire chapter (1 Corinthians 13) to explaining that without love, even the greatest spiritual gifts are meaningless. Love provides the motivation and framework to use the gifts in a way that builds others up rather than tearing them down.

<!-- p101 -->
Moreover, the gifts operate according to the will of the Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:11</span>). They cannot be produced or controlled by human effort—sovereignly bestowed and empowered by the Holy Spirit, often in moments of need or ministry. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 13:8</span> says that ultimately prophecy and tongues will cease. Theologically, those who believe the gifts of the Holy Spirit have ceased are called “cessationists.” <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 13:10</span> says “when the perfect \[Jesus\] comes, that which is partial will cease.” Since Jesus has not yet returned for His Church, the gifts are still at work on the earth.

<!-- p102 -->
It is very important to recognize that the gifts of the Spirit should not be separated from the fruit of the Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-23</span>). While the gifts display power, the fruit reveals character—love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control.

<!-- p103 -->
- Gifts without fruit: A believer can operate in the gifts but lack maturity or character, which leads to pride, abuse, or harm.

<!-- p104 -->
- Fruit without gifts: A believer may have godly character but lack the empowerment needed for effective ministry.

<!-- p105 -->
Both are necessary. The gifts equip believers for ministry. The fruit is the sign of their spiritual health (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 7:16</span>) and provides ‘a pleasant taste’ for those who partake of it.

<!-- p106 -->
### Explanation of the 9 manifestation gifts of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p107 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span> lists the 9 manifestation gifts of the Holy Spirit:

<!-- p108 -->
1.  **Word of wisdom** — A supernatural insight into God’s purposes or direction for a situation. This gift applies divine wisdom to complex matters or decisions (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:10</span>).

<!-- p109 -->
2.  **Word of knowledge** — A supernatural revelation of information about a person, event, or circumstance that could not have been known naturally (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 4:17-18</span>).

<!-- p110 -->
3.  **Faith** — The supernatural ability to trust God for the miraculous, even in impossible situations. This gift inspires boldness and confidence (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 3:6-7</span>).

<!-- p111 -->
4.  **Gifts of healings** — The supernatural power to heal physical, emotional, or spiritual ailments and restore wholeness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 3:6-8</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 9:17-18</span>).

<!-- p112 -->
5.  **Working of miracles** — The ability to perform supernatural acts that defy natural laws, such as multiplying food or raising the dead (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40-41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 9:40-41</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 2:9-11</span>).

<!-- p113 -->
6.  **Prophecy** — Declaring God’s truth or will under the inspiration of the Holy Spirit. It should be encouraging, edifying, and comforting (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>).

<!-- p114 -->
7.  **Discernment (distinguishing) between spirits** — The ability to determine whether a spiritual influence is from God, the enemy, or a human source (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 16:16-18</span>).

<!-- p115 -->
8.  **Various kinds of tongues** — Speaking in languages unknown to the speaker, often as an act of worship, prayer, or a sign to unbelievers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:4</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:22</span>).

<!-- p116 -->
9.  **Interpretation of tongues** — The supernatural ability to interpret a message given in tongues so that the church may be edified (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:27</span>).

<!-- p117 -->
Paul makes it clear that all the gifts are valuable and should work together for the common good of the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:7</span>). However, he also urges believers to eagerly desire the greater gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:31</span>). The greater gifts are often understood to be those that more directly edify the church, such as prophecy (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:1-5</span>).

<!-- p118 -->
- Prophecy is especially emphasized because it strengthens, encourages, and comforts others (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>). Paul teaches believers to earnestly desire this gift because it promotes clarity and understanding in the church.

<!-- p119 -->
- Gifts like tongues are highly valuable for personal edification and prayer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:4</span>), but Paul teaches that when a message in tongues is given to the church, they should pray for interpretation to ensure that the church is edified (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:27-28</span>).

<!-- p120 -->
While the Holy Spirit gives the gifts, we must be surrendered and obedient to the Holy Spirit so that He may employ them. Like muscles and talents, as we practice yielding to the Holy Spirit in the exercise of His gifts, we become more confident and dependable in using them.
