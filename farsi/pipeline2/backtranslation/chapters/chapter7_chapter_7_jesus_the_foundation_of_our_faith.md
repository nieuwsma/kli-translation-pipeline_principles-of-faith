# Chapter 7 - Jesus-Foundation of Our Faith

<!-- p1 -->
Building on our study of Jesus as the Messiah, in this chapter we continue to examine the life, person, and work of Jesus. We will see that Jesus is the mediator of a new and better covenant—and that he perfectly fulfills the roles of prophet, priest, and king. It is in this New Covenant that we as believers are placed in a favorable standing before God and move from a covenant based on works to a covenant based on trust (faith). Finally, as a summary of our study of Jesus, we will read and examine the Apostles’ Creed.

<!-- p2 -->
## Mediator of the New Covenant

<!-- p3 -->
The concept of «mediator» refers to an intermediary who bridges the gap between two parties and facilitates a relationship or agreement. In the story of the Exodus, Moses is the first mediator of the covenant that God established with his people. Moses received the Law, offered sacrifice (priest), called God’s people to obedience (prophet), and judged them (judge/king). Throughout the Old Testament after Moses, mediating roles were carried out through the office of the prophet, the Levitical priesthood, and then the judges and later the kings of the people.

<!-- p4 -->
Mediating roles in the Old Testament:

<!-- p5 -->
1.  **Prophet:** Prophets were God’s envoys, sent to speak his heart to the people and to restore them when they strayed. Moses, for example, served as a prophet who, by receiving the Law from God and teaching it to Israel, mediated the Old Covenant. The prophets also foretold the coming of the Messiah and the ultimate redemption of humanity. Other prominent examples of prophets include: Isaiah, Jeremiah, Elijah, Elisha, Samuel.

<!-- p6 -->
2.  **Levitical priesthood:** The priests served as mediators by offering sacrifices for the atonement of sins and interceding for the people before God. Especially on the Day of Atonement, the high priest had a vital role; he entered the Holy of Holies to sprinkle blood on the “mercy seat,” which symbolized reconciliation with God. The priest’s ministry is intercession for the people.

<!-- p7 -->
3.  **Judge/King:** The judges, and later the kings, were charged with ruling God’s people, enforcing his laws, judging among the people, delivering them from enemies, and leading them in the way of righteousness. The book of Judges recounts the stories of these leaders—people like Deborah, Samson, and Gideon—whom God raised up to save and guide Israel. Israel later had a line of kings; many wicked and some good (see First and Second Kings, First and Second Samuel, and 1<sup>st</sup> and 2<sup>nd</sup> Chronicles). Some prominent examples include Saul, David, and Solomon.

<!-- p8 -->
It is worth noting that all the mediators of the Old Testament were entirely human and not divine. Because they were human, they were capable of sinning and often fell into sin. Some of these mediators were sinners and led the people away from God into impure worship: false prophets, lying and corrupt priests, and evil kings.

<!-- p9 -->
### Christ as the Mediator of the New Covenant

<!-- p10 -->
The death, resurrection, and ascension of Jesus fulfill the Old Covenant, and establish the New Covenant.

<!-- p11 -->
The New Covenant is a transformative relationship between God and his people, established through the life, death, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. Unlike the Old Covenant, which focused on external laws, the New Covenant emphasizes internal transformation and grace. This covenant began at the Last Supper, where Jesus declared that his blood establishes this covenant and replaces the repeated animal sacrifices of the Old Covenant with his once-for-all atonement (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 22:20</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:10</span>). Through this sacrifice, the New Covenant provides complete forgiveness and cleansing from sin and meets the condition for full and permanent reconciliation with God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 31:34</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:12-18</span>).

<!-- p12 -->
One of the central features of the New Covenant is its internal nature; for God writes his law on the hearts of his people and, through the Holy Spirit, brings about a heart-level relationship with himself (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 31:33</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 3:3</span>). This change enables believers to do God’s will not merely by outward conformity to external commands, but by the empowering of the Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:1-4</span>). The New Covenant also grants direct access to God and removes the need for an earthly priesthood. Jesus is the eternal High Priest, and believers are called a “royal priesthood”; they can, by virtue of Christ’s sacrifice, draw near to God’s presence with confidence (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 7:24-25</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:19-20</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:9</span>).

<!-- p13 -->
Unlike the Old Covenant which was specific to Israel, the New Covenant is universal and extends to all who believe in Christ—regardless of ethnicity or background (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 3:28</span>). This covenant is entirely based on grace and offers salvation and forgiveness freely through faith in Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>). This covenant is not maintained by human effort, but by the empowering of the Holy Spirit, who transforms and enables believers to live in obedience and reflect the character of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EZK" data-chapter="36" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ezekiel 36:26-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:9-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-23</span>). Moreover, the New Covenant is everlasting and surpasses the temporary and conditional nature of the Old Covenant (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="32" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 32:40</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:13</span>). This covenant represents the ultimate fulfillment of God’s redemptive plan, reconciling humanity to himself and enabling believers to live forever as his people under his reign.

<!-- p14 -->
Jesus is explicitly presented as the mediator of the New Covenant in

<!-- p15 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p16 -->
> **<u>Hebrews 9:15</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And for this reason He is the Mediator of the new covenant, by means of death, for the redemption of the transgressions under the first covenant, that those who are called may receive the promise of the eternal inheritance.
>

<!-- p17 -->
</span>

<!-- p18 -->
Through his atoning death and resurrection, Christ inaugurates this new covenant and replaces the old system of sacrifices and external regulations with a covenant written on the hearts of believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 31:31-34</span>). He stands between God and humanity; he is not only the intercessor of his people, but also provides the means of reconciliation through his blood (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:12</span>). Unlike the mediators of the Old Covenant, Jesus is sinless, eternal, and fully able to meet the divine requirements of justice and mercy and to guarantee a lasting relationship between God and believers.

<!-- p19 -->
Jesus fulfills and perfects these roles

<!-- p20 -->
1.  **Prophet:** Jesus is the ultimate Prophet; not merely the bearer of God’s word, but the Word who became flesh (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:14</span>). He fully and perfectly reveals God’s character, will, and plan of salvation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 1:1-2</span>). His teachings, miracles, and prophetic declarations fulfill the prophecies of the Old Testament and provide the foundation of the New Covenant.

<!-- p21 -->
2.  **Priest:** Jesus is the perfect High Priest who offered himself once for all as a sacrifice for sin (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 7:27</span>). Unlike the priests of the Old Testament who had to offer sacrifices repeatedly for their own sins and for the people, Jesus, since he was sinless, offered his own blood to secure eternal redemption (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:14</span>).

<!-- p22 -->
3.  **King &amp; Judge:** Jesus is the eternal King who reigns in righteousness and justice. As the Son of David, he fulfills the promise of an everlasting kingdom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2SA" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Samuel 7:16</span>). His kingdom is not merely earthly but cosmic; he rules over all creation and establishes peace through his victory over sin and death (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 2:9-11</span>). Jesus will return as King and Judge and will judge «both the living and the dead» (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 10:42</span>).

<!-- p23 -->
In Christ, the roles of prophet, priest, and king meet and are fully realized. As the mediator of the New Covenant, he bridges the gap between the holy God and sinful humanity and empowers believers to come to God with confidence (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 4:16</span>) and guarantees the promised eternal inheritance in him.

## The Apostles’ Creed

<!-- p25 -->
The Apostles’ Creed is not found in Scripture; this creed was composed and refined based on the earliest teachings of the church. A creed is a formal confession of beliefs; think of it as an oath or covenant alongside a statement of belief. Creeds are important because they are used to unify and also to distinguish those who believe in a particular way from those who do not. In the United States, it is customary in civic ceremonies such as city council meetings or legislative gatherings for those present to recite the Pledge of Allegiance. This pledge is widely used in the United States, but it is not used in other countries, because the citizens of those countries are not committed to allegiance to the United States. The Pledge of Allegiance is not what makes someone “American,” but for citizens it represents a fundamental and important belief.

<!-- p26 -->
The earliest identifiable versions of this creed were written in the fourth century AD. This creed has changed slightly over the centuries and, depending on the denomination, has minor differences. Not all Christian denominations use this creed, but overall it has been widely accepted throughout Christianity in one form or another.

<!-- p27 -->
For centuries, the Apostles’ Creed has been used as a tool for teaching and catechesis and conveys the most fundamental truths of the Christian faith; that is, the basic beliefs that must be accepted and held in order to be a Christian. Some denominations have a formal catechetical process by which a new believer joins that particular denomination and is instructed in its beliefs.

<!-- p28 -->
To come to God and receive salvation, a person does not need to fully understand these beliefs; salvation is through faith, not a doctrinal exam! However, in the continuance of that faith, all of us must come to understand these foundational truths.

## A brief explanation of the Apostles’ Creed

<!-- p31 -->
The foundational truths contained in the Apostles’ Creed are many and yet profound; a full exposition would surely require several volumes. Although this chapter is about Jesus, many topics also pertain to the other members of the Godhead. This explanation will be brief and will focus only on the most central topics. Several such topics are examined in greater depth in other chapters of this book.

<!-- p32 -->
#### *I believe in God, the Father Almighty, Creator of heaven and earth.*

<!-- p33 -->
This statement declares faith in God as the sovereign Creator and Father. The title “Father” emphasizes God’s loving relationship with his creation, especially his children through faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:12</span>). As Creator, he brought the world into being by his word and brought order out of chaos (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 1:1-3</span>). His almighty power is evident in the vastness of creation, and his fatherly care is seen in his ongoing provision and governance. This foundational declaration presents God as the source and ruler of all life, who is worthy of worship and trust.

<!-- p34 -->
#### *I believe in Jesus Christ, his only Son, our Lord.*

<!-- p35 -->
Jesus Christ is the only Son of God, eternal and begotten, not created, and shares in the divine nature (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:1-2</span>, 14). Jesus has been the “Word” from the beginning and became flesh and dwelt among us. As the “Christ,” he is the promised Messiah who fulfills the Old Testament promises about the Savior. The title “Lord” affirms his authority over all creation and his rightful place as ruler and king (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 2:10-11</span>). He is not only Lord, but the Lord who saves (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 16:31</span>).

<!-- p36 -->
Jesus was not merely sent by God; he is part of God’s own essence. Jesus is in the Father and the Father in Jesus. The Gospel of John repeatedly emphasizes that Jesus and the Father are one (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="30" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:30</span>), that Jesus did not act on his own (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 5:19</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="49-50" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 12:49-50</span>), and that everything he did was to glorify his Father (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="17" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 17:4</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:13</span>) and was done by the authority and power of his Father (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 8:28-29</span>).

<!-- p37 -->
#### *He was conceived by the power of the Holy Spirit and born of the Virgin Mary.*

<!-- p38 -->
This statement affirms the miraculous conception of Jesus through the Holy Spirit and emphasizes his divine origin. Born of the Virgin Mary, Jesus fulfills the prophecy of <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 7:14</span> that foretold the virgin birth of the Messiah. This event is the Incarnation; where the eternal Word of God became human (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:14</span>). Being fully God and fully human, Jesus bridges the gap between God and humanity and makes him the perfect mediator and Savior.

<!-- p39 -->
Jesus was conceived of the seed of the Holy Spirit and did not inherit the sinful nature that we—who are born of the seed of men—have inherited. Jesus was tempted and tested in every way, but was perfect and without sin. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 4:15</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 5:21</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:22</span>.

<!-- p40 -->
#### *He suffered under Pontius Pilate, was crucified, died, and was buried.*

<!-- p41 -->
The sufferings and death of Jesus were real and historical events that occurred during the time of the Roman governor، Pontius Pilate، and they ground the Christian faith in a history that can be examined. His crucifixion fulfilled Old Testament prophecies، such as <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="53" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 53:5</span>، which speaks of the suffering servant who would bear the sins of many. The death of Jesus was sacrificial and he took upon himself the penalty of sin in place of humanity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:24</span>). His burial confirms the reality of his death and sets the stage for the significance of the resurrection.

<!-- p42 -->
#### *He descended to the realm of the dead.*

<!-- p43 -->
After his death، Jesus descended to the realm of the dead, which Scripture often refers to as *Hades* or *Sheol*. This act shows that he fully identified with humanity، even in death، and displays his victory over sin، death، and the devil (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 3:18-20</span>). By descending، he proclaimed his victory and the completion of his saving work.

<!-- p44 -->
#### *He rose again on the third day.*

<!-- p45 -->
The resurrection of Jesus is the cornerstone of our Christian faith and confirms his divinity and the truth of his teachings. By rising from the dead، he triumphed over sin and death and provided believers with the assurance of eternal life (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 15:17-22</span>). The resurrection also fulfills Scripture, including Jesus’ own predictions (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 16:21</span>). This miraculous event turned the disciples’ despair into bold faith and formed the foundation of the early church’s witness.

<!-- p46 -->
#### *He ascended into heaven، and is seated at the right hand of the Father.*

<!-- p47 -->
The ascension of Jesus signifies his return in glory to the presence of the Father. Seated at the Father’s right hand (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:20</span>)، he assumes the role of our eternal High Priest and intercedes for believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 7:25</span>). His position of authority also indicates his supremacy as King؛ he reigns over all creation and directs the church’s mission (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:22</span>) to do greater works (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:12</span>) and to fulfill the great commission he entrusted to us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:18-20</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 16:15-18</span>). The ascension assures believers of his continuing presence and power through the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p48 -->
#### *He will come again to judge the living and the dead.*

<!-- p49 -->
This refers to Christ’s future return, when he will bring final justice and restoration. Jesus will judge all people، reward the faithful، and bring justice for the unrepentant (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 4:1</span>). His return will fulfill God’s ultimate plan for creation and establish his eternal kingdom of righteousness and peace. This hope encourages believers to live faithfully and eagerly, knowing that their labor in the Lord is not in vain (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="58" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 15:58</span>).

<!-- p50 -->
#### *I believe in the Holy Spirit.*

<!-- p51 -->
The Holy Spirit, the third person of the Trinity, is the presence and power of God active in the world today. He convicts of sin، gives new birth to hearts، and empowers believers to live holy lives (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:8-13</span>). The Spirit unites Christians as one body, that is, the church, and equips them with spiritual gifts for service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:4-11</span>). He is the Comforter, Advocate, and Guide, and he dwells within believers as a seal of their eternal inheritance (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>).

<!-- p52 -->
#### *The holy catholic Church, the communion of saints.*

<!-- p53 -->
The phrase "the holy catholic Church" refers to the global body of Christ that includes all believers across time and around the world. The church is set apart for God’s purposes، to reflect his holiness and proclaim the gospel. "the communion of saints" emphasizes the unity of all Christians, living and departed, as members of the body of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:4-6</span>). This fellowship goes beyond earthly divisions and shows the shared hope and mission of believers.

<!-- p54 -->
Jesus is the head of the church. The church is called both «the Bride of Christ» (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="25-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:25-27</span>) and «the body of Christ» (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 1:18</span>).

<!-- p55 -->
#### *The forgiveness of sins.*

<!-- p56 -->
This highlights the heart of the gospel: God, through the sacrificial death of Jesus, offers complete forgiveness of sins to all who repent and believe (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:7</span>). This forgiveness restores the broken relationship between humanity and God and removes guilt and shame. It also calls believers to extend forgiveness to others and reflect the grace they have found (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 6:14-15</span>).

<!-- p57 -->
#### *The resurrection of the body.*

<!-- p58 -->
Like Jesus, we will experience a bodily resurrection at the end of time. This resurrection will transform mortal and perishable bodies into glorified and imperishable bodies suited for eternal life (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42-44" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 15:42-44</span>). This highlights the Christian hope of restoration, not only for individuals but for all creation؛ for God is making all things new (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="21" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 21:4-5</span>).

<!-- p59 -->
#### *And life everlasting. Amen.*

<!-- p60 -->
Eternal life with God is the highest promise of the Christian faith. This life begins now through a relationship with Christ and will be fully realized in the new heaven and new earth, where believers will dwell with God forever (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:16</span>؛ <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 22:1-5</span>). This final affirmation reflects the joy and assurance of salvation and is an echo of the trust and hope expressed throughout the Apostles’ Creed.

<!-- p61 -->
## Conclusion

<!-- p62 -->
In the foundational truths articulated in the Apostles’ Creed, we see the life and work of Jesus at the center of God’s plan for humanity. Each statement in the Creed portrays key aspects of our faith in God, and who Jesus is-deity, humanity, his mission, and his ultimate victory. These truths point to his unique role as the mediator of the New Covenant. As mediator he invites us to draw near to God with confidence and to trust in his perfect sacrifice and ongoing intercession. This calls us to worship him as the rightful King and to follow his teachings as the final Prophet. Ultimately, the study of Jesus leads to a deeper relationship with him and transforms us into those who live in the light of his grace and truth.

<!-- p63 -->
Understanding who Jesus is and what he has done is the foundation of the Christian faith. The many names, titles, and roles of Jesus illuminate various facets of his person and mission and provide a fuller picture of his love and authority.

<!-- p64 -->
Just as in our study about God, Scripture is filled with various names that refer to Jesus. Each name represents a specific aspect of the whole of his character, ministry, and role. These names are given to us to reveal a dimension of Jesus.
