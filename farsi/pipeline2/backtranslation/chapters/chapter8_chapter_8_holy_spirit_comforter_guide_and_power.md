# Chapter 8 - The Holy Spirit—the Comforter, Guide, and Power

<!-- p1 -->
The Holy Spirit is not a newly arisen spirit, but is from God and has been with God from the very beginning. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 1:2</span>, the Spirit of God was moving over the waters at creation. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="NEH" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Nehemiah 9:20</span>, the Word says that it was this Spirit who taught the people in the wilderness. It was by this same Holy Spirit that God anointed kings, judges, and prophets in the Old Testament. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JOL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Joel 2:28–29</span> it is promised that the Spirit will be poured out on all flesh. It was by this same Holy Spirit that God raised Jesus from the dead, and He now dwells in us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:11</span>). There is only one Holy Spirit, and He has always been the same. The way He has worked with people has seemed different at various times, but His heart has never changed.

<!-- p2 -->
Jesus gave His disciples—and all of us—a powerful promise concerning the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p3 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p4 -->
> **<u>John 14:16-18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And I will ask the Father, and He will give you another Comforter to be with you forever—
>
> that is, the Spirit of truth, whom the world cannot receive, because it neither sees Him nor knows Him; but you know Him, because He dwells with you and will be in you.
>
> I will not leave you as orphans; I will come to you.
>

<!-- p5 -->
</span>

<!-- p6 -->
At the time when Jesus walked the earth in His physical body, He was limited as we are; His body needed the same things our bodies need: rest, water, food, sleep, and so on. This limitation affected not only Jesus but also His followers, because He could be in only one place at a time. Often, when Jesus traveled, great crowds gathered around Him; they tried to call out to Him and to touch Him. Jesus often needed to spend time with His heavenly Father to be refreshed and to pray.

<!-- p7 -->
In order for the Father’s work to be multiplied, Jesus had to raise up disciples to continue it. Jesus knew that a vital aspect of His ministry on earth depended on His connection with and abiding in the Father. In fact, Jesus said that He could do nothing unless His Father did it through Him and showed Him to do it (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 5:19</span>). In His wisdom, God sent His Holy Spirit to dwell in us, to lead and guide us, and to grant us the power to do the Father’s work.

<!-- p8 -->
## When was the Holy Spirit sent for all believers?

<!-- p9 -->
Jesus said:

<!-- p10 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p11 -->
> **<u>John 16:7</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Nevertheless, I tell you the truth: it is to your advantage that I go away; for if I do not go away, the Comforter will not come to you; but if I go, I will send Him to you.
>

<!-- p12 -->
</span>

<!-- p13 -->
After Jesus was crucified, His death made it possible for the Spirit of God to dwell within us. Until that time, the tangible presence of God’s Spirit was largely confined behind a thick veil in the temple. From time to time, certain people were chosen and anointed by God (consecrated and set apart for a unique service), and the Holy Spirit would come upon them. God’s anointing and the Spirit that was upon them were specific, temporary, and conditional. After his sin with Bathsheba, David prayed: ‘Do not take Your Holy Spirit from me!’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="51" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 51:11</span>), because David had seen that, due to acts of disobedience, the Spirit was taken from King Saul.

<!-- p14 -->
We (humanity) could not receive the Holy Spirit within us because of our sinful nature, but when Jesus resolved the issue of sin through His sacrifice, a way was made for us to become, by faith, God’s children and heirs (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:17</span>).

<!-- p15 -->
In John 20, after His resurrection, Jesus appeared to His disciples in the midst of a locked room and breathed on them and said, ‘Receive the Holy Spirit’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 20:22</span>). The precise theology of that moment is somewhat debated, but most Pentecostal interpretations regard this event as the receiving of God’s Spirit within them, signifying new birth, being born again. However, this was not the time of receiving the Spirit of God upon them to empower them with boldness for service. This was when they received the Holy Spirit within themselves so they could abide in God and have fellowship with Him. This subject will be explored in greater depth later in this chapter and also in the chapters on sanctification and baptism.

<!-- p16 -->
Later, at the end of the 40-day period between the resurrection and the ascension (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:3</span>), Jesus gave the promise of the Holy Spirit:

<!-- p17 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="4-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p18 -->
> **<u>Acts 1:4-9</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And being assembled together with them, He commanded them not to depart from Jerusalem, but to wait for the promise of the Father, "which," He said, "you heard from Me;
>
> for John truly baptized with water, but you shall be baptized with the Holy Spirit not many days from now."
>
> Therefore, when they had come together, they asked Him, saying, "Lord, will You at this time restore the kingdom to Israel?"
>
> And He said to them, "It is not for you to know times or seasons which the Father has put in His own authority.
>
> But you shall receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you; and you shall be My witnesses in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the ends of the earth."
>
> Now when He had spoken these things, while they watched, He was taken up, and a cloud received Him out of their sight.
>

<!-- p19 -->
</span>

<!-- p20 -->
The disciples were to wait for the promised outpouring of the Spirit, and this was to empower them to be witnesses to the ends of the earth. The Holy Spirit is an inseparable part of God’s plan for humanity, and is essential for us as believers to complete our mission, and for the world.

<!-- p21 -->
## Getting to Know the Holy Spirit

<!-- p22 -->
The Holy Spirit, first and foremost, is from God and belongs to God. The Scriptures refer to the Spirit with titles such as the Spirit of God, or the Spirit of the Lord, or the Spirit of Christ; although these titles highlight different aspects, they are all one and the same. The Holy Spirit, being fully God, is also eternal and holy (without blemish, without sin, pure, not impure). In some translations the Holy Spirit is also called the “Holy Ghost.”

<!-- p23 -->
The word for spirit in Greek is **Pneuma** (**NYOO-mah** - **G4151**); meaning breath, or wind. The Hebrew word for spirit has the same meaning. Breath matters because we continually need it to live. It was the breath of God that was breathed into Adam to make him alive; likewise it was the breath of Jesus that he exhaled for the last time before he died on the cross. Without breath there is no life. Breath, like wind, can be felt and experienced but is not as easily seen as the other ‘elements’ of water, fire, earth.

<!-- p24 -->
There are several other fundamental attributes used to describe the Holy Spirit. Each of these attributes is used with “Spirit of ...,” meaning these qualities are vital in defining the Spirit. For example, “Spirit of Truth” or “Spirit of Life.”

<!-- p25 -->
Spirit of:

<!-- p26 -->
- **Truth** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:17</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span> refers to the Spirit’s role in guiding believers into all truth and revealing God’s will and the reality of Christ.

<!-- p27 -->
- **Life** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:2</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:8</span> highlights the Spirit’s role in granting spiritual and eternal life to believers and setting them free from the law of sin and death.

<!-- p28 -->
- **Grace** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:29</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ZEC" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Zechariah 12:10</span> reflects the Spirit’s work in granting grace, forgiveness, and God’s unmerited kindness to humanity.

<!-- p29 -->
- **Glory** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 4:14</span> shows the Spirit’s role in revealing and manifesting the glory of God, especially in times of suffering and persecution.

<!-- p30 -->
- **Adoption** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:15</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 4:5</span> refers to the Spirit’s work in making believers children of God and granting them the status of sons and daughters.

<!-- p31 -->
- **Holiness** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 1:4</span> refers to the sanctifying work of the Spirit who sets believers apart for God’s purposes.

<!-- p32 -->
- **Power** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 1:7</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span> describes the Spirit’s empowering of believers for boldness, service, and the ability to live their faith effectively.

<!-- p33 -->
In all these attributes, the Holy Spirit is rightly also known as the “Helper” or “Comforter.” Jesus refers to the Holy Spirit as the Helper to come; he uses the Greek word ***Paraklētos** (*pah-RAH-klay-tos - **G3875**) which means advocate, helper, one called to stand alongside.

<!-- p34 -->
An advocate speaks on behalf of another, like a defense attorney. He intercedes and helps the one for whom he is responsible.

<!-- p35 -->
A helper helps. He empowers the work, but his primary duty is to assist the work. The helper is not solely responsible for doing the work, but comes alongside to help bring it to completion.

<!-- p36 -->
The Holy Spirit, in his ministry, has several distinct purposes and roles. His work is inward (in the hearts and lives of believers), upward (in serving and knowing God), and outward (toward the field of our mission, namely the world).

<!-- p37 -->
## The Roles of the Holy Spirit—Inward, Upward, Outward

<!-- p38 -->
### Inward - The Holy Spirit unites us with Jesus

<!-- p39 -->
Before Jesus performed a miracle or began his public ministry, he was baptized by John the Baptist, and the Spirit of God descended upon him, and a voice from heaven declared: ‘This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:17</span>).

<!-- p40 -->
When we receive Jesus by faith, we receive salvation and the seal of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p41 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p42 -->
> **<u>Ephesians 1:13-14</u>** (NKJV)
>
> In Him you also, after you heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation, trusted; and in Him also, when you believed, you were sealed with the Holy Spirit of promise,
>
> who is the guarantee of our inheritance until the redemption of the purchased possession, to the praise of His glory.
>

<!-- p43 -->
</span>

<!-- p44 -->
This seal is not metaphorical but real. This seal is a sign of our salvation and is like a down payment for the full inheritance we receive as children of God. Romans 8 states that through the new birth we receive in our mortal bodies the very Spirit of God—the One who raised Christ from the dead—and become the true temple of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 6:16</span>).

<!-- p45 -->
This receiving of God’s Spirit transforms us and causes rivers of living water to flow from us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 7:38</span>). This renewal is immediate and complete, yet it continues throughout our whole lives. The river of the Holy Spirit must never cease flowing in our lives!

<!-- p46 -->
Through salvation and the indwelling of the Holy Spirit, we can abide in Christ and He in us. The Spirit of God is no longer confined outside, but can dwell with us deeply. From now into eternal life we can walk in fellowship and harmony with God.

<!-- p47 -->
There is much to say about the inward work of the Holy Spirit and His role in our sanctification, but these matters will be addressed in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p48 -->
### Upward-The Holy Spirit testifies about Jesus

<!-- p49 -->
The Holy Spirit is from God and belongs to God. Just as Jesus said He did nothing except what the Father showed Him (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 5:19</span>), the Holy Spirit also does nothing unless He is directed by the Father. Because the Holy Spirit is the Comforter, He does not act on His own discretion, but works in perfect harmony with the Father.

<!-- p50 -->
The Spirit bears witness about Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:26</span>), glorifies Jesus, and declares and reveals what Jesus has for us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:14</span>).

<!-- p51 -->
The Holy Spirit will testify about Jesus, will glorify Him, will teach, and will bring to our remembrance the teachings of Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:26</span>), and will guide us into all truth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span>). Jesus told His disciples that He had many other things to teach them, but they were not yet able to bear them; yet the Holy Spirit would lead them into all truth. This is another nuanced point in theology. The teachings that Jesus deemed necessary for His disciples to know were conveyed to them in several ways. Jesus Himself taught them both before the crucifixion and after the resurrection and before the ascension.

<!-- p52 -->
Jesus also promised that the Spirit of truth, who was poured out on them on the day of Pentecost, would lead them into all truth through divine revelation. Is there still divine revelation today? Absolutely, yes! The gifts of the Holy Spirit, including prophecy and words of knowledge and wisdom, have not ceased! However, no prophecy or word from the Spirit is a matter of private interpretation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2PE" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Peter 1:20</span>). This is critical. Some wrongly claim that they listen to the Holy Spirit, but their actions and words do not bear witness to Jesus. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 4:3</span> warns against every spirit that does not testify of Jesus and says that such a spirit is the spirit of antichrist. This bears repeating: if a spirit deviates from the teaching of Christ, that spirit is not the Holy Spirit, but an unclean spirit!

<!-- p53 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p54 -->
> **<u>1 John 4:1</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Beloved, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits to see whether they are from God; for many false prophets have gone out into the world.
>

<!-- p55 -->
</span>

<!-- p56 -->
Throughout Scripture we see examples of false claims; often from false prophets or well-meaning individuals who spoke contrary to God’s will. For example, Jeremiah warned against prophets who told the dreams of their own hearts and claimed peace while ruin was lying in wait (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="23" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 23:16-17</span>). Also, in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 16:16-18</span>, the words of a slave girl about Paul and Silas were true, but were inspired by an evil spirit, not by the Holy Spirit. These examples remind us that even words that appear true can originate from non-divine sources.

<!-- p57 -->
Every claim of the kind “God told me!” or a “new” teaching must be measured against the biblical standards of truth. For proper discernment we must ask:

<!-- p58 -->
- Does this align with Scripture?

<!-- p59 -->
- Does this glorify Jesus Christ?

<!-- p60 -->
- Does it bear divine fruit?

<!-- p61 -->
By applying these tests, we can discern the true guidance of the Holy Spirit from error or deception.

<!-- p62 -->
God is in the habit of granting us abundant &amp; overflowing wisdom and guidance (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:5</span> &amp; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="33" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 33:3</span>). God’s divine wisdom never conflicts with Scripture, but it goes beyond the scope of Scripture. His commands, that is, *entolē* *(*commands*)*, are immediate prescriptions for obedience; they are *rhema* words (as discussed in the chapter on faith).

<!-- p63 -->
This wisdom, understanding, and guidance are unlikely to appear word-for-word in Scripture. For example, Scripture gives excellent general guidance about what to look for in a spouse, but it does not give specific instructions about the exact person. But God has promised to direct our path, to lead us into all truth, and we can be assured that this wisdom and guidance from the Holy Spirit will never conflict with any teaching of Scripture.

<!-- p64 -->
### Outward—the Holy Spirit empowers the mission

<!-- p65 -->
Jesus entrusted his mission to us, the Great Commission:

<!-- p66 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p67 -->
> **<u>Matthew 28:18-20</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And Jesus came and spoke to them and said: "All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me.
>
> Therefore go and make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit,
>
> and teach them to observe all that I have commanded you; and behold, I am with you always, to the end of the age." Amen.
>

<!-- p68 -->
</span>

<!-- p69 -->
The Holy Spirit has an integral role in this mission and convicts the world of sin, righteousness, and judgment:

<!-- p70 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="8-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p71 -->
> **<u>John 16:8-11</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And when he comes, he will convict the world of sin, and of righteousness, and of judgment:
>
> of sin, because they do not believe in me;
>
> of righteousness, because I go to my Father and you see me no more;
>
> of judgment, because the ruler of this world is judged.
>

<!-- p72 -->
</span>

<!-- p73 -->
Looking more deeply, the Spirit convicts with respect to these three truths:

<!-- p74 -->
1.  **Conviction regarding sin**: revealing the reality of humanity’s rebellion against God and drawing people's attention to the ruin of their own lives.

<!-- p75 -->
2.  **Conviction regarding righteousness**: showing Jesus’ complete fulfillment of the Father’s will, and that Jesus’ sacrifice provided the once-for-all atonement necessary for the forgiveness of sins.

<!-- p76 -->
3.  **Conviction regarding judgment**: affirming the defeat of Satan, the certainty of God’s justice, and that all will stand before judgment for the deeds and acts of their lives.

<!-- p77 -->
The Holy Spirit also empowers the mission. The Holy Spirit gives believers power to be bold witnesses of the gospel in the face of persecution. Boldness does not mean being loud. It means having courage in spite of fear. Power means strength, might, and capability. The Holy Spirit imparts ability and capability so that, despite threats and fear of punishment, we may be witnesses of the gospel.

<!-- p78 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p79 -->
> **<u>Acts 1:8</u>** (NKJV)
>
> "But you shall receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you; and you shall be my witnesses in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the ends of the earth."

<!-- p80 -->
</span>

<!-- p81 -->
The entire book of Acts is full of stories and accounts that follow this overall cycle:

<!-- p82 -->
1.  **Prayer**: Believers ask for boldness and God's intervention.

<!-- p83 -->
2.  **Empowerment**: The Holy Spirit fills them with courage, power, and guidance.

<!-- p84 -->
3.  **Proclamation**: They boldly proclaim the gospel, often with signs, wonders, and miracles.

<!-- p85 -->
4.  **Opposition**: Resistance arises, leading to persecution or challenges. Sometimes believers were martyred, sometimes imprisoned or beaten.

<!-- p86 -->
5.  **Renewal**: The church returns to prayer, seeks fresh empowerment, and the cycle continues.

<!-- p87 -->
The New Testament church largely grew in the face of determined opposition. Around the world the church experiences violence at the hands of oppressors and evil laws that intend to suppress the knowledge of the truth of Jesus.

<!-- p88 -->
God knew that this opposition would occur; Jesus prophesied about it:

<!-- p89 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p90 -->
> **<u>Luke 12:11-12</u>** (NKJV)
>
> "Now when they bring you to the synagogues and magistrates and authorities, do not worry about how or what you should answer, or what you should say.
>
> For the Holy Spirit will teach you in that very hour what you ought to say."
>

<!-- p91 -->
</span>

<!-- p92 -->
Do not be afraid! Jesus promised us that:

<!-- p93 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p94 -->
> **<u>Matthew 16:18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And I also say to you that you are Peter, and on this rock I will build My church, and the gates of Hades shall not prevail against it.
>

<!-- p95 -->
</span>

<!-- p96 -->
This is an amazing and comforting promise. Gates are fixed defensive equipment. When Jesus says that *the gates of hell will not prevail*, he paints a picture of a conquering army that smashes the gates and takes the city!

<!-- p97 -->
The Holy Spirit grants supernatural boldness to proclaim the testimony of the gospel in the face of persecution. The result of this empowerment can be described, but it is best experienced. This is a supernatural (beyond human) boldness that moves believers to share. This preaching boldness is often accompanied by supernatural acts demonstrating the Spirit and power (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 2:1-5</span>). People are healed physically and emotionally, the dead are raised, those who are tormented by evil spirits are set free, the hard-hearted turn and repent.

<!-- p98 -->
Jesus said we will do these works and greater than these! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:12</span>). These collective supernatural, divine works are desperately needed in a world that is tearing itself apart, wretched and full of pain, and awaiting the clear revealing of the mature sons of God! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:22-23</span>)

<!-- p99 -->
Jesus' chosen instrument for change on the earth is his church, which is empowered, emboldened, and led by the Holy Spirit.

## Establishing a Relationship with the Holy Spirit

<!-- p101 -->
Every relationship takes time and experience to deepen. The Holy Spirit is our Advocate, our Comforter, our Helper, and our Guide. He empowers us and sanctifies us.

<!-- p102 -->
Start by considering the Holy Spirit as a person. In our relationship we can both work to build it and do things that weaken it. He can be ignored, he can be quenched, he can be blasphemed, or he can be listened to, honored, and obeyed.

<!-- p103 -->
Do not ignore the voice of the Holy Spirit. He is always speaking to us and to the Church. If you still don’t know how to discern the voice of the Holy Spirit, it takes time, willingness, and effort (like all healthy and beneficial relationships). But don’t worry, everything necessary to begin cultivating a personal relationship with the Holy Spirit has already been placed within you.

<!-- p104 -->
The quickest and most reliable ways to build an effective relationship with the Holy Spirit are to

<!-- p105 -->
1.  In prayer, call on Him **individually**, pause, and wait for Him to speak.

<!-- p106 -->
2.  As part of a **group** of people who know Him (the Church), call on Him, pause, and wait for Him to speak.

<!-- p107 -->
I use the phrase ‘call on’ in its literal sense: with your mouth, or however you speak (with your hands if you cannot speak aloud with your mouth). Why speak? A few reasons:

<!-- p108 -->
1.  We are speaking spirits made in the image of God. God speaks, and so do we.

<!-- p109 -->
2.  We use speech as the primary way of communication. You can also write what you want to say (journaling) and speak it out loud.

<!-- p110 -->
3.  Speaking is a deliberate act and shows the one you are talking to that you are willing to engage them in conversation.

<!-- p111 -->
#### What should you say?

<!-- p112 -->
If you have never spoken with God, try introducing yourself. It will, of course, feel a bit strange, but that discomfort is okay and temporary. Along with introducing yourself, tell God that you want to talk with Him and that you want Him to talk with you.

<!-- p113 -->
Consider asking Him a question. It doesn’t have to be a deep theological question; it can start simple. Can you show me your love for me? Why do I feel a certain way? What would you like to say to me?

<!-- p114 -->
#### So what next?

<!-- p115 -->
Well, in all effective relationships, you talk a bit, then you pause and listen. God will speak.

<!-- p116 -->
God speaks in many ways, but He wants to do so in a personal and inward way. It likely will not be an audible voice (like when your best friend says your name out loud) but an inner voice that speaks to your heart and mind. Elijah refers to this as the ‘still small voice’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1KI" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Kings 19:11-13</span>).

<!-- p117 -->
The most common ways I have personally seen God speak to people—and I have had the privilege of helping dozens learn how to speak directly with God—are a combination of the following:

<!-- p118 -->
- An awareness - the person who is talking with God immediately receives an impression that helps them.

<!-- p119 -->
- An image - which in the Scriptures is also known as a vision or a dream (a vision is when you are awake, a dream is when you are asleep), God places an image in your mind. It might be a memory, or a place you have been or know. Examine the image: what do you see? As a result, how do you feel? What is happening?

<!-- p120 -->
- An inner voice - like an audible voice, but one that only you yourself hear and that speaks in your heart and mind.

<!-- p121 -->
#### What will God say?

<!-- p122 -->
I'm not sure! But that's the beauty of it. I can say that He is not a spirit of fear, but the Spirit of perfect love, and He will never violate the teachings of Scripture. For example, God will never tell you to engage in an extramarital relationship.

<!-- p123 -->
So what should we do now?

<!-- p124 -->
Keep the conversation going! It really is that simple.

<!-- p125 -->
A few good books that I have read and personally recommend to help learn to talk with God are:

<!-- p126 -->
- *Authentic Christianity* by Larry Low ([<u>www.larrylow.com</u>](http://www.larrylow.com))

<!-- p127 -->
- *Good Morning, Holy Spirit* by Benny Hinn ([<u>www.bennyhinn.org</u>](http://www.bennyhinn.org))

<!-- p128 -->
### Why build a relationship?

<!-- p129 -->
The Holy Spirit is the Helper. A helper helps us! Things go much more smoothly if we have a common language and a relationship with Him. Can you imagine trying to work with someone at your job when you have no relationship, don't speak the same language, and can't communicate?!

<!-- p130 -->
We serve a God who wants to communicate with us. Jesus said that His sheep know His voice (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:27</span>), we all already have the natural abilities needed within us, but we may lack the skill that comes from experience.

<!-- p131 -->
From personal experience, having a personal and active relationship with the Holy Spirit is one of the best aspects of being a Christian. His presence brings us peace, comfort, and guidance, but only if you know how to seek Him, listen, and obey.

<!-- p132 -->
Cultivate a sensitivity to the Holy Spirit within yourself. Like many married couples, over time you can communicate with your spouse in a unique way, and sometimes with just a look. That look alone tells you everything you need to know about what has happened and what is going to happen. Build that same kind of sensitivity and familiarity with the Holy Spirit. Involve Him in your whole life; this is a wonderful part of your relationship with Him that will add great depth and richness to your walk of faith.

<!-- p133 -->
The Holy Spirit has come to lead and guide us, to empower us to be the people of God, and to see the people of the earth fundamentally transformed, delivered from the kingdom of darkness and transferred into the kingdom of light. Knowing Him and building trust with Him is essential for daily life and for accomplishing what Jesus has appointed for us.
