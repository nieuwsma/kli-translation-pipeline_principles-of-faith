# Chapter 13 - Spiritual Authority

<!-- p1 -->
Jesus is the ultimate and complete source of spiritual authority. As the victorious Messiah he has the ultimate authority over all things in heaven and on earth.

<!-- p2 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p3 -->
> **<u>Matthew 28:18-20</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And Jesus came near and spoke with them and said: "All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to Me.
>
> Therefore go and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit,
>
> and teach them to keep everything I have commanded you; and behold, I am always with you, to the end of the age." Amen.
>

<!-- p4 -->
</span>

<!-- p5 -->
In this chapter we will study the authority and power of Christ, the authorities and powers that he has delegated to us (his church), and how to operate in the authority of the name of Jesus. We will also examine angelic and demonic beings and assess their authority, power, and natures.

<!-- p6 -->
Authority means having the right to act. Power is the ability to do it. Both are essential in the spiritual life. Authority is different from power. Let us look at the table below that compares authority &amp; power and consider a few examples.

<!-- p7 -->
[See Table 7](../tables/table7.md)

<!-- p8 -->
Here are a few examples to help understand the difference between power and authority.

<!-- p9 -->
Example 1: At the workplace, an employee may have the talents and skills to carry out the sale of company assets (power). This power is lawful when they also have the relevant permission (authority) from the owners of the company. Without this authority, selling the company's assets is theft.

<!-- p10 -->
Example 2: A police officer receives special training in weapons and hand-to-hand combat in order to know how to arrest a criminal and physically compel them (power). Police officers are given a job responsibility that is supported by laws (authority) to enforce criminal laws.

## The source of Jesus’ authority

<!-- p74 -->
Now that we have examined precisely what authority is، we must know from whom that authority originates. Jesus’ authority is both divine and delegated. This authority originates from God the Father، is confirmed by his death and resurrection، and operates in complete harmony with the Father’s will. His authority, though absolute، respects human free will and demonstrates God’s love rather than coercive control.

<!-- p75 -->
Jesus’ authority is rooted in his divine identity as the Son of God and has been granted to him by the Father. The Father entrusted all authority to Christ، including judgment and rule over heaven and earth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 11:27</span>). Jesus’ authority reflects the unity and love within the Trinity، where the Son perfectly carries out the Father’s plan (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="35" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:35</span>).

<!-- p76 -->
Although Jesus, as the Son of God, had inherent authority، his victory over sin، death، and hell was fully established through his death and resurrection. Christ’s obedience in his sacrifice led to his exaltation (sitting at the right hand of the Father)، and established his authority over all creation. By overcoming death، Christ obtained absolute rule over life and eternity.

## Laying on of hands

<!-- p116 -->
Laying on of hands is an important biblical practice used throughout the Old and New Testaments to symbolize blessing, commissioning, healing, and the impartation of authority or spiritual gifts. It is more than merely a physical act - it represents a spiritual connection and transfer under the guidance of God’s authority.

<!-- p117 -->
In the Old Testament, laying on of hands was often associated with blessing and consecration. For example, Jacob blessed his grandsons, Ephraim and Manasseh, by laying his hands on them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="48" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 48:14</span>). Likewise, Moses laid hands on Joshua to commission him as his successor and to impart leadership authority and wisdom to him (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="NUM" data-chapter="27" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Numbers 27:18-23</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="DEU" data-chapter="34" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Deuteronomy 34:9</span>). This act publicly signified God’s choosing and empowerment for the work ahead.

<!-- p118 -->
In the New Testament, laying on of hands is closely linked with the ministry of Jesus and the early church. Jesus regularly laid His hands on the sick to heal them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 6:5</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 4:40</span>), demonstrating both compassion and authority over sickness and disease. The apostles continued this practice, using it to impart the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:17</span>), spiritual gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 4:14</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 1:6</span>), and to bless believers. It also played a role in commissioning leaders and missionaries for service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 13:3</span>).

<!-- p119 -->
Laying on of hands is a practical symbol of faith and obedience to God’s word. It is not a mechanical rite, but a spiritual practice rooted in reliance on the Holy Spirit. For example, in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:6</span>, the apostles laid hands on those chosen to serve as deacons, committing them to God’s service and publicly affirming their calling. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 19:6</span>, Paul laid hands on the believers and they received the Holy Spirit, showing its role in spiritual empowerment.

<!-- p120 -->
However, the Bible also gives warnings about this practice:

<!-- p121 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p122 -->
> **<u>1 Timothy 5:22</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Do not lay hands on anyone hastily, nor share in other people's sins; keep yourself pure.
>

<!-- p123 -->
</span>

<!-- p124 -->
This emphasizes the importance of discernment and spiritual maturity when appointing individuals to leadership roles. As we studied in the chapter on the church, leaders must be tested and proven to prevent elevating unqualified or immature believers who can harm the body of Christ.

<!-- p125 -->
A few additional guidelines for laying on of hands:

<!-- p126 -->
1.  Contextually appropriate - the person on whom hands are laid should participate with their own consent.

<!-- p127 -->
2.  Physically appropriate - laying on of hands does not include sexual or erotic contact. In practice, placing a hand on the shoulder, hand, or forehead is generally accepted (with permission) in the Western world.

<!-- p128 -->
3.  Culturally appropriate - in some cultures, contact between men and women, unless they are blood relatives or married, is not appropriate.

<!-- p129 -->
The laying on of hands remains a powerful expression of faith and obedience in the church today. This practice is a means of blessing, healing, commissioning, and conferring authority, and it always points to the authority of Christ and the work of the Holy Spirit. Whether in praying for the sick, sending out evangelists, or appointing leaders, it is a reminder that believers do not act in their own power but under the authority of Jesus Christ and in harmony with his purposes.

<!-- p130 -->
Now that we have examined the authority of Jesus and the authority of the believer, we will discuss other beings in the spiritual realm, namely, angels and demons.

<!-- p131 -->
## Angels and Demons

<!-- p132 -->
### Angels

<!-- p133 -->
Angels are an important part of God’s creation and serve as his messengers and agents to carry out his divine purposes. Scripture reveals a spiritual realm in which these beings dwell—beings powerful, wise, and obedient to God’s commands. Although angels are often unseen, their presence and actions affect human history and spiritual warfare. Understanding their roles, classifications, and activities gives us deeper insight into God’s plan and the unseen realities around us.

<!-- p134 -->
The very word "angel" means "messenger" and highlights their primary function. Angels act in obedience to God’s will—whether delivering messages, providing protection, executing judgment, or engaging in spiritual battles. They are not objects of worship but examples of obedience and reverence, always pointing to the glory of God. This chapter examines the nature, classifications, and roles of angels, clarifying their significance in Scripture and in our lives today.

<!-- p135 -->
In Scripture, angels are presented as **messengers**, **servants**, and **warriors**. Angels often act as messengers, delivering important announcements, warnings, and encouragements. They reveal God’s plans and provide guidance in times of need (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26-38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 1:26-38</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 2:13</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 1:1</span>). Angels actively participate in protecting, strengthening, and delivering believers. They minister to God’s people in times of distress and provide supernatural help (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="91" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 91:11</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 12:7-11</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 4:11</span>). Angels engage in spiritual warfare, fighting against demonic forces to advance God’s kingdom and defend believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="DAN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Daniel 10:13</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:7-9</span>).

<!-- p136 -->
#### The Nature of Angels

<!-- p137 -->
Angels are created; God brought them into existence before the foundation of the world. They are distinct from humans and possess unique characteristics that equip them for their heavenly roles. Scripture teaches that angels are spiritual beings not bound by physical limitations, though when interacting with humans they can take on visible forms.

<!-- p138 -->
Angels are described as immortal; that is, they do not die or age. They are innumerable, and their vast hosts fill the heavenly realms. Although they are often referred to with masculine pronouns, Scripture clearly shows that angels are sexless and neither marry nor reproduce. These features highlight their purpose as God’s servants—wholly devoted to his will and commands. Angels possess remarkable attributes that reflect their divine purpose. They are obedient, dignified, wise, and powerful. These qualities equip them to carry out God’s will effectively and faithfully.

<!-- p139 -->
[See Table 8](../tables/table8.md)

<!-- p140 -->
Although the Bible highlights several roles and titles for angels, it does not provide a comprehensive classification system. However, some angels are introduced with specific roles or functions:

<!-- p141 -->
- **The Angel of the Lord** - Often regarded as a pre-incarnate manifestation of Christ, appearing with divine authority. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="23" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 23:20-23</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JDG" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Judges 6:11-24</span>*)

<!-- p142 -->
- **Archangels** - High-ranking angels such as Michael, who leads the hosts, and Gabriel, who delivers divine messages. (*Jude 9; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:7</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 1:19-26</span>*)

<!-- p143 -->
- **Cherubim** - Guardians of the glory and presence of God, like those in Eden and over the Ark of the Covenant. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 3:24</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 25:22</span>*)

<!-- p144 -->
- **Seraphim** - Worshipers of God, continually proclaiming His holiness. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 6:1-6</span>*)

<!-- p145 -->
- **Elect angels** - Faithful angels who resisted Satan’s rebellion and remained loyal to God. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:21</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 25:41</span>*)

<!-- p146 -->
These classifications emphasize the diversity and order within the angelic realm, but their primary focus is always the fulfillment of God’s purposes.

<!-- p147 -->
#### Human interaction with angels

<!-- p148 -->
People in the Bible often encounter angels, but we are never instructed to command them or pray to them. Rather, angels act according to God’s direction. As **ministers of God** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 1:14</span>) they serve believers. Their interactions include protection, guidance, and deliverance, and they always point us back to God’s power and sovereignty.

<!-- p149 -->
- **Warnings against the worship of angels**: The Bible forbids worshiping angels or seeking encounters with them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:18</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 22:8-9</span>).

<!-- p150 -->
- **Pray to God, not to angels**: Believers should pray to God; the One who dispatches angels according to His will (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="91" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalms 91:11</span>).

<!-- p151 -->
- **Encouragement and trust**: Angels remind us of God’s care, but our faith must rest entirely on God, not on angels.

<!-- p152 -->
Angels are wondrous beings created to serve God and minister to His people. Though their work is often unseen, the Bible opens windows into their activity, revealing their faithfulness, strength, and reverence. Angels remind us of God’s provision and protection, but they always point to His glory. As believers, we are called to trust in God’s care, knowing that angels, as His ministers, act to accomplish His divine purposes.

<!-- p153 -->
### Demons

<!-- p154 -->
Just as the study of angels is essential for understanding the spiritual realm, so is the study of demons. The Bible clearly describes their activity, influence, and defeat through Jesus Christ. Recognizing their role in spiritual warfare helps believers stand firm in faith without fear.

<!-- p155 -->
#### Origin and nature of demons

<!-- p156 -->
Demons are fallen angels who followed Satan’s rebellion against God. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:7-9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 25:41</span>).* Satan, formerly known as Lucifer (“light-bearer”), was an exalted angel who sought to exalt himself above God and was cast out of heaven along with the other rebellious angels. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 14:12-15</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EZK" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ezekiel 28:12-17</span>).*

<!-- p157 -->
Demons are bodiless spirits, but they can oppress and torment humans and animals. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 5:11-13</span>).* They seek to deceive, tempt, oppress, and destroy. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 11:14</span>). Unlike God,* they are limited, and neither omnipresent (everywhere), nor omniscient (all-knowing), nor omnipotent (almighty)*.* Their ultimate defeat is certain, and they will face eternal punishment. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 20:10</span>)*

<!-- p158 -->
The Bible describes the work and activity of the devil as follows:

<!-- p159 -->
- He opposes the work of God *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Thessalonians 2:18</span>)*

<!-- p160 -->
- He deceives the world *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 4:4</span>)*

<!-- p161 -->
- He tempts and accuses believers *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 22:31</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:10</span>)*

<!-- p162 -->
- He torments and harms *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JOB" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Job 1:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 13:16</span>)*

<!-- p163 -->
There are many names and descriptions for the devil, each of which describes a different aspect of his work.

<!-- p164 -->
[See Table 9](../tables/table9.md)

<!-- p165 -->
The Gospels and the epistles further clarify the work of the enemy. Evil spirits, because they are bodiless, must mostly carry out their work through willing and open individuals. Just as God works through people, so does Satan. This pressure is often characterized by torment, self-harm, and destructive behaviors *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 8:27-29</span>).* Their attacks cause fear, temptation, and discouragement *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 12:7</span>).* The most common source of their power (not authority) in someone’s life is the influence of lies and emotional strongholds *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 10:3-5</span>).*

<!-- p166 -->
It is important to note that every lie someone believes, every ailment in their body, or every stronghold in their life is not necessarily the direct result of the evil one’s influence, but these points of wounding are places where the enemy can exploit us and keep us in a state of defeat and oppression.

<!-- p167 -->
#### Jesus’ Authority over the Works of the Enemy

<!-- p168 -->
Do not be afraid, Jesus has complete and absolute authority over all the works of the enemy. During his ministry Jesus demonstrated power over demons *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 1:34</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 5:1-20</span>).* The works of the enemy and Satan himself have been defeated through the death and resurrection of Jesus *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:15</span>).*

<!-- p169 -->
As believers, the authority that Jesus has delegated has been given to us so that in his name we may cast out demons:

<!-- p170 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p171 -->
> **<u>Mark 16:17</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And these signs will accompany those who believe: in my name they will cast out unclean spirits; they will speak with new tongues;
>

<!-- p172 -->
</span>

<!-- p173 -->
Furthermore, as heirs of salvation, we have this authority to submit ourselves to God, resist the devil, and he will flee from us! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 4:7</span>).

<!-- p174 -->
To deal with an evil or unclean spirit, the pattern stated in the Scriptures is to take authority over it in the name of Jesus and command it to leave. The Holy Spirit has promised that if we ask he will give us wisdom and discernment! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:5</span>). We do not need to put on a show, dramatize it like in the movies, or even talk with them; this is a trick of Satan to distract us. Satan’s power is only established and maintained by the lies he can get us to believe.

<!-- p175 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="18" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p176 -->
> **<u>Matthew 18:18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> "Truly I say to you, whatever you bind on earth will be bound in heaven, and whatever you loose on earth will be loosed in heaven.
>

<!-- p177 -->
</span>

<!-- p178 -->
We speak to them in the name of Jesus and command them to leave.

<!-- p179 -->
There are two primary warnings in the Scriptures when dealing with evil forces:

<!-- p180 -->
1.  **Only those who have a relationship with Jesus have authority over evil forces**. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 19:11-20</span> speaks about the sons of a Jewish chief priest named Sceva. They tried to intervene in the life of a person who was oppressed by Satan, but the evil spirit, because they did not have a relationship with Jesus, was not willing to leave him. The tormented man leaped on them, stripped them, and beat them. Just as you should not impersonate a police officer, **do not impersonate someone who has a relationship with Jesus; rather, have a relationship with Jesus**!

<!-- p181 -->
2.  **When an evil spirit goes out, it goes away for a time and will try to return**. If it finds the place where it had dwelt empty, it will come back worse than before. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="43-45" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 12:43-45</span>. When we minister to someone who is oppressed by Satan, that person must fill the void that the evil spirit had occupied in their life. This means at a minimum establishing a personal relationship with Jesus and strengthening it through obedience to the Holy Spirit. Often an evil or unclean spirit gains room to operate in a person’s life through choices the person has made based on their beliefs. Anyone who has been delivered from Satan must replace that old belief system and be transformed by the renewing of their mind (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:1-2</span>).

<!-- p182 -->
We serve a wonderful and mighty God, and are ‘more than conquerors’ over a defeated enemy (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="37" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:37</span>). We have no need to fear the defeated enemy, because Jesus has given His authority to us to crush his head! Satan’s time is short, and he will ultimately be judged along with every principality and power on earth that rejects the reign of Christ.
