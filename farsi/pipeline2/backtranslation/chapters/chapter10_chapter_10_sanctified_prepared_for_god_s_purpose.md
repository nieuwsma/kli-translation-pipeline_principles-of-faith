# Chapter 10 - Sanctified-Ready for God's Purpose

<!-- p1 -->
In the previous two chapters, we examined several important topics. We explored the role and ministry of the Holy Spirit, as well as the role and ministry of the Church.

<!-- p2 -->
We have a role to play in the Church, and while we know we need training, we also need cleansing. In order to truly be ready for God’s purposes, we must be sanctified.

<!-- p3 -->
The definition of **sanctification** is to cleanse something for a special purpose. This term is close to the word **consecration**, which means setting something apart for a special purpose.

<!-- p4 -->
Imagine a master sculptor preparing to work with a large piece of marble. The master envisions what this raw, unshaped marble can be transformed into through his precise artistry. He has a very specific design in mind, and instructs his assistants to set this piece of marble aside for that particular design (**consecration**). As he refines and works the marble, adding texture, depth, and form, he cleanses it for that specific design (**sanctifies** it). The act of setting the marble apart for his special project was quick, whereas the process of shaping the marble to the final design requires great patience.

<!-- p5 -->
At salvation we were set apart, dedicated to God. Through the work of cleansing our souls we are transformed with the help of the Spirit. This cleansing process is what sanctifies us.

<!-- p6 -->
When we received Jesus through salvation, we were born again. We were justified, our sins were forgiven. Our spirit was renewed, we were made alive in Christ. We were accepted into the family of God and literally became the temple of the Holy Spirit. What an amazing transformation, as Scripture says, “*There is joy in the presence of the angels of God over one sinner who repents.” (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 15:10</span>).

<!-- p7 -->
Receiving Jesus through salvation (**justification**) saves our spirit, cleanses us, and makes us acceptable to God, and enables us to receive His Holy Spirit. But even with such a great salvation, our **souls** (our mind, will, and emotions) still **need work**!

<!-- p8 -->
Was the work of salvation incomplete? No! The work of salvation is effective, but it must continue in the believer’s life through the renewal that the Holy Spirit brings about. This process of renewal and transformation is the work of **sanctification.**

<!-- p9 -->
We know that after conversion more work must be done, because our painful memories were not resolved, many of our beliefs remained the same, and many of our habits or rituals did not disappear. Moreover, conversion did not separate our minds from the carnal (fleshly) nature.

<!-- p10 -->
The word ‘carnal’ comes from a Latin root meaning ‘body’ or ‘flesh.’ The fleshly nature exists within each of us from birth; like the instincts of an animal, it is there to help us survive. The way the fleshly nature operates is to be self-centered, self-focused, self-absorbed. Children are natural examples of a fleshly nature. Despite their innocence, their nature is largely focused on their wants, desires, and needs; and ‘watch out!’ if you don’t meet their every expectation.

<!-- p11 -->
Part of growing up is maturing beyond our childish ways, but when we are under pressure, it is very easy to revert to a pattern of thinking and living that revolves around self. As James says:

<!-- p12 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p13 -->
> **<u>James 4:1</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Where do wars and fights come from among you? Do they not come from your desires for pleasure that war in your members?
>

<!-- p14 -->
</span>

<!-- p15 -->
Paul explains this fleshly nature in more detail in his letter to the Romans (chapters 6–8). He says this fleshly nature compels him to do what he hates and causes him to do evil. He describes this inner struggle as a war between his human nature and his God-made spirit.

<!-- p16 -->
This conflict between the flesh and the Spirit exists within each of us, and it is our job to do something about it. If we do not confront our fleshly nature, our souls will remain trapped in patterns of spiritual decay, even though our spirits have been redeemed.

<!-- p17 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p18 -->
> **<u>Romans 8:6-8</u>** (NKJV)
>
> For to be carnally minded is death, but to be spiritually minded is life and peace.
>
> Because the carnal mind is enmity against God; for it is not subject to the law of God, nor indeed can be.
>
> So then, those who are in the flesh cannot please God.
>

<!-- p19 -->
</span>

<!-- p20 -->
These words of Paul are both a warning and a promise to us. Although we have been born again, we still have a fleshly mind that must be submitted to the knowledge of Christ. And the more we do so, the more we walk in the life and health of God.

<!-- p21 -->
Take heart, there is hope for us! We must walk according to the Spirit and be led by the Spirit of God. And in that case, we will not walk according to the flesh.

<!-- p22 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="1,13,14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p23 -->
> **<u>Romans 8:1,13,14</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Therefore there is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus, that is, those who do not walk according to the flesh but according to the Spirit.
>
> For if you live according to the flesh you will die; but if by the Spirit you put to death the deeds of the body, you will live.
>
> For whoever is led by the Spirit of God, he is a son of God.
>

<!-- p24 -->
</span>

<!-- p25 -->
How do we learn to walk in the Spirit? Is it *just a conscious choice*? Just as we learn to walk in the natural, we also learn to walk in the Spirit. We are prompted to move and grow, so we grapple, strive, crawl, and wobble until we can stand on our own feet. Our natural parents cannot walk in our place, but they can support, encourage, help, and empower us in walking.

<!-- p26 -->
In the same way, God cannot work out our faith in our place. He will teach us, but we must make the effort. As Paul said to the Philippians:

<!-- p27 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="12-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p28 -->
> **<u>Philippians 2:12-13</u>** (NKJV)
>
> So then, my beloved, just as you have always obeyed, not only in my presence, but now much more in my absence, work out your own salvation with fear and trembling;
>
> for it is God who works in you both to will and to act for His good pleasure.
>

<!-- p29 -->
</span>

<!-- p30 -->
We must work out our own salvation. This does not mean that we go up on a wooden cross and are physically crucified, but we must crucify our fleshly nature (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:24</span>). God promises that He will work in us and help us, but we must choose to walk this way and remain steadfast in it.

<!-- p31 -->
It is true to say that “we” deal with our fleshly nature, but it is also true to say that “God” deals with it. The work of the cross (justification) was hard for Jesus—He had to give His life in place of ours—but it is easy for us, because it is a free gift. The work of the Spirit (sanctification) is easy for God—He walks in perfect truth and is willing and eager to dwell with us—but it is hard for us, because we must crucify our old nature, and our nature is not very interested in being crucified.

<!-- p32 -->
This process is collaborative. God helps, aids, guides, leads, and corrects. We listen, obey, follow, and respond. The result is growth and transformation. This is the pruning that Jesus said God would do in us. Jesus is the true vine, His Father the gardener, and we are the branches:

<!-- p33 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p34 -->
> **<u>John 15:1-4</u>** (NKJV)
>
> "I am the true vine, and My Father is the gardener.
>
> Every branch in Me that does not bear fruit He removes; and every branch that bears fruit He prunes, that it may bear more fruit.
>
> You are already clean because of the word which I have spoken to you.
>
> Abide in Me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself unless it abides in the vine, neither can you unless you abide in Me.
>

<!-- p35 -->
</span>

<!-- p36 -->
Pruning is tremendously beneficial for a plant. If we return to the chapter on repentance, we are called to turn from dead works (those works that no longer bear fruit) and turn back to God. The result of this turning away from the dead places in our lives and turning toward God empowers the great Gardener of our lives to prune the branches, improve growth, and produce more spiritual fruit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:2</span>).

<!-- p37 -->
Although pruning may seem painful, it reflects God’s caring love, for He is shaping us into vessels for His purpose (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 12:11</span>). Some may feel a sense of loss, even fear, as they go through the pruning process. But God is gentle and will not break even a bruised reed (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="42" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 42:3</span>). Grapevines are pruned during their dormant season to minimize stress on the plant. A gardener who watches over growth cuts back the old, woody growths that no longer produce fruit as before; this allows the vine to direct energy toward the branches that have the greatest capacity for good fruit.

<!-- p38 -->
Just as a gardener cuts branches to direct growth, God may allow times of discipline and chastening to refine our faith and character, but His work is patient and kind. God prunes those who abide in Him. Our focus should be more on abiding in Christ and less on the work of pruning.

<!-- p39 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 12:11</span> reminds us that for those who are trained by it, pruning “*yields the peaceable fruit of righteousness*.”

<!-- p40 -->
How are we trained by godliness? By being transformed through the renewing of our minds.

<!-- p41 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p42 -->
> **<u>Romans 12:1-2</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Therefore, brothers, I beseech you by the mercies of God to present your bodies as a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable to God, which is your reasonable service.
>
> And do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind, that you may be able to discern what is the good, acceptable, and perfect will of God.
>

<!-- p43 -->
</span>

<!-- p44 -->
Paul states two simple conditions for transformation:

<!-- p45 -->
<u>1. Present your bodies to God as a living sacrifice</u>. A **living** sacrifice does not physically die at the time of sacrifice, but because a living sacrifice cannot die, you must continually bring it to the altar!

<!-- p46 -->
<u>2. Renew your mind!</u> Renewal, based on the Greek word: **Anakainōsis** (*An-ak-ah'-ee-no-sis -* **G342**), means renovation or restoration and focuses on an ongoing process of inner change and growth.

<!-- p47 -->
This renewal leads to transformation. Transformation is based on the Greek word: **Metamorphoō** (*met-am-or-fo'-o -* **G3339**) meaning to become of another form; to be transformed or transfigured, and it emphasizes a complete change. Metamorphoō is the same word from which the English word metamorphosis comes, the process that caterpillars undergo to become butterflies.

<!-- p48 -->
God wants to transform our minds and pattern of thinking at the core. His will is that we not be conformed to the way of this world, but that we know what God’s good (beneficial), acceptable (approved), and perfect (complete and lacking nothing) will is.

<!-- p49 -->
The conditions for transformation really are this simple, **though they require discipline**!

<!-- p50 -->
Our war against the flesh is not won by the flesh, but by God’s equipping for the battle of the mind. His Word (the sword of truth) is powerful to correct every imagination and arrogant thought, and to take captive every thought that is against obedience to God.

<!-- p51 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="3-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p52 -->
> **<u>2 Corinthians 10:3-6</u>** (NKJV)
>
> For though we walk in the flesh, we do not war according to the flesh.
>
> For the weapons of our warfare are not carnal, but mighty in God for pulling down strongholds,
>
> casting down arguments and every high thing that exalts itself against the knowledge of God, and bringing every thought into captivity to the obedience of Christ,
>
> and being ready to punish all disobedience when your obedience is fulfilled.
>

<!-- p53 -->
</span>

<!-- p54 -->
When authorities arrest someone, they chain him and put him in a jail cell. If that person escapes custody, they do not remain indifferent; they employ tremendous resources and do not rest until that person is brought back under control. We must act with such zeal in the fight for our minds as well!

<!-- p55 -->
We are renewed through obeying and experiencing the truth. Jesus said that if we abide in His word, we will be His disciples, we will experience (<u>know</u>) the truth, and it will set us free (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-32" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 8:31-32</span>)!

<!-- p56 -->
Experiencing the truth is different from knowledge of the truth. “Experience” is the Greek word **Ginōskō** (Ghin-oce'-ko **- G1097**); meaning to know through personal experience or intimate understanding; and it implies gradual learning.

<!-- p57 -->
It is not our academic knowledge of the truth that transforms us and renews our minds, but our personal experience of the truth. We are sanctified by His truth! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="17" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 17:17</span>).

## How to renew our minds

<!-- p59 -->
The renewal of our minds is not a one-size-fits-all process—it differs depending on which part of the mind we’re talking about! God designed our minds with remarkable complexity, including several different memory systems, each serving a specific purpose. Some operate automatically in the background, while others require focused and conscious thinking. These systems are gifts from God to help us survive, grow, and thrive, but when misused or left unrenewed they can lead us astray. Understanding how these systems work gives us insight to cooperate with God’s process of transformation.

<!-- p60 -->
In the next section, I will describe each of the memory systems, their purpose and function, a few practical steps to renew this dimension of your mind, and resources for ongoing practice and study.

<!-- p61 -->
### **Memory Systems and How to Renew Them**

<!-- p62 -->
#### **1. Explicit Memory (Declarative Memory)**

<!-- p63 -->
**Purpose:** It stores facts, events, and experiences that we can consciously recall. It is divided into two types-**semantic** and **episodic**.

<!-- p64 -->
**Semantic memory - facts and knowledge.** Semantic memory is where we store facts, definitions, and general knowledge-things like historical dates, scripture verses, and practical information. It helps us predict outcomes, make decisions, and avoid danger (for example, identifying poisonous plants). While it gives us stability and order, it can also lead to rigidity-overreliance on rules instead of adapting to unexpected situations.

<!-- p65 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p66 -->
- **Study Scripture** to shape our worldview (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="119" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="105" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 119:105</span>).

<!-- p67 -->
- **Memorize truths** and teachings so that we remain grounded in faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 3:16</span>).

<!-- p68 -->
- **Read Christian books** such as *"Renewing the Mind" by Casey Treat* or *"Battlefield of the Mind" by Joyce Meyer* to cultivate godly thought patterns.

<!-- p69 -->
**Episodic memory - personal experiences.** Episodic memory stores our personal experiences and links them to specific times and places-like a wedding day or a traumatic event. These memories provide context and help us learn from the past and build relationships. However, episodic memory is prone to bias and emotional distortion. Past traumas can create fearful associations and make it difficult to move forward.

<!-- p70 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p71 -->
- **Reflect and confess** to bring hidden pain into God's light (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 1:9</span>).

<!-- p72 -->
- **Reframe past experiences** from the perspective of God's grace (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:28</span>).

<!-- p73 -->
- **Seek inner healing through prayer and counseling** (*“Your Pain is Showing” by Larry F. Low, "The Bondage Breaker" by Neil T. Anderson*, *"Emotionally Healthy Spirituality" by Peter Scazzero*).

<!-- p74 -->
#### **2. Implicit Memory (Non-declarative Memory)**

<!-- p75 -->
**Purpose:** Unconscious memory used for habits, skills, and emotional associations. It operates automatically and helps us respond quickly to situations. It is divided into **procedural memory** and **emotional conditioning**.

<!-- p76 -->
**Procedural memory - skills and habits.** Procedural memory allows us to do things without thinking-like driving, typing, or playing an instrument. It is efficient and conserves our mental energy and time. However, bad habits and negative patterns can also become deeply ingrained and be difficult to break.

<!-- p77 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p78 -->
- **Practice spiritual disciplines** such as prayer and fasting to build godly habits (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 5:14</span>).

<!-- p79 -->
- **Train in godliness** through repetition and obedience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 4:7</span>).

<!-- p80 -->
- **Books such as** *"Atomic Habits" by James Clear* (practical) and *"Spiritual Disciplines for the Christian Life" by Donald Whitney* (biblical) to establish habits that honor God.

<!-- p81 -->
**Emotional conditioning - emotional associations.** Emotional conditioning links sensory cues (such as smells or sounds) to emotions and triggers responses before we consciously process them. This system protects us, but when past experiences are negative it can create irrational fears or emotional reactions.

<!-- p82 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p83 -->
- **Pray for healing** from past wounds (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="41" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 41:10</span>).

<!-- p84 -->
- **Practice forgiveness** and release grudges (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-32" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:31-32</span>).

<!-- p85 -->
- **Replace fear with God’s promises** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 1:7</span>).

<!-- p86 -->
- **Seek guidance from books such as** *“Your Pain is Showing” by Larry F. Low، "The Bondage Breaker" by Neil T. Anderson*, “*Soul Care" by Rob Reimer* for overcoming emotional triggers.

<!-- p87 -->
#### **3. Working memory (short-term memory)**

<!-- p88 -->
**Purpose:** It provides temporary storage for problem-solving and decision-making. It helps us focus, hold information for a short time, and process tasks - like remembering instructions or making quick decisions. Though powerful, it is also limited, prone to distraction, and easily overloaded, especially under stress.

<!-- p89 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p90 -->
- **Focus on and meditate on God’s Word daily** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 1:2</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
- **Renew your mind** through deliberate thinking (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:2</span>).

<!-- p92 -->
- **Practice mindfulness** and simplify mental clutter (*"Habits of Grace" by David Mathis*).

<!-- p93 -->
- **Read the book** *"The Celebration of Discipline" by Richard Foster* to strengthen focus through spiritual disciplines.

<!-- p94 -->
## Conclusion

<!-- p95 -->
Renewing the mind is not a quick fix - it is a gradual, lifelong process of transformation. Each memory system plays a role in shaping how we think, feel, and behave. By intentionally partnering with the Holy Spirit, applying Scripture, and cultivating godly habits, we can train our minds to align with God’s truth. Whether facing past wounds, learning new habits, or maintaining focus in the moment, God has provided both **spiritual tools** and **practical wisdom** to help us walk in renewal and freedom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 10:5</span>).

<!-- p96 -->
Our world is full of hardship, suffering, and trials. Through disciplined renewal of the mind, our souls are transformed, and we can live a life of freedom for God, filled with the fruit of His Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-25</span>).
