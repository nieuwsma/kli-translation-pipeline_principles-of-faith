# Chapter 9 - The Church and Our Role in It

<!-- p1 -->
The church is the way God reveals himself on earth-through his people, who do his work. We are called to be his body; his hands and feet to accomplish his work and purpose. Without our efforts, the harvest remains in the field and will not be reaped.

<!-- p2 -->
## Who is the Church?

<!-- p3 -->
Church, ***Ekklesia** (eh-klee-SEE-ah - **G1577**),* is derived from two Greek words:

<!-- p4 -->
- **"Ek"**: "out of."

<!-- p5 -->
- **"Kaleo"**: "to call."

<!-- p6 -->
Together, **Ekklesia** literally means "the called-out" or "the assembly of the called-out." In the city-states of ancient Greece, it referred to a gathering of citizens who were called together for a specific purpose, such as legislative or judicial meetings. The term was a non-religious word that denoted an assembly of people.

<!-- p7 -->
In the New Testament, it is used primarily to refer to the community of believers in Jesus Christ. It can refer to:

<!-- p8 -->
- **The local church**: a particular gathering of believers in a specific place (for example, the church of Corinth, Philippi, etc.).

<!-- p9 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p10 -->
> **<u>1 Corinthians 1:2</u>** (NKJV)
>
> To the church of God which is in Corinth, to those who are sanctified in Christ Jesus, who are called to be saints, together with all who in every place call on the name of Jesus Christ, our Lord, both theirs and ours:
>

<!-- p11 -->
</span>

<!-- p12 -->
- **The universal church**: the collective body of all believers across all time and place.

<!-- p13 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p14 -->
> **<u>Matthew 16:18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And I also say to you that you are Peter, and on this rock I will build my church, and the gates of Hades shall never prevail against it.
>

<!-- p15 -->
</span>

<!-- p16 -->
The church is the people, not the building or the place where they gather. **The called-out**: reflecting the idea that Christians are called out from the world and set apart for God's purposes (for example, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:9</span>) for a specific mission and purpose (the Great Commission). Technically, the church is any two or more believers who gather together (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="18" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 18:20</span>). Usually, the church is organized into regular gatherings, likely with a set place and time; but wherever two or more gather in the name of Jesus, that is the church!

<!-- p17 -->
The church was built by Jesus and spread in the first century AD through the work of the disciples who followed him. The church we have and experience today, in all its local bodies across the earth, is the direct result of the initial work of Christ and his apostles. As believers, we should commit ourselves to a local church for fellowship, provision, equipping for service, and doing good works!

<!-- p18 -->
To better understand what the church is, Scripture gives us powerful images that show what it is meant to be.

<!-- p19 -->
## Understanding the Church

<!-- p20 -->
Several primary metaphorical images are used to describe the characteristics of the church:

<!-- p21 -->
1.  Body

<!-- p22 -->
2.  Bride

<!-- p23 -->
3.  Temple

<!-- p24 -->
4.  Family

<!-- p25 -->
5.  Flock

<!-- p26 -->
6.  Vine and branches

<!-- p27 -->
Each of these metaphors highlights a unique aspect of the church, but together they reveal the church’s identity as a unified and diverse community under the leadership of Christ.

<!-- p28 -->
### Body

<!-- p29 -->
The church is often described as the **Body of Christ**, which highlights both its unity and diversity. With Christ as the head, the church functions as a living organism in which every believer plays an essential and potentially diverse role. Each member is uniquely gifted and, according to its function, contributes to the health and mission of the body. This image highlights the mutual dependence of believers, especially that the body must be connected in order to function, and that our functioning is cooperative rather than competitive. When each part works together, the body grows and flourishes in unity and love (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:12-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:15-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 1:18</span>).

<!-- p30 -->
### Bride

<!-- p31 -->
The church is described as the **Bride of Christ**, reflecting an intimate, loving relationship with Jesus. Just as a bride is cherished and prepared for her husband, the church is sanctified and cleansed through Christ’s sacrificial love. This metaphor portrays the faithfulness and devotion expected of the church, as well as the deep love Christ has for his people. This image also points to the ultimate union between Christ and the church, celebrated at the marriage supper of the Lamb, where the church is presented in glory, without spot and without blemish (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="25-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:25-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 19:7-9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="21" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 21:2</span>).

<!-- p32 -->
### Temple

<!-- p33 -->
The church is described as the **Temple of God**, the dwelling place of his Spirit. Believers are described as living stones being built together on the foundation of the apostles and prophets, with Christ himself as the cornerstone. This sacred structure signifies God’s presence among his people and their role as a community for worship and prayer. The temple image emphasizes the importance of holiness and the church’s collective purpose in glorifying God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 3:16-17</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:19-22</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:4-5</span>).

<!-- p34 -->
### Family

<!-- p35 -->
The church is described as the **Family of God**, emphasizing relational unity and care among its members. Through the Spirit of adoption, believers become God’s children and members of his household. This family image highlights the love, belonging, and shared inheritance believers have in Christ. It also reflects the call to treat one another with compassion and humility as brothers and sisters in God’s family (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:19</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:15-17</span>). Furthermore, through the family image it shows that the members of the church must mature, that they are taught and nourished by spiritual fathers and mothers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:1-2</span>), so that we may be transformed from infants who drink the pure milk of the word into mature adults who are able to eat the solid food of the word (Hebrews 5).

<!-- p36 -->
### Flock

<!-- p37 -->
The church is described as a **Flock**, sheep under the care of Jesus, the Good Shepherd. This image conveys the church’s reliance on Christ for guidance, provision, and protection. It also emphasizes the responsibility of church leaders to shepherd God’s flock faithfully as under-shepherds, while reflecting Christ’s example of love and care. The flock image speaks of trust, obedience, and the close relationship between the Chief Shepherd and his sheep (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:14-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:2</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>).

<!-- p38 -->
### Vine and Branches

<!-- p39 -->
The church is described as the **Vine and Branches**, showing the vital connection between believers and Christ. Jesus is the true vine, and believers are branches that must abide in him to bear fruit. This metaphor emphasizes dependence on Christ for spiritual vitality, growth, and fruitfulness. Without him, believers can do nothing, but by abiding in him, they glorify God and fulfill their purpose. Jesus will prune so that he can bring forth more fruit in our lives (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:1-5</span>).

<!-- p40 -->
## The Roles of the Church

<!-- p41 -->
The church’s mission, like the role of the Holy Spirit, can be understood in three dimensions: upward, inward, and outward; each reflects a unique aspect of its calling in God’s plan. These dimensions guide the church in carrying out its role as a worshiping, edifying, and mission-oriented community.

<!-- p42 -->
### Upward: Worship and Glorifying God

<!-- p43 -->
The church’s first purpose is to worship and glorify God. Corporate worship, in which believers gather to praise God together, is a clear reflection of His glory and a response to His majesty. As the psalmist declares, “*Worship the Lord in the beauty of holiness; tremble before Him, all the earth*” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="96" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 96:9</span>). Beyond corporate worship, the church is called to live a life of obedience and devotion and to present themselves as “living sacrifices” as spiritual worship (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:1-2</span>). This upward focus keeps the church centered on the glory of God and aligns its priorities with His will.

<!-- p44 -->
### Inward: Building up the Body

<!-- p45 -->
The Great Commission commands us to ‘teach them to observe’, and this mandate goes far beyond formal instructional methods such as this book. Discipleship (which is explored further in the chapter on discipleship) is shaping the follower until they take on the likeness of the teaching, just as parents raise their child. Good parents do not hand a five-year-old an encyclopedia and challenge them to find the answers! Rather, they model, show, correct, guide, nurture, love, care, and shepherd.

<!-- p46 -->
Parents sacrifice for their children because they love them, because they are part of one body, because they share one blood. As members of the body and family of Christ, we share one body, we share one blood. The Lord’s Supper, through its symbolism, reminds us that we are joined to one another through Christ’s sacrifice. The act of the Lord’s Supper should be a point of unity and remind us that all need deliverance, and that salvation is only from God. In this unity we see that no member of the body is superior to another, except our head, that is, Christ.

<!-- p47 -->
### Outward: Reaching the World

<!-- p48 -->
The most well-known part of the church’s mission extends outward to reach the world through evangelism. Jesus’ Great Commission calls His followers to proclaim the gospel and make disciples of all nations (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19-20</span>). Empowered by the Holy Spirit, the church must be His witnesses to the ends of the earth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>).

<!-- p49 -->
Jesus is the light of the world (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 8:12</span>). Light shines outward from its source in all directions. His light shines through its manifestations: the Word, His Spirit, and us, His body. We are the light of the world; our lives must shine His light into our darkened worlds and display the good works that glorify God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 5:14-16</span>). Practically, this means the church is called to advocate for justice and mercy—both spiritual and physical. As James says, our pure worship is to care for the vulnerable, the orphans and widows (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:27</span>).

<!-- p50 -->
## Church Leadership

<!-- p51 -->
Across the Christian world there are various denominations, meetings, gatherings, and assemblies. There are few universal rules for church leadership, but those few are very important:

<!-- p52 -->
1.  Jesus is the church’s only head. All serve Him (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 1:18</span>).

<!-- p53 -->
2.  There are high standards for leaders; they are worthy of double honor, because they will receive double judgment (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:17</span>).

<!-- p54 -->
2.1  They must be mature, not self-important, and responsible (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:1-13</span>، <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 1:5-9</span>).

<!-- p55 -->
2.2.  They must be servants of all (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42-45" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 10:42-45</span>).

<!-- p56 -->
2.3.  They must devote themselves to the Word and prayer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:4</span>).

<!-- p57 -->
2.4.  They are responsible for equipping the body for the work of ministry.

<!-- p58 -->
3.  In the body, there is no member greater or lesser. Verses that support this include:

<!-- p59 -->
3.1.  There must be no favoritism between the rich and the poor (James 2).

<!-- p60 -->
3.2.  There is no distinction between Jew and Gentile، slave or free، male or female (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 3:27-28</span>).

<!-- p61 -->
Church structure, beyond leadership rules, is important, but much of it is situational and more a matter of tradition than a universal command. “<u>We’ve always done it this way!</u>” is a common saying؛ if you go back far enough, there’s usually a good reason why they did it that way. This is generally neither good nor bad، but depends on taste، needs، and availability.

<!-- p62 -->
A helpful example of how tradition can be the basis of structure is family dinnertime. The most important part of family dinnertime is that everyone eats and the meal is nutritious. The variations are endless: what they eat، how they eat، how they season their food، and so on. Various combinations are generally acceptable and largely come down to taste and preference.

<!-- p63 -->
A church with 10,000 members has different needs and capabilities than a church with 50 members. The pattern established in the book of Acts and throughout the New Testament was the pattern of **elders** and **deacons**. Although leadership structures can vary among churches and denominations، ultimately this pattern represents the most minimal workable structure for church leadership. Let’s take a look at these leadership offices and how they are selected.

<!-- p64 -->
### Elders (Overseers)

<!-- p65 -->
Roles and responsibilities:

<!-- p66 -->
- **Spiritual leadership** - provide oversight، guidance، and pastoral care for the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:2</span>).

<!-- p67 -->
- **Teaching and preaching** - responsible for teaching sound doctrine and protecting against false teaching (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:17</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 1:9</span>).

<!-- p68 -->
- **Shepherding the flock** - attending to the spiritual needs of the congregation، modeling humility and service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:2-3</span>).

<!-- p69 -->
- **Guarding the church** - serve as watchmen and defend the church against false teaching and division (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29-31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:29-31</span>).

<!-- p70 -->
- **Equipping the saints** - training and equipping believers for works of service and disciple-making (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-12</span>).

<!-- p71 -->
Selection process:

<!-- p72 -->
- **Appointment in each church** - elders were appointed by the apostles or their delegates, often through prayer and fasting (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 14:23</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 1:5</span>).

<!-- p73 -->
- **Affirmation by the Holy Spirit** - their appointment was considered Spirit-led and recognized God’s calling and gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>).

<!-- p74 -->
- **Tested and approved** - candidates had to demonstrate maturity and faithfulness before being entrusted with leadership (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:10</span>). They cannot be recent converts! ((<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:6</span>)

<!-- p75 -->
### Deacons (Servants)

<!-- p76 -->
Roles and responsibilities:

<!-- p77 -->
- **Practical service** - focusing on meeting material needs in the church, so that the elders can focus on prayer and teaching (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-4</span>).

<!-- p78 -->
- **Support of ministry** - assisting with hospitality، care for the poor، and administrative matters (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:3</span>).

<!-- p79 -->
- **Strengthening unity** - addressing issues of justice and care to prevent division (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-7</span>).

<!-- p80 -->
- **Examples of faith** - serve as models in humility, faith, and service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:13</span>).

<!-- p81 -->
Selection process (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-6</span>):

<!-- p82 -->
- **Known among the congregation** - chosen based on their reputation and spiritual character.

<!-- p83 -->
- **Affirmed by the leaders** - The apostles examined and appointed the selected deacons through prayer and the laying on of hands.

<!-- p84 -->
- **Confirmed in service** - The deacons were first tested before being formally appointed, so that their faithfulness would be confirmed (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:10</span>).

<!-- p85 -->
### Leadership qualifications

<!-- p86 -->
#### Shared qualifications for elders and deacons:

<!-- p87 -->
- Moral character - both must be blameless and respectable and exhibit self-control (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:2</span>, 8).

<!-- p88 -->
- Family life - both must be faithful in marriage (“husband of one wife”) and manage their households well (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:4-5</span>, 12).

<!-- p89 -->
- Spiritual maturity - both must be tested and proven before appointment (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:6</span>, 10).

<!-- p90 -->
- Reputation - both must have a good reputation inside and outside the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:7</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
- Faith and integrity - both must hold the faith with a clear conscience and put their beliefs into practice (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:9</span>).

<!-- p92 -->
### Example structures

<!-- p93 -->
Drawing on what we have learned so far about rules and leadership structures, let us look at two examples of church structure found in the New Testament: Jerusalem and Antioch.

<!-- p94 -->
### The church in Jerusalem

<!-- p95 -->
The church in Jerusalem was primarily led by the **apostles**; they focused on teaching, prayer, and spiritual oversight (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:42</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:4</span>). As the congregation grew, the need for administrative structure became apparent, especially to address practical matters such as caring for widows and distributing resources. This led to the appointment of **deacons** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-7</span>). These individuals, chosen for their spiritual maturity and good reputation, took on practical ministries so that the apostles could focus on their spiritual responsibilities. Over time, **elders** also emerged as leaders of the church in Jerusalem. For example, at the Jerusalem Council (Acts 15), both apostles and elders participated in decision-making, which shows a structure of shared leadership.

<!-- p96 -->
### The church in Antioch

<!-- p97 -->
The church in Antioch, compared to Jerusalem, had a more decentralized and diverse leadership structure. This church was led by a group of **prophets and teachers**, including Barnabas, Saul (Paul), Simeon, Lucius, and Manaen (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 13:1</span>). The church in Antioch was also much more focused on bringing the message to the Gentiles, while the church in Jerusalem reflected its Jewish roots more. Although the New Testament does not explicitly mention deacons in Antioch, given the church’s growth in size and its participation in sending aid to Jerusalem, practical roles likely existed (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29-30" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 11:29-30</span>). The church in Antioch became a model for sending missionaries; the leaders, with prayer, fasting, and the laying on of hands, sent Paul and Barnabas off (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 13:2-3</span>). This shows a structure that balanced spiritual oversight (prophets and teachers) with outward mission and practical support.

<!-- p98 -->
Unlike Paul’s admonitions to the Corinthians and Galatians, Scripture does not criticize the leadership structures of Jerusalem or Antioch. This indicates that these churches, under the guidance of the Holy Spirit, were free to adapt their congregations to meet their particular needs and circumstances within the framework of sound teaching and apostolic doctrine (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:42</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>).

<!-- p99 -->
Throughout these sections, terms are used that we have not yet defined and studied: apostle, prophet, evangelist. These gifts are part of the fivefold ministry that Jesus gave to the church.

<!-- p100 -->
### The Fivefold Ministry Gifts

<!-- p101 -->
Jesus established five distinct ministry gifts, often known as the Fivefold Ministry Gifts, to equip believers for service and to build up the body of Christ.

<!-- p102 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p103 -->
> **<u>Ephesians 4:11–13</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And He Himself gave some to be apostles, some prophets, some evangelists, and some pastors and teachers,
>
> for the equipping of the saints for the work of ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ,
>
> till we all come to the unity of the faith and of the knowledge of the Son of God, to a perfect man, to the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ;
>

<!-- p104 -->
</span>

<!-- p105 -->
The five gifts given to equip the saints for the work of ministry are:

<!-- p106 -->
1.  **Apostles** - pioneers and visionaries who lay foundations, plant churches, and advance the kingdom of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:20</span>). Apostles model boldness and faith and show how to step into new territories and take spiritual initiative.

<!-- p107 -->
2.  **Prophets** - messengers and guides who declare God’s truth and bring encouragement, edification, and guidance (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>). Prophets inspire faith, call others to align with God’s word, and demonstrate reliance on hearing God’s voice.

<!-- p108 -->
3.  **Evangelists** - proclaimers of the gospel who focus on reaching the lost and stir others to action in sharing Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:5–8</span>). Evangelists teach disciples how to share their faith effectively, model boldness in witness, and awaken compassion for the lost.

<!-- p109 -->
4.  **Pastors (shepherds)** - caretakers and nurturers who protect the church, provide guidance, and offer spiritual oversight (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>). Pastors teach disciples the importance of community, caring for others, and accountability in the body of Christ.

<!-- p110 -->
5.  **Teachers** - instructors and coaches who clarify doctrine and help believers grow in understanding and applying God’s word (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 2:15</span>). Teachers equip disciples to study Scripture deeply, apply it in daily life, and teach others effectively.

<!-- p111 -->
## Leadership versus Gifts

<!-- p112 -->
One commonly misunderstood aspect of leadership in the church is the distinction between the authority and responsibility of leadership on the one hand, and specific gifts and endowments on the other.

<!-- p113 -->
This confusion centers on how we use terms like teacher, pastor, prophet, apostle, evangelist. These terms are gifts. In the New Testament church there is no “office” of ‘apostle.’ There are people who do the work of an apostle, who have the gift or calling of an apostle. Having the gift of an apostle does not confer authority or responsibility on them.

<!-- p114 -->
I am a skilled musician; I have been playing the saxophone for over 20 years. I played in high school and college, and for a few summers I performed semi-professionally while partnering with an evangelistic team. Even though I have the skill, gift, and ability of a saxophonist, I do not have the right to walk into your church and stand on stage next to the worship team.

<!-- p115 -->
Likewise, there are people who have a gift for pastoring/shepherding, but that does not make them the ‘lead pastor’ of the local church, nor does it make them ‘your pastor.’ The fivefold ministry gifts do not lead the church; it is the leaders (elders and deacons) who lead. It is common for leaders to have particular gifts from the fivefold ministries, but that gift strengthens their leadership rather than creating it. It is also common for people whose fivefold gifts are still developing to serve and aspire to leadership, but God prioritizes character first and then gifts. We all must mature, be able to discern the word of God, be filled with the Spirit, and do good works.

<!-- p116 -->
Every leader and every gift must first of all submit to being a disciple and a member of the body.

<!-- p117 -->
Jesus gave specific ministry gifts to equip the saints for the work of ministry. These differ from motivational gifts (which are examined later in this chapter) or manifestational gifts (which are covered in the chapter on baptisms). As disciples grow, the fivefold ministry gifts equip them for service, while elders and deacons oversee to ensure sound teaching and practice.

<!-- p118 -->
## Your Role in the Church

<!-- p119 -->
So far in this chapter we have spoken about church leadership and the primary metaphors for the church. Let’s build on the specific metaphor of the “body” and consider a marathon runner.

<!-- p120 -->
### You are part of the whole

<!-- p121 -->
The Church, as the body of Christ, depends on the cooperation of all the members to fulfill its mission. Just as a body cannot function properly if a part of it is missing, the Church needs every believer to participate actively, grow, and serve. Every member of the body has a very important role. Being part of the body means staying connected. A body might survive with reduced capacity without an organ, but that organ itself cannot live when separated from the body. We must be an active part of the Church and not become disconnected. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:24</span> says that we should gather together and not forsake the assembly.

<!-- p122 -->
To truly work and function together you need fellowship! You need to know others, love others, and ideally even like others! Build strong relationships and friendships, and repair them when they become strained or hurt. When a joint is injured or inflamed, you need to work to rest it or set it back in place; so that a dislocated joint does not break! A limb that is broken and left untreated will eventually be separated, and you cannot be part of the body on your own.

<!-- p123 -->
We do not all have the same function. Some of us may proclaim the gospel from a stage, others around a dinner table. But we are all called to be ministers of reconciliation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 5:18-19</span>). We have all been brought into the priesthood of believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:9</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 1:6</span>). This means all of us should be able to do the work of ministry by the power of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p124 -->
Toes work with feet, feet with ankles, and so on-each part shares in the mission. Marathon runners must train if they want to finish the race. You must be prepared-not only by learning, but by practicing what you have learned. The Church, like a marathon runner, flourishes when each member plays its role in harmony.

<!-- p125 -->
There are many people in your life whom only you may be able to reach. The pastor cannot be the only one trying to shepherd them, and the evangelist cannot be the only one trying to reach them. God wants to use you by the power of His Holy Spirit to reach unbelievers. We all do the work of ministry; the Holy Spirit helps us.

<!-- p126 -->
### You need training

<!-- p127 -->
Believers must be trained and equipped for their unique roles in the Church, just as a runner strengthens specific muscles for a race. If you enter a marathon without training you will not succeed, and not all training is the same; you need to be adequately prepared for the race. Jesus has given specific ministry gifts to equip believers, not so that they do everything themselves, but to empower each member to serve effectively in their particular roles.

<!-- p128 -->
Training for a marathon brings transformation. You need strength and you need to be transformed. It is our responsibility to present ourselves to God and let Him transform us in renewal (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:1-2</span>). Transformation should lead you to maturity, so that you can carry your own loads and support others. You should be able to bear much fruit. This will be discussed in detail in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p129 -->
Training is hard, sometimes uncomfortable. Sometimes you would prefer to ignore the transformation you need to go through. But if you want to reach the goal, you must stay on the narrow path. At times you may need warning, comfort, or support-all offered with patience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Thessalonians 5:14</span>).

<!-- p130 -->
Every great marathon runner has a coach to train them; you too will need a coach to help you. Of course you have the Holy Spirit, but He often relies on His coaches, mature men and women of God, in the body of Christ whom you are called to work alongside.

<!-- p131 -->
As individuals, first and foremost we must submit ourselves to God and then, in this context, to a body of believers. Submission in the Church means honoring godly leadership (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 13:17</span>) and submitting to one another in humility (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:21</span>).

<!-- p132 -->
Submitting can be difficult, especially when you have previously experienced mistrust or failed leadership. This does not mean blind obedience or control, but reflects a spirit of honor and cooperation. Worldly submission is demanded by those in power and can be humiliating and lead to resentment. Biblical submission, rooted in humility and discernment, strengthens the Church and fosters accountability. Be willing to listen to guidance and constructive feedback.

<!-- p133 -->
Now that we have laid a solid foundation-you are a meaningful part of the body but you need training-let us move on to where you fit.

<!-- p134 -->
### Finding where you fit

<!-- p135 -->
Finding your role in the Church involves seeking God’s guidance, understanding your spiritual gifts, and actively engaging with your church community. It is a journey of prayer, seeking, and growth in which you discover how God has uniquely equipped you to serve His kingdom. It is very important to note that your role in the Church will likely change over time and is unlikely to remain the same throughout your life.

<!-- p136 -->
Here is a practical approach to help you find your place, they are presented as a set of steps for clarity, but you will likely repeat the same step many times throughout your walk with God.

<!-- p137 -->
#### Step 1 - Seek God’s guidance through prayer and the Word

<!-- p138 -->
Begin by **seeking God’s guidance through prayer**. God is the ultimate source of your purpose and calling (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="29" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 29:11</span>). Pray regularly for clarity on how you can serve in the Church, and ask Him to reveal your spiritual gifts and passions. Trust that when you submit your plans to His will, He will direct your steps (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 3:5-6</span>).

<!-- p139 -->
**Study God’s Word** to deepen your understanding of the Church’s mission and your role in it. Key passages such as 1 Corinthians 12 and <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-12</span> highlight the spiritual gifts and the diversity of roles in the body of Christ. Reflect on examples of people in Scripture who discovered and fulfilled their roles, such as Paul, Timothy, and Lydia.

<!-- p140 -->
#### Step 2 - Identify your gifts, passions, and skills

<!-- p141 -->
Identifying your **spiritual gifts** is a foundational step. Taking a spiritual gifts assessment may be helpful for you. These gifts are given by God for the building up of the church. Several categories of gifts are listed in the New Testament:

<!-- p142 -->
- **Motivational gifts** given by God: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:6-8</span>

<!-- p143 -->
- Discussed following this list.

<!-- p144 -->
- The fivefold ministry gifts given by Jesus: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-13</span>

<!-- p145 -->
- Discussed earlier in this chapter.

<!-- p146 -->
- Manifestation gifts given by the Holy Spirit: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span>

<!-- p147 -->
- Discussed in the chapter on baptism.

<!-- p148 -->
**Motivational gifts** reflect how a believer thinks, feels, and acts. These gifts motivate believers to serve in specific ways that build up the church. Each gift complements the others so that the church functions as one body with diverse roles (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:4-5</span>). They operate as grace-given abilities (Greek: ***charis** - khar'-ece - **G5463 -** an unearned gift*); that is, they are given by God, not earned or learned. Although the gifts are from God, the person who has a gift can become more skilled in applying it.

<!-- p149 -->
The motivational gifts are:

<!-- p150 -->
1.  **Prophecy** - a God-given inclination to boldly declare truth, usually with a focus on conviction and righteousness, guiding others into alignment with the Word of God.*

<!-- p151 -->
2.  **Service (Helps)** - meets practical needs through acts of service and help to support others.

<!-- p152 -->
3.  **Teaching** - explains and expounds the truth, helping others understand and apply the Word of God.

<!-- p153 -->
4.  **Exhortation (Encouragement)** - motivates, encourages, and comforts to inspire growth and perseverance.

<!-- p154 -->
5.  **Giving** - provides resources generously to meet needs and advance God’s work, without seeking recognition.

<!-- p155 -->
6.  **Leadership (Administration)** - organizes and guides others to achieve goals with vision and efficiency.

<!-- p156 -->
7.  **Mercy** - shows compassion and care to comfort and support those who are hurting or in need.

<!-- p157 -->
* “Prophecy” in the context of Romans is understood differently from “prophecy” as a manifestation gift of the Holy Spirit (1 Corinthians). The Greek word used in both passages of Scripture is the same, but the context differs. For a deeper study of the distinction, I recommend reading:

<!-- p158 -->
- *The Gift of Prophecy* by Kenneth E. Hagin ([www.rhema.org](http://www.rhema.org))

<!-- p159 -->
<!-- -->

<!-- p160 -->
- *Gifts and Ministries of the Holy Spirit* by Lester Sumrall

<!-- p161 -->
When identifying your gifts, pay attention to your interests and skills. God often calls us to serve in areas that we are interested in or skilled in (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 31:2-6</span>). Reflect on what energizes you in serving God and others, and consider how your professional skills, interests, and life experiences can be leveraged in service.

<!-- p162 -->
Seek guidance from leaders and mentors who can offer wisdom and insight (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 15:22</span>). Meet with a pastor or ministry leader to share your interests and receive honest feedback about a role that might be a good fit for you. Pray over the feedback you receive. Join a discipleship group for ongoing training and support.

<!-- p163 -->
#### Step 3 - Be faithful now

<!-- p164 -->
The parable of the "Talents" (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-30" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 25:14-30</span>) reminds us that we must be faithful in small things in order to be entrusted with greater things (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 16:10</span>).

<!-- p165 -->
Get involved and serve to discover where God is calling you. Start small; volunteer in areas of need in your church and in supporting ministries. For example:

<!-- p166 -->
- Join the hospitality team, children’s ministry, or outreach programs. Consider leading a discipleship group for new believers based on this book, *Principles of Faith,* and use the group discussion questions and end-of-chapter activities to facilitate conversation.

<!-- p167 -->
- Be ready to step out of your comfort zone to discover new opportunities.

<!-- p168 -->
- Notice where God is working through you and where you feel more effective.

<!-- p169 -->
Practice generosity. We can give to the church in many significant ways and are called to do so to fulfill the mission.

<!-- p170 -->
- Time - We can give our time and volunteer. When we give time, we put our efforts and talents to work. This can also be a great way to learn new skills and strengthen existing ones.

<!-- p171 -->
- Resources - We can give from our resources. The most common example today is probably financial giving to the church and ministries (not only to “the institution,” but also to those in need within the church). Giving money is helpful because the church can obtain needed resources with it; but people have also given tangible resources beyond money; for example, extra homes, clothing, vehicles, food, building materials, tools, etc. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="32-35" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 4:32-35</span> we see an example of early believers who gave freely, sharing and giving the proceeds from selling extra land and houses so that ‘no one was in need’.

<!-- p172 -->
Serving alongside others can reveal your strengths and deepen your understanding of your role. Participate in small groups or church activities to cultivate connections. Listen to the burdens and needs of those around you and consider how you can support them. Building relationships within the church helps you connect with others and better understand the needs of the community.

<!-- p173 -->
#### Step 4 - Get help!

<!-- p174 -->
Jesus said that in life we will have trouble! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:33</span>) We are encouraged not to carry our heavy burden alone, but to cast it on God who cares for us! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:7</span>)

<!-- p175 -->
At times along the way we may get injured. Marathon runners, even when they "do everything right," can push their bodies to the point of injury, or they may fall.

<!-- p176 -->
Not all “falls” are due to sin, though some are; not all weaknesses are “our fault”; sometimes others sin against us. Regardless of the cause of the weakness or injury we have, we need to be restored and healed.

<!-- p177 -->
Seeking inner healing and restoration brings great freedom. We are called to cover the vulnerable parts of the body (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:23</span>) and to restore those caught in a fault (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:1</span>).

<!-- p178 -->
There is much that can be said about this topic, and in the chapter on sanctification we will, as an overview, explore themes of healing.

<!-- p179 -->
#### Step 5 - Be patient

<!-- p180 -->
Pay attention to affirmation from God and others. Often, God confirms your calling through fruitfulness, joy, and the affirmation of those around you. Reflect on where your efforts have borne fruit and listen to feedback about the impact of your service. This can help you discern where you are most effective in serving the body of Christ.

<!-- p181 -->
Be patient and trust God’s timing. Discovering your role may take time and involve trial and error (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 1:6</span>). Be confident that God will guide you as long as you remain faithful and open to His leading. In the meantime, commit yourself to growth and training by attending workshops, Bible studies, and other resources that equip you for ministry. Do this with the church; engage with the body of Christ to discover your gifts.

<!-- p182 -->
Remember, the church is not a building or an institution; it is a living, dynamic body made up of people like you. By stepping into your role, you contribute to its mission and glorify Christ, who is its head.
