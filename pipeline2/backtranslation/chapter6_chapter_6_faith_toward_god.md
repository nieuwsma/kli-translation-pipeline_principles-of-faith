# Chapter 6 - Faith Toward God

<!-- p1 -->
Faith is a foundational concept in Christianity. Faith is a word used so much in Christianity that at times the meaning of the word gets distorted by hidden misconceptions and inaccurate understanding. But it is not something you can hold in your hands-it is something formed in your heart as you begin to trust God. Persuasion is faith.

<!-- p2 -->
The writer of Hebrews teaches us that we should have ‘faith toward God’.

<!-- p3 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV">
<!-- p4 -->

</span>
<!-- p6 -->
In this chapter we will discuss what faith is, and what it is not, how to increase our faith, how we use our faith (obedience), the benefits of living a life of obedience, and practical steps to increase our faith.

<!-- p7 -->
As we did in our lesson on repentance, let us look at the Greek to lay the foundation for our lesson. Understanding these terms helps us see how faith is directional (toward God) and relational (rooted in believing and persuasion).

### Defining Faith

<!-- p10 -->
Faith is an aspect of Christianity that is often misunderstood and misrepresented. As the saying goes, “there is a ditch on both sides of the road”! Because the word faith is used so much without sufficient explanation in our modern Christianity, it can take on distorted interpretations reinforced by misunderstanding. Often faith is presented either as a general statement of abstract belief or as a near-mystical or “magical” power, something you possess in a specific and limited measure.

<!-- p11 -->
#### What Faith Is Not

<!-- p12 -->
When faith is presented <u>abstractly</u> it has no specific stance or persuasion. This is often evident upon a little examination, because this picture of faith cannot be defined with specificity. If you ask, ‘what do you believe’ or ‘why do you believe’ a person with this understanding of faith often cannot explain beyond ‘I believe.’ <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV">1 Peter 3:15</span> says that we should be ready to give an answer for the hope that is in us.

<!-- p13 -->
Sometimes people think faith is like a magical power-but that is not what Scripture teaches. Faith is about a real relationship built on trust. In Scripture, faith is more vivid and relational-it is rooted in persuasion, believing, and assurance in God’s character and promises. It is not an impersonal force but a personal response to God that shapes thoughts, feelings, and actions.

<!-- p14 -->
Why is faith related to persuasion?

<!-- p15 -->
Our beliefs shape how we see and interact with the world around us. Beliefs shape perspective and drive our actions. That is how our minds are designed-we live according to what we believe is possible.

<!-- p16 -->
We carry out what we see, understand, and believe is possible. As we walk down a hallway, our brain recognizes the boundaries of the floor and the wall and automatically tells our feet where we can place steps, and what to avoid. This reality extends beyond our physical navigation of the world around us.

<!-- p17 -->
Suppose you believe that it is impossible for you to be financially free or to live a healthy and active lifestyle. Then your actions will follow that belief, and you, being faithful to that belief, will not act or may not even try what you believe to be impossible.

<!-- p18 -->
If you believe that God has an unloving and punitive character, that view of Him will keep you from walking with Him as His child.

<!-- p19 -->
#### Faith Is Persuasion

<!-- p20 -->
So, what is faith? Persuasion! The Greek word ***pistis*** means persuasion, or the result of being persuaded. We should have persuasion toward God. In English we might say faith (persuasion) ‘**in** God’. If you are married, you may have faith ‘**in’** your spouse. You believe that they will do what they said they would do, and you believe that they are real and true. This trust should exceed what they have said, and extend to them overall.

<!-- p21 -->
Developing faith, persuasion, and trust in a relationship takes time and experience. Hopefully in life you have learned how to cultivate a trusting relationship with another person. The principles you learned that allow you to increase your trust toward someone you know will also enable you to increase your faith toward God. Faith in a relationship grows over time, in experience, through consistency, and through small and large actions that build your confidence in that other person’s character and nature.

<!-- p22 -->
‘Faith toward God’ is relational. Relationships are built by two parties, not one. The good news about our faith toward God is that God is responsible for His side of the relationship (made possible by the atoning death of Jesus, and the filling of His Spirit at the new birth), and He also helps us on our side (through the work of the Comforter - the Holy Spirit). God does almost all the work; our part (as we will discuss later in this chapter) is to walk *WITH HIM* in obedience.

<!-- p23 -->
### Jesus Matures Our Faith

<!-- p24 -->
Jesus is the author and finisher of our faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 12:2</span>). Our faith is intended to continue to grow and expand always. We do not reach a point where we have enough ‘faith’, rather our faith increases. Think of a deep and personal relationship you have in your life today. As in any relationship we do not arrive at a particular point where we now ‘know’ that person. We continue to know them, and increase our knowledge about them, and this refines, shapes, and grows our relationship.

<!-- p25 -->
How can we grow our faith toward God? By continuing to experience Him in His many revelations, manifestations, and attributes. Again, think of a deep personal relationship in your life. You increase the depth of the relationship by engaging with them in different ways, in different formats, in different media, in different settings. You talk to them, you send them messages, you might write to them. You see them personally, and in groups. You talk about what you value, and perhaps you challenge one another. Those many avenues are endless. Through these many manifestations you come to know that person more intimately.

<!-- p26 -->
We all should know God, through Jesus, as our Savior. Consider the names of God (*see the appendix),* that we know God as Savior does not mean that we have already known Him as Redeemer, Healer, Counselor, Lord.

<!-- p27 -->
If you are in a place where you have been persuaded (faith) to know God as your Savior, building this relationship will give you confidence in His character so that you may know Him in other ways.

<!-- p28 -->
I have been blessed to have several very deep relationships in my life. One of those relationships is with my wife. In our relationship, because of the level of trust (faith) we have in each other, my wife can tell me things she will do, which I have not yet seen manifest, and I have the confidence to trust her because of her proven character.

<!-- p29 -->
This example shows how our deep relationship with God can be experienced, with trust and confidence! We may not have fully experienced Him yet (indeed we will not fully experience Him in this life), but as we mature in our relationship, we can trust Him beyond the limits of what I personally know, and what I have already seen happen.

<!-- p30 -->
Jesus increases our faith through experience with Him. A key way we can and should experience Him is through His Word.

### Faith Increases by the Word of God

<!-- p32 -->
Consider this passage from Romans:

<!-- p33 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV">
<!-- p34 -->
&gt; **<u>Romans 10:16-17</u>** (NKJV) **16** *But they have not all obeyed the gospel. For Isaiah says, "LORD, WHO HAS BELIEVED OUR REPORT?"* ***17** So then faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God.*

<!-- p35 -->
</span>
<!-- p36 -->
When we substitute the word *faith* with *persuasion* it reads:

<!-- p37 -->
*‘So then persuasion comes by hearing and hearing by the Word of God’*. This truth is clearly evident by nature. The primary way a person gains persuasion is by being persuaded (assured), and we become assured when someone lays out an ‘argument’-or provides logical and well-explained reasoning.

<!-- p38 -->
There are two Greek words for ‘Word’ in the Bible-**logos** and **rhema**. Understanding the distinction helps us see how faith grows.

<!-- p39 -->
**Logos**: generally refers to the **concept, idea, reason, or broad expression of the word of God**. It encompasses universal or foundational truth or message, and is often used to describe the eternal wisdom of God and Christ Himself as the living Word.

<!-- p40 -->
**Rhema (G4487)**: emphasizes a specific word, utterance, or declaration. It usually refers to a particular word or message given at a certain time, especially a spoken or revealed word that applies directly to a person's situation.

<!-- p41 -->
*Logos* is like the overall message of a book, whereas *rhema* is like a specific passage or sentence that whispers directly to a person at a certain moment. While *logos* is the grand truth of God, *rhema* is when a portion of that truth is spoken to an individual in a personal and applicable way. Together, they display the fullness of God's communication: *logos* as broad, foundational truths about God's character and will, and *rhema* as the specific, practical word that makes that truth come alive in a person's life and situation.

<!-- p42 -->
### An Example of a Rhema Word in Action

<!-- p43 -->
God promises revelation and wisdom to all who ask. When He speaks to us, that utterance is a rhema word. Rhema words come from God and serve to confirm, guide, and direct us. They do not take the place of the *logos* word, nor do they carry the authority of Scripture. Rhema words should be tested by the same standard as prophecy or interpretation of tongues (see the chapter on the Holy Spirit for an in-depth study of this topic).

<!-- p44 -->
At several points in my work, I have asked for divine guidance. A few years ago, I was considering beginning the process of looking for a new opportunity, and during my prayer time, I heard God say to me, ‘*Just wait, I have an opportunity for you.’* I was starting to look for a new job because I was not satisfied with my current position and felt discouraged. God's directional word ‘*wait*’ helped me find peace and confidence in a season that otherwise would have been anxious.

<!-- p45 -->
Three or four months passed; I continued to work hard and faithfully, maintaining a positive attitude. God kept reminding me, ‘wait,’ and then His promise was fulfilled. I received an opportunity for a promotion and a significant increase in salary.

<!-- p46 -->
I could have continued my job search; I could have left. I could have started somewhere else. Surely someone else would have gotten that promotion! But because I sought the Lord's counsel and was convinced by His **rhema**, I obeyed and benefited. God's words are **living and personal**, and He promises to give us abundant, generous wisdom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV">James 1:5</span>).

<!-- p47 -->
I have several, if not hundreds, of instances where I prayed and asked God what to do, and He gave me a rhema word. Not every one had equally dramatic results, but all of them brought a sense of peace and confidence that I was obeying God and that He would work things out. They were exactly the words I needed at that time! Often, when God brings you a rhema word, it comes from the logos. He will remind me of a passage of Scripture, “Do not fear, for I am with you!” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="41" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV">Isaiah 41:10</span>), “By His stripes we are healed” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="53" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV">Isaiah 53:5</span>), “Do not grow weary in doing good!” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV">Galatians 6:9</span>), the examples go on!

<!-- p48 -->
Receiving a rhema word not only strengthens our faith but also deepens our relationship with God, as we trust Him more fully in our daily decisions. We will continue our lesson on learning to hear God in the chapter on the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p49 -->
Our faith is also strengthened by what we see God doing, but sometimes what we observe in the natural world contradicts what we hear from God. Reconciling the discrepancy between what we see and what we hear is an essential ministry of the Holy Spirit, who promises to guide us into all truth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV">John 16:13</span>).

### Faith Greater Than What We Can See

<!-- p51 -->
After His resurrection, Jesus appeared several times to many of His disciples. Thomas (one of the Twelve) was not with the other disciples, so he had missed the appearance of the risen Savior. Thomas believed in Jesus, and he trusted his fellow disciples, but he did not believe that Jesus had been bodily raised; he insisted that he could not fully believe until he experienced it for himself.

<!-- p52 -->
<u>**<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24-29" data-version="NKJV">John 20:24-29</span>**</u> (NKJV)

<!-- p53 -->
***24** Now Thomas, called the Twin, one of the Twelve, was not with them when Jesus came.*

<!-- p54 -->
***25** So the other disciples said to him, "We have seen the Lord." And he said to them, "Unless I see in His hands the marks of the nails, and put my finger into the marks of the nails, and put my hand into His side, I will not believe."*

<!-- p55 -->
***26** And after eight days His disciples were again inside, and Thomas with them. Jesus came, the doors being shut, stood in the midst, and said, "Peace be with you!"*

<!-- p56 -->
***27** Then He said to Thomas, "Reach your finger here, and look at My hands; and reach your hand here, and put it into My side. Do not be unbelieving, but believing."*

<!-- p57 -->
***28** Thomas answered and said to Him, "My Lord and my God!"*

<!-- p58 -->
***29** Jesus said to him, "Thomas, because you have seen Me, you have believed. Blessed are those who have not seen and yet have believed."*

<!-- p59 -->
When Jesus appeared to His disciples and startled them (I imagine it's a scary thing when someone shows up in your midst, having passed through locked doors), He spoke to Thomas. He did not rebuke or blame Thomas nor accuse him of being part of a wicked and perverse generation that seeks a sign (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 16:4</span>) but He revealed Himself to Thomas.

<!-- p60 -->
Jesus goes on to say, ‘*blessed are those who have not seen and have believe*d’’. This is not Jesus promoting ‘blind or uninformed faith’ or rejecting the use of empirical evidence. Rather, He is saying that we are more blessed when we can believe without needing empirical proof. Why? If our faith, our hope, were limited only to what we have seen verified in an empirical way, we would never be able to have confidence about future work. We would have great confidence regarding what has already been done but we would not walk in the peace of God concerning our tomorrows and our days ahead.

<!-- p61 -->
Using the example of a relationship, how challenging would my relationship with my wife be if my outlook were always ‘<u>I will believe once I see it!</u>’? Instead, a relationship built on trust says ‘I know you are a trustworthy person’.

<!-- p62 -->
If we could only have faith in what we have seen manifested, then Paul’s encouragement by the Holy Spirit to us would have no effect:

<!-- p63 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p65 -->
</span>
<!-- p66 -->
How could the peace of God guard our hearts if we could not have confidence in Him beyond what we have already seen!?

<!-- p67 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV">
<!-- p68 -->

<!-- p69 -->
</span>
<!-- p70 -->
It is in cultivating our relationship with God that we can move beyond only what we can see or naturally understand. Like the account of Abraham found in Romans:

<!-- p71 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="19-22" data-version="NKJV">

***19** And not being weak in faith, he did not consider his own body, already as good as dead (for he was about a hundred years old), and the deadness of Sarah’s womb.*

<!-- p75 -->
***20** He did not waver at the promise of God through unbelief, but was strengthened in faith, giving glory to God,*

<!-- p76 -->
***21** and being fully convinced that what He had promised, He was also able to perform.*

My relationship with my wife began when we started dating. As we came to know each other more deeply and built our relationship, we moved from merely being acquaintances, to being friends, to being romantic, to reaching the point where we wanted to commit to one another in marriage. On the basis of this rich history and this promise, we are building a family and a life together. In the same way, our faith in God should move forward. We grow from knowing about God, to knowing God, to committing ourselves to Him (receiving His Salvation), then building our lives upon that relationship.

### Faith Produces Obedience and Good Works

<!-- p80 -->
Our relationship of trust with God is strengthened through obedience, resulting in good works! These works do not save us nor do they earn us God’s love—they are the natural outcome of a life rooted in Him.

<!-- p81 -->
The Scriptures present a natural progression of faith. As we know God and taste His presence, we grow in trusting Him. This faith pushes us to act in a way that aligns with our belief, leading us to obey His commandments. Our obedience produces good works, which bring a harvest of positive and beneficial results.

<!-- p82 -->
Reflect again on this passage from Romans:

<!-- p83 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV">
<!-- p84 -->
&gt; **<u>Romans 10:16-17</u>** (NKJV) **16*** But they have not all obeyed the gospel. For Isaiah says, “LORD, WHO HAS BELIEVED OUR REPORT?” **17** So then faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God.*

<!-- p85 -->
</span>
<!-- p86 -->
Breaking this down in reverse order:

<!-- p87 -->
1.  Hearing the Word (**rhema**) of God produces faith (**pistis**)

<!-- p88 -->
2.  Faith, which is the state of believing or being persuaded, will produce the action or verb of believing.

<!-- p89 -->
1.  Again, consider a person who believes they can be free from debt and acts accordingly!

<!-- p90 -->
3.  Our act of believing is expressed in obedience.

<!-- p91 -->
The word ‘obeyed’ is **hupakouō (***hoop-ak-oo'-o **- G5219)***: **hupakouō** means to *hear under* (as one *under authority*), that is, to *listen attentively*; in the sense of *give heed* or *follow* a command or authority: - hear, be obedient to, obey.

<!-- p92 -->
In a work setting, when a boss or supervisor gives instructions, they are not “just talking,” but issuing directions they intend their employees to follow.

<!-- p93 -->
When we truly listen to God, that should lead us to action—to believe Him and obey Him. As the Scriptures say in:

<!-- p94 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV">
<!-- p95 -->
&gt; **<u>James 1:22</u>** (NKJV) *But be doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving yourselves.*

<!-- p96 -->
</span>
<!-- p97 -->
Consider again the example of financial freedom. A person with debt who desires to be free from that burden will seek financial counsel. The counselor will teach them the principles of financial freedom and help them create a budget. This counsel will bring (confidence / faith) in what they need to do to be debt-free. But confidence is only the first step; the person must obey the principles they learned through disciplined practice to receive the good work and the blessing of being debt-free.

<!-- p98 -->
The book of James says that our faith must produce works! We already know what works are (for example, ‘dead works’ from <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 6:1</span>).

<!-- p99 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="14-20" data-version="NKJV">
<!-- p100 -->
&gt; **<u>James 2:14-20</u>** (NKJV)
&gt;
&gt;**14** *What does it profit, my brothers, if someone says he has faith but does not have works? Can faith save him?*
&gt;
&gt;***15** If a brother or sister is naked and destitute of daily food,*
&gt;
&gt;***16** and one of you says to them, "Go in peace, be warmed and filled," but you do not give them the things needed for the body, what does it profit?*
&gt;
&gt;***17** Thus also faith by itself, if it does not have works, is dead.*
&gt;
&gt;***18** But someone will say, "You have faith, and I have works." Show me your faith without your works, and I will show you my faith by my works.*
&gt;
&gt;***19** You believe that God is one. You do well. Even the demons believe-and they tremble!*
&gt;
&gt;***20** But do you want to know, O foolish man, that faith without works is dead?*

<!-- p101 -->
</span>
<!-- p102 -->
What stands out to you from that passage in James? For me the key sentence is that even the demons believe! Believing alone is not enough. Our faith must rise to the level of obedience. We need and want to obey Jesus, our risen Savior and Lord.

<!-- p103 -->
We do not receive salvation by works! When we receive salvation (by faith) we do works! Let me repeat that: you cannot work hard enough, nor offer enough sacrifices to obtain salvation; it is God’s free gift that we receive by faith!

<!-- p104 -->
The result of these works (obedience) is that they bring about a good and profitable harvest. The time it takes to receive the harvest, or the measure of that harvest, varies, but it will produce a good harvest! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV">Genesis 8:22</span>).

<!-- p105 -->
Recalling again our lesson about sin and sacrifice, we know that several sacrifices instituted in the Old Testament were for sin, so that a person might be reconciled to God or to his fellow man. However, there is a more desirable way!

<!-- p106 -->
<u>**<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1SA" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV">1 Samuel 15:22</span>**</u> (NKJV) *Then Samuel said: "Has the LORD as great delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices, as in obeying the voice of the LORD? Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, and to heed than the fat of rams."*

<!-- p107 -->
The truth that has endured since the Old Testament is that obedience is better than sacrifice. It is better to follow precisely what we ought to do than to need to offer a sacrifice to make up for what we did not do correctly. Fortunately, there is grace from God when we lose our way, miss the mark (sin), and need to return to God.

<!-- p108 -->
As we build our faith in God, it will naturally bear obedience within us. This obedience is for our benefit and yields a good harvest. Returning to my personal example from this chapter, I would not have found peace in that season, nor the blessing of a promotion, if I had not obeyed God’s word to me to *wait for Him*!

### Practical Steps to Grow Your Faith

<!-- p153 -->
Walking with God is a matter of relationship, but there are skills to learn if you want healthy relationships. In human relationships, the skills that produce strong and secure relationships are built on:

<!-- p154 -->
- Conversation and open and free communication

<!-- p155 -->
- Conflict resolution in a healthy way

<!-- p156 -->
- Commitment

<!-- p157 -->
- Consistency

<!-- p158 -->
You must show up consistently, reveal yourself (communicate) yourself (our thoughts and feelings), receive the other (listen), and walk with them, if you want a strong, living relationship! All these skills apply to our relationship with God.

<!-- p159 -->
Beyond the basic relational skills, there are specific practices and disciplines that will strengthen your relationship with God:

<!-- p160 -->
- **Build and Maintain a Life of Prayer, Listen and Communicate with God.** Frequent and heartfelt prayer is essential for an intimate relationship with God. Through prayer, you can share your concerns, your joys, and your thanksgivings, seeking His guidance and strength. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV">Philippians 4:6-7</span>*) Being sensitive to the voice of the Holy Spirit helps you discern God’s will, gain wisdom in decisions, and grow in spiritual maturity. Set aside time for quiet reflection to hear His direction. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV">Romans 8:14</span>*)

<!-- p161 -->
- **Spend Time in Worship.** Worship is a powerful way to draw near to God, expressing love and reverence. Through praise, song, or quiet reflection, worship heightens your awareness of His presence. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="Psalm" data-chapter="100" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-4" data-version="NKJV">Psalm 100:2-4</span>*)

<!-- p162 -->
- **Read, Study, and Meditate on Scripture.** God’s Word reveals His character, His promises, and His instructions for life. Setting aside time to study Scripture helps you grow in faith, understanding, and spiritual resilience. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>*)

<!-- p163 -->
- **Obey His Commands.** Obedience to God’s commands demonstrates your love, reverence, and trust in Him. Walking in obedience aligns your life with His purpose and brings blessing. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV">John 14:15</span>*)

<!-- p164 -->
- **Attend a local church diligently and serve.** Engaging with the church community provides spiritual support, encouragement, and opportunities to serve others. Participation helps build a sense of belonging and strengthens your commitment to God’s mission. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24-25" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 10:24-25</span>*)

<!-- p165 -->
- **Build Relationships with Other Believers.** Fellowship with other Christians provides accountability, encouragement, and shared wisdom. These relationships enrich your faith journey and help you grow through mutual support. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="27" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV">Proverbs 27:17</span>*)

<!-- p166 -->
Our ‘faith toward God’ is not a one-time event for salvation but an ongoing practice and relationship we must cultivate throughout our lives. The benefits of a trusting and beneficial relationship are many, but we must do the good work of nurturing that trust and growth so it thrives.
