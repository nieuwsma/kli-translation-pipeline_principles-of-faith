# Chapter 8 - The Holy Spirit-Comforter, Guide, and Power

<!-- p1 -->
The Holy Spirit is not a new spirit but is of God and has been with God since the very beginning. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV">Genesis 1:2</span> the Spirit of God was hovering over the waters during creation. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="NEH" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV">Nehemiah 9:20</span>, Scripture says that it was the Spirit who taught the people in the wilderness. It was by that same Holy Spirit that God anointed kings, judges, and prophets in the Old Testament. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JOL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28-29" data-version="NKJV">Joel 2:28-29</span> the Spirit is promised to be poured out on all mankind. It was by that same Holy Spirit that God raised Jesus from the dead, and He now dwells in us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV">Romans 8:11</span>). There is only one Holy Spirit, and He has always been the same. The way He works with people has appeared different at different times, but His heart has never changed.

<!-- p2 -->
Jesus gave His disciples, and all of us, the powerful promise of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p3 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV">
<!-- p4 -->
&gt; **<u>John 14:16-18</u>** (NKJV) **16** *And I will pray the Father, and He will give you another Helper, that He may abide with you forever-**17** the Spirit of truth, whom the world cannot receive, because it neither sees Him nor knows Him; but you know Him, for He dwells with you and will be in you.* ***18** I will not leave you orphans; I will come to you.*

<!-- p5 -->
</span>
<!-- p6 -->
When Jesus was walking the earth in His physical body, He had limitations like we do; His body needed the same things our bodies need: rest, water, food, sleep, etc. This limitation affected not only Jesus, but also His followers, because He could be in only one place at a time. Often, as Jesus was traveling, He was pressed by large crowds; they tried to call to Him and to touch Him. Jesus often needed to spend time with His heavenly Father to be renewed and to pray.

<!-- p7 -->
For the Father's work to be multiplied, Jesus needed to raise up disciples, and for them to continue this work. Jesus knew that a crucial aspect of His ministry on earth depended on His being united with and abiding in fellowship with His Father. Indeed, Jesus said He could do nothing unless His Father did it through Him and showed Him to do it (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV">John 5:19</span>). God, in His wisdom, sent His Holy Spirit to dwell in us, to guide us and show us the way, giving us power to do the Father's work.

<!-- p9 -->
Jesus said:

<!-- p10 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV">
<!-- p11 -->

<!-- p12 -->
</span>
<!-- p13 -->
After Jesus was crucified, His death made it possible for the Spirit of God to dwell within us. Until that time, the Spirit of God in a manifest way was largely behind the thick veil in the temple. From time to time, special people were chosen by God and anointed (sanctified and set apart for a unique service), and they received the Holy Spirit upon them. The anointing of God and the Spirit upon them was unique, temporary, and conditional. David prayed after his sin with Bathsheba, 'Do not take Your Holy Spirit from me!' (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="Psalm" data-chapter="51" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV">Psalm 51:11</span>), for David had seen the Spirit depart from King Saul because of his acts of disobedience.

<!-- p14 -->
We (human beings) could not receive the Holy Spirit within us because of our sinful nature, but when Jesus resolved the issue of sin by His sacrifice, it opened the way by faith for us to become children and heirs of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV">Romans 8:17</span>).

<!-- p15 -->
In John 20, after His resurrection, Jesus appeared to His disciples in the middle of a locked room and breathed on them, saying, 'Receive the Holy Spirit' (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV">John 20:22</span>). Theology has debated somewhat about exactly what happened at that time, but many Pentecostal interpretations see that event as the disciples receiving the Spirit of God within them, signifying regeneration, being born again. However, this was not the time when they received the Spirit of God upon them, empowering them for ministry. This was the time when they received the Holy Spirit within them, allowing them to abide and have fellowship with God. This will be discussed in more detail later in this chapter along with the chapters on sanctification and baptism.

The disciples were to wait for the promise of the poured-out Spirit, and that this was to empower them to be witnesses to the ends of the earth. The Holy Spirit is an essential part of God’s plan for humankind, and is necessary for us as believers, for the fulfillment of our mission, and for the world.

## Knowing the Holy Spirit

<!-- p22 -->
The Holy Spirit, first and foremost, comes from God and belongs to God. The Scriptures refer to the Spirit as the Spirit of God, or the Spirit of the Lord, or the Spirit of Christ; though these emphasize different aspects, they are all the same. The Holy Spirit, being fully God, is also eternal and holy (blameless, guiltless, pure, not impure). Other translations refer to the Holy Spirit as “Holy Ghost”.

<!-- p23 -->
The word for spirit in Greek is ***Pneuma** (NYOO-mah -* **G4151***),* meaning breath, or wind. The Hebrew word for spirit has the same meaning. Breath is essential because we need it continuously in order to live. It was the breath of God that He breathed into Adam to give him life; likewise, it was the breath of Jesus that He breathed last before dying on the cross. Without breath there is no life. Breath, like wind, can be felt and sensed but is not easily seen like other ‘elements’ water, fire, earth.

<!-- p24 -->
There are several other basic adjectives used to describe the Holy Spirit. Each of these adjectives is used together with ‘Spirit of’, meaning that these qualities are essential to the description of the Spirit. For example, *Spirit of Truth* or *Spirit of Life*.

<!-- p25 -->
Spirit of:

<!-- p26 -->
- **Truth** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV">John 14:17</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV">John 16:13</span> Refers to the Spirit’s role in guiding believers into all truth, revealing God’s will and the reality of Christ.

<!-- p27 -->
- **Life** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV">Romans 8:2</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV">Galatians 6:8</span> Highlights the Spirit’s role in granting believers spiritual and eternal life, freeing them from the law of sin and death.

<!-- p28 -->
- **Grace** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 10:29</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ZEC" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV">Zechariah 12:10</span> Demonstrates the Spirit’s work of dispensing God’s favor, forgiveness, and unmerited goodness to humanity.

<!-- p29 -->
- **Glory** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV">1 Peter 4:14</span> Shows the Spirit’s role in revealing and manifesting the glory of God, especially in times of suffering and reproach.

<!-- p30 -->
- **Adoption** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV">Romans 8:15</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV">Galatians 4:5</span> Refers to the Spirit’s work of making believers children of God, granting them the status of sons and daughters.

<!-- p31 -->
- **Holiness** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV">Romans 1:4</span> Indicates the sanctifying work of the Spirit, setting believers apart for God’s purposes.

<!-- p32 -->
- **Power** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV">2 Timothy 1:7</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV">Acts 1:8</span> Explains how the Spirit empowers believers for boldness, ministry, and the ability to live out their faith effectively.

<!-- p33 -->
In all these attributes the Holy Spirit is also rightly recognized as the Helper or Comforter. Jesus refers to the Holy Spirit as the coming helper, using the Greek word ***Paraklētos** (*pah-RAH-klay-tos - **G3875**), which means advocate, helper, one called to stand alongside.

<!-- p34 -->
An advocate speaks on behalf of another, like a defense attorney. He intercedes for and helps the one entrusted to him.

<!-- p35 -->
A helper assists. They help to strengthen the work, but their task is essentially to support the work. A helper is not solely responsible to carry out the task, but comes alongside to help it be accomplished.

<!-- p36 -->
The Holy Spirit has several specific purposes and roles in His ministry. His work is internal (in the hearts and lives of believers), upon (in ministry and in knowing God), and external (for our field of mission, the world).

<!-- p37 -->
## Roles of the Holy Spirit-Within, Upon, Outside

<!-- p38 -->
### Within-The Holy Spirit Unites Us with Jesus

<!-- p39 -->
Before Jesus performed any miracles or began His public ministry, He was baptized by John the Baptist and the Spirit of God descended upon Him, and a voice from heaven declared, ‘This is My beloved Son in whom I am well pleased’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 3:17</span>).

<!-- p40 -->
When we receive Jesus by faith, we receive salvation and the seal of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p41 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV">
<!-- p42 -->
&gt; **<u>Ephesians 1:13-14</u>** (NKJV) ***13** In Him you also trusted, after hearing the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation; in Him also, having believed, you were sealed with the Holy Spirit of promise, **14** who is the guarantee of our inheritance until the redemption of the purchased possession, to the praise of His glory.*

<!-- p43 -->
</span>
<!-- p44 -->
This seal is not symbolic but real. It serves as a sign of our redemption and is the guarantee of the full inheritance we receive as children of God. Romans 8 explains that through the new birth we receive in our mortal bodies the very same Spirit of God who raised Christ from the dead, and we become a true temple of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV">2 Corinthians 6:16</span>).

<!-- p45 -->
This receiving of the Spirit of God transforms us, and causes rivers of living water to flow from us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV">John 7:38</span>). This renewal is immediate and complete, yet it continues throughout our entire lives. The river of the Holy Spirit should never cease to flow in our lives!

<!-- p46 -->
Through salvation and the indwelling of the Holy Spirit we can abide in Christ and He in us. The Spirit of God is no longer outside but can dwell intimately with us. We can walk in fellowship and harmony with God from now into everlasting life.

<!-- p47 -->
Much more could be said about the inner work of the Holy Spirit and His role in our sanctification, but that will be addressed in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p48 -->
### On-The Holy Spirit Testifies of Jesus

<!-- p49 -->
The Holy Spirit comes from God and is of God. Just as Jesus said He did nothing except what the Father showed Him (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV">John 5:19</span>), likewise the Holy Spirit does nothing except as directed by the Father. Since the Holy Spirit is the Helper, He does not act by His own will, but works in perfect harmony with the Father.

<!-- p50 -->
The Spirit bears witness to Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV">John 15:26</span>), glorifies Jesus, and proclaims and declares what Jesus has for us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV">John 16:14</span>).

<!-- p51 -->
The Holy Spirit will bear witness to Jesus, will glorify Him, will teach, and will remind us of the teachings of Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV">John 14:26</span>), and will guide us into all truth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV">John 16:13</span>). Jesus told His disciples that He had much more to teach them, but they were not yet able to bear it; however, the Holy Spirit would guide them into all truth. This is another nuanced point in theology. These teachings that Jesus needed His disciples to know were conveyed in several ways. Jesus taught them face to face before His crucifixion, and also after His resurrection and before His ascension.

<!-- p52 -->
Jesus also promised that the Spirit of Truth, who was poured out upon them on the day of Pentecost, would lead them into all truth through divine revelation. Is there still divine revelation today? **Absolutely yes!** The gifts of the Holy Spirit, including prophecy and words of knowledge and wisdom, have not ceased! However, no prophecy or word from the spirit is of private interpretation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2PE" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV">2 Peter 1:20</span>). This is very important. Some people will wrongly claim that they are listening to the Holy Spirit, but their actions and words do not bear witness to Jesus. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV">1 John 4:3</span> warns against any spirit that does not bear witness to Jesus, saying that such a spirit is of the antichrist. This bears repeating, if any spirit is distorted away from the doctrine of Christ that is not the Holy Spirit, but an unclean spirit!

<!-- p53 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV">
<!-- p54 -->
&gt; **<u>1 John 4:1</u>** (NKJV) *Beloved, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits, whether they are of God; because many false prophets have gone out into the world.*

<!-- p55 -->
</span>
<!-- p56 -->
Throughout Scripture, we see examples of claims made in error, often by false prophets or well-intentioned people who spoke contrary to the will of God. For example, Jeremiah warned against prophets who uttered the visions of their own hearts, claiming peace when destruction was approaching (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="23" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV">Jeremiah 23:16-17</span>). Likewise, in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV">Acts 16:16-18</span>, the words of the slave girl about Paul and Silas were accurate but were prompted by a demonic spirit, not the Holy Spirit. These examples remind us that even words that appear to be true can come from ungodly sources.

<!-- p57 -->
Every claim of ‘God told me!’ or ‘new’ teaching should be measured by the standards of biblical truth. To discern accurately, we must ask ourselves:

<!-- p58 -->
- Does this align with Scripture?

<!-- p59 -->
- Does this glorify Jesus Christ?

<!-- p60 -->
- Does this bear divine fruit?

<!-- p61 -->
By using these criteria, we can distinguish the true guidance of the Holy Spirit from error or deception.

<!-- p62 -->
God customarily gives us sufficient &amp; abundant wisdom and guidance (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV">James 1:5</span> &amp; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="33" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV">Jeremiah 33:3</span>). God's divine wisdom will not violate Scripture, but it will go beyond the scope of Scripture. *entolē* *(*commandments*)* of His are immediate, instilled directives, they are *rhema* words (as discussed in the chapter on faith).

<!-- p63 -->
This wisdom and insight and direction may not be word-for-word within Scripture. For example, Scripture gives good general guidance about what to look for in a spouse, but it does not give specific instructions about the exact person. God promises to direct our path, to guide us into all truth, and we can be comforted knowing that this wisdom and guidance from the Holy Spirit will never go against any teachings of Scripture.

### Outwardly-The Holy Spirit Empowers the Mission

<!-- p65 -->
Jesus gave us His mission, the Great Commission:

<!-- p66 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV">
<!-- p67 -->

Amen.*

<!-- p68 -->
</span>
<!-- p69 -->
The Holy Spirit has an essential part in the mission, convicting the world about sin, righteousness, and judgment:

<!-- p70 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="8-11" data-version="NKJV">
<!-- p71 -->
&gt; **<u>John 16:8-11</u>** (NKJV) **8** W*hen He comes, He will convict the world about sin, and about righteousness, and about judgment:* ***9** of sin, because they do not believe in Me;* ***10** of righteousness, because I go to My Father and you see Me no more;* ***11** of judgment, because the ruler of this world has been judged.*

<!-- p72 -->
</span>
<!-- p73 -->
Digging deeper, the Spirit brings conviction of these three truths:

<!-- p74 -->
1.  **Conviction of sin**: Revealing the reality of humanity's rebellion against God, drawing people's attention to the depravity of their own lives.

<!-- p75 -->
2.  **Conviction of righteousness**: Showing the perfect fulfillment of the Father's will accomplished by Jesus, and that Jesus' sacrifice provided the once-for-all atonement needed for the forgiveness of sins.

<!-- p76 -->
3.  **Conviction of judgment**: Affirming the defeat of Satan, the certainty of God's justice, and that all will stand in judgment for the works and deeds of their lives.

<!-- p77 -->
The Holy Spirit also empowers the mission. The Holy Spirit gives believers power to be bold witnesses in the face of persecution in order to share the Gospel. Boldness does not mean being loud. It means having courage in spite of fear. Power is strength, might, and ability. The Holy Spirit gives the power and ability to be a witness of the Gospel despite threats and the fear of punishment.

<!-- p78 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV">
<!-- p79 -->
&gt; **<u>Acts 1:8</u>** *(NKJV) But you shall receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you; and you shall be My witnesses in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and even to the end of the earth.*

<!-- p80 -->
</span>
<!-- p81 -->
The entire book of Acts is filled with stories and accounts that generally follow this cycle:

<!-- p82 -->
1.  **Prayer**: Believers pray for boldness and God's intervention.

<!-- p83 -->
2.  **Empowerment**: The Holy Spirit fills them with boldness, power, and guidance.

<!-- p84 -->
3.  **Proclamation**: They proclaim the gospel boldly, often with signs, wonders, and miracles.

<!-- p85 -->
4.  **Opposition**: Opposition arises, leading to persecution or challenges. Sometimes believers were killed for their faith, at other times imprisoned and/or beaten.

<!-- p86 -->
5.  **Renewal**: The church returns to prayer, seeking fresh empowerment, and the cycle continues.

<!-- p87 -->
The New Testament church has largely grown in the face of deliberate opposition. Around the world the church suffers brutality at the hands of persecutors and evil rulers determined to suppress the knowledge of the truth of Jesus.

<!-- p88 -->
God knew that this opposition would occur; Jesus prophesied about it:

<!-- p89 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV">
<!-- p90 -->
&gt; **<u>Luke 12:11-12</u>** (NKJV) **11** *"Now, when they bring you to the synagogues and before magistrates and authorities, do not worry about how or what you should answer, or what you should say. **12** For the Holy Spirit will teach you in that very hour what you ought to say."*

<!-- p91 -->
</span>
<!-- p92 -->
Do not be afraid! Jesus promised us that:

<!-- p93 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV">
<!-- p94 -->
&gt; **<u>Matthew 16:18</u>** (NKJV) *… I \[Jesus\] will build My Church, and the gates of Hades shall not prevail against it.*

<!-- p95 -->
</span>
<!-- p96 -->
This is an amazing and encouraging promise. Gates are defensive strongholds set in place. When Jesus says that *the gates of Hades will not prevail*, He paints a picture of a conquering army breaking down gates and taking a city!

<!-- p97 -->
The Holy Spirit brings miraculous boldness to proclaim the testimony of the Gospel in the face of persecution. The results of this empowerment can be described, but are far better to be lived. It is a miraculous boldness (greater than human) that emboldens believers to share. This boldness to preach is often accompanied by miraculous acts that demonstrate the Spirit and power (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 2:1-5</span>). People are healed physically and emotionally, the dead are raised, those tormented by evil spirits are set free, people with hard hearts turn and repent.

<!-- p98 -->
Jesus said we will do these works and greater ones! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV">John 14:12</span>). These works together are miraculous, divine, and greatly needed in a world tearing itself apart, full of sorrow, filled with pain, awaiting the mature sons of God to be revealed! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-23" data-version="NKJV">Romans 8:22-23</span>)

<!-- p99 -->
The instrument Jesus has chosen for transformation in the world is His Church, empowered, emboldened, and led by the Holy Spirit.

### Why build a relationship?

<!-- p129 -->
The Holy Spirit is a Helper. A Helper helps us! Work goes much more smoothly if we have a shared language, and a relationship with Him. Can you imagine trying to work with someone at your job if you have no relationship, you don't speak the same language, and you can't communicate?!

<!-- p130 -->
We serve a God who wants to communicate with us. Jesus said that His sheep know His voice (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV">John 10:27</span>), already within all of us we have been given the natural capacity we need, but we may lack the skill that comes from experience.

<!-- p131 -->
Speaking from personal experience, a personal and living relationship with the Holy Spirit is one of the best things about being a Christian. His presence brings us peace, comfort, and direction, but only if you know how to seek Him, listen to Him, and obey Him.

<!-- p132 -->
Cultivate a sensitivity to the Holy Spirit. As with many married couples over time, you can communicate with your spouse in a unique way and sometimes just by looking at each other. That look alone tells you everything you need to know about what has happened and what will happen. Build that kind of sensitivity and closeness with the Holy Spirit. Include Him in your whole life, this is a wonderful part of your relationship with Him that will bring great depth and richness to your walk of faith.

<!-- p133 -->
The Holy Spirit has come to lead us and guide us, to empower us to be the people of God, and to see the people of the world greatly transformed, redeemed from the kingdom of darkness into the kingdom of light. Knowing Him, building faith in Him is foundational for everyday life and for accomplishing the work that Jesus has entrusted us to do.

<!-- p134 -->
Our discussion about the work of the Holy Spirit does not end in this chapter. His work is discussed in depth in the remaining chapters of this book, especially those on sanctification and baptism.
