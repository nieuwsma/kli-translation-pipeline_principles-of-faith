# Chapter 9 - The Church and Our Role Within It

<!-- p1 -->
The church is how God manifests Himself in the world-through His people, doing His work. We are called to be His body, we are His hands and feet to carry out His work and purposes. Without our efforts the harvest will remain in the field and will not be harvested.

<!-- p2 -->
## Who Is the Church?

<!-- p3 -->
The Church, ***Ekklesia** (eh-klee-SEE-ah - **G1577**),* comes from two Greek words:

<!-- p4 -->
- **"Ek"**: "Out of."

<!-- p5 -->
- **"Kaleo"**: "To call."

<!-- p6 -->
Together, **Ekklesia** literally means "the called" or "a called assembly." In the ancient Greek city-states, it referred to an assembly of citizens gathered for a specific purpose, such as legislative or court meetings. It was a civic term that signified an assembly of people.

<!-- p7 -->
In the New Testament**,** it is used primarily to refer to the community of believers in Jesus Christ. It can mean:

<!-- p8 -->
- **The Local Church**: A specific gathering of believers in a given place (e.g., the church at Corinth, Philippi, etc.).

<!-- p9 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="1 Corinthians" data-chapter="1" data-verses="2" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p10 -->
> **<u>1 Corinthians 1:2</u>** (NKJV) "*To the church of God which is at Corinth.*"

<!-- p11 -->
</span>

<!-- p12 -->
- **The Universal Church**: The collective body of all believers across time and place.

<!-- p13 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="fragment" data-book="Matthew" data-chapter="16" data-verses="18" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p14 -->
> **<u>Matthew 16:18</u>** (NKJV) …*I \[Jesus\] will build My church, and the gates of Hades shall not prevail against it.*

<!-- p15 -->
</span>

<!-- p16 -->
The church is people, not a building or the place where they meet. **The Called**: It conveys the idea that Christians have been called out of the world and consecrated for God’s purposes (e.g., <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Peter" data-chapter="2" data-verses="9" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Peter 2:9</span>) for a specific mission and purpose (the Great Commission). Essentially, the church is two or more believers meeting together (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Matthew" data-chapter="18" data-verses="20" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 18:20</span>). Typically, the church is organized into regular gatherings, perhaps with a set location and time, but wherever two or more people meet in Jesus’ name, there is the church!

<!-- p17 -->
The church was established by Jesus and grew in the 1st century A.D. through the work of the disciples who followed Him. The church we have and experience today in all its local bodies around the world is the direct result of the original work of Christ and His Apostles. As believers we should commit ourselves to the local church for fellowship, giving, being equipped for ministry, and doing good works!

<!-- p18 -->
To help us understand what the church is, Scripture gives us powerful images that show what it is intended to be.

<!-- p19 -->
## Understanding the Church

<!-- p20 -->
There are several major metaphorical images used to depict the characteristics of the church:

<!-- p21 -->
1.  Body

<!-- p22 -->
2.  Bride

<!-- p23 -->
3.  Temple

<!-- p24 -->
4.  Family

<!-- p25 -->
5.  Flock

<!-- p26 -->
6.  Vine and Branches

<!-- p27 -->
Each metaphor highlights a unique aspect of the church, while together they reveal the church’s identity as a community that is one and diverse under the leadership of Christ.

<!-- p28 -->
### Body

<!-- p29 -->
The church is often defined as the **Body of Christ**, emphasizing its unity and diversity. With Christ as the head, the church functions as a living organism in which every believer has an important and varying role. Each member has been uniquely gifted and contributes to the health and mission of the body according to their function. This imagery shows the interdependence of believers, especially that the body must be joined together in order to function, and that our functioning is cooperative rather than competitive. When each part works together, the body grows and thrives in unity and love (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Corinthians" data-chapter="12" data-verses="12-27" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 12:12-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Ephesians" data-chapter="4" data-verses="15-16" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 4:15-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Colossians" data-chapter="1" data-verses="18" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Colossians 1:18</span>).

<!-- p30 -->
### Bride

<!-- p31 -->
The church is described as the **Bride of Christ**, reflecting a close and loving relationship with Jesus. Just as a bride is cherished and prepared for her husband, the church is sanctified and cleansed by the sacrificial love of Christ. This illustration captures the devotion and faithfulness expected of the church, together with the deep love that Christ has for His people. This imagery also points to the ultimate union between Christ and the church, celebrated in the marriage supper of the Lamb, where the church is presented in glory without spot or blemish (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Ephesians" data-chapter="5" data-verses="25-27" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 5:25-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Revelation" data-chapter="19" data-verses="7-9" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Revelation 19:7-9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Revelation" data-chapter="21" data-verses="2" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Revelation 21:2</span>).

<!-- p32 -->
### Temple

<!-- p33 -->
The church is described as the **Temple of God**, the dwelling of His Spirit. Believers are described as living stones built together upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets, with Christ Himself being the chief cornerstone. This holy structure signifies the presence of God among His people and their role as a community of worship and prayer. The imagery of the temple emphasizes the importance of holiness and the shared purpose of the church in glorifying God (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Corinthians" data-chapter="3" data-verses="16-17" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 3:16-17</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Ephesians" data-chapter="2" data-verses="19-22" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 2:19-22</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Peter" data-chapter="2" data-verses="4-5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Peter 2:4-5</span>).

<!-- p34 -->
### Family

<!-- p35 -->
The church is described as the **Family of God**, emphasizing the unity of relationship and care among its members. Through the Spirit of adoption, believers are made children of God and members of His household. This familial imagery highlights the love, belonging, and shared inheritance that believers have in Christ. It also reflects the call to treat one another with compassion and humility as siblings in the family of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Galatians" data-chapter="6" data-verses="10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Galatians 6:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Ephesians" data-chapter="2" data-verses="19" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 2:19</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Romans" data-chapter="8" data-verses="15-17" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 8:15-17</span>). Moreover, through the image of the family it shows how church members are to mature, that they be taught and nurtured by spiritual mothers and fathers (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Timothy" data-chapter="5" data-verses="1-2" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Timothy 5:1-2</span}), so that we may move from being infants who drink the pure milk of the Word, to mature adults who can eat the solid food of the Word (Hebrews 5).

<!-- p36 -->
### Flock

<!-- p37 -->
The church is described as a **Flock**, sheep under the care of Jesus, the Good Shepherd. This imagery shows the church’s dependence on Christ for guidance, provision, and protection. It also emphasizes the responsibility of church leaders to faithfully shepherd the flock of God as under-shepherds, demonstrating the example of Christ’s love and care. The imagery of the flock speaks of hope, obedience, and the close relationship between the Chief Shepherd and His sheep (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="John" data-chapter="10" data-verses="14-16" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 10:14-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Peter" data-chapter="5" data-verses="2" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Peter 5:2</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="20" data-verses="28" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 20:28</span>).

<!-- p38 -->
### Vine and Branches

<!-- p39 -->
The church is described as the **Vine and Branches**, showing the vital relationship between believers and Christ. Jesus is the true vine, and believers are the branches that must abide in Him to bear fruit. This illustration highlights dependence on Christ for spiritual life, growth, and fruitfulness. Without Him, believers can do nothing, but by abiding in Him, they glorify God and fulfill their purpose. Jesus will prune so that He may bear even more fruit in our lives (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="John" data-chapter="15" data-verses="1-5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 15:1-5</span>).

<!-- p40 -->
## Responsibilities of the Church

<!-- p41 -->
The mission of the church, like the role of the Holy Spirit, can be understood through three dimensions: upward, inward, and outward, each reflecting a unique aspect of its calling in God’s plan. These dimensions guide the church in fulfilling its place as a worshiping, edifying, and missional community.

<!-- p42 -->
### Upward: Worshiping and Glorifying God

<!-- p43 -->
The primary purpose of the church is to worship and glorify God. Corporate worship, where believers gather to exalt God together, is a visible reflection of His glory and a response to His majesty. As the psalmist declares, “*Worship the Lord in the beauty of holiness; tremble before Him, all the earth*” (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Psalm" data-chapter="96" data-verses="9" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Psalm 96:9</span>). Beyond corporate worship, the church is called to live lives of obedience and sacrifice, offering itself as “living sacrifices” as an act of spiritual worship (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Romans" data-chapter="12" data-verses="1-2" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 12:1-2</span>). This upward perspective keeps the church centered on the majesty of God and aligns its priorities with His will.

<!-- p44 -->
### Inward: Building Up the Body

<!-- p45 -->
The Great Commission commands us to ‘*teach them to observe*’, a charge that goes far beyond formal teaching methods like this book. Making disciples (to be discussed further in the chapter on discipleship) is forming a follower to conform to that discipline, as a parent raises their child. Good parents do not hand a five-year-old an encyclopedia and challenge them to figure it out on their own! Instead they set an example, demonstrate, correct, guide, nurture, love, care, and shepherd.

<!-- p46 -->
Parents give themselves for their children because they love them, because they are part of the same body, because they share the same blood. As members of the body and family of Christ, we share the same body, we share the same blood. Communion reminds us through its signs that we are united to one another through the sacrifice of Christ. The act of Communion should be a place of unity, reminding us that we all need redemption, and that salvation is from God alone. In this unity we see that no part of the body is greater than another except our Head, Christ.

<!-- p47 -->
### Outward: Reaching the World

<!-- p48 -->
The most recognizable part of the church’s mission extends outward to reach the world with the gospel. Jesus’ Great Commission calls His followers to preach and make disciples of all nations (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Matthew" data-chapter="28" data-verses="19-20" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 28:19-20</span>). Empowered by the Holy Spirit, the church is to be His witnesses to the ends of the earth (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="1" data-verses="8" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 1:8</span>).

<!-- p49 -->
Jesus is the light of the world (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="John" data-chapter="8" data-verses="12" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 8:12</span>). Light shines in every direction as it goes out from the source. His light shines through His expressions: the Word, His Spirit, and us, His body. We are the light of the world; by our lives we should shine His light into our worlds shrouded in darkness and display good deeds that glorify God (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Matthew" data-chapter="5" data-verses="14-16" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 5:14-16</span>). Practically, this means that the church is called to advocate for justice and mercy, spiritually and physically. As James says, our pure religion is to care for the vulnerable, the fatherless, the widow (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="James" data-chapter="1" data-verses="27" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">James 1:27</span>).

<!-- p50 -->
## Church Leadership

<!-- p51 -->
Across Christianity there are many kinds of denominations, conferences, assemblies, and councils. There are very few universal principles regarding church leadership, but the principles we do have are very significant:

<!-- p52 -->
1.  Jesus is the sole head of the Church. Everyone serves Him (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Colossians" data-chapter="1" data-verses="18" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Colossians 1:18</span>).

<!-- p53 -->
2.  There are high standards for those who lead; they are worthy of double honor, for they will receive double judgment (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Timothy" data-chapter="5" data-verses="17" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Timothy 5:17</span>).

<!-- p54 -->
1.  They should be mature, not puffed up with pride, responsible (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Timothy" data-chapter="3" data-verses="1-13" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Timothy 3:1-13</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Titus" data-chapter="1" data-verses="5-9" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Titus 1:5-9</span>).

<!-- p55 -->
2.  They must be servants of all (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Mark" data-chapter="10" data-verses="42-45" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Mark 10:42-45</span>).

<!-- p56 -->
3.  They must devote themselves to the Word and to prayer (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="6" data-verses="4" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 6:4</span>).

<!-- p57 -->
4.  They are responsible to equip the body for the work of ministry.

<!-- p58 -->
3.  There are no greater or lesser members in the body. Scriptures that support this are:

<!-- p59 -->
1.  There should be no favoritism between the rich and the poor (James 2).

<!-- p60 -->
2.  There is no division between Jew and Gentile, slave or free person, male or female (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Galatians" data-chapter="3" data-verses="27-28" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Galatians 3:27-28</span>).

<!-- p61 -->
The structure of the Church, beyond leadership principles, is important but much of it depends on context and is a matter of tradition rather than being universally mandated. “<u>That's how we've always done it!</u>” is a popular saying; if you go back far enough, there was usually a good reason why they did it that way. This is generally neither good nor bad, but a matter of taste, needs, and availability.

<!-- p62 -->
A good example of tradition being the basis of structure is mealtime in a family. The most important part of a family meal is that everyone gets food and the meal is nutritious. The differences are countless: what they eat, how they eat, how they season their food, etc. Various combinations are generally acceptable and are largely a matter of taste and preference.

<!-- p63 -->
A church with 10,000 members has different needs and capacities than a church of 50. The pattern set forth in the book of Acts and in the canon of the New Testament was the model of **elders** and **deacons**. Although leadership structures may look different among churches and denominations, ultimately this pattern represents the most basic suitable structure of leadership for a church. Let us look at these leadership positions and how they are appointed.

<!-- p64 -->
### Elders (Overseers)

<!-- p65 -->
Roles and Responsibilities:

<!-- p66 -->
- **Spiritual Leadership** - Providing oversight, guidance, and pastoral care for the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="20" data-verses="28" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 20:28</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Peter" data-chapter="5" data-verses="2" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Peter 5:2</span>).

<!-- p67 -->
- **Teaching and Preaching** - Responsible for teaching sound doctrine and guarding against false teaching (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Timothy" data-chapter="5" data-verses="17" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Timothy 5:17</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Titus" data-chapter="1" data-verses="9" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Titus 1:9</span>).

<!-- p68 -->
- **Shepherding the Flock** - Caring for the spiritual needs of the congregation, showing humility and service (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Peter" data-chapter="5" data-verses="2-3" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Peter 5:2-3</span>).

<!-- p69 -->
- **Protecting the Church** - Serving as watchmen, defending the church against false teaching and division (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="20" data-verses="29-31" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 20:29-31</span>).

<!-- p70 -->
- **Equipping the Saints** - Teaching and preparing believers for works of service and discipleship (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Ephesians" data-chapter="4" data-verses="11-12" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 4:11-12</span>).

<!-- p71 -->
Appointment Process:

<!-- p72 -->
- **Appointed in Every Church** - Elders were appointed by the apostles or their delegates, often with prayer and fasting (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="14" data-verses="23" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 14:23</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Titus" data-chapter="1" data-verses="5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Titus 1:5</span>).

<!-- p73 -->
- **Confirmed by the Holy Spirit** - Their appointment was seen as guided by the Spirit, recognizing God's calling and gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="20" data-verses="28" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 20:28</span>).

<!-- p74 -->
- **Tested and Approved** - Candidates had to demonstrate maturity and faithfulness before being entrusted with leadership (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Timothy" data-chapter="3" data-verses="10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Timothy 3:10</span>). They cannot be recent converts! ((<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Timothy" data-chapter="3" data-verses="6" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Timothy 3:6</span>)

<!-- p75 -->
### Deacons (Servants)

<!-- p76 -->
Roles and Responsibilities:

<!-- p77 -->
- **Practical Service** - Focuses on meeting physical needs in the church, freeing the elders to devote themselves to prayer and teaching (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="6" data-verses="1-4" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 6:1-4</span>).

<!-- p78 -->
- **Supportive Ministry** - Assists in hospitality, caring for the poor, and administrative tasks (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="6" data-verses="3" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 6:3</span>).

<!-- p79 -->
- **Promoting Unity** - Addressing matters of fairness and care to prevent division (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="6" data-verses="1-7" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 6:1-7</span>).

<!-- p80 -->
- **Examples of Faith** - Serve as examples in humility, faith, and service (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Timothy" data-chapter="3" data-verses="13" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Timothy 3:13</span>).

<!-- p81 -->
The Selection Process (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="6" data-verses="1-6" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 6:1-6</span>):

<!-- p82 -->
- **Recognized by the Congregation** - Chosen based on their qualifications and spiritual character.

<!-- p83 -->
- **Approved by the Leaders** - The apostles examined and entrusted the ministry to the appointed deacons with prayer and the laying on of hands.

<!-- p84 -->
- **Confirmed in Ministry** - The deacons were first tested to prove their faithfulness before being formally appointed (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Timothy" data-chapter="3" data-verses="10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Timothy 3:10</span>).

<!-- p85 -->
### Leadership Qualifications

<!-- p86 -->
#### Qualifications of Elders and Deacons:

<!-- p87 -->
- Moral Character - All should be above reproach, respectable, and demonstrate self-control (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Timothy" data-chapter="3" data-verses="2" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Timothy 3:2</span>, 8).

<!-- p88 -->
- Family Life - All should be faithful in marriage (“husband of one wife”) and manage their households well (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Timothy" data-chapter="3" data-verses="4-5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Timothy 3:4-5</span>, 12).

<!-- p89 -->
- Spiritual Maturity - All must be tested and proven before being appointed (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Timothy" data-chapter="3" data-verses="6" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Timothy 3:6</span>, 10).

<!-- p90 -->
- Good Reputation - All need a good reputation inside and outside the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Timothy" data-chapter="3" data-verses="7" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Timothy 3:7</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
- Faith and Integrity - All must hold the faith with a clear conscience and live out their faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Timothy" data-chapter="3" data-verses="9" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Timothy 3:9</span>).

<!-- p92 -->
### Examples of Structures

<!-- p93 -->
Using what we have learned so far about principles and structures of leadership, let us look at two examples of church structure found in the New Testament, Jerusalem and Antioch.

<!-- p94 -->
### The Church in Jerusalem

<!-- p95 -->
The Church in Jerusalem was led primarily by the **apostles**, who focused on teaching, prayer, and spiritual oversight (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="2" data-verses="42" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 2:42</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="6" data-verses="4" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 6:4</span>). As the community grew, the need for an administrative structure became apparent, particularly to address practical matters such as caring for widows and distributing resources. This led to the appointment of **deacons** (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="6" data-verses="1-7" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 6:1-7</span>). These individuals, chosen for their spiritual maturity and good reputation, handled practical ministries to enable the apostles to concentrate on their spiritual responsibilities. Over time, **elders** also emerged as leaders in the church in Jerusalem. For example, during the Jerusalem Council (Acts 15), the apostles and elders were involved in decision-making, which demonstrated a collaborative leadership structure.

<!-- p96 -->
### The Church in Antioch

<!-- p97 -->
The Church in Antioch had a more decentralized and diverse leadership structure compared to Jerusalem. It was led by a group of **prophets and teachers**, including Barnabas, Saul (Paul), Simeon, Lucius, and Manaen (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="13" data-verses="1" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 13:1</span>). The Church in Antioch was also more oriented toward reaching the Gentiles, whereas the church in Jerusalem more clearly reflected its Jewish roots. Although the New Testament does not explicitly mention deacons in Antioch, there were likely practical roles due to the growth of the church and their participation in sending relief to Jerusalem (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="11" data-verses="29-30" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 11:29-30</span>). The church in Antioch became a model for sending missionaries, where the leaders prayed, fasted, and laid hands on Paul and Barnabas to send them (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="13" data-verses="2-3" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 13:2-3</span>). This demonstrates a structure that balanced spiritual oversight (prophets and teachers) with evangelistic mission along with practical support.

<!-- p98 -->
Unlike Paul’s corrections to the Corinthians and the Galatians, Scripture does not criticize the leadership structures of Jerusalem or Antioch. This suggests that these churches, under the guidance of the Holy Spirit, were free to adapt their assemblies to meet their unique needs and circumstances within the framework of sound doctrine and apostolic teaching (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="2" data-verses="42" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 2:42</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="2 Timothy" data-chapter="3" data-verses="16-17" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>).

<!-- p99 -->
In these passages certain terms have been used that we have not yet defined and studied: *apostle, prophet, evangelist*. These gifts are part of the fivefold ministry that Jesus gave to the church.

<!-- p100 -->
### The Fivefold Ministry Gifts

<!-- p101 -->
Jesus established five specific ministry gifts, often called the **fivefold ministry gifts**, to equip believers for service and to build up the Body of Christ.

<!-- p102 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="Ephesians" data-chapter="4" data-verses="11-13" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p103 -->
> **<u>Ephesians 4:11-13</u>** (NKJV) **11** *And He Himself gave some to be apostles, some prophets, some evangelists, and some pastors and teachers,* ***12** for the equipping of the saints for the work of ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ,* ***13** till we all come to the unity of the faith and of the knowledge of the Son of God, to a perfect man, to the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ;*

<!-- p104 -->
</span>

<!-- p105 -->
The five gifts given to the saints to prepare them for the work of ministry are:

<!-- p106 -->
1.  **Apostles** - Pioneers and visionaries who lay foundations, plant churches, and advance the Kingdom of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Ephesians" data-chapter="2" data-verses="20" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 2:20</span>). Apostles model courage and faith, showing how to enter new areas and take spiritual steps.

<!-- p107 -->
2.  **Prophets** - Messengers and guides who proclaim God's truth, bringing others comfort, edification, and direction (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Corinthians" data-chapter="14" data-verses="3" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>). Prophets stir up faith, call others to align with the Word of God, and model reliance on hearing God's voice.

<!-- p108 -->
3.  **Evangelists** - Proclaimers of the Gospel who focus on reaching the lost and stirring others to act in sharing Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="8" data-verses="5-8" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 8:5-8</span>). Evangelists train disciples how to share their faith effectively, model boldness in witness, and inspire compassion for the lost.

<!-- p109 -->
4.  **Pastors (Pastor)** - Caretakers and nurturers who protect, lead, and provide spiritual oversight for the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="20" data-verses="28" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 20:28</span>). Pastors teach disciples the importance of fellowship, caring for others, and accountability within the body of Christ.

<!-- p110 -->
5.  **Teachers** - Instructors and trainers who clarify doctrine and help believers grow in understanding and application of the Word of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="2 Timothy" data-chapter="2" data-verses="15" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">2 Timothy 2:15</span>). Teachers equip disciples to study the Scriptures deeply, apply them to their daily lives, and teach others effectively.

<!-- p111 -->
## Leadership versus Gifts

<!-- p112 -->
A common misunderstanding about leadership in the church is the distinction between the authority and responsibility of leadership and specific gifts.

<!-- p113 -->
This confusion relates to our use of words like teacher, pastor, prophet, apostle, evangelist. These terms are gifts. There is no **office** of ‘apostle’ in the New Testament church. There are people who do the work of an apostle, who have the gift or calling of apostleship. Having the gift of apostleship does not give them authority or responsibility.

<!-- p114 -->
I am an accomplished musician; I have played the saxophone for over 20 years. I played in school and college and performed at a semi-professional level for several summers while working with an evangelism team. Despite having the skill, talent, and ability to be a saxophonist, I have no right to walk into your church and stand on the platform with the praise and worship team.

<!-- p115 -->
Similarly, there are people with a gift for being a pastor/shepherd, but that does not make them the ‘PASTOR’ of a local church or make them your ‘pastor’. The fivefold ministry gifts do not lead the church; leaders (elders and deacons) lead. It is common for leaders to have particular fivefold ministry gifts, but that gift strengthens their leadership; it does not establish it. Likewise, it is common for people who are still growing and who have fivefold ministry gifts to serve and to aspire to leadership, but God looks first at character and then at gifts. We should all mature, be able to discern the word of God, be filled with the Spirit, and do good works.

<!-- p116 -->
Every leader and every gift must first submit to being a disciple, as part of the body.

<!-- p117 -->
Jesus gave specific ministry gifts to equip the saints for the work of ministry. These are different from the motivational gifts (to be discussed later in this chapter) or the manifestation gifts (to be discussed in the chapter on baptisms). As disciples grow, the fivefold ministry gifts empower them for service, while elders and deacons provide oversight to ensure sound doctrine and conduct.

<!-- p118 -->
## Your Place in the Church

<!-- p119 -->
Up to this point in this chapter we have discussed church leadership and basic metaphors about the church. Let's build on the specific metaphor of the body and consider a marathon runner.

<!-- p120 -->
### You Are Part of the Whole

<!-- p121 -->
The church, as the Body of Christ, depends on every member working together to fulfill its mission. Just as the body cannot function properly if one part is missing, the church needs every believer to participate fully, to grow, and to serve. Every member of the body has a very important role. Being part of the body means remaining connected. The body can continue to live with diminished capacity without a limb, but the limb itself cannot live when separated from the body. We must be a living part of the church and not become cut off. <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Hebrews" data-chapter="10" data-verses="24" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 10:24</span> says that we should gather and not forsake the assembly.

<!-- p122 -->
To truly work and act together you need fellowship! You need to know others, love others, and, if possible, be loved as well! Build strong relationships and friendships and repair them when they are under strain or hurt. When a part is injured or inflamed, you need to do the work of resting it and/or setting it right; so that a dislocated part does not end up broken! A broken part that does not heal is eventually separated, and you cannot be part of the body while you are on your own.

<!-- p123 -->
We do not all have the same task. Some of us can share the Gospel from a platform, others at the dinner table. But we are ALL called to be ministers of reconciliation (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="2 Corinthians" data-chapter="5" data-verses="18-19" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">2 Corinthians 5:18-19</span>). We have all been received into the priesthood of believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Peter" data-chapter="2" data-verses="9" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Peter 2:9</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Revelation" data-chapter="1" data-verses="6" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Revelation 1:6</span>). This means that we should all be able to do the work of ministry by the power of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p124 -->
Toes work with feet, feet with ankles, and so on—each part contributing to the mission as a whole. Marathon runners must train if they want to finish the race. You need to be prepared—not just by learning, but by doing what you have learned. The church, like a marathon runner, thrives when every member fulfills their role in unity.

<!-- p125 -->
There are a great many people in your life whom only you can reach. The Pastor cannot be the only one striving to shepherd them, nor can the Evangelist be the only one striving to reach them. God wants to use you to reach unbelievers by the power of His Holy Spirit. We all do the work of ministry; the Holy Spirit helps us.

<!-- p126 -->
### You Need Training

<!-- p127 -->
Believers must train and equip themselves for their unique roles in the church, just as a runner strengthens specific muscles for the race. You will not succeed if you jump into a marathon without training, and not all training is the same—you need to prepare appropriately for the race. Jesus has given special ministry gifts to equip believers—not to do all the work themselves, but to empower each member to serve effectively in their unique roles.

<!-- p128 -->
Marathon training will bring change. You need strength and you need to be transformed. It is our responsibility to present ourselves to God and allow Him to transform us by renewal (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Romans" data-chapter="12" data-verses="1-2" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 12:1-2</span>). The change should lead you to maturity, enabling you to carry your own burdens and support others. You should be able to bear MUCH FRUIT. This will be discussed in depth in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p129 -->
Training is hard and sometimes not enjoyable. Sometimes the changes you need to go through are ones you would prefer to avoid. But if you want to reach the goal, you need to stay on the narrow path. At times you may need warning, comfort, or help—all delivered with patience (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Thessalonians" data-chapter="5" data-verses="14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Thessalonians 5:14</span>).

<!-- p130 -->
Every skilled marathon runner has a coach to train them, and you will need a trainer to help you. Of course, you have the Holy Spirit, but He often relies on His coaches—God’s mature men and women in the Body of Christ—whom you are called to work with.

<!-- p131 -->
As individuals we need to submit first and foremost to God and secondly, in this context, to the body of believers. Submitting to the church means honoring godly leadership (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Hebrews" data-chapter="13" data-verses="17" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 13:17</span>) and submitting to one another in humility (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Ephesians" data-chapter="5" data-verses="21" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 5:21</span>).

<!-- p132 -->
Submission can be difficult, especially where you have seen trust be broken or leadership fail in the past. This does not mean blind obedience or control but reflects a spirit of honor and cooperation. Worldly submission is demanded by those in power and can be demeaning and breed resentment. Biblical submission, rooted in humility and discernment, strengthens the church and fosters accountability. Be ready to listen to guidance and constructive feedback.

<!-- p133 -->
Now that we have laid a solid foundation—you are a meaningful part of the body but you need training—let’s begin to find where you fit.

<!-- p134 -->
### Finding Where You Fit

<!-- p135 -->
Finding your role in the church involves seeking God’s guidance, understanding your spiritual gifts, and participating actively in your church community. It is a journey of prayer, seeking, and growth, in which you discover how God has uniquely prepared you to contribute to His Kingdom. It is very important to recognize that your role in the church may change over time and does not have to be the same throughout your whole life.

<!-- p136 -->
Here is a practical approach to help you find your place, presented as a series of steps for clarity, though you may repeat the same step several times in your walk with God.

<!-- p137 -->
#### Step 1 - Seek God’s Guidance Through Prayer and the Word

<!-- p138 -->
Begin by **seeking God’s guidance through prayer**. God is the ultimate source of your purpose and calling (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Jeremiah" data-chapter="29" data-verses="11" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Jeremiah 29:11</span>). Pray regularly for clarity about how you can serve in the church, asking Him to show you your spiritual gifts and passions. Believe that He will direct your steps as you submit your plans to His will (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Proverbs" data-chapter="3" data-verses="5-6" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Proverbs 3:5-6</span>).

<!-- p139 -->
**Study God’s Word** to increase your understanding of the church’s mission and your place within it. Key passages such as 1 Corinthians 12 and <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Ephesians" data-chapter="4" data-verses="11-12" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 4:11-12</span> highlight the spiritual gifts and the diversity of roles in the Body of Christ. Reflect on examples of people in Scripture who discovered and fulfilled their roles, such as Paul, Timothy, and Lydia.

<!-- p140 -->
#### Step 2 - Identify Your Gifts, Passions, and Skills

<!-- p141 -->
Identifying **spiritual gifts** is an important step. You may benefit from taking a spiritual gifts assessment. These gifts are given by God to build up the church. There are several categories of gifts mentioned in the New Testament:

<!-- p142 -->
- **Motivational Gifts** given by God: <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Romans" data-chapter="12" data-verses="6-8" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 12:6-8</span>

<!-- p143 -->
- Discussed after this list.

<!-- p144 -->
- Fivefold Ministry Gifts given by Jesus: <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Ephesians" data-chapter="4" data-verses="11-13" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 4:11-13</span>

<!-- p145 -->
- Discussed earlier in this chapter.

<!-- p146 -->
- Manifestation Gifts given by the Holy Spirit: <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Corinthians" data-chapter="12" data-verses="8-10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span>

<!-- p147 -->
- Discussed in the chapter on baptism.

<!-- p148 -->
**Motivational gifts** reflect how a believer is shaped to think, feel, and act. They motivate believers to serve in specific ways that build up the church. Each gift complements the others, ensuring the church functions as one united body with various roles (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Romans" data-chapter="12" data-verses="4-5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 12:4-5</span>). They operate as grace-given abilities (Greek: ***charis** - khar'-ece - **G5463 -** undeserved gift*), meaning they are given by God, not attained through effort or learning. Although the gifts come from God, a gifted person can grow in skill in using it.

<!-- p149 -->
The motivational gifts are:

<!-- p150 -->
1.  **Prophecy** - A God-given inclination to boldly proclaim truth, often focusing on prompting conscience and righteousness, leading others to align with the Word of God.*

<!-- p151 -->
2.  **Service (Helps)** - Meets practical needs through acts of service and assistance to help others.

<!-- p152 -->
3.  **Teaching** - Explains and clarifies truth, helping others understand and apply the Word of God.

<!-- p153 -->
4.  **Exhortation (Encouragement)** - Motivates, encourages, and comforts others to spur growth and endurance.

<!-- p154 -->
5.  **Giving** - Provides resources generously to meet needs and advance God's work without seeking recognition.

<!-- p155 -->
6.  **Leadership (Administration)** - Organizes and leads others to accomplish goals with vision and efficiency.

<!-- p156 -->
7.  **Mercy** - Shows compassion and care to comfort and help those who are hurting or in need.

<!-- p157 -->
\* Prophecy as used in the context of Romans is understood to be different from prophecy as a manifestation gift of the Holy Spirit (1 Corinthians). The Greek word used is the same in both passages, but the context is different. For a more in-depth study on that distinction, I recommend reading:

<!-- p158 -->
- *The Gift of Prophecy* by Kenneth E. Hagin ([www.rhema.org](http://www.rhema.org))

<!-- p159 -->
<!-- -->

<!-- p160 -->
- *Gifts and Ministries of the Holy Spirit* by Lester Sumrall

<!-- p161 -->
When you identify your gifts, consider your **passion and skills**. God often calls us to serve in areas we love or have expertise in (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Exodus" data-chapter="31" data-verses="2-6" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Exodus 31:2-6</span>). Reflect on what motivates you in serving God and others, and consider how your professional skills, hobbies, and life experiences can contribute to ministry.

<!-- p162 -->
Seek guidance from **leaders and mentors** who can offer wisdom and insight (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Proverbs" data-chapter="15" data-verses="22" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Proverbs 15:22</span>). Meet with a pastor or ministry leader to discuss your interests and ask for honest feedback about where you may fit best. Pray about the feedback you receive. Consider joining a discipleship group to receive training and ongoing support.

<!-- p163 -->
#### Step 3 - Be Faithful Now

<!-- p164 -->
The parable of the talents (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Matthew" data-chapter="25" data-verses="14-30" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 25:14-30</span>) reminds us that we need to be faithful in little things so we can be trusted with greater things (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Luke" data-chapter="16" data-verses="10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Luke 16:10</span>).

<!-- p165 -->
**Get involved and serve** in various ministries to explore where God is calling you. Start small by volunteering in areas of need within your church and in the ministries they support. For example:

<!-- p166 -->
- Join the hospitality team, children’s ministry, or community outreach programs. Consider leading a discipleship group for new believers through this book, *Principles of Faith,* using the group discussion questions and end-of-chapter activities to facilitate conversation.

<!-- p167 -->
- Be willing to step out of your comfort zone to discover new opportunities.

<!-- p168 -->
- Notice where God is working through you and where you feel most effective.

<!-- p169 -->
Practice giving. We can give to the church in many meaningful ways, and we are commanded to do so to fulfill the mission.

<!-- p170 -->
- Time - we can give our time and volunteer. When we give our time, we expend our effort and our gifts. This can also be a great way to learn new skills and build on those we have.

<!-- p171 -->
- Resources - we can give our resources. Commonly today this may be giving financially to the church and to ministries (not just the “institution,” but those in need in the church). Giving money is beneficial, because the church can purchase needed resources, but people have also given their physical resources beyond money, for example extra houses, clothing, cars, food, building materials, tools, etc. In <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="4" data-verses="32-35" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 4:32-35</span> we see an example of early believers giving voluntarily, sharing proceeds from sales of land and extra houses and giving so that ‘there was no one in need’.

<!-- p172 -->
Working side by side with others in ministry can reveal your strengths and deepen your understanding of your role. Participate in small groups or church activities to develop relationships. Listen to the burdens and needs of those around you and consider how you can help them. Building relationships within the church helps you connect with others and understand the needs of the community.

<!-- p173 -->
#### Step 4 - Seek Help!

<!-- p174 -->
Jesus said that in life we will face tribulation! (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="John" data-chapter="16" data-verses="33" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 16:33</span>) We are encouraged not to carry the weight of our burdens alone but to cast them on God who cares for us! (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Peter" data-chapter="5" data-verses="7" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Peter 5:7</span>)

<!-- p175 -->
Sometimes on our journey we may be injured. Marathon runners, even when they do “everything right,” can push their bodies to the point of injury, or they may trip and fall.

<!-- p176 -->
Not every fall is related to sin, but some is; not all weakness is ‘our fault’; sometimes others will sin against us. Whatever the source of our weakness or injury, we should be restored and healed.

<!-- p177 -->
There is great freedom in seeking inner healing and restoration. We are called to cover the weaker parts of the body (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Corinthians" data-chapter="12" data-verses="23" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 12:23</span>) and to restore those caught in transgression (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Galatians" data-chapter="6" data-verses="1" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Galatians 6:1</span>).

<!-- p178 -->
There is much to say about this topic, and we explore themes of healing as an overview in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p179 -->
#### Step 5 - Be Patient

<!-- p180 -->
Consider confirmation from God and from others. Often, God confirms your calling through bearing fruit, joy, and affirmation from those around you. Reflect on where your efforts have borne fruit and listen to feedback about the impact of ministry. This can help you identify where you are most effective in serving the Body of Christ.

<!-- p181 -->
Be patient and trust God’s timing. Discovering your place can take time and involve trial and error (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Philippians" data-chapter="1" data-verses="6" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Philippians 1:6</span>). Believe that God will guide you as you continue to be faithful and open to His leading. During this period, commit to growth and training by attending workshops, Bible studies, and other resources that prepare you for service. Do this together with the church; participate with the Body of Christ to discover your gift.

<!-- p182 -->
Remember, the church is not a building or an institution; it is a living, breathing body made up of people like you. By stepping into your place, you contribute to its mission and glorify Christ as its head.
