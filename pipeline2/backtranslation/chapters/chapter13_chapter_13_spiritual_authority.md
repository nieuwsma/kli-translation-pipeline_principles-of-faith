# Chapter 13 - Spiritual Authority

<!-- p1 -->
Jesus is the supreme and perfect source of spiritual authority. As the victorious Christ, He has ultimate authority over all things in heaven and on earth.

<!-- p2 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV">
<!-- p3 -->
&gt; **<u>Matthew 28:18-20</u>** (NKJV) ***18** And Jesus came and spoke to them, saying, "All authority has been given to Me in heaven and on earth.* ***19** Go therefore and make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit,* ***20** teaching them to observe all things that I have commanded you; and behold, I am with you always, even to the end of the age." Amen.*

<!-- p4 -->
</span>
<!-- p5 -->
In this chapter we will study the authority and power of Christ, which authority and power He has entrusted to us (His church), and how to operate in the authority of the name of Jesus. We will also study angelic and demonic beings and examine their authority, powers, and nature.

<!-- p6 -->
Authority means having the right to act. Power is the ability to carry it out. Both are essential in the spiritual life. Authority is different from power. Let us look at the following table that compares authority &amp; power and consider a few examples.

<!-- p7 -->
[See Table 7](../tables/table7.md)

<!-- p8 -->
Here are a few examples to help understand the difference between power and authority.

<!-- p9 -->
Example 1: At work, an employee may have the talents and skills to sell the company's assets (power). This power is legitimate if he also has the relevant authorization (authority) from the owners of the company. Without this authority, selling the company's assets is theft.

<!-- p10 -->
Example 2: A police officer is given special training in weapons and hand-to-hand combat to learn how to arrest and physically subdue a criminal (power). Police officers are entrusted with a work mandate backed by law (authority) to enforce criminal laws.

## The Source of Jesus' Authority

<!-- p74 -->
Now that we have specifically looked at what authority is, we need to know who the source of that authority is. Jesus' authority is divine and also delegated. It originates from God the Father, is confirmed through His death and resurrection, and operates in perfect harmony with the Father's will. Though perfect, His authority respects human free will, reflecting God's love rather than coercive control.

<!-- p75 -->
Jesus' authority is rooted in His divine identity as the Son of God and is granted by the Father. The Father entrusted Christ with all authority, including judgment and rule over heaven and earth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 11:27</span>). Jesus' authority reflects the unity and love within the Trinity, wherein the Son perfectly carries out the Father's plan (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="35" data-version="NKJV">John 3:35</span>).

<!-- p76 -->
Although Jesus had inherent authority as the Son of God, His victory over sin, death, and hell was fully affirmed through His death and resurrection. Christ's obedience in His sacrifice resulted in His exaltation (seated at the right hand of the Father), securing His authority over all creation. By conquering death, Christ obtained complete dominion over life and eternity.

<!-- p77 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p79 -->
</span>
<!-- p80 -->
Although Jesus has all authority, He uses it in complete agreement with the heart and purpose of the Father:

<!-- p81 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV">

Jesus does not act independently; instead, He reflects the Father’s works, ensuring His authority is always righteous and just. Even His words and teachings reflect the Father’s will, emphasizing their unity in purpose (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="49" data-version="NKJV">John 12:49</span>).

Jesus never forces anyone to obey. He respects our freedom and gently invites us to follow Him.

## Laying on of Hands

<!-- p116 -->
The laying on of hands is an important biblical practice used throughout the Old and New Testaments to signify blessing, commissioning, healing, and the impartation of authority or spiritual gifts. It is more than a physical act—it represents a spiritual connection and transfer under the oversight of God's authority.

<!-- p117 -->
In the Old Testament, the laying on of hands was often associated with blessing and consecration. For example, Jacob blessed his grandsons, Ephraim and Manasseh, by laying his hands on them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="48" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV">Genesis 48:14</span>). Likewise, Moses laid his hands on Joshua to commission him as his successor, granting him leadership authority and wisdom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="NUM" data-chapter="27" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-23" data-version="NKJV">Numbers 27:18-23</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="DEU" data-chapter="34" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV">Deuteronomy 34:9</span>). This act publicly signified God's appointment and empowerment for the work ahead.

<!-- p118 -->
In the New Testament, the laying on of hands is closely associated with the ministry of Jesus and the early church. Jesus frequently laid hands on the sick to heal them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV">Mark 6:5</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV">Luke 4:40</span>), demonstrating compassion and authority over sickness and disease. The apostles continued this practice, using it to impart the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV">Acts 8:17</span>), spiritual gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV">1 Timothy 4:14</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV">2 Timothy 1:6</span>), and blessings to believers. It also contributed to commissioning leaders and missionaries for ministry (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV">Acts 13:3</span>).

<!-- p119 -->
The laying on of hands signifies an act of faith and obedience to the Word of God. It is not mechanical rituals, but a spiritual exercise rooted in dependence on the Holy Spirit. For example, in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV">Acts 6:6</span>, the apostles laid hands on those appointed to serve as deacons, consecrating them to God's service and publicly recognizing their calling. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV">Acts 19:6</span>, Paul laid hands on the believers, and they received the Holy Spirit, which demonstrated his role in empowering them spiritually.

<!-- p120 -->
However, the Bible also provides warnings regarding that practice:

<!-- p121 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV">
<!-- p122 -->
&gt; **<u>1 Timothy 5:22</u>** (NKJV) *Do not lay hands on anyone hastily, nor share in other people's sins; keep yourself pure.*

<!-- p123 -->
</span>
<!-- p124 -->
This highlights the importance of discernment and spiritual maturity when appointing people to positions of leadership. As we learned in the chapter about the Church, leaders must be tested and confirmed to avoid promoting unqualified or immature believers who could bring harm to the Body of Christ.

<!-- p125 -->
A few additional guidelines regarding the laying on of hands:

<!-- p126 -->
1.  Contextually appropriate - the person having hands laid on them should be a willing participant.

<!-- p127 -->
2.  Physically appropriate - the laying on of hands does not involve sexual or romantic touching. In practice, placing a hand on the shoulder, hand, or forehead are generally acceptable places to touch (with permission) in the Western world.

<!-- p128 -->
3.  Culturally appropriate - in some cultures it is not proper for men and women to touch unless they are related by blood or marriage.

<!-- p129 -->
The laying on of hands continues to be a powerful expression of faith and obedience in the Church today. It serves as a means of blessing, healing, consecration, and empowerment, always pointing to the authority of Christ and the work of the Holy Spirit. Whether praying for the sick, sending missionaries, or ordaining leaders, it is a reminder that believers act not in their own strength but under the authority of Jesus Christ and in alignment with His purposes.

## Angels and Demons

<!-- p132 -->
### Angels

<!-- p133 -->
Angels are an important part of God's creation, serving as His messengers and agents to carry out His divine purposes. The Bible reveals a spiritual realm inhabited by these beings, who are powerful, intelligent, and obedient to God's commands. Although angels are often unseen, their presence and actions affect human history and spiritual warfare. Understanding their roles, classifications, and activities gives us deeper insight into God's plan and the unseen reality that surrounds us.

<!-- p134 -->
The word "angel" itself means "messenger," highlighting their primary function. Angels operate in obedience to the will of God, whether delivering messages, providing protection, executing judgment, or engaging in spiritual battles. They are not objects of worship but serve as examples of obedience and reverence, always directing glory to God. This chapter explores the nature, classifications, and roles of angels, highlighting their significance in Scripture and in our lives today.

<!-- p135 -->
In the Scriptures angels appear as **messengers**, **servants**, and **warriors**. Angels often act as messengers, bringing important announcements, warnings, and encouragement. They reveal God's plans and provide guidance in times of need (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26-38" data-version="NKJV">Luke 1:26-38</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 2:13</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV">Revelation 1:1</span>). Angels are actively involved in protecting, strengthening, and delivering believers. They minister to God's people in times of distress and provide miraculous assistance (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="Psalm" data-chapter="91" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV">Psalm 91:11</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-11" data-version="NKJV">Acts 12:7-11</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 4:11</span>). Angels also participate in spiritual warfare, fighting against demonic forces to advance the kingdom of God and defend believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="DAN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV">Daniel 10:13</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-9" data-version="NKJV">Revelation 12:7-9</span>).

<!-- p136 -->
#### The Nature of Angels

<!-- p137 -->
Angels are created beings, brought into existence by God before the foundations of the world were laid. They are distinct from humans, possessing unique attributes that equip them for their heavenly assignments. The Bible teaches that angels are spiritual beings, not bound by physical limitations, yet they can take on visible forms when interacting with humans.

<!-- p138 -->
Angels are described as immortal, meaning they neither die nor age. They are innumerable, vast hosts filling the heavenly realms. Although they are often referred to with masculine pronouns, Scripture makes it clear that angels are without gender and neither marry nor reproduce. These characteristics emphasize their purpose as God's servants, wholly devoted to His will and commands. Angels possess remarkable qualities that reflect their divine purpose. They are obedient, reverent, wise, and powerful. These attributes enable them to carry out God's will effectively and faithfully.

<!-- p140 -->
Although the Bible highlights various roles and ranks of angels, it does not provide a detailed system of classification. However, some angels are identified by specific roles or functions:

<!-- p141 -->
- **The Angel of the Lord** - Often understood as pre-incarnate manifestations of Christ, appearing with divine authority. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="23" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20-23" data-version="NKJV">Exodus 23:20-23</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JDG" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-24" data-version="NKJV">Judges 6:11-24</span>*)

<!-- p142 -->
- **Archangels** - High-ranking angels such as Michael, who leads armies, and Gabriel, who delivers divine messages. (*Jude 9; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV">Revelation 12:7</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-26" data-version="NKJV">Luke 1:19-26</span>*)

<!-- p143 -->
- **Cherubim** - Guardians of the glory and presence of God, as in Eden and on the Ark of the Covenant. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV">Genesis 3:24</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV">Exodus 25:22</span>*)

<!-- p144 -->
- **Seraphim** - Worshipers of God, continually proclaiming His holiness. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-6" data-version="NKJV">Isaiah 6:1-6</span>*)

<!-- p145 -->
- **Elect Angels** - Faithful angels who refused Satan’s rebellion and remain loyal to God. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV">1 Timothy 5:21</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="41" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 25:41</span>*)

<!-- p146 -->
This classification emphasizes the diversity and order within the angelic realm, while their primary orientation is always to fulfill God’s purposes.

<!-- p147 -->
#### Human Interaction with Angels

<!-- p148 -->
Humans often encounter angels in Scripture, but we are never instructed to command them or pray to them. Instead, angels act according to God’s direction. They minister to believers as **servants of God** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 1:14</span>). Their interactions include protection, guidance, and deliverance, always pointing us back to God’s power and sovereignty.

<!-- p149 -->
- **Warning Against Angel Worship**: Scripture forbids worshiping angels or seeking encounters with them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV">Colossians 2:18</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV">Revelation 22:8-9</span>).

<!-- p150 -->
- **Prayer to God, Not to Angels**: Believers should pray to God, who sends angels as He wills (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="Psalm" data-chapter="91" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV">Psalm 91:11</span>).

<!-- p151 -->
- **Encouragement and Hope**: Angels remind us of God’s care, but our faith should rely fully on God, not on angels.

<!-- p152 -->
Angels are awe-inspiring and fearsome beings created to serve God and minister to His people. Though their work is often unseen, Scripture sheds light on their activities, revealing their faithfulness, power, and reverence. Angels remind us of God’s sufficiency and protection, but always direct us back to His glory. As believers, we are called to rely on God’s care, knowing that angels operate as His servants to accomplish His divine purposes.

<!-- p153 -->
### Demons

<!-- p154 -->
Just as the study of angels is important for understanding the spiritual realm, so too it is important to learn about demons. Scripture clearly explains their activities, their influence, and their defeat through Jesus Christ. Recognizing their place in spiritual warfare helps believers stand firm in faith without fear.

<!-- p155 -->
#### The Origin and Nature of Demons

<!-- p156 -->
Demons are fallen angels who followed Satan in rebellion against God. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-9" data-version="NKJV">Revelation 12:7-9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="41" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 25:41</span>).* Satan, formerly known as Lucifer (“light-bearer”), an exalted angel, sought to exalt himself above God and was cast out of heaven along with the other rebellious angels. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-15" data-version="NKJV">Isaiah 14:12-15</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EZK" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-17" data-version="NKJV">Ezekiel 28:12-17</span>).*

<!-- p157 -->
Demons are spirits without physical bodies but can harass and afflict humans and animals. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV">Mark 5:11-13</span>).* They seek to deceive, tempt, oppress, and destroy. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV">John 10:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV">2 Corinthians 11:14</span>). Unlike God,* they are limited, and are not omnipresent (everywhere), not omniscient (all-knowing), not omnipotent (all-powerful)*.* Their complete defeat is certain, and they will face eternal punishment. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV">Revelation 20:10</span>)*

<!-- p158 -->
Scripture describes the works and activities of the devil as:

<!-- p159 -->
- Opposes the Work of God *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV">1 Thessalonians 2:18</span>)*

<!-- p160 -->
- He deceives the world *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV">Revelation 12:9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV">2 Corinthians 4:4</span>)*

<!-- p161 -->
- He tempts and accuses believers *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV">Luke 22:31</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV">Revelation 12:10</span>)*

<!-- p162 -->
- He persecutes and harms *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JOB" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV">Job 1:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV">Luke 13:16</span>)*

<!-- p163 -->
There are many names and descriptions of the devil, each describing a different aspect of his work.

<!-- p165 -->
The Gospels and the epistles further define the work of the enemy. Evil spirits, since they do not have bodies, are largely forced to do their work through vessels that are willing and open. God works through people, and so does the devil. This oppression often manifests as suffering, self-harm, and destructive behaviors *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-29" data-version="NKJV">Luke 8:27-29</span>).* Their attacks cause fear, temptations, and discouragement *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV">2 Corinthians 12:7</span>).* The most common source of their power (not authority) in a person's life is through the influence of lies and emotional strongholds *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-5" data-version="NKJV">2 Corinthians 10:3-5</span>).*

<!-- p166 -->
It is important to recognize that not every lie a person believes, weakness in their body, or stronghold in their life is a direct result of demonic influence, but these areas of woundedness are places where the enemy can exploit us and keep us in a state of discouragement and oppression.

<!-- p167 -->
#### The Authority of Jesus Over the Works of the Enemy

<!-- p168 -->
Do not be afraid, Jesus has full and absolute authority over the works of the enemy. Jesus demonstrated power over demons during His ministry *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="34" data-version="NKJV">Mark 1:34</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-20" data-version="NKJV">Mark 5:1-20</span>).* The works of the enemy and Satan himself have been defeated through the death and resurrection of Jesus *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV">Colossians 2:15</span>).*

<!-- p169 -->
As believers we have been given authority delegated by Jesus to cast out demons in His Name:

<!-- p170 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV">
<!-- p171 -->
&gt; **<u>Mark 16:17</u>** (NKJV) …In My name they will cast out demons…

<!-- p172 -->
</span>
<!-- p173 -->
Moreover, as heirs of salvation we have authority to submit to God, resist the devil, and he will flee from us! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV">James 4:7</span>).

<!-- p174 -->
When confronting an evil or unclean spirit, the pattern shown in Scripture is to take authority over it in the name of Jesus and command it to leave. The Holy Spirit promises to give us wisdom and discernment when we ask Him! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV">James 1:5</span>). We do not need to make it a spectacle, or a drama like in the movies, nor even talk to them; that is a trick of the devil to distract us. Satan's power is initiated and sustained only by the lies he can make us believe.

<!-- p175 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="18" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV">
<!-- p176 -->
&gt; **<u>Matthew 18:18</u>** (NKJV) whatever we bind on earth will be bound in heaven

<!-- p177 -->
</span>
<!-- p178 -->
We tell them in the name of Jesus and command them to leave.

<!-- p179 -->
There are two main cautions in Scripture when we deal with the demonic:

<!-- p180 -->
1.  **Only those who have a relationship with Jesus have authority over demons**. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-20" data-version="NKJV">Acts 19:11-20</span> speaks about the sons of a Jewish chief priest named Sceva. They tried to intervene in the life of a man who had been oppressed and tormented by the devil, and the evil spirit refused to leave because they had no relationship with Jesus. The oppressed man leapt on them, stripped them, and beat them. Just as you should not pretend to be a police officer, **do not pretend to be someone who has a relationship with Jesus—rather, have a relationship with Jesus**!

<!-- p181 -->
2.  **When an evil spirit departs, it goes away for a time and will try to return**. If it finds the place that had been its dwelling empty, it will return with greater wickedness. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="43-45" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 12:43-45</span>. When we minister to someone oppressed by the devil, that person needs to fill the gap that the evil spirit had occupied in their life. That means at least initiating a personal relationship with Jesus and strengthening it through obedience to the Holy Spirit. Often an evil or unclean spirit gains power to operate in a person's life through choices they have made, in line with their beliefs. Anyone delivered from the devil needs to change that old belief system and be transformed by the renewing of their mind (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV">Romans 12:1-2</span>).

<!-- p182 -->
We serve a wonderful and mighty God, and we are ‘more than conquerors’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="37" data-version="NKJV">Romans 8:37</span>) over a defeated enemy. We do not need to fear our defeated enemy, for Jesus has given us His authority to crush his head! The devil’s time is short, and he will ultimately be judged together with every dominion and authority on earth that refuses the rule of Christ.
