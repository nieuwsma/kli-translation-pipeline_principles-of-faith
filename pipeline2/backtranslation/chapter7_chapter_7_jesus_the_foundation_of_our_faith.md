# Chapter 7 - Jesus-The Foundation of Our Faith

<!-- p1 -->
Building on our lesson on Jesus as the Messiah, in this chapter we continue to look at the life, person, and work of Jesus. We will see that Jesus is the Mediator of a new and better covenant-and that He fully fulfills the offices of Prophet, Priest, and King. Within this New Covenant we as believers stand rightly before God and undergo a shift from a works-based covenant to a covenant based on faithfulness (faith-based). Finally, we will read and examine the *Apostles’ Creed* as a way to complete our study about Jesus.

<!-- p2 -->
## Mediator of the New Covenant

<!-- p3 -->
The idea of a mediator involves a go-between who bridges the gap between two parties, facilitating relationship or agreement. In the Exodus narrative, Moses is the initial mediator of the covenant that God made with His people. Moses received the law, offered sacrifices (priest), called God’s people to obedience (prophet), and judged them (judge / king). In the remainder of the Old Testament after Moses, the mediatorial role was carried out through the office of the Prophet, the Levitical Priesthood, and the Judges and later the Kings of the nation.

<!-- p4 -->
The Offices of Mediators in the Old Testament:

<!-- p5 -->
1.  **Prophet:** Prophets were God’s messengers, sent to speak His heart to the people and to call them back when they strayed. For example, Moses served as a prophet who mediated the Old Covenant by receiving the Law from God and teaching Israel. The prophets also foretold the coming of the Messiah and the ultimate redemption of humanity. Other notable examples of prophets are: Isaiah, Jeremiah, Elijah, Elisha, Samuel.

<!-- p6 -->
2.  **The Levitical Priesthood:** The priests acted as mediators by offering sacrifices for the atonement of sin and interceding for the people before God. The High Priest in particular had a crucial role on the Day of Atonement, entering the Holy of Holies to sprinkle blood on the mercy seat, signifying reconciliation with God. The ministry of the priest is to intercede for the people.

<!-- p7 -->
3.  **Judge/King:** Judges, and later Kings, were entrusted with the responsibility of ruling God’s people, enforcing His laws, judging between the people, redeeming them from their enemies, and leading them in righteousness. The Book of Judges recounts the stories of these leaders-leaders-people like Deborah, Samson, and Gideon-who God commissioned to save and lead Israel. Israel later had a succession of kings, many evil, a few good (see 1 and 2 Kings, 1 and 2 Samuel, and 1<sup>st</sup> and 2<sup>nd</sup> Chronicles). Some notable examples are Saul, David, and Solomon.

<!-- p8 -->
It is worth noting that all the mediators of the Old Testament were fully human and not divine. As humans they were able to sin, and often gave themselves over to sin. Some mediators were sinful and led the people away from God and toward unholy worship: false prophets, lying and corrupt priests, and evil kings.

<!-- p9 -->
### Christ as Mediator of the New Covenant

<!-- p10 -->
The death, resurrection, and ascension of Jesus fulfill the Old Covenant and inaugurate the New Covenant.

<!-- p11 -->
The New Covenant is a transforming relationship between God and His people, established through the life, death, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. Unlike the Old Covenant, which focused on external laws, the New Covenant emphasizes inner transformation and grace. It was formally instituted by Jesus at the Last Supper, where He declared that His blood would inaugurate this covenant, replacing the repeated animal sacrifices of the Old Covenant with His once-for-all atonement (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Luke" data-chapter="22" data-verses="20" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Luke 22:20</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Hebrews" data-chapter="9" data-verses="12" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 9:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Hebrews" data-chapter="10" data-verses="10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 10:10</span>). Through this sacrifice, the New Covenant offers complete forgiveness and cleansing of sins, fulfilling the requirement for a perfect and enduring reconciliation with God (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Jeremiah" data-chapter="31" data-verses="34" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Jeremiah 31:34</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Hebrews" data-chapter="8" data-verses="12" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 8:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Hebrews" data-chapter="10" data-verses="12-18" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 10:12-18</span>).

<!-- p12 -->
One of the chief characteristics of the New Covenant is its internal nature, as God writes His law on the hearts of His people, cultivating a heart-level relationship with Him through the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Jeremiah" data-chapter="31" data-verses="33" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Jeremiah 31:33</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Hebrews" data-chapter="8" data-verses="10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 8:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="2 Corinthians" data-chapter="3" data-verses="3" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">2 Corinthians 3:3</span>). This transformation enables believers to live out God’s will not merely by adhering to external commands but by being empowered by the Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Romans" data-chapter="8" data-verses="1-4" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 8:1-4</span>). The New Covenant also grants direct access to God, removing the need for an earthly priesthood. Jesus serves as the eternal High Priest, and believers are called a royal priesthood, having confidence to draw near to God through Christ’s sacrifice (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Hebrews" data-chapter="7" data-verses="24-25" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 7:24-25</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Hebrews" data-chapter="10" data-verses="19-20" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 10:19-20</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Peter" data-chapter="2" data-verses="9" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Peter 2:9</span>).

<!-- p13 -->
Unlike the Old Covenant, which was specific to Israel, the New Covenant is universal, extending to all who believe in Christ, regardless of ethnicity or background (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="John" data-chapter="3" data-verses="16" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 3:16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Galatians" data-chapter="3" data-verses="28" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Galatians 3:28</span>). This covenant rests entirely on grace, offering salvation and forgiveness freely through faith in Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Ephesians" data-chapter="2" data-verses="8-9" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>). It is not sustained by human effort but by the power of the Holy Spirit, who transforms and empowers believers to live in obedience and reflect God’s character (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Ezekiel" data-chapter="36" data-verses="26-27" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ezekiel 36:26-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Romans" data-chapter="8" data-verses="9-16" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 8:9-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Galatians" data-chapter="5" data-verses="22-23" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Galatians 5:22-23</span>). Moreover, the New Covenant is eternal, superseding the temporary and conditional nature of the Old Covenant (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Jeremiah" data-chapter="32" data-verses="40" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Jeremiah 32:40</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Hebrews" data-chapter="8" data-verses="13" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 8:13</span>). It represents the ultimate fulfillment of God’s plan of redemption, reconciling humanity to Him and enabling believers to live as His people under His reign forever.

<!-- p14 -->
Jesus is explicitly identified as the Mediator of the New Covenant in

<!-- p15 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="Hebrews" data-chapter="9" data-verses="15" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p16 -->
> **<u>Hebrews 9:15</u>** (NKJV) - *For this reason Christ is the mediator of a new covenant, so that those who are called may receive the promised eternal inheritance*.

<!-- p17 -->
</span>

<!-- p18 -->
Through His atoning death and resurrection, Christ establishes this new covenant, replacing the old system of sacrifices and external laws with a covenant written on the hearts of believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Jeremiah" data-chapter="31" data-verses="31-34" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Jeremiah 31:31-34</span>). He stands between God and humanity, not only interceding for His people but also providing the way of atonement through His own blood (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Hebrews" data-chapter="9" data-verses="12" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 9:12</span>). Unlike the mediators of the Old Covenant, Jesus is sinless, eternal, and can fully satisfy the divine demands of justice and mercy, securing a lasting relationship between God and believers.

<!-- p19 -->
Jesus Fulfills and Completes These Offices

<!-- p20 -->
1.  **Prophet:** Jesus is the supreme Prophet, not only a messenger of the word of God but the Word made flesh (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="John" data-chapter="1" data-verses="14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 1:14</span>). He fully and perfectly reveals the character of God, His will, and His plan of salvation (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Hebrews" data-chapter="1" data-verses="1-2" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 1:1-2</span>). His teachings, His miracles, and His prophetic proclamations fulfill Old Testament prophecy and lay the foundation for the New Covenant.

<!-- p21 -->
2.  **Priest:** Jesus is the perfect High Priest, offering Himself as a once-for-all sacrifice for sin (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Hebrews" data-chapter="7" data-verses="27" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 7:27</span>). Unlike the priests of the Old Covenant who had to offer sacrifices repeatedly for their own sins and those of the people, Jesus, being without sin, shed His own blood to obtain eternal redemption (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Hebrews" data-chapter="9" data-verses="14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 9:14</span>).

<!-- p22 -->
3.  **King and Judge:** Jesus is the eternal King, who reigns in righteousness and justice. As the Son of David, He fulfills the promise of an everlasting kingdom (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="2 Samuel" data-chapter="7" data-verses="16" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">2 Samuel 7:16</span>). His kingdom is not merely earthly but universal, ruling over all creation and establishing peace through His victory over sin and death (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Philippians" data-chapter="2" data-verses="9-11" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Philippians 2:9-11</span>). Jesus will return as king and judge, and He will judge 'the living and the dead' (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="10" data-verses="42" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 10:42</span>).

<!-- p23 -->
In Christ, the offices of Prophet, Priest, and King unite and are fully fulfilled. As Mediator of the New Covenant, He bridges the gap between a holy God and sinful humanity, enabling believers to approach God with confidence (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Hebrews" data-chapter="4" data-verses="16" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 4:16</span>) and securing the eternal inheritance promised in Him.

## The Apostles' Creed

<!-- p25 -->
*The Apostles' Creed* is not found in Scripture; it was composed and refined in accordance with the early teachings of the church. A creed is an official confession of faith; think of it as a pledge or oath paired with a statement of belief. Creeds are important because they serve both to unite and to distinguish those who believe in a certain way from those who do not. In the United States it is common in civic activities such as city council meetings or legal assemblies for participants to recite the Pledge of Allegiance. The Pledge is widely used in the United States, but it is not used in other countries, because the citizens of those nations owe no allegiance to the United States. The Pledge is not what makes a person 'an American,' but it represents foundational beliefs important to those who are citizens.

<!-- p26 -->
The earliest recognizable versions of this creed were written in the fourth century A.D. The creed has changed slightly over the centuries, and there are minor variations depending on denomination. Not every Christian denomination uses this creed, but by and large it has been accepted in some form throughout Christianity.

<!-- p27 -->
The Apostles' Creed has been used as a tool of teaching and catechesis for centuries and presents the most basic truths of the Christian faith, core beliefs that must be confessed and held in order to be a Christian. Some denominations have a formal catechesis process that is the way a new believer joins that specific denomination and is initiated into their teachings.

<!-- p28 -->
A person does not need to fully understand these beliefs in order to come to God and receive salvation; salvation is by faith, not by a doctrinal exam! However, as we continue in that faith we all should understand these foundational truths.

## A Brief Explanation of the Apostles' Creed

<!-- p31 -->
The foundational truths in the Apostles' Creed are many and profound; a full explanation would certainly take many volumes to set them forth. Although this chapter is about Jesus, many topics also touch the other members of the Trinity. These will be condensed notes on this creed, focusing only on the most central topics. Some of these subjects are studied in greater depth in various chapters of this book.

<!-- p32 -->
#### *I believe in God, the Father Almighty, maker of heaven and earth.*

<!-- p33 -->
This affirms belief in God as the Creator and Almighty Father. The title "Father" emphasizes God's loving relationship with His creation, especially His children through faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="John" data-chapter="1" data-verses="12" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 1:12</span>). As Creator, He spoke the world into being, bringing order out of chaos (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Genesis" data-chapter="1" data-verses="1-3" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Genesis 1:1-3</span>). His supreme power is seen in the vastness of creation, while His fatherly care is seen in His continual sustenance and providence. This foundational statement affirms God as the source and ruler of all life, worthy of worship and trust.

<!-- p34 -->
#### *I believe in Jesus Christ, His only Son, our Lord.*

<!-- p35 -->
Jesus Christ is the unique Son of God, eternally begotten, not created, and sharing the divine nature (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="John" data-chapter="1" data-verses="1-2" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 1:1-2</span>, 14). Jesus is the 'Word' from the beginning, and He became flesh and lived among us. As "Christ," He is the anointed Messiah, fulfilling Old Testament promises of a Savior. The title "Lord" recognizes His authority over all creation and His rightful place as ruler and King (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Philippians" data-chapter="2" data-verses="10-11" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Philippians 2:10-11</span>). He is not only Lord, but the Lord who brings salvation (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-type="ref" data-book="Acts" data-chapter="16" data-verses="31" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 16:31</span>).

<!-- p36 -->
Jesus not **only** was sent from God; He **is** part of God. Jesus abides in the Father and the Father abides in Jesus. The Gospel of John repeatedly affirms that Jesus and the Father are one (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="John" data-chapter="10" data-verses="30" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 10:30</span>), that Jesus did not act by His own will (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="John" data-chapter="5" data-verses="19" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 5:19</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="John" data-chapter="12" data-verses="49-50" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 12:49-50</span>), and that everything He did was to glorify His Father (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="John" data-chapter="17" data-verses="4" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 17:4</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="John" data-chapter="14" data-verses="13" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 14:13</span>), and was done by the authority and power of His Father (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="John" data-chapter="14" data-verses="10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 14:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="John" data-chapter="8" data-verses="28-29" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 8:28-29</span>).

<!-- p37 -->
#### *He was conceived by the power of the Holy Spirit and born of the Virgin Mary.*

<!-- p38 -->
This affirms the miraculous conception of Jesus through the Holy Spirit, emphasizing His divine nature. Born of the Virgin Mary, Jesus fulfills the prophecy of <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Isaiah" data-chapter="7" data-verses="14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Isaiah 7:14</span>, which foretold the virgin birth of the Messiah. This event signifies the Incarnation, where the eternal Word of God took on human flesh (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="John" data-chapter="1" data-verses="14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 1:14</span>). Being fully God and fully man, Jesus bridges the gap between God and humanity, enabling Him to be the perfect Mediator and Savior.

<!-- p39 -->
Jesus was conceived by the seed of the Holy Spirit, and He did not inherit the sin nature that we, who are born of the seed of man, have. Jesus was tempted and tested in every way, but He was perfect and without sin. <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-type="ref" data-book="Hebrews" data-chapter="4" data-verses="15" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 4:15</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="2 Corinthians" data-chapter="5" data-verses="21" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">2 Corinthians 5:21</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Peter" data-chapter="2" data-verses="22" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Peter 2:22</span>.

<!-- p40 -->
#### *He suffered under Pontius Pilate, was crucified, died, and was buried.*

<!-- p41 -->
The sufferings and death of Jesus were real historical events that took place under the Roman governor Pontius Pilate, anchoring the Christian faith in verifiable history. His crucifixion fulfilled Old Testament prophecy, such as <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Isaiah" data-chapter="53" data-verses="5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Isaiah 53:5</span>, which speaks of the Suffering Servant who would bear the sins of many. Jesus' death was sacrificial, taking the penalty of sin on behalf of humanity (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Peter" data-chapter="2" data-verses="24" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Peter 2:24</span>). His burial confirms the reality of His death and lays the foundation for the significance of the resurrection.

<!-- p42 -->
#### *He descended to the dead.*

<!-- p43 -->
After His death, Jesus descended into the realm of the dead, often referred to as *Hades* or *Sheol* in Scripture. This act demonstrates His full identification with humanity, even in death, and signifies His victory over sin, death, and the devil (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Peter" data-chapter="3" data-verses="18-20" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Peter 3:18-20</span>). By descending, He proclaimed His victory and the completion of His work of redemption.

<!-- p44 -->
#### *On the third day He rose again.*

<!-- p45 -->
The resurrection of Jesus is the cornerstone of our Christian faith, confirming His divinity and the truth of His teachings. By rising from the dead, He defeated sin and death, providing the assurance of eternal life for believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Corinthians" data-chapter="15" data-verses="17-22" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 15:17-22</span>). The resurrection also fulfills the Scriptures, including Jesus' own predictions (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Matthew" data-chapter="16" data-verses="21" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 16:21</span>). This miraculous event turned the disciples' despair into bold faith, and formed the foundation of the early Church's witness.

<!-- p46 -->
#### *He ascended into heaven, and is seated at the right hand of the Father.*

<!-- p47 -->
Jesus' ascension signifies His return to the Father's presence in glory. He is seated at the right hand of the Father (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Ephesians" data-chapter="1" data-verses="20" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 1:20</span}), assuming the office of the eternal High Priest, interceding for believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Hebrews" data-chapter="7" data-verses="25" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 7:25</span>). His position of authority also signifies His exaltation as King, ruling over all creation and directing the Church's mission (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Ephesians" data-chapter="1" data-verses="22" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 1:22</span>) to do greater works (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="John" data-chapter="14" data-verses="12" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 14:12</span>) and to fulfill the Great Commission He gave us (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Matthew" data-chapter="28" data-verses="18-20" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 28:18-20</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Mark" data-chapter="16" data-verses="15-18" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Mark 16:15-18</span>). This ascension assures believers of His ongoing presence and power through the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p48 -->
#### *He will come again to judge the living and the dead.*

<!-- p49 -->
This points to Christ's future return when He will bring final justice and restoration. Jesus will judge all people, rewarding the faithful and bringing justice to the unrepentant (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="2 Timothy" data-chapter="4" data-verses="1" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">2 Timothy 4:1</span>). His return will fulfill God's ultimate plan for creation, ushering in His eternal Kingdom of righteousness and peace. This hope encourages believers to live faithfully and expectantly, knowing that their labor in the Lord is not in vain (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Corinthians" data-chapter="15" data-verses="58" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 15:58</span>).

<!-- p50 -->
#### *I believe in the Holy Spirit.*

<!-- p51 -->
The Holy Spirit, the third Person of the Trinity, is God's presence and power at work in the world today. He convicts of sin, renews hearts, and empowers believers to live holy lives (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="John" data-chapter="16" data-verses="8-13" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 16:8-13</span>). The Spirit unites Christians into one body, that is, the Church, and grants spiritual gifts for service (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Corinthians" data-chapter="12" data-verses="4-11" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 12:4-11</span>). He is the Comforter, Advocate, and Guide, indwelling believers as the seal of their eternal inheritance (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Ephesians" data-chapter="1" data-verses="13-14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>).

<!-- p52 -->
#### *The holy catholic Church, the communion of saints.*

<!-- p53 -->
"The holy catholic Church" refers to the worldwide body of Christ, encompassing all believers across times and places. The Church is set apart for God's purposes, called to reflect His holiness and to share the gospel. "The communion of saints" emphasizes the unity of all Christians, living and dead, as members of the body of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Ephesians" data-chapter="4" data-verses="4-6" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 4:4-6</span>). This fellowship transcends worldly divisions, expressing the shared hope and mission of believers.

<!-- p54 -->
Jesus is the head of the Church. The Church is referred to as the bride of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Ephesians" data-chapter="5" data-verses="25-27" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 5:25-27</span>) and the body of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Colossians" data-chapter="1" data-verses="18" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Colossians 1:18</span>).

<!-- p55 -->
#### *The forgiveness of sins.*

<!-- p56 -->
This highlights the heart of the gospel: through Jesus' sacrificial death, God grants full forgiveness of sins to all who repent and believe (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Ephesians" data-chapter="1" data-verses="7" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 1:7</span>). This forgiveness restores the broken relationship between humanity and God, removing guilt and shame. It also calls believers to forgive others, reflecting the grace they have received (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Matthew" data-chapter="6" data-verses="14-15" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 6:14-15</span>).

<!-- p57 -->
#### *The resurrection of the body.*

<!-- p58 -->
Like Jesus, we will experience the resurrection of the body at the end of the age. This resurrection will transform mortal, perishable bodies into glorified, imperishable bodies suitable for eternal life (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1 Corinthians" data-chapter="15" data-verses="42-44" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 15:42-44</span>). It underscores the Christian hope of restoration, not only for individuals but for all creation, as God makes all things new (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="Revelation" data-chapter="21" data-verses="4-5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Revelation 21:4-5</span>).

<!-- p59 -->
#### *And the life everlasting. Amen.*

<!-- p60 -->
Eternal life with God is the highest promise of the Christian faith. This life begins now through a relationship with Christ and will be fully realized in the new heavens and new earth, where believers will dwell with God forever (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="John" data-chapter="3" data-verses="16" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 3:16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-type="ref" data-book="Revelation" data-chapter="22" data-verses="1-5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Revelation 22:1-5</span>). This final declaration reflects the joy and confidence of salvation, echoing the faith and hope expressed throughout the Apostles' Creed.

<!-- p61 -->
## Conclusion

<!-- p62 -->
In the foundational truths presented in the Apostles' Creed, we see the life and work of Jesus as the center of God's plan for humanity. Every statement in that Creed captures essential aspects of our faith in God, and of who Jesus is—his divinity, his humanity, his mission, and his final victory. These truths highlight his unique role as the Mediator of the New Covenant. As Mediator he invites us to draw near to God with confidence, trusting in his perfect sacrifice and his ongoing intercession. This calls us to worship him as the rightful King and to follow his teachings as the supreme Prophet. Ultimately, learning about Jesus leads us into a deeper relationship with him, transforming us into people who live in the light of his grace and truth.

<!-- p63 -->
Understanding who Jesus is and what he has done is the foundation of the Christian faith. The many names, titles, and roles of Jesus illuminate different facets of his character and mission, giving a more complete picture of his love and authority.

<!-- p64 -->
Just like our lesson about God, the Scriptures are filled with various names that refer to Jesus. Each name represents a specific aspect of his whole character, his ministry, and his role. These names were given to reveal a facet of Jesus. For your personal study a reference table has been included in the Appendix: *Table 12 - Names of Jesus.* Review these names and read the Scripture references.
