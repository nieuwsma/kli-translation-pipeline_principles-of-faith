# Chapter 10 - Sanctified-Prepared for God's Purpose

<!-- p1 -->
In the last two chapters we discussed several important topics. We examined the role and ministry of the Holy Spirit, along with the role and ministry of the church.

<!-- p2 -->
We have a part to play in the church, and although we know that we need training, we also need sanctification. To be truly prepared for God’s purposes we must be sanctified.

<!-- p3 -->
The definition of **sanctification** is to cleanse something for a specific purpose. This is closely related to the word **consecrated** which means to set something aside for a specific purpose.

<!-- p4 -->
Imagine a master sculptor preparing to work with a large piece of marble. The master envisions how this piece of marble, still raw and shapeless, can be transformed through his careful craftsmanship. He has a very specific design in mind; he instructs his assistants to set this piece of marble aside for that particular design (**consecrated**). As he refines it and works on it, adding design, depth, and form, he sanctifies it (**he sanctifies it**) for that specific design. The act of consecrating that marble for his special project was quick, whereas the process of shaping it to its final design requires great patience.

<!-- p5 -->
We were set apart, consecrated to God at salvation. Through the act of sanctifying our souls with the help of the Spirit we are transformed. This process of sanctification is what sanctifies us.

<!-- p6 -->
When we received Jesus through salvation, we were born again. We were justified, our sins were forgiven. Our spirit was renewed; we became alive in Christ. We were adopted into the family of God and became an actual temple of the Holy Spirit. Such an astounding transformation that the Scriptures say, “*there is joy in the presence of the angels of God over one sinner who repents.” (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV">Luke 15:10</span>).

<!-- p7 -->
Receiving Jesus for salvation (**justification**) saves our spirit, cleanses us, and makes us able to stand before God so we can receive His Holy Spirit. However, despite such great salvation our souls (our minds, wills, and emotions) still need work!

<!-- p8 -->
Was the work of salvation not completed? No! The work of salvation is effective, but it must be carried forward in the life of the believer by the renewal brought by the Holy Spirit. This process of being renewed and transformed is the work of **sanctification.**

<!-- p9 -->
We know there is ongoing work being done after conversion because our bitter memories did not disappear, many of our beliefs remained the same, and many of our behaviors or habits did not vanish. Moreover, conversion did not separate our minds from their carnal nature (carnal).

<!-- p10 -->
The word ‘carnal’ comes from the Latin root meaning ‘body’ or ‘flesh.’ The fleshly nature is in each of us from birth; like an animal’s instincts, it exists to help us survive. The way the carnal nature operates is by being self-focused, self-reliant, self-involved. Children are natural examples of the nature of the flesh. Despite their sweetness, their nature is primarily oriented toward their desires, their cravings, their needs; and ‘watch out!’ if you don’t meet its every expectation.

<!-- p11 -->
Part of growing up is maturing beyond our childish ways, but when we are pressed, we very easily revert to a way of thinking and living that revolves around self. As James says:

<!-- p12 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV">
<!-- p13 -->
&gt; **<u>James 4:1</u>** (NKJV) *Where do the wars and fights that come from among you come from? Do they not come from your desires for pleasure that wage war in your members?*

<!-- p14 -->
</span>
<!-- p15 -->
Paul explains this carnal nature in more detail in his letter to the Romans (chapters 6–8). He says this fleshly nature keeps him doing what he hates and makes him do evil. He describes the inner conflict as a war between his human nature and his spirit fashioned by God.

<!-- p16 -->
This conflict between the flesh and the spirit exists within each of us, and it is our task to do something about it. If we do not confront our carnal nature, our souls will remain filled with patterns of spiritual decay, even though our spirits have been redeemed.

<!-- p17 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV">
<!-- p18 -->
&gt; **<u>Romans 8:6-8</u>** (NKJV) **6** *For to be carnally minded is death; but to be spiritually minded is life and peace.* ***7** Because the carnal mind is enmity against God; for it is not subject to the law of God, nor indeed can be.* ***8** So then, those who are in the flesh cannot please God.*

<!-- p19 -->
</span>
<!-- p20 -->
These words of Paul are both a warning and a promise to us. Even though we have been born again, we still have a carnal mind that needs to be brought under the knowledge of Christ. As we do so, we walk in the life and peace of God.

<!-- p21 -->
Take heart, there is hope for us! We must walk according to the Spirit and be led by the Spirit of God. By doing so, we will not walk according to the flesh.

<!-- p22 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="1,13,14" data-version="NKJV">
<!-- p23 -->
&gt; **<u>Romans 8:1,13,14</u>** (NKJV)
&gt;
&gt;**1** *There is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus, who do not walk according to the flesh, but according to the Spirit…*
&gt;
&gt;***13** For if you live according to the flesh you will die; but if by the Spirit you put to death the deeds of the body, you will live.*
&gt;
&gt;***14** For as many as are led by the Spirit of God, these are sons of God.*

<!-- p24 -->
</span>
<!-- p25 -->
How do we learn to walk in the Spirit? Is it merely a deliberate choice? We learn to walk in the Spirit the way we learn to walk naturally. We are stirred to move and to grow, so we struggle, we strain, we crawl, we wobble, until we can stand on our own feet. Our natural parents cannot walk in our place, but they can support us, encourage us, help us, and empower us in our walk.

<!-- p26 -->
In the same way, God cannot exercise our faith for us. He will teach us, but we must put in the effort. As Paul told the Philippians:

<!-- p27 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="12-13" data-version="NKJV">
<!-- p28 -->
&gt; **<u>Philippians 2:12-13</u>** (NKJV) **12** *Therefore, my beloved, as you have always obeyed, not as in my presence only, but now much more in my absence, work out your own salvation with fear and trembling; **13** for it is God who works in you both to will and to do for His good pleasure.*

<!-- p29 -->
</span>
<!-- p30 -->
We must work out our own salvation. That does not mean that we climb up onto a wooden cross and are physically crucified, but rather that we must crucify our carnal nature (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV">Galatians 5:24</span>). God promises that He will work within us and help us, but we must make the decision to live this out, and we must hold to it.

<!-- p31 -->
It is true to say that ‘we’ deal with our carnal nature, but it is also true to say that ‘God’ is the one who deals with it. The work of the cross (justification) was hard for Jesus—He had to give His life for ours—but easy for us; it is a free gift. The work of the Spirit (sanctification) is easy for God—He walks in perfect truth and is willing and desires to dwell with us—but it is hard for us, because we must crucify our old nature, and our nature is not very eager to be crucified.

<!-- p32 -->
This process is cooperative. God helps, provides support, leads us, directs us, and corrects us. We listen, obey, follow, and respond. The result is growth and transformation. This is the pruning that Jesus said God would do in us. Jesus is the vine, His Father is the vinedresser, we are the branches:

<!-- p33 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV">
<!-- p34 -->
&gt; **<u>John 15:1-4</u>** (NKJV)
&gt;
&gt;***1** "I am the true vine, and My Father is the vinedresser."*
&gt;
&gt;***2** Every branch in Me that does not bear fruit He takes away; and every branch that bears fruit He prunes, that it may bear more fruit.*
&gt;
&gt;***3** You are already clean because of the word which I have spoken to you.*
&gt;
&gt;***4** Abide in Me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself, unless it abides in the vine, neither can you, unless you abide in Me."*

<!-- p35 -->
</span>
<!-- p36 -->
Pruning is very healthy for a plant. Remembering our chapter on repentance, we are called to turn from dead works (those works that no longer bear fruit) and turn to God. The result of this turning from the dead areas of our lives, and toward God, enables the Master Gardener of our lives to prune branches, improve growth, and bear much more spiritual fruit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV">John 15:2</span>).

<!-- p37 -->
Although pruning can feel painful, it reflects God’s loving care as He shapes us into vessels for His purpose (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 12:11</span>). Some may feel a sense of loss, even fear, as they go through the pruning process. However, God is gentle, and even a bruised reed He will not break (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="42" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV">Isaiah 42:3</span>). Vines are pruned in their season of rest to reduce stress on the plant. The gardener overseeing growth cuts away the old, tough growth that no longer produces fruit as before; this allows the vine to direct energy to branches with the greatest potential for good fruit.

<!-- p38 -->
Just as a gardener cuts branches to direct growth, God may allow seasons of warning and discipline to refine our faith and character, but His work is patient and kind. God prunes those who abide in Him. Let our focus be more on abiding in Christ, and less on the work of pruning.

<!-- p39 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 12:11</span> reminds us that pruning “*yields the peaceable fruit of righteousness*” for those who have been trained by it.

<!-- p40 -->
How are we taught by righteousness? By being transformed through the renewing of our minds.

<!-- p41 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV">
<!-- p42 -->
&gt; **<u>Romans 12:1-2</u>** (NKJV) ***1** Therefore, I beseech you, brothers, by the mercies of God, present your bodies as a living sacrifice, holy, pleasing to God, which is your worship that is fitting. **2** And do not be conformed to this world, but <u>be transformed</u> by the <u>renewal</u> of your minds, that you may prove what are the things that are good, acceptable, and perfect, the will of God.* (emphasis added)

<!-- p43 -->
</span>
<!-- p44 -->
Paul sets two simple conditions for transformation:

<!-- p45 -->
<u>1. Offer your bodies to God as a living sacrifice</u>. A **living** sacrifice does not die physically at the time of the sacrifice, but since a living sacrifice cannot die, you must continually bring it to the altar!

<!-- p46 -->
<u>2. Renew your minds!</u> Renewal, according to the Greek word: **Anakainōsis** (*An-ak-ah'-ee-no-sis -* **G342**) means renewal or renovation, emphasizing the ongoing process of inner change and growth.

<!-- p47 -->
This renewal brings transformation. Transformation comes from the Greek word: **Metamorphoō** (*met-am-or-fo'-o -* **G3339**) meaning to change into another form; to be transformed or glorified, emphasizing a complete change. From Metamorphoō we get the English word metamorphosis, which is the process a caterpillar undergoes to become butterflies.

<!-- p48 -->
God wants to fundamentally change our minds and our way of thinking. His desire is that we not conform to the ways of the world, but that we know what the will of God is that is good (beneficial), pleasing (acceptable), and perfect (complete &amp; lacking nothing).

<!-- p49 -->
The conditions for transformation are indeed that simple, although they require discipline!

<!-- p50 -->
Our struggle against the flesh will not be won by our flesh, but by God’s empowerment for the battle of the mind. His Word (the true sword) has tremendous power to correct every mental image, proud thoughts, and to imprison (take captive) every thought that goes against obeying God.

<!-- p51 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="3-6" data-version="NKJV">
<!-- p52 -->
&gt; **<u>2 Corinthians 10:3-6</u>** (NKJV)
&gt;
&gt; ***3** For though we walk in the flesh, we do not wage war according to the flesh.*
&gt;
&gt; ***4** For the weapons of our warfare are not of the flesh but are mighty in God for the pulling down of strongholds,*
&gt;
&gt; ***5** casting down arguments and every high thing that exalts itself against the knowledge of God, taking every thought captive to obey Christ,*
&gt;
&gt; ***6** and being ready to punish every disobedience when your obedience is fulfilled.*

<!-- p53 -->
</span>
<!-- p54 -->
When those in authority apprehend someone, they shackle him with handcuffs and put him in a jail cell. If that person escapes from their custody, they do not ignore it; they will use extensive resources and will not rest until that person is brought back under control. We too should act with that diligence in the battle for our minds!

<!-- p55 -->
We are renewed by obedience and the experience of truth. Jesus said that if we abide in His words, we will be His disciples, we will experience (<u>know</u>) the truth, and it will set us free (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-32" data-version="NKJV">John 8:31-32</span>)!

<!-- p56 -->
The experience of truth is different from the knowledge of truth. Experience is the Greek word **Ginōskō** (Ghin-oce'-ko **- G1097**) meaning to know through personal experience or intimate understanding; it signifies step-by-step learning.

<!-- p57 -->
It is not our academic knowledge of truth that transforms us and renews our minds, but our personal experience of truth. We are sanctified by His truth! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="17" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV">John 17:17</span>).

## How to Renew Your Mind

<!-- p59 -->
The renewing of our minds is not a one-size-fits-all process—it depends on which part of the mind we are talking about! God designed our minds with remarkable complexity, including several different memory systems, each serving a specific purpose. Some operate automatically behind the scenes, while others require focused and deliberate thought. These systems are gifts from God to help us live, grow, and thrive, but they can also mislead us when misused or when they have not been renewed. Understanding how these systems work sheds light on how to partner with God’s process of transformation.

<!-- p60 -->
In the following section I will explain each of the memory systems, their purposes and functions, some practical steps to renew this facet of your mind, along with resources to continue practicing and studying.

<!-- p61 -->
### **Memory Systems and How to Revitalize Them**

<!-- p62 -->
#### **1. Explicit Memory (Declarative Memory)**

<!-- p63 -->
**Purpose:** Stores facts, events, and experiences we can consciously recall. It is divided into two types-**semantic** and **episodic**.

<!-- p64 -->
**Semantic Memory - Facts and Knowledge.** Semantic memory is where we store facts, definitions, and general knowledge—things like historical dates, Bible verses, and practical information. It helps us predict outcomes, make decisions, and avoid danger (e.g., recognizing poisonous plants). While it gives us stability and order, it can also lead to inflexibility—overreliance on rules instead of adapting to unexpected situations.

<!-- p65 -->
**How to Renew It:**

<!-- p66 -->
- **Read Scripture** to shape our worldview (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="Psalm" data-chapter="119" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="105" data-version="NKJV">Zaburi 119:105</span>).

<!-- p67 -->
- **Commit facts** and teachings to memory to stand firm in the faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV">Wakolosai 3:16</span>).

<!-- p68 -->
- **Read Christian books** such as *"Renewing the Mind" by Casey Treat* or *"Battlefield of the Mind" by Joyce Meyer* to cultivate godly thought patterns.

<!-- p69 -->
**Episodic Memory - Personal Experience.** Episodic memory stores our personal experiences, linking them to specific times and places—like a wedding day or a traumatic event. These memories provide context, help us learn from the past, and build relationships. However, episodic memory is easily influenced by emotional bias and distortion. Past trauma can create fear associations, making it hard to move forward.

<!-- p70 -->
**How to Renew It:**

<!-- p71 -->
- **Reflect and confess** to bring hidden pain into God’s light (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV">1 Yohana 1:9</span>).

<!-- p72 -->
- **Reframe past experiences** through the lens of God’s grace (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV">Warumi 8:28</span>).

<!-- p73 -->
- **Seek inner healing** through prayer and counseling (*“Your Pain Is Seen” by Larry F. Low, "The Bondage Breaker" by Neil T. Anderson*, *"Emotionally Healthy Spirituality" by Peter Scazzero*).

<!-- p74 -->
#### **2. Implicit Memory (Non-declarative Memory)**

<!-- p75 -->
**Purpose:** Unconscious memory used for behaviors, skills, and emotional associations. It operates automatically, helping us respond quickly to various situations. It is divided into **procedural memory** and **emotional conditioning**.

<!-- p76 -->
**Procedural Memory - Skills and Habits.** Procedural memory enables us to perform tasks without thinking—like driving, typing, or playing a musical instrument. It is efficient, saving mental energy and time. However, bad habits and negative patterns can also take deep root and become hard to break.

<!-- p77 -->
**How to Renew It:**

<!-- p78 -->
- **Practice spiritual disciplines** such as prayer and fasting to build godly habits (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV">Waebrania 5:14</span>).

<!-- p79 -->
- **Train yourself in godliness** through repetition and obedience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV">1 Timotheo 4:7</span>).

<!-- p80 -->
- **Read books like** *"Atomic Habits" by James Clear* (practical) and *"Spiritual Disciplines for the Christian Life" by Donald Whitney* (biblical) to establish habits that honor God.

<!-- p81 -->
**Emotional conditioning - Emotional associations.** Emotional conditioning links sensory cues (such as smells or sounds) with emotions, triggering responses before we have consciously processed them. This system protects us but can also cause unfounded fears or emotional reactions when past experiences are negative.

<!-- p82 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p83 -->
- **Pray for healing** from past wounds (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="41" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV">Isaiah 41:10</span>).

<!-- p84 -->
- **Practice forgiveness** and let go of bitterness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-32" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 4:31-32</span>).

<!-- p85 -->
- **Replace fear with God’s promises** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV">2 Timothy 1:7</span>).

<!-- p86 -->
- **Seek guidance from books like** “Your Pain is Showing” by Larry F. Low, "The Bondage Breaker" by Neil T. Anderson, “Soul Care" by Rob Reimer for overcoming emotional triggers.

<!-- p87 -->
#### **3. Working Memory (Short-Term Memory)**

<!-- p88 -->
**Purpose:** It provides temporary storage for problem-solving and decision-making. It helps us focus, hold information for a short time, and process tasks such as remembering instructions or making quick decisions. Although powerful, it also has limits, is easily distracted, and becomes overloaded readily, especially under stress.

<!-- p89 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p90 -->
- **Focus on and meditate on** God’s Word daily (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="Psalm" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV">Psalm 1:2</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
- **Renew your mind** through deliberate thinking (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV">Romans 12:2</span>).

<!-- p92 -->
- **Practice mindfulness** and simplify mental clutter (*"Habits of Grace" by David Mathis*).

<!-- p93 -->
- **Read** *"The Celebration of Discipline" by Richard Foster* to increase focus through spiritual disciplines.

<!-- p94 -->
## Conclusion

<!-- p95 -->
Renewing the mind is not a quick fix—it is a gradual, lifelong process of transformation. Every memory system plays a role in shaping how we think, feel, and act. By working intentionally with the Holy Spirit, applying Scripture, and cultivating godly habits, we can train our minds to align with God’s truth. Whether it’s addressing past wounds, learning new habits, or staying present in the moment, God has already provided all the **spiritual tools** and **practical wisdom** to help us walk in renewal and freedom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV">2 Corinthians 10:5</span>).

<!-- p96 -->
Our world is full of trouble, suffering, and trials. Through disciplined mind renewal, our souls are transformed, and we can live lives of freedom for God, filled with the fruit of His Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-25" data-version="NKJV">Galatians 5:22-25</span>).
