# Chapter 6 - Faith Toward God

<!-- p1 -->
Faith is a foundational concept in Christianity. Faith is such a commonly used word in Christianity that sometimes its meaning is altered through subtle misunderstandings and misinterpretations. But it is not something you can hold in your hands-it is something that forms in your heart as you learn to trust God. Being persuaded is faith.

<!-- p2 -->
The author of Hebrews instructs us that we are to have ‘faith toward God’.

<!-- p3 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="6" data-verses="1-2" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p4 -->
> **<u>Hebrews 6:1-2</u>** (NKJV) *Therefore, when we leave the discussion of the elementary things of Christ, let us go on to perfection, not laying again the foundation of repentance from dead works and of faith toward God,*

</span>

<!-- p6 -->
In this chapter we will discuss what faith is, and what it is not, how to increase our faith, how we put our faith to work (obedience), the benefits of living a life of obedience, and practical steps to increase our faith.

<!-- p7 -->
As we did in our study of repentance, let us look at the Greek language to lay a foundation for our study. Understanding these terms helps us see that faith is directional (toward God) and relational (rooted in trust and being persuaded).

### Definition of Faith

<!-- p10 -->
Faith is an aspect of Christianity that is commonly misunderstood and misrepresented. As the saying goes, “There is a ditch on both sides of the road”! Because in much of contemporary Christianity the word faith is often used without proper clarification, it takes on distorted meanings further fueled by misunderstanding. Faith is often presented either as a blanket statement of abstract belief or as an almost mystical force or “magic”, that is, something you possess in a specific, measured, and limited quantity.

<!-- p11 -->
#### What Faith Is Not

<!-- p12 -->
Presenting faith <u>abstractly</u> does not indicate any specific belief or persuasion. A little scrutiny usually makes this evident, because this representation of faith cannot be made specific. If you ask, ‘What do you have faith in?’ or ‘Why do you have faith?’ the person holding this understanding often cannot explain beyond, ‘I have faith.’ <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="3" data-verses="15" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Peter 3:15</span> says that we should be ready to give an answer concerning the hope that is in us.

<!-- p13 -->
Sometimes people think of faith as a magical force-but Scripture does not teach that. Faith is about a real relationship based on trust. In Scripture, faith is far more dynamic and relational-it is rooted in being persuaded of, trusting, and relying on God's character and promises. It is not an impersonal force but an active response to God that shapes thoughts, emotions, and actions.

<!-- p14 -->
Why is belief related to being persuaded?

<!-- p15 -->
Our beliefs shape the way we see and interact with the world around us. Belief shapes our perception and gives rise to our actions. Our minds are built this way-we live on the basis of what we consider possible.

<!-- p16 -->
We act according to what we perceive, understand, and believe to be possible. As we walk down a corridor, our brain senses the boundaries of the floor and wall and automatically tells our steps where to land and what to avoid. This reality extends beyond physical guidance in the world around us.

<!-- p17 -->
Suppose you believe that it is impossible for you to be financially independent or to live a healthy and active lifestyle. Then your actions will follow that belief, and you, in line with your belief, will not do-and likely will not even attempt-the things you consider impossible.

<!-- p18 -->
If you believe that God's disposition is unloving and punitive, that impression of Him will keep you from walking with Him as His child.

<!-- p19 -->
#### Faith Is Being Persuaded

<!-- p20 -->
So, what is faith? Persuasion! The Greek word ***pistis*** means persuasion, or the result of being persuaded. We are to have persuasion about God. In English we can say faith (persuasion) ‘**in** God’. If you are married, hopefully you have faith ‘**in’** your spouse. You believe that they will do what they said they would do, and you believe that they are genuine and real. This trust ought to extend beyond the things they have said, to their person in general.

<!-- p21 -->
Growing faith, persuasion, and trust in a relationship takes time and experience. Hopefully in life you have learned how to build a trusting relationship with another person. The same principles you have learned that increase your confidence in a familiar person will allow you to increase your faith in God as well. In a relationship, faith grows over time, through experience, with consistency, and through small and great actions that build your confidence in the other person’s character and nature.

<!-- p22 -->
‘Faith toward God’ is relational. Relationships take two parties, not one. The good news about our faith with God is that the responsibility for His side of the relationship is on God (made possible by Jesus’ atoning death, and by being filled with His Spirit in the new birth), and He also helps us on our side (through the work of the Comforter - the Holy Spirit). God is doing almost all the work; our part (as we will describe later in this chapter) is to walk *with* Him in obedience.

<!-- p23 -->
### Jesus perfects our faith

<!-- p24 -->
Jesus is the author and perfecter of our faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-verses="2" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 12:2</span>). Our faith is designed to keep growing and expanding continually. We do not arrive at a place where we have enough ‘faith’; rather, our faith keeps increasing. Consider a deep and personal relationship in your life today. As in any relationship, we do not reach a specific milestone where we now ‘know’ that person. We continue to know them and increase in our knowledge about them, and this refines, shapes, and grows our relationship.

<!-- p25 -->
How do we increase our faith in God? By continually experiencing Him through His multiple revelations, manifestations, and attributes. Then, consider a deep personal relationship in your life. You deepen this relationship by engaging with them in different ways, different formats, different means, in different environments. You talk with them, send them messages, perhaps even write to them. You see them individually and also in group settings. You discuss the things you consider important, and perhaps challenge one another. These countless ways are endless. Through these many expressions you come to know that person more deeply.

<!-- p26 -->
We should all come to know God as our Savior through Jesus. Remember the names of God (*see appendix),* just because we know God as Savior does not mean we have yet experienced Him as Deliverer, Healer, Counselor, Lord.

<!-- p27 -->
If you are at the point that you have been persuaded to know God as your Savior (faith), then building this relationship will give you such confidence in His character that you will be able to know Him in other ways as well.

<!-- p28 -->
I am blessed to have several very deep relationships in my life. One such relationship is with my wife. In our relationship, because of the level of trust (faith) we have in one another, my wife can tell me things she will do, the manifestation of which I have not yet seen, and because of her proven character I have the confidence to believe her.

<!-- p29 -->
This example shows how our deep relationship with God can be experienced with confidence and assurance! We may not yet have fully experienced Him (indeed, in this life we will never fully experience Him), but as our relationship matures, we can trust Him beyond the limit of what I personally know and what I have already seen happen.

<!-- p30 -->
Jesus increases our faith through our experience with Him. One important way we can and should experience Him is through His Word.

### Faith increases through God’s Word

<!-- p32 -->
Consider this passage from Romans:

<!-- p33 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="10" data-verses="16-17" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p34 -->
> **<u>Romans 10:16-17</u>** (NKJV) **16** *But not all obeyed the gospel. For Isaiah says, "Lord, who has believed our report?"* ***17** So then faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God.*

<!-- p35 -->
</span>

<!-- p36 -->
When we replace the word *faith* with *persuasion*, it reads like this:

<!-- p37 -->
‘Then persuasion comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God.’ This fact is naturally self-evident. A major way for persuasion to be formed in someone is that they have been persuaded (convinced), and we are persuaded when someone provides an ‘argument’-or a reasonable and clear explanation.

<!-- p38 -->
There are two Greek words for ‘word’ in the Bible-**logos** and **rhema**. Understanding the difference shows us how faith grows.

<!-- p39 -->
**Logos**: Generally refers to the concept, idea, reason, or broader expression of God’s Word. It encompasses a universal, foundational truth or message and is often used to describe God’s eternal wisdom and Christ Himself as the living Word.

<!-- p40 -->
**Rhema (G4487)**: emphasizes a **specific, spoken word or phrase**. It generally points to a particular word or message given at a specific moment, especially spoken or revealed speech that applies directly to a person’s condition.

<!-- p41 -->
*Logos* is like the entire message of a book, whereas *Rhema* is like a specific passage or sentence that speaks directly to someone at a particular moment. While *Logos* is God’s all-encompassing truth, *Rhema* is when a portion of that same truth is spoken to an individual in a personal and applicable way. Together they highlight the perfection of God’s communication: *Logos* as the broad, foundational realities of God’s character and will, and *Rhema* as the specific, applied word that brings that truth to life in a person’s situation and condition.

<!-- p42 -->
### An example of a Rhema word in action

<!-- p43 -->
God promises revelation and wisdom to all who ask. When He speaks to us, that utterance is a *Rhema* word. Rhema words are from God and serve to confirm, lead, and guide us. They are not a substitute for the *Logos* word, nor do they carry the same authority as Scripture. *Rhema* words should be tested by the same standard by which we test prophecy or the interpretation of tongues (see the chapter on the Holy Spirit for a detailed study on this topic).

<!-- p44 -->
At various stages in my career I have prayed for divine guidance. A few years ago I was considering beginning the process of looking for a new opportunity, and during prayer I heard God say: ‘*Just wait for Me; I have an opportunity for you.*’ I had started looking for a new job because I was not satisfied with my current assignment and felt discouraged. God’s guiding word, ‘*Wait for Me*,’ gave me peace and confidence even in a season that otherwise could have been restless.

<!-- p45 -->
Three or four months passed; I kept working hard and faithfully, and kept my attitude positive. God would remind me, *‘Wait for Me’*, then His promise was fulfilled. I was given a promotion opportunity and a substantial increase in pay.

<!-- p46 -->
I could have kept searching; I could have left. I could have started somewhere new. Surely someone else would have gotten that promotion! But since I sought the Lord’s counsel and was convinced by His **Rhema word**, I obeyed and benefited. God’s words are **living and personal**, and He promises that He will give us manifold and generous wisdom (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-verses="5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">James 1:5</span>).

<!-- p47 -->
I have had, if not dozens then hundreds of times, occasions when I prayed and asked God what to do, and He gave me a Rhema word. Not every time was the result equally extraordinary, but all of them gave the assurance and peace that I was obeying God and that He would set things right. They were the very words I needed at that moment! Often when God gives a Rhema word, it is from the Logos itself. He brings a Scripture reference to my memory, “*Do not fear, for I am with you!*” (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="41" data-verses="10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Isaiah 41:10</span>), “*By His stripes we are healed”* (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="53" data-verses="5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Isaiah 53:5</span>), “*Do not grow weary in doing good!*” (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-verses="9" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Galatians 6:9</span>), countless examples continue!

<!-- p48 -->
Receiving a Rhema word not only strengthens our faith but also deepens our relationship with God, because we rely on Him more fully in our everyday decisions. We will continue the study of learning to hear God’s voice in the chapter on the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p49 -->
Our faith is also strengthened by the things we see God do, but sometimes what we see in the natural world challenges what we hear from God. Reconciling the discrepancy between what we see and what we hear is an important ministry of the Holy Spirit, who promises to lead us into all truth (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-verses="13" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 16:13</span>).

### The faith that is beyond our sight

<!-- p51 -->
After His resurrection, Jesus appeared several times to many of His disciples. Thomas (one of the Twelve) was not with the other disciples, so he missed the appearance of the risen Savior. Thomas believed in Jesus and trusted his fellow disciples, but he did not believe that Jesus had been physically raised; he insisted that unless he experienced it personally, he simply could not truly believe.

When Jesus appeared to His disciples and calmed them (I imagine it would be quite startling when someone appears among you after coming through locked doors), He spoke to Thomas. He did not criticize or rebuke Thomas, nor did He count him among that wicked and perverse generation that seeks a sign (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-verses="4" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 16:4</span>), but He revealed Himself to Thomas.

<!-- p56 -->
Jesus goes on to say, ‘*Blessed are those who have believed without seeing*’. This is not Jesus encouraging ‘blind or ignorant faith’ or a dismissal of using experiential testimony. Rather, He is saying that we are more blessed when we can believe without experiential proof. Why? If our faith—our trust—were limited only to the things we have seen empirically proven, we would never have confidence about what is yet to be done. We would have great confidence in what had already happened, but we would not walk in God’s peace for our tomorrow and our future.

<!-- p57 -->
Using a relational example, how difficult would my relationship with my own wife become if my posture were always, ‘<u>I will believe it when I see it!</u>’? In contrast, a trust-based relationship says, ‘I know that you are a trustworthy person.’

### Faith produces obedience and good works

<!-- p76 -->
Our trust-based relationship with God is strengthened through obedience, which results in bringing forth good works! These works do not save us nor earn God's love-they are the natural outcome of a life rooted in Him.

<!-- p77 -->
Scripture presents a natural progression of faith. As we begin to know God and experience His presence, our confidence in Him grows. This confidence compels us to act in ways that align with our faith, which leads us to obey His commandments. Our obedience produces good works, which yield a harvest of positive and beneficial results.

<!-- p78 -->
Consider this passage in Romans again:

<!-- p79 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="10" data-verses="16-17" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p80 -->
> **<u>Romans 10:16-17</u>** (NKJV) **16*** But they have not all obeyed the gospel. For Isaiah says, "Lord, who has believed our report?" **17** So then faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God.*

<!-- p81 -->
</span>

<!-- p82 -->
An analysis of this in reverse order:

<!-- p83 -->
1. Hearing the word of God (**rhema**) produces faith (**pistis**)

<!-- p84 -->
2. Faith, which is a state of belief or persuasion, will give rise to the act or action of believing.

<!-- p85 -->
1. Again, consider the person who believes that he can be free from debt and acts accordingly!

<!-- p86 -->
3. Our act of believing is manifested in obedience.

<!-- p87 -->
The word for ‘obeyed‘ is **hupakouō (***hoop-ak-oo'-o **- G5219)***: **hupakouō** means *to listen in subordination* (as a *subordinate*), that is, *to listen attentively*; by implication, *to pay attention* or *to conform* to a command or authority: - to hear, to be obedient, to obey.

<!-- p88 -->
In a work environment, when the boss or a supervisor is giving instructions, he is not merely "speaking to speak," rather he is giving commands that he wants his employees to carry out.

<!-- p89 -->
When we truly hear God, it should lead us to action—to trust and obey Him. As the Scripture says:

<!-- p90 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-verses="22" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p91 -->
> **<u>James 1:22</u>** (NKJV) *But be doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving yourselves.*

<!-- p92 -->
</span>

<!-- p93 -->
Consider again the example of financial independence. An indebted person who desires to be free from that burden will seek financial counseling. The counselor will teach him the principles of financial freedom and help him to make a budget. This counseling builds in them (confidence / faith)—in what they need to do to be free from debt. But confidence is only the first step; the person must obey the principles he has learned, through disciplined practice, in order to obtain the good outcome and blessing of being free from debt.

<!-- p94 -->
The book of James says that our belief (faith) must necessarily produce works! We already know what works are (for example, ‘dead’ works from <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="6" data-verses="1" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 6:1</span>).

What stands out to you in this portion of James? For me, an important phrase is that even the demons believe! Mere belief is not enough. Our belief must reach the level of obedience. We must obey our risen Savior and Lord Jesus—and we want to as well.

<!-- p99 -->
We are not saved by works! When we are saved (through faith) then we do works! I will say it again, you can neither work hard enough nor sacrifice enough to earn your salvation; it is God’s free gift which we receive through faith!

<!-- p100 -->
The result of these acts (obedience) is that they produce a good and beneficial harvest۔ The time it takes to obtain the harvest, or the amount of that harvest, may vary، but it will certainly produce a good harvest! (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="8" data-verses="22" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Genesis 8:22</span>).

Recalling our previous teaching about sin and sacrifices، we know that many of the sacrifices appointed in the Old Testament were for sin، so that a person could be reconciled to God or to their fellow human۔ However، there is also a more preferred way!

<!-- p102 -->

One truth that has remained consistent from the Old Testament is that obedience is better than sacrifice۔ It is better that we do precisely what we ought to do، rather than having to offer a sacrifice to make up for a mistake۔ Thank God that when we stray off the path، miss the mark (sin)، and need to return to God، there is grace from God۔

<!-- p106 -->
When we strengthen our faith in God He naturally produces obedience within us۔ This obedience is beneficial for us and brings a good harvest۔ Returning to my personal example in this chapter، if I had not obeyed the word from God that came to me that I should *wait for Him*، I would have had neither peace in that season nor the blessing of advancement!

### Practical steps to grow in your faith

<!-- p151 -->
Walking with God is a relational matter, but if you want good relationships you have to learn some skills. In earthly relationships, the skills that create strong and secure relationships are built on these foundations:

<!-- p152 -->
- Open and free dialogue and communication

<!-- p153 -->
- Healthy resolution of conflicts

<!-- p154 -->
- Commitment

<!-- p155 -->
- Consistency

<!-- p156 -->
If you want a strong, flourishing relationship, you should consistently show up, reveal yourself (communication), (your thoughts and feelings), accept the other person (listening), and walk with them! All these skills apply to our relationship with God as well.

<!-- p157 -->
Beyond the basic relational skills, there are some specific practices and trainings that will strengthen your relationship with God:

<!-- p158 -->
- **Establish and maintain a life of prayer; listen to God and converse with Him.** Regular, heartfelt prayer is essential for a close relationship with God. Through prayer you can share your concerns, joys, and gratitude, while seeking His guidance and strength. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="4" data-verses="6-7" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Philippians 4:6-7</span>*) Being sensitive to the Holy Spirit's voice helps you recognize God's will, find wisdom in decisions, and grow in spiritual maturity. Make time for quiet reflection to listen for His guidance. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-verses="14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 8:14</span>*)

<!-- p159 -->
- **Spend time in worship.** Worship is a powerful way to draw near to God with expressions of love and reverence. Through praise, song, or quiet reflection, worship deepens the awareness of His presence. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="100" data-verses="2-4" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Psalm 100:2-4</span>*)

<!-- p160 -->
- **Read, study, and reflect on the Holy Scripture.** God’s Word reveals His nature, promises, and instructions for life. Taking time to study the Scriptures helps your growth in faith, understanding, and spiritual perseverance. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-verses="16-17" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>*)

<!-- p161 -->
- **Obey His commandments.** Obedience to God’s instructions shows your love, respect, and trust for Him. Walking in obedience aligns your life with His purpose and brings blessings. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-verses="15" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 14:15</span>*)

<!-- p162 -->
- **Actively participate in and serve in a local church.** Participation in the church community provides spiritual support, encouragement, and opportunities to serve others. Staying involved helps build a sense of belonging and strengthens your commitment to God’s mission. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-verses="24-25" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 10:24-25</span>*)

<!-- p163 -->
- **Build relationships with other believers.** Fellowship with other Christians provides accountability, encouragement, and shared wisdom. These relationships enrich your journey of faith and help your growth through mutual support. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="27" data-verses="17" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Proverbs 27:17</span>*)

<!-- p164 -->
Our ‘faith toward God’ is not a one-time event for salvation but a continual process and relationship that we must cultivate throughout our lives. The benefits of a trusting and fruitful relationship are exceedingly many, but we should do the good work of nurturing that trust and growth so that it may thrive.
