# Chapter 6 - Faith Toward God

<!-- p1 -->
Faith is a cornerstone concept in Christianity. The word faith is so commonly used in Christianity that at times its meaning shifts due to subtle misconceptions and misunderstandings. But it is not something you can hold in your hands—it takes shape in your heart when you learn to trust God. Persuasion is belief.

<!-- p2 -->
The author of Hebrews instructs us that we are to have ‘faith toward God.’

### Definition of Faith

<!-- p10 -->
Faith is a commonly misunderstood and misrepresented aspect of Christianity. As the saying goes, “there’s a ditch on both sides of the road”! Because in modern Christianity the word faith is often used without proper explanation, it can take on misdefinitions amplified by misunderstandings. Faith is often presented either as a general, abstract statement of belief, or as almost a mystical force or “magic,” something you possess in a specific, measured, and limited amount.

<!-- p11 -->
#### What Faith Is Not

<!-- p12 -->
When faith is presented as <u>abstract</u>, it has no specific conviction or persuasion. A little probing makes this clear, because this representation of faith cannot become specific. If you ask, ‘What do you believe?’ or ‘Why do you believe?’ the person with this understanding of faith often cannot explain beyond ‘I believe.’ <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 3:15</span> says that we should be ready to give an answer about the hope that is in us.

<!-- p13 -->
Sometimes people think of faith as a magical power—but Scripture does not teach this. Faith is about a real relationship built on trust. In Scripture, faith is far more dynamic and relational—it is rooted in persuasion, confidence, and trust in God’s character and promises. It is not an impersonal force but an active response before God that shapes thoughts, emotions, and actions.

<!-- p14 -->
Why is belief related to persuasion?

<!-- p15 -->
Our beliefs shape how we see and interact with the world around us. Belief shapes perception and gives rise to our actions. Our minds are made this way—we live on the basis of what we consider possible.

<!-- p16 -->
We act on what we see, understand, and deem possible. When we pass through a hallway, our brain perceives the boundaries of the floor and walls and automatically tells our feet where we can step and what to avoid. This reality extends beyond mere physical guidance in the world around us.

<!-- p17 -->
Suppose you believe it is impossible for you to become financially independent or to adopt a healthy and active lifestyle. Then your actions will follow that belief, and in line with your belief you will not do, and likely will not even attempt, what you consider impossible.

<!-- p18 -->
If you believe that God’s disposition is unloving and punitive, that view of Him will keep you from walking with Him as His child.

<!-- p19 -->
#### Faith Is Persuasion

<!-- p20 -->
So, what is faith? Being persuaded! The Greek word ***pistis*** means being persuaded, or the result of being persuaded. We are to be persuaded about God. In English we might say: faith (persuasion) ‘**in** God’. If you are married, then hopefully you have faith ‘**in’** your spouse. You believe that they will do what they said they would do, and you also believe that they are sincere and genuine. This trust should go beyond the things they have said and extend, more generally, to their very person.

<!-- p21 -->
Building faith, persuasion, and trust in a relationship requires time and experience. Hopefully, in life you have learned how to build a trustworthy relationship with another person. The principles you have learned that allow you to increase your trust in someone you know are the same principles that will allow you to increase your faith in God. In a relationship, faith grows over time, through experience, with consistency, and through small and large actions that build your confidence in the other person’s character and nature.

<!-- p22 -->
‘Faith toward God’ is relational. Relationships are made of two parties, not one. The good news about our faith in God is that God Himself takes responsibility for His part of the relationship (made possible by Jesus’ atoning death and the infilling of His Spirit at new birth), and He also helps us with our part (through the work of the Comforter—the Holy Spirit). God is doing almost all the work; our part (as we will explain further in this chapter) is to walk (*WITH*) with Him in obedience.

<!-- p23 -->
### Jesus perfects our faith

<!-- p24 -->
Jesus is the author and finisher of our faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 12:2</span>). Our faith is designed to keep growing and expanding continually. We do not arrive at a point where we have enough ‘faith’; rather, our faith keeps increasing. Consider a deep and personal relationship in your life. As with any relationship, we do not reach a specific milestone at which we have now ‘known’ the person. We keep on knowing them, increase our familiarity with them, and this process refines, shapes, and grows our relationship.

<!-- p25 -->
How do we grow our faith in God? By continually experiencing Him in His many revelations, manifestations, and attributes. Again, consider a deep personal relationship in your life. You deepen the relationship by engaging with them in different ways, different forms, different means, and different settings. You talk with them, send them messages, perhaps even write to them. You meet them in private and also in group settings. You talk about your values, and perhaps even challenge one another. The number of ways is virtually endless. Through these countless expressions you come to know that person more deeply.

<!-- p26 -->
We all ought to know God as our Savior through Jesus. Recall the names of God (*See Appendix),* just because we know God as Savior does not mean that we have yet experienced Him as Redeemer, Healer, Counselor, Lord.

<!-- p27 -->
If you are at the stage where you have been persuaded (faith) to know God as your Savior, then building this relationship will give you the confidence in His character to be able to know Him in other ways as well.

<!-- p28 -->
I am blessed to have several very deep relationships in my life. One of them is with my wife. Because of the level of trust (faith) we have in each other in our relationship, my wife can tell me things she will do that I have not yet seen come to pass, and because of her proven character I believe her.

<!-- p29 -->
This example shows how our deep relationship with God can be experienced with confidence and assurance! We may not yet have fully experienced Him (indeed, in this life we will never fully experience Him), but as our relationship matures, we can trust Him beyond what I personally know and what I have already seen happen.

<!-- p30 -->
Jesus grows our faith through our experience with Him. One important way we must walk in to experience Him is through His Word.

<!-- p36 -->
When we replace the word *faith* with *persuasion*, it becomes like this:

<!-- p37 -->
*‘Then persuasion comes by hearing, and hearing by the Word of God’*. This fact is quite obvious in itself. A major way persuasion comes into someone is that they have been persuaded (have been convinced), and we are convinced when someone provides an ‘argument’—or a reasonable and clear rationale.

<!-- p38 -->
There are two Greek words for ‘word’ in the Bible—**logos** and **rhema**. Understanding their difference shows us how faith grows.

<!-- p39 -->
**Logos**: Generally points to the **concept, idea, logic, or broader expression** of God’s word. It encompasses a universal, fundamental truth or message and is often used to describe God’s eternal wisdom and Christ Himself as the living Word.

<!-- p40 -->
**Rhema (G4487)**: Emphasizes a specific, spoken word or utterance. It generally points to a particular word or message given at a particular moment, especially speech or a revealed word that applies directly to a person’s situation.

<!-- p41 -->
*Logos* is like the entire message of a book, whereas *Rhema* is like a specific passage or sentence that directly addresses someone at a particular moment. While *Logos* is God’s all-encompassing truth, *Rhema* is the moment when a portion of that truth is spoken to an individual in a personal and applicable way. Together, they present a picture of the full comprehensiveness of God’s communication: *Logos* the broad, foundational truths of God’s character and will, and *Rhema* the specific, operative word that brings that truth to life in a person’s life and circumstances.

<!-- p42 -->
### Example of the Rhema Word in Action

<!-- p43 -->
God promises revelation and wisdom to all who ask. When He speaks to us, that utterance is a *Rhema* word. A Rhema word is from God and serves to give us confirmation, direction, and guidance. It is not a substitute for the *Logos* word, nor does it carry the authority of Scripture. A *Rhema* word should be tested by the same standard by which prophecy or the interpretation of tongues is tested (see the chapter on the Holy Spirit for an in-depth study on this topic).

<!-- p44 -->
At several points in my career, I have prayed for divine guidance. A few years ago, I was thinking about starting to look for a new opportunity, and during a time of prayer I heard God saying to me: ‘*Just wait for Me; I have an opportunity for you.’* I had begun to look for a new job because I was unhappy and discouraged with my current responsibility. God’s instruction, ‘*Wait for Me*,’ gave me peace and confidence in a restless season.

<!-- p45 -->
Three or four months went by; I continued to work diligently and faithfully and kept my attitude positive. God would remind me, *‘Wait for Me’*, then His promise was fulfilled. I received a promotion opportunity and a generous increase in pay.

<!-- p46 -->
I could have continued my job search; I could have left. I could have started somewhere new. Without a doubt someone else would have gotten that promotion! But because I sought the Lord’s counsel and became convinced in His **Rhema word**, I obeyed and benefited. God’s words are **living and personal**, and He promises that He will give us abundant and generous wisdom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:5</span>).

<!-- p47 -->
I have dozens, if not hundreds, of such occasions when I prayed and asked God what to do, and He gave me a Rhema word. Not every outcome was that spectacular, but all imparted a peace and confidence that I am obeying God and that He will set everything in order. They were the very words I needed at that moment! Many times when God brings a Rhema word, it is from the Logos itself. He refreshes some reference from Scripture in my mind, “*Do not fear, for I am with you!*” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="41" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 41:10</span>), “*By His stripes we are healed”* (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="53" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 53:5</span>), “*Do not grow weary in doing good!*” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:9</span>), the countless examples go on!

<!-- p48 -->
Receiving a Rhema word not only strengthens our faith but also deepens our relationship with God, because we learn to rely on Him more completely in our everyday matters. We will continue the study of learning to hear God’s voice in the chapter on the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p49 -->
Our faith is also strengthened by the things we see God doing, but sometimes what we see in the natural world challenges what we hear from God. Reconciling this discrepancy between what we see and what we hear is an important ministry of the Holy Spirit, who promises that He will guide us into all truth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span>).

### Faith Beyond Sight

<!-- p51 -->
After His resurrection, Jesus appeared many times to many of His disciples. Thomas (one of the Twelve) was not with the other disciples, so he missed the appearance of the risen Savior. Thomas believed in Jesus, and he trusted his fellow disciples as well, but he did not believe that Jesus had risen in bodily form; he insisted that unless he experienced it personally, he could not actively believe it.

When Jesus appeared to His disciples and took them by surprise (I think when someone passes through locked doors and suddenly appears among you, that’s quite a startling thing), then He spoke to Thomas. He did not criticize or accuse Thomas nor count him among the wicked and perverse generation that seeks a sign (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 16:4</span>) but revealed Himself to Thomas.

<!-- p60 -->
Jesus goes on to say, ‘*blessed are they who have not seen and have believe*d’. This is not Jesus encouraging ‘blind or ignorant faith’ nor rejecting the use of empirical evidence. Rather, He is saying that when we can believe without empirical proof we are more blessed. Why? If our faith, our trust, were limited only to the things we had observed empirically, we would never have any confidence in the work that lies ahead. We would have great confidence about what had already happened, but we would not walk in God’s peace for our coming tomorrow and our future.

<!-- p61 -->
Taking an example from relationships, how difficult would my relationship with my own wife become if my attitude were always: ‘<u>I will only believe it when I see it!</u>’? By contrast, a trust-based relationship says: ‘I know that you are a trustworthy person’.

Returning to my personal example in this chapter, if I had not obeyed God’s word to me to *wait on Him*, I would not have experienced peace in that season, nor would I have received the blessing of promotion!

### Practical steps to grow your faith

<!-- p153 -->
Walking with God is relational, but if you want a good relationship, you have to learn some skills. In earthly relationships, the skills that give rise to strong and secure relationships are based on the following:

<!-- p154 -->
- Open and free dialogue and communication

<!-- p155 -->
- Healthy conflict resolution

<!-- p156 -->
- Commitment

<!-- p157 -->
- Consistency

<!-- p158 -->
If you want a strong and vibrant relationship, you need to show up regularly, make yourself known (communicate) (our thoughts and feelings), accept the other person (listen), and walk with them! All these skills also apply to our relationship with God.

<!-- p159 -->
Beyond basic relational skills, there are certain practices and disciplines that will strengthen your relationship with God:

<!-- p160 -->
- **Establish and maintain a life of prayer; listen to God and converse with Him.** Regular, heartfelt prayer is essential for a close relationship with God. Through prayer you can express your concerns, joys, and thanksgiving, and seek His guidance and strength. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 4:6-7</span>*) Remaining sensitive to the voice of the Holy Spirit helps you discern God’s will, gain wisdom in decisions, and grow in spiritual maturity. Set aside quiet time for reflection to listen for His guidance. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:14</span>*)

<!-- p161 -->
- **Spend time in worship.** Worship is a powerful way to draw near to God, expressing love and reverence. Through praise, song, or quiet reflection, worship deepens awareness of His presence. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="100" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 100:2-4</span>*)

<!-- p162 -->
- **Read, study, and meditate on Scripture.** God’s Word reveals His character, promises, and instructions for life. Setting aside time to study the Word helps you grow in faith, understanding, and spiritual steadfastness. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>*)

<!-- p163 -->
- **Obey His commandments.** Obedience to God’s instructions shows your love, respect, and trust for Him. Walking in obedience aligns your life with His purpose and brings blessings. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:15</span>*)

<!-- p164 -->
- **Participate actively in and serve your local church.** Being part of the church community provides spiritual support, encouragement, and opportunities to serve others. Staying engaged strengthens your sense of belonging and your commitment to God’s mission. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:24-25</span>*)

<!-- p165 -->
- **Build relationships with other believers.** Fellowship with other Christians provides accountability, encouragement, and shared wisdom. These relationships enrich your journey of faith and help your growth through mutual support. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="27" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 27:17</span>*)

<!-- p166 -->
Our ‘faith toward God’ is not a one-time event for salvation but an ongoing practice and relationship that we must nurture throughout life. The benefits of a trusting and fruitful relationship are exceedingly great, but we must do the good work of cultivating that trust and growth so that it flourishes.
