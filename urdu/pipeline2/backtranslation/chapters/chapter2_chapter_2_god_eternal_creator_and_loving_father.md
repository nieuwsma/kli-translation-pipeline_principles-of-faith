# Chapter 2 - God-Eternal Creator and Loving Father

<!-- p1 -->
It is extremely necessary that we establish a firm foundation in recognizing who God is. Through study we can understand:

<!-- p2 -->
- His desires, intentions, and actions,

<!-- p3 -->
- His nature, character, and attributes,

<!-- p4 -->
- What has been said about Him and what He says about Himself.

<!-- p5 -->
The Bible reveals God’s characteristics in a unique way, and each facet deepens our understanding of the unchanging, eternal God.

<!-- p6 -->
It is difficult—perhaps even impossible—to confine God to a single attribute, because He is so vast and multidimensional that He cannot be simplified. Although we can, and will, make great efforts to explain, describe, and portray God, ultimately—like art and music—He must be experienced to be understood.

<!-- p7 -->
When someone listens to music, they describe it by how it moves them—how it resonates within, beyond mere notes and rhythm. A melody may carry warmth or intensity, with a rhythm that pulses like a heartbeat, or with a rising swell that creates a sense of ecstasy or yearning. Music sometimes soothes like a gentle wave or electrifies like a storm, lifting the listener’s spirits, steadying their thoughts, or taking them somewhere else entirely. This experience is far more than the arrangement of sounds; it is the spirit of the music that remains, shaping how it forms the listener’s emotions and personal journey.

<!-- p8 -->
Similarly, a viewer looks beyond colors or brushstrokes and explores what these elements embody. A landscape can evoke a peaceful escape, while an abstract work can stir curiosity or self-reflection. Certain palettes can bring warmth, while a composition can produce a sense of calm, disorder, or beauty. A painting becomes a living experience, prompting a change in perspective or a deep emotional response that mere words cannot contain. Then both music and art become more than their physical forms—they become encounters that touch something fundamental, transforming the listener or viewer in profound and personal ways.

## Exploring God through the Old Testament

<!-- p10 -->
### Genesis: The God who dwells with His creation

<!-- p11 -->
A central theme in God’s story is His **desire to dwell** with His creation. This is evident throughout the stories of Genesis. Its fifty chapters and genealogies span approximately ~2,400 years from Adam (the first human) to Joseph (the last major figure of Genesis, the last significant leader of God’s people before Moses). Through these narratives we see that, despite human wickedness, God’s goodness remains constant.

<!-- p12 -->
In Genesis, God establishes relationship with several particular individuals.

<!-- p13 -->
1.  **Adam and Eve** - the first humans, whom God created and placed in the Garden of Eden. God walked and talked with them, gave them instructions, and established a relationship of mutual love and obedience. However, this relationship was affected by their disobedience when they ate from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil (Genesis 1-3).

<!-- p14 -->
2.  **Cain** - Although Cain ultimately rebelled by killing his brother Abel, God spoke to him directly, warned him about the danger of sin, and gave him guidance before the murder. Even after Cain’s punishment, God marked him to keep him safe so that no one would harm him (Genesis 4).

<!-- p15 -->
3.  **Noah** - In a time of widespread depravity, God established a unique relationship with a righteous man named Noah. God commanded Noah to build an ark to save his household and the animals from the flood. Afterwards, God made a covenant with Noah, promising that He would never again destroy the earth by a flood and setting the rainbow as the sign of this covenant (Genesis 6-9).

<!-- p16 -->
4.  **Abraham (Abram)** - One of the most significant relationships in Genesis, God calls Abram to leave his homeland and promises to make him the father of a great nation. God makes a covenant with Abram, promising land, offspring, and blessings for all nations through him. Abram’s faith and obedience become central themes, and his name is changed to Abraham as part of this covenant (Genesis 12, 15, 17, 22).

<!-- p17 -->
5.  **Hagar** - Sarah’s Egyptian maidservant, Hagar encounters God in the wilderness after mistreatment. God comforts her, promises a future for her son Ishmael, and instructs her to return to Abraham and Sarah. Hagar calls God “El Roi” (“the God who sees me”), recognizing His care for her (Genesis 16).

<!-- p18 -->
6.  **Isaac** - The son of Abraham and Sarah, Isaac inherits the covenant promises made to his father. God reaffirms these promises directly to Isaac, including the promise of offspring and blessing. During a famine God also guides Isaac, preserves him, and maintains the covenant relationship (Genesis 26).

<!-- p19 -->
7.  **Rebekah** - Though her relationship with God is indirect, God answers Rebekah’s prayer about her difficult pregnancy and reveals that her twin sons, Jacob and Esau, will become two nations with distinct destinies (Genesis 25).

<!-- p20 -->
8.  **Jacob** - God establishes a personal relationship with Jacob, appearing to him in a dream and confirming the covenant promises of land, offspring, and blessings. Jacob encounters God several times, including the famous wrestling in which God changes his name to Israel, which is a sign of his transformation and of the establishment of Israel as a nation (Genesis 28, 32, 35).

<!-- p21 -->
9.  **Joseph** - Although Joseph’s relationship with God is not based on direct manifestations or covenants like Abraham’s or Jacob’s, God is deeply involved in his life. Through dreams, divine favor, and providence, God guides Joseph’s journey from being sold into slavery to becoming a powerful leader in Egypt. Joseph’s faith in God’s sovereignty enables him to forgive his brothers, recognizing that God used his suffering for good (Genesis 37, 39-50).

<!-- p22 -->
Throughout this entire narrative, God, who desires to dwell with His creation, is content to dwell with chosen individuals, and ultimately establishes a nation through the covenant with Abraham (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="17" data-verses="7" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Genesis 17:7</span>).

<!-- p23 -->
### Exodus: The God who dwells with His people

<!-- p24 -->
God’s desire to dwell with His people is also clearly seen in the Bible’s second book, Exodus. In the book of Exodus, the people of Israel, who for a time had lived in Egypt with relative peace and harmony, are now subjected to the oppression of a Pharaoh who did not know Joseph and feared the Hebrews (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="1" data-verses="6-8" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Exodus 1:6-8</span>). God’s people lived in Egypt for about ~400 years (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="12" data-verses="40-41" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Exodus 12:40-41</span>), the latter portion of which was spent in slavery (there is no universal consensus on how long Israel was in Egyptian bondage).

<!-- p25 -->
In the powerful presence of the burning bush that was not consumed, God reveals Himself to an exiled murderer—Hebrew by descent and Egyptian-raised—who is tending his father-in-law’s flock in the wilderness: Moses.

<!-- p26 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="3" data-verses="14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p27 -->
> **<u>Exodus 3:14</u>** (NKJV) *And God said to Moses, "I AM WHO I AM." And He said, "Thus you shall say to the children of Israel, 'I AM' has sent me to you."*

<!-- p28 -->
</span>

<!-- p29 -->
The phrase **"I AM WHO I AM"** (Ehyeh-Asher-Ehyeh) is one of the most profound expressions of self-revelation in Scripture. When Moses asks God’s name, God answers with this phrase, which can be translated as: "I am who I am," "I will be who I will be," or simply "I am." This declaration may seem somewhat puzzling, yet it contains deep meaning. Can you introduce yourself to someone new by saying "I am"? When God reveals Himself as "I AM WHO I AM," He testifies to His nature, His eternal existence, and His sovereignty. "I AM" is infinite in meaning and purpose.

<!-- p30 -->
God uses Moses to reveal Himself to Pharaoh:

<!-- p31 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="7" data-verses="16" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p32 -->
> **<u>Exodus 7:16</u>** (NKJV) *And you shall say to him, "The LORD God of the Hebrews has sent me to you, saying, 'Let My people go, that they may serve Me in the wilderness'; but indeed, until now you would not listen!"*

<!-- p33 -->
</span>

<!-- p34 -->
Moses tells Pharaoh that God is "I AM" and that God demands that the Israelites be released so they may worship Him in the wilderness.

<!-- p35 -->
Plagues and calamities come, but eventually Pharaoh softens and lets the Israelites go.

<!-- p36 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="12" data-verses="31" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p37 -->
> **<u>Exodus 12:31</u>** (NKJV) *Then he called for Moses and Aaron by night, and said, "Rise, go out from among my people, both you and the children of Israel. And go, as you have said, serve the LORD.*

<!-- p38 -->
</span>

<!-- p39 -->
In the wilderness God miraculously delivers His people from the Egyptian army bent on revenge (Exodus 14), provides a pillar of fire for protection from the sun and to give warmth in the cold desert air at night (Exodus 13), food from heaven (Exodus 16), and water to drink (Exodus 17).

<!-- p40 -->
God brings His people to Mount Sinai where God commands Moses to consecrate the people and gives them the Law (including the Ten Commandments) and establishes His covenant with them (the Old Covenant) (Exodus 19, 20, 24)۔ This covenant (the Mosaic Covenant) is based on the Abrahamic Covenant and remains from Moses to Jesus۔

<!-- p41 -->
Despite binding a covenant with His people, this relationship is that of a loving and jealous God who pursues and dwells with a very stubborn and ungrateful people (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="32" data-verses="9" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Exodus 32:9</span>)۔ Ignoring God’s miraculous intervention, they maintain a rebellious posture through large portions of the text۔ God even brings them to the Promised Land and tells them that they may possess it as an inheritance (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="33" data-verses="3" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Exodus 33:3</span>)، yet in fear and disobedience they disregard the supernatural salvation that God had given them, and instead they have to wander in the wilderness for 40 years until nearly all of that generation had died (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="NUM" data-chapter="14" data-verses="33-34" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Numbers 14:33-34</span>)۔

<!-- p42 -->
### The rest of the Old Testament

<!-- p43 -->
In the roughly 1,400 years between Moses’ death and the birth of Christ, the Israelites went through a recurring cycle: devotion that brought God’s favor and blessings, then rebellion, God’s judgment, followed by their repentance, and finally God’s deliverance۔ This pattern is especially prominent in the books of **Judges, Kings, and Chronicles**, which describe such cycles when the people turned away from God, often by worshiping other deities, resulting in periods of oppression or exile۔ In response to their cry, God repeatedly raised up judges, prophets, or leaders to deliver them۔

<!-- p44 -->
During these years, despite the Israelites repeatedly failing to keep their covenant with Him, God remained faithful, showing judgment as well as mercy۔ He continually saved and forgave His people, displaying His enduring commitment and love۔

<!-- p45 -->
As we see in Scripture, God reveals Himself through His actions, relationships, and even through the names that tell of His character۔ Each name represents a specific aspect of His overall character۔ These names were given as part of the story of how God revealed Himself to His people۔ For your personal study a reference table is included in the appendix: *Table 10 - The Names of God in the Old Testament.* Read these names and study the Scripture references۔ Each reference is a story that shows exactly how God saved, delivered, and rescued His people۔

<!-- p46 -->
The appendix also includes a further synthesized collection that describes the attributes that express His nature, and provides details of His works and intentions: *Table 11 - The Attributes of God.* These attributes, though somewhat more abstract than the names of God, are in no way less authentic!

<!-- p47 -->
These names help us understand God—the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit (**Trinity**)—working together as one۔ In our ongoing study we will take a deeper look at the names and attributes of Jesus and the Holy Spirit۔

<!-- p48 -->
God’s character and His works are fully revealed in the unity of the Father, the Son, and the Spirit۔ Scripture teaches that in essence God is one, yet exists as three distinct persons: the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit۔ Scripture affirms the unity of God, saying, “There is no God besides Me” (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="45" data-verses="5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Isaiah 45:5</span>)۔ However, the Godhead is understood as a compound unity—three distinct persons who act together with one mind and purpose, and are truly united۔ Each person of the Trinity has a unique role: **the Father creates**, **the Son redeems**, and **the Holy Spirit sanctifies**۔ The term “Trinity” is not found in the Bible; it was coined as a theological expression to articulate this concept, which is revealed through Scripture۔

<!-- p49 -->
The Trinity can be illustrated with examples from nature, which help in understanding this concept of unity in diversity۔ Just as water can be liquid, ice, or vapor yet remains H₂O, so each person of the Trinity is fully God yet distinct in person۔ Or consider a triangle, which has three sides and three angles that together form a complete shape; if one side is removed it is no longer a triangle۔ Likewise, **God’s nature is incomplete without all three persons**, who work together in perfect unity۔

<!-- p50 -->
- **God the Father**: He is the Creator and Sustainer of all things, and is often understood as the initiator in the Godhead۔ His love for humanity is central to the biblical story, exemplified in <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-verses="16" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 3:16</span>۔ In our study we will see God the Father as a loving Father and a righteous Judge, who made a way for our redemption۔

<!-- p51 -->
- **God the Son**: Coexistent and coeternal with the Father, Jesus took on human nature, was conceived by the Holy Spirit and born of the virgin Mary۔ He lived a sinless life, gave His life for the redemption of humanity, and then rose again, conquering sin and death۔ He now sits at the right hand of the Father and intercedes for us (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-verses="1-3" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 1:1-3</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="1" data-verses="23" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 1:23</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="2" data-verses="5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Timothy 2:5</span>)۔ In our study we will see Jesus as the perfect atoning sacrifice, the risen Lord, and the Head of the Church۔

<!-- p52 -->
- **God the Holy Spirit**: The Holy Spirit, coexistent and equal with the Father and the Son, is sent by the Father through the Son to convict the world of sin and to prepare believers as the Bride of Christ۔ He empowers, comforts, and sanctifies those who are in Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-verses="26" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 14:26</span>; 15:26; 16:8)۔ In our study we will see the Holy Spirit as the Comforter, given by God to equip us, empower us, sanctify us, and be with us۔

<!-- p53 -->
Through these distinct persons, God’s triune nature is revealed as a perfect unity in purpose, intent, and love, where each person plays a vital role in the divine mission of creation, redemption, and sanctification۔ The Trinity invites believers to know God fully—as a loving Father, a redeeming Savior, and a transforming Spirit۔

<!-- p54 -->
## Conclusion

<!-- p55 -->
We can be identified uniquely by our names: husband, father, son, brother, friend۔ Each of these is a declaration of who we are in relation to someone else۔ We can also be known by our attributes: faithful, patient, kind, intelligent, humorous۔ Likewise we can know God by His attributes and names—He is our Healer, Provider, Shepherd, and Redeemer۔ However, since titles and words are only a small part of our being, others must experience us in order truly to know us۔ All the more so with God۔ To know Him in truth and in fullness, we must experience Him۔

<!-- p56 -->
God’s names reveal His character, but personal encounters deepen our understanding of what those names mean۔ For example, “God my Healer” takes on a richer and deeper meaning when we understand the depths of sickness, weariness, or helplessness from which God saves۔ Knowing Him as our Provider becomes transformative when we have felt true need and found Him faithful۔

<!-- p57 -->
Scripture provides a context for these experiences, showing us what God’s faithfulness with His people has been like across ages and circumstances, and reinforcing that His names and attributes are not mere titles but realities He animates, experienced in the lives of individuals۔ Through this interplay of personal experience and the biblical narrative we know God - not only as a distant explanation but as a present and active relationship in every season of life۔
