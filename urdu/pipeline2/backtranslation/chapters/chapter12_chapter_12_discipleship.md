# Chapter 12 - Discipleship

<!-- p1 -->
Discipleship means following Jesus, learning from Him, and becoming like Him in our way of life and in the way we love. It consists of a lifelong journey of obedience, transformation, and service, led by the Holy Spirit and rooted in God's Word.

<!-- p2 -->
Discipleship is the fundamental command in the Great Commission. Jesus described this mission as follows:

<!-- p3 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p4 -->
> **<u>Matthew 28:18-20</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And Jesus came and spoke to them, "All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to Me.
>
> Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit,
>
> and teaching them to observe all things that I have commanded you; and behold, I am with you always, to the end of the world." Amen.
>

<!-- p5 -->
</span>

<!-- p6 -->
We, His followers, are to *“Go and make disciples of all nations.”* Discipleship is personal growth as well as teaching others to follow Christ in order to multiply that growth. Discipleship is the means by which we build God's kingdom; Jesus' teachings are the message.

<!-- p7 -->
The call to be a disciple is more than merely attending church or learning Bible verses—it is an invitation to live like Jesus and, together with Him, advance God's kingdom. True discipleship makes believers spiritually mature and effective for ministry (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:13</span>). It shapes both the heart and the skills that are necessary to serve others and share the gospel. As Paul instructed Timothy in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 2:2</span>, discipleship follows a pattern of spiritual multiplication, where trained disciples keep raising up new followers to bring others to Christ.

<!-- p8 -->
Discipleship is both an individual calling and a global mission. On a personal level, it involves growing in faith, cultivating a vibrant spiritual life, being in relationship with others &amp; with God. At the same time, it fuels the church's mission to bring the gospel to every nation, so that the message of Christ continues to spread from generation to generation and transform lives.

<!-- p9 -->
## Why Discipleship Matters

<!-- p10 -->
The impact of discipleship goes beyond personal transformation to a kingdom impact. Paul reminded Timothy in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 2:2</span> to entrust what he had learned to faithful men who would then teach others also. This generational pattern makes clear that discipleship is the engine that drives the church's growth and sustainability. Discipleship is important because it was Jesus' chosen method for spreading the gospel and building His church.

<!-- p11 -->
Rather than focusing on large crowds, Jesus intentionally invested in a small group, taught them, modeled faithfulness, and prepared them to carry forward His mission. This personal, relational approach ensured that His message multiplied, because each disciple was equipped to make more disciples.

<!-- p12 -->
At its core, discipleship is central to fulfilling the Great Commission (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19-20</span>). Jesus called us not merely to conversion but to making disciples—followers who are rooted in God's Word, filled with the Holy Spirit, and equipped for service. Through discipleship, believers grow in knowledge of Scripture, develop spiritual habits, and learn to walk in obedience to Christ. It prepares the church to influence the world, advance the gospel, and display a glimpse of God's glory to all nations.

<!-- p13 -->
### Nature vs. Nurture

<!-- p14 -->
In the study of biology, psychology, and anthropology we often consider the effects of nature versus nurture. The question is often: Is a person more the result of his or her inborn, inherent genetic heritage? Or are they more the result of their environment and upbringing? The truth is that every person is a combination of nature and nurture. The strong interplay of both elements shapes the development of the human soul.

<!-- p15 -->
When you accepted Jesus, the miracle of salvation caused you to be born again of incorruptible seed. You received the divine nature! Your spiritual DNA was rewritten! This divine nature stirs our hearts to be close to our heavenly Father, to know Him, to experience Him.

<!-- p16 -->
Like a newborn whose marvelous, beautiful, innocent nature must be shaped by the nurture of loving parents in order to become a healthy and mature person, so we too must be shaped and nurtured in order for us to grow.

<!-- p17 -->
Discipleship is the process of spiritual growth that builds upon our divine nature and matures us spiritually. Through investment in discipleship we ensure that the faith is passed on, leaders are raised up, and the church remains healthy and vibrant—both locally and globally.

<!-- p18 -->
### Didactic Model vs. Discipleship/Apprenticeship Model

<!-- p19 -->
In modern Western education, the student's primary engagement is with the content. The student's learning centers on content, books, videos, assignments, etc. Transformation in education occurs through the curriculum. This approach to teaching is called the *didactic model*.

<!-- p20 -->
The paradigm of the classroom model has not always been the norm. In Jesus' time, the model and method used was the *discipleship model*. This model of learning emphasizes that knowledge is more often 'caught' than taught and focuses more on the relationship between student and teacher than on the student and the curriculum.

<!-- p21 -->
The classroom model gradually became the standard method over roughly a thousand years: the early medieval universities of Europe (11<sup>th</sup> - 13<sup>th</sup> centuries), the invention of the printing press in the 15<sup>th</sup> century, the Enlightenment of the 17<sup>th</sup> &amp; 18<sup>th</sup> centuries, and the effects of the Industrial Revolution on information and education in the 19<sup>th</sup> &amp; 20<sup>th</sup> centuries.

<!-- p22 -->
The advantage of the classroom model is that it can scale more effectively than the discipleship model. Massive Open Online Courses (MOOCs) can accommodate thousands of students at once, whereas even a very skilled teacher may be able to have only a few dozen disciples at a time.

<!-- p23 -->
The outcome of the classroom model is that the student masters the material; however, the student does not become like the teacher. The teacher is a facilitator for the curriculum. The teacher transfers knowledge.

<!-- p24 -->
In the discipleship model the teacher shares not only knowledge, but also imparts wisdom. The result of the discipleship model is that the disciple becomes so like the teacher that they can replicate the teacher's work and make their own disciples.

<!-- p25 -->
The classroom model has its place and usefulness in God's Kingdom; a good example is this book or any Christian educational material. Jesus, as a rabbi who was skilled and trained in the rabbinic way, primarily adopted the discipleship model for his disciples.

<!-- p26 -->
Jesus describes the discipleship model as follows:

<!-- p27 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p28 -->
> **<u>Luke 6:40</u>** (NKJV)
>
> A disciple is not above his teacher, but everyone who is fully trained will be like his teacher.
>

<!-- p29 -->
</span>

<!-- p30 -->
In the classroom model the student becomes a possessor of knowledge, whereas in the discipleship model the student becomes like the teacher. Let us study the key words of this passage in Luke.

<!-- p32 -->
In summary, the purpose and aim of the discipleship model used in discipling consists of two facets:

<!-- p33 -->
**First, the disciple becomes fully trained**, like the teacher. This does not mean that the disciple copies everything their teacher does. We have not been commanded to make clones, but to develop mature men and women who can carry Jesus' message to the ends of the earth.

<!-- p34 -->
**Second, the disciple now becomes a teacher for others**. The disciple must pass on what they have learned to someone else, otherwise the message of the Kingdom stops with them. This does not mean that their gifts will necessarily be the same as their teacher's, or that they will enter full-time ministry, but it does mean that for their training to be complete they must reproduce.

<!-- p35 -->
Every living thing that does not reproduce is barren, and its lineage dies out. An animal that cannot reproduce is not healthy; likewise, a church that cannot reproduce is unhealthy.

## Jesus' model and method for discipleship

<!-- p37 -->
In the Gospels, Jesus presented a robust and complete example of discipleship with his chosen disciples. We should broadly adopt these stages as a foundation for becoming and making disciples in our lives.

<!-- p38 -->
In the discipleship model there are two roles: teacher and student. Every teacher was once a student, and hopefully every student will become a teacher. I will use the term *disciple* for the student, and *disciple-maker* for the teacher/instructor.

<!-- p39 -->
Every discipling relationship progresses through eight stages. The stages, or steps, are not strictly separate from one another; often the relationship flows back and forth between stages. In each stage there is a particular emphasis for the disciple and for the disciple-maker. Later in this chapter we will study the qualities of disciple-makers in detail.

<!-- p40 -->
The eight stages of discipleship are:

<!-- p41 -->
1.  Invitation/Call

<!-- p42 -->
2.  Teaching

<!-- p43 -->
3.  Practical Demonstration

<!-- p44 -->
4.  Involvement/Effort

<!-- p45 -->
5.  Correction

<!-- p46 -->
6.  Empowerment/Authorization

<!-- p47 -->
7.  Multiplication

<!-- p48 -->
8.  Staying Connected

<!-- p49 -->
### Invitation/Call

<!-- p50 -->
Discipleship begins with a personal invitation from Jesus to follow Him. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 4:19</span> Jesus called His first disciples, saying: *"Follow Me, and I will make you fishers of men."* This invitation was both relational and transformative-it required leaving the old way of life behind and stepping into a new purpose centered on God’s mission. Jesus’s call was not toward becoming spectators but toward participation in His ministry and the work of the Kingdom.

<!-- p51 -->
Those who make disciples today should invite others into genuine relationships that spur spiritual growth and obedience. The call to discipleship is profoundly personal. Jesus meets individuals where they are, addressing their unique needs and circumstances, yet He also calls them to go deeper-grow, serve, and become like Him. This pattern reveals the relational nature of discipleship, where the focus is not merely on knowledge but on a life-transforming relationship with Christ.

<!-- p52 -->
### Teaching

<!-- p53 -->
Teaching is at the heart of discipleship. Jesus devoted much of His ministry to teaching the principles of God’s Kingdom, especially in the Sermon on the Mount (Matthew 5-7). His teachings provided the foundation for living righteously, loving others, and walking in obedience to God. Even today, instruction in the Scriptures is central to discipleship, because the Bible is the primary instrument for spiritual growth, reproof, and training in righteousness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>).

<!-- p54 -->
Disciple-makers should teach the Bible faithfully, helping others understand it and apply it to their lives. Teaching is not merely the transfer of information-it also requires modeling a Christlike life so that disciples can see the faith in action. Jesus embodied the truths He taught, showing that effective teaching is both spoken and visible. Likewise, disciple-makers should live what they teach, providing practical examples of obedience and faithfulness.

<!-- p55 -->
### Practical Demonstration

<!-- p56 -->
Jesus did not merely teach His disciples- He also showed them how to live out the truths He proclaimed. Jesus demonstrated what it means to love, serve, and minister in practical ways. Here are a few examples from the Gospels: Jesus washed the disciples’ feet as an act of humility and service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 13:12-15</span>), healed the sick, and fed the hungry. Through His actions He modeled compassion, faith, and courage, leaving a clear example for His followers to imitate.

<!-- p57 -->
Discipleship requires not only telling but also showing. Today’s disciple-makers should be transparent about their faith journeys, including both struggles and victories, so that others can see how faith works in real life. Whether praying with someone in need, sharing testimonies, or serving the community, discipleship thrives when it is rooted in practical demonstration. Actions often speak louder than words, making demonstration a powerful means of training others in the faith.

<!-- p58 -->
### Involvement/Effort

<!-- p59 -->
Jesus did not keep His disciples on the sidelines- He actively involved them in ministry experiences. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 9:1-6</span> and <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 10:5-15</span>, Jesus sent the disciples out to proclaim the gospel, heal the sick, and cast out demons. These hands-on opportunities allowed the disciples to learn by doing, so that they would gain confidence and skill under His guidance.

<!-- p60 -->
Modern discipleship should also include practical involvement. Disciple makers should invite disciples to participate in service, whether through leading prayer groups, sharing testimonies, or serving at outreach events. Providing safe spaces for practice gives disciples the opportunity to grow in courage while they also receive support and encouragement. Learning is best when it is put into practice, and involvement is the key that turns theory into action.

<!-- p61 -->
### Correction

<!-- p62 -->
Growth often requires correction, and Jesus lovingly corrected his disciples when they misunderstood his teachings or failed in their actions. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33-37" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 9:33-37</span>, he addressed their pride by teaching about servant leadership. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-32" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 22:31-32</span>, he prayed for Peter, preparing him to recover from failure and strengthen others.

<!-- p63 -->
Correction in discipleship should be grace-filled and constructive—rooted in Scripture and focused on restoration (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 4:2</span>). It is not about condemnation but about helping disciples grow and avoid pitfalls. Disciple makers should provide accountability, using God’s Word to guide and encourage growth, while modeling humility in receiving correction themselves.

<!-- p64 -->
### Empowerment/Authorization

<!-- p65 -->
Jesus not only taught and corrected—he empowered his disciples to carry out his mission. He gave them authority to preach the gospel, heal the sick, and cast out demons (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:18-20</span>). After his resurrection, he promised the Holy Spirit as the source of power for their ministry (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>).

<!-- p66 -->
Modern discipleship should include empowering others to operate in their spiritual gifts and minister effectively. This includes identifying gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-13</span>), encouraging their use, and sending disciples out for service. Empowerment also emphasizes reliance on the Holy Spirit for power, boldness, and wisdom, so that disciples are spiritually equipped for the work God has called them to.

<!-- p67 -->
### Multiplication

<!-- p68 -->
The goal of discipleship is not merely personal growth—it is multiplication. Jesus modeled this by training disciples who would make more disciples (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19-20</span>). After his resurrection, the apostles continued this expanding mission, spreading the gospel and planting churches throughout the known world of their time. Paul’s instructions in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 2:2</span> reinforce this principle, urging Timothy to teach others who will in turn teach others.

<!-- p69 -->
Discipleship involves equipping disciples to disciple others, creating a chain reaction of faith that advances God’s kingdom. This process requires intentionality—teaching disciples how to guide, teach, and train others, while also sending them with confidence and purpose (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 10:15</span>).

<!-- p70 -->
### Maintain Contact

<!-- p71 -->
Discipleship does not end when someone is sent. Jesus modeled ongoing contact with his disciples, continuing to encourage and instruct them even after his resurrection (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="21" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 21:15-19</span>). The early church maintained the same pattern, gathering regularly for teaching, fellowship, and prayer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42-47" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:42-47</span>).

<!-- p72 -->
Modern discipleship should also cultivate ongoing relationships, providing accountability, encouragement, and support as disciples continue to grow and lead. Maintaining unity in the body of Christ ensures that disciples are never alone but remain strengthened and encouraged in their journey of faith.

<!-- p73 -->
## Who Does Discipleship?

<!-- p74 -->
Everyone in the body of Christ bears the responsibility first to be a disciple, then to participate in making disciples. There is diversity in gifts and roles, so our contributions may look different, but we are all responsible. In the local church, the people ultimately most responsible for the state and health of discipleship are the leaders (**elders and deacons - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 14:23</span>**), but everyone plays their part.

<!-- p75 -->
Discipleship is a living, growing process—like nurturing a child to maturity. Healthy discipleship empowers and gives life to people. Just as a parent strengthens and feeds a child, thereby making them healthy and mature, so disciple makers help to feed, strengthen, and mature disciples. The ultimate goal of discipleship is not 'transfer of knowledge,' but transformation.

<!-- p76 -->
We are all to become perfect (mature) and make disciples. Although our gifts will differ, there are certain common characteristics for all disciple makers.

<!-- p77 -->
### Characteristics of a Disciple Maker

<!-- p78 -->
A disciple maker is someone who not only follows Christ but remains committed to helping others grow in their faith. This role requires specific qualities that reflect both spiritual maturity and practical dedication. Disciple makers should continually rely on God’s power, wisdom, and grace to overcome the challenges that come with guiding others in their spiritual journey.

<!-- p79 -->
#### Rooted in Christ

<!-- p80 -->
The foundation of a disciple-maker’s life should be deeply rooted in Christ. Jesus said، *“I am the vine؛ you are the branches. If you remain in Me and I in you، you will bear much fruit؛ apart from Me you can do nothing”* (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:5</span>)۔ Effective disciple-makers know that the ability to grow and to help others comes from staying close to Jesus daily۔ However، personal growth and resistance to spiritual transformation can hinder this dependence۔ Disciple-makers should continually cultivate their personal relationship with Jesus through prayer، study of Scripture، and obedience, and model practically for those they are training what it means to abide in Christ۔

<!-- p81 -->
#### Committed to the Mission

<!-- p82 -->
A disciple-maker should remain committed to the mission of making disciples as Jesus commanded in the Great Commission (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19-20</span>)۔ This mission requires intentional focus and prioritization، knowing that making disciples is not an optional task but the church’s primary purpose۔ Even so، challenges such as lack of time، busyness، and personal distractions can make it difficult to stay focused۔ Disciple-makers should intentionally set aside time for mentoring، teaching، and building relationships, and view disciple-making not as an occasional activity but as a lifestyle (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 19:10</span>)۔

<!-- p83 -->
#### Led by the Spirit

<!-- p84 -->
Disciple-making cannot be done by human strength alone؛ it requires the guidance and power of the Holy Spirit۔ Jesus promised His disciples that when the Holy Spirit came upon them they would receive power (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>)، and they would be equipped to be witnesses and servants of the gospel۔ Disciple-makers should remain sensitive to the Spirit’s leading، and rely on His wisdom to navigate complex situations، cultural barriers، and difficult conversations۔ This dependence on the Holy Spirit enables disciple-makers to act with love، courage، and spiritual discernment, so that their efforts are Spirit-directed rather than self-directed۔

<!-- p85 -->
#### Humble and Teachable

<!-- p86 -->
Humility is a distinguishing mark of an effective disciple-maker۔ As Paul instructed، *“Do nothing out of selfish ambition or vain conceit. Rather, in humility value others above yourselves”* (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 2:3</span>)۔ Disciple-makers acknowledge that they themselves are lifelong learners who must remain teachable and accountable۔ They welcome correction and feedback، and model humility for those they train۔ Pride، can be a major barrier to growth، whether for disciple-makers or for their disciples۔ Embracing accountability and maintaining a servant’s heart fosters trust and creates an environment for growth and transparency۔

<!-- p87 -->
#### Patient and Steadfast

<!-- p88 -->
Discipleship is rarely immediate-it is a long-term process that requires patience and perseverance۔ Paul reminds believers that، *“Let us not grow weary while doing good, for in due season we shall reap if we do not lose heart”* (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:9</span>)۔ Progress in faith often includes setbacks، questions، and failures, but disciple-makers are called to walk alongside others، providing encouragement and support at every stage۔ When progress seems slow, it is easy to lose heart، but disciple-makers trust that God is at work, even when the results are not immediately visible۔

<!-- p89 -->
Being a disciple-maker requires a combination of spiritual dependence، commitment، and humility۔ It is a calling that demands patience and perseverance while remaining Spirit-led and teachable۔ Disciple-makers are not expected to be perfect، but they must be faithful-abiding in Christ، investing in others، and trusting God to bring growth and transformation in His time۔ These qualities reflect the love of Christ and help others become mature disciples who can, in turn, make disciples of others۔

<!-- p90 -->
## Practical Resources for Discipleship

<!-- p91 -->
Effective discipleship requires intentional resources and practices that foster spiritual growth، accountability، and community۔ These resources help disciples establish a strong foundation in faith، grow in closeness with God، and be equipped to make disciples of others۔ Practical methods, such as Bible study، prayer، small groups، and mentoring, provide the structure and support needed for ongoing progress۔

<!-- p92 -->
### Bible Study

<!-- p93 -->
Engaging deeply with Scripture is the foundation of discipleship۔ Jesus emphasized the importance of God’s word in spiritual growth, saying, *“Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceeds from the mouth of God”* (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 4:4</span>)۔ Through regular Bible study and memorization, disciples internalize truth، resist lies، and apply God’s wisdom to everyday life۔

<!-- p94 -->
Disciples should be encouraged to immerse themselves in the Word, not merely as an academic exercise but to know God and see their hearts transformed (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="119" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="105" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 119:105</span>)۔ Practical methods such as reading plans، study guides، and group discussions provide opportunities to explore doctrines and discuss application together۔ By building regular study habits، disciples lay a strong foundation for spiritual maturity and fruitful ministry (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>)۔

<!-- p95 -->
### Prayer

<!-- p96 -->
Prayer is the lifeline of discipleship, connecting believers to God’s presence، power، and guidance۔ Jesus taught His disciples to pray in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 11:1-4</span>، and emphasized both dependence on God and spiritual alignment with His will۔ Through prayer, disciples learn to seek God’s wisdom، intercede for others، and find strength for service۔

<!-- p97 -->
Teaching disciples how to pray-individually and corporately-equips them to grow in closeness with God and to discern His guidance (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 4:6-7</span>)۔ Incorporating prayer journals، prayer groups، and fasting practices fosters spiritual discipline and strength، and ensures that disciples remain Spirit-led and mission-focused۔

<!-- p98 -->
### Small Groups and Community

<!-- p99 -->
Discipleship thrives in community۔ The early church modeled relational discipleship through fellowship، prayer، and breaking bread together (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42-47" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:42-47</span>)۔ Small groups and accountability partnerships provide a safe environment where disciples can grow together، share struggles، and celebrate victories۔

<!-- p100 -->
The local church serves as the central hub for discipleship relationships، and provides opportunities for group Bible study، prayer meetings، and fellowship events۔ These gatherings give disciples the opportunity to practice love، to bear one another’s burdens (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:2</span>)، and to encourage one another toward spiritual maturity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:24-25</span>)۔ Small groups also provide a structure for developing leaders، as mature disciples train and send others to lead groups and mentor new believers۔

<!-- p101 -->
### Mentoring

<!-- p102 -->
Mentoring provides a personal and intentional approach to discipleship۔ Jesus set the example by investing deeply in the twelve disciples، walking with them through teaching، correction، and encouragement۔ Likewise، one-on-one relationships allow disciple-makers to tailor guidance to individual needs، enabling disciples to grow in faith، character، and leadership۔

<!-- p103 -->
Mentors serve as accountability partners، and provide support، prayer، and wisdom during times of growth and struggle۔ Practical tools like spiritual assessments، goal setting، and regular check-ins help ensure that disciples remain focused and encouraged۔ Peer relationships within mentoring structures also promote mutual growth، as both mentor and disciple learn from shared experiences (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="27" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 27:17</span>)۔

## **Personal Reflection Questions**

<!-- p105 -->
1.  What does it personally mean to you to be a disciple of Jesus؟ How are you practically following Him in your daily life؟

<!-- p106 -->
2.  Reflect on Jesus’ model of discipleship۔ Which aspect of His method-calling، teaching، modeling، or empowering-stands out to you the most، and why؟

<!-- p107 -->
3.  How can you grow your faith by making disciples of others؟ What challenges or fears might hold you back، and how can you overcome them؟ (Note that I said grow your faith، not your disciples’!)

<!-- p108 -->
4.  Which qualities of a disciple-maker (e.g., patience، humility، relational focus) do you see in yourself؟ Which traits do you want to develop further؟

<!-- p109 -->
5.  Think about the people in your life۔ Whom might God be calling you to disciple؟ How can you take the first step in building that relationship؟
