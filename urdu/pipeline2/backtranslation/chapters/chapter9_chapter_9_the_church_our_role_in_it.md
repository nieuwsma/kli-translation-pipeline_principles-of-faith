# Chapter 9 - The Church and Our Role in It

<!-- p1 -->
The Church is the means through which God reveals Himself on earth—through His people, who do His work. We are called to be His body; we are His hands and feet to accomplish His work and purpose. Without our efforts, the harvest will remain in the field and will not be reaped.

<!-- p2 -->
## Who is the Church?

<!-- p3 -->
The Church, ***Ekklesia** (eh-klee-SEE-ah - **G1577**),* is derived from two Greek words:

<!-- p4 -->
- **"Ek"**: "out of."

<!-- p5 -->
- **"Kaleo"**: "to call."

<!-- p6 -->
Taken together, **Ekklesia** literally means "the called-out" or "the called assembly." In ancient Greek city-states it was used for the assembly of citizens who were summoned for a specific purpose, such as legislation or judicial sessions. It was a non-religious term that denoted an association of people.

<!-- p7 -->
In the New Testament it is primarily used for the assembly of those who believe in Jesus Christ. It can carry the following meanings:

<!-- p8 -->
- **Local church**: A particular gathering of believers in a certain place (e.g., the church of Corinth, Philippi, etc.).

<!-- p9 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p10 -->
> **<u>1 Corinthians 1:2</u>** (NKJV)
>
> To the church of God which is in Corinth, to those who have been sanctified in Christ Jesus, called to be saints, and together with all who in every place call on the name of our and their Lord Jesus Christ:
>

<!-- p11 -->
</span>

<!-- p12 -->
- **Universal Church**: The collective body of all believers across time and space.

<!-- p13 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p14 -->
> **<u>Matthew 16:18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And I also say to you that you are Peter, and on this rock I will build My church, and the gates of Hades shall not prevail against it.
>

<!-- p15 -->
</span>

<!-- p16 -->
The Church is people, not the building or place where they meet. "The called-out people": reflects the concept that Christians are called out of the world and set apart for God's purposes (e.g., <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:9</span>) for a specific mission and purpose (the Great Commission). The church is technically also called the gathering together of two or more believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="18" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 18:20</span>). Generally the church is based on regular gatherings, possibly with a set place and time; but wherever two or more people gather in Jesus’ name, there is the church!

<!-- p17 -->
The Church was established by Jesus, and was grown in the first century A.D. through the ministry of the disciples who followed Him. The church that we have today throughout the world with all its local congregations, and which we experience, is a direct result of the early work of Christ and His apostles. As believers we should be connected with a local church for fellowship, provision, preparation for service, and to do good works!

<!-- p18 -->
To help us understand what the church is, the Scriptures give us powerful images that show what it should be.

<!-- p19 -->
## Understanding the Church

<!-- p20 -->
Several foundational metaphorical images are used to describe the characteristics of the Church:

<!-- p21 -->
1.  Body

<!-- p22 -->
2.  Bride

<!-- p23 -->
3.  Temple

<!-- p24 -->
4.  Family

<!-- p25 -->
5.  Flock

<!-- p26 -->
6.  The Vine and the Branches

<!-- p27 -->
Each metaphor highlights a unique aspect of the Church، however together they reveal the Church’s identity as a united and diverse community under the leadership of Christ۔

<!-- p28 -->
### Body

<!-- p29 -->
The Church is often called **the body of Christ**, which highlights both its unity and diversity. With Christ as the head, the Church functions like a living organism where every believer plays an essential and, potentially, varied role. Each member possesses unique gifts and, according to their own responsibility, contributes to the health and mission of the body. This image underscores the interdependence of believers, particularly that for the body to function it must be connected, and that our work is collaborative rather than competitive. When each part works together, the body grows and flourishes in unity and love (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:12-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:15-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 1:18</span>)۔

<!-- p30 -->
### Bride

<!-- p31 -->
The Church is called **the bride of Christ**, which expresses an intimate and loving relationship with Jesus. Just as a bride is cherished and prepared for her husband, so the Church is sanctified and cleansed by Christ’s sacrificial love. This metaphor describes the devotion and faithfulness expected of the Church, as well as the deep love Christ has for His people. This imagery also points to the ultimate union of Christ and the Church, which will be celebrated at the marriage supper of the Lamb, where the Church is presented in glory without spot or blemish (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="25-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:25-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 19:7-9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="21" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 21:2</span>)۔

<!-- p32 -->
### Temple

<!-- p33 -->
The Church is called **the temple of God**, meaning the dwelling place of His Spirit. Believers are called living stones who are being built together on the foundation of the apostles and prophets, with Christ Himself as the cornerstone. This holy building is a sign of God’s presence among His people and reveals their role as a community of worship and prayer. The imagery of the temple highlights the importance of holiness and the Church’s corporate purpose in glorifying God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 3:16-17</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:19-22</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:4-5</span>)۔

<!-- p34 -->
### Family

<!-- p35 -->
The Church is called **the family of God**, which highlights relational unity and care among its members. Through the Spirit of adoption, believers are made children of God and members of His household. This family imagery clarifies the love, commitment, and shared inheritance of believers in Christ. It also reflects the call that we should treat one another with compassion and humility as brothers and sisters in God’s family (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:19</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:15-17</span>)۔ Moreover, through the family image it is shown how the members of the Church should mature, that they be taught and nurtured by spiritual parents (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:1-2</span>)، so that we may grow from newborns drinking the pure milk of the word to become mature, able to eat the solid food of the word (Hebrews 5)۔

<!-- p36 -->
### Flock

<!-- p37 -->
The Church is called **a flock**, that is, sheep under the care of Jesus, the good shepherd. This image shows the Church’s dependence on Christ for guidance, provision, and protection. It also highlights the responsibility of the Church’s leaders to serve as faithful under-shepherds of God’s flock, reflecting Christ’s example of love and care. The imagery of the flock points to trust, obedience, and the close relationship between the great Shepherd and His sheep (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:14-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:2</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>)۔

<!-- p38 -->
### The Vine and the Branches

<!-- p39 -->
The Church is called **the vine and the branches**, which clarifies the essential connection between believers and Christ. Jesus is the true vine, and believers are the branches who must abide in Him to bear fruit. This metaphor highlights dependence on Christ for spiritual vitality, growth, and fruitfulness. Without Him believers can do nothing, but by abiding in Him they glorify God and fulfill their purpose. Jesus will prune us so that more fruit may be produced in our lives (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:1-5</span>)۔

<!-- p40 -->
## The Roles of the Church

<!-- p41 -->
The mission of the Church, like the role of the Holy Spirit, can be understood through three dimensions: upward, inward, and outward, each of which reflects a unique aspect of its calling in God’s plan. These dimensions guide the Church in fulfilling its role as a worshiping, edifying, and missional community.

<!-- p42 -->
### Upward: Worship and the Glory of God

<!-- p43 -->
The first purpose of the Church is to worship and glorify God. Corporate worship, where believers gather together to exalt God, is a prominent expression of His glory and a response to His majesty. As the psalmist declares, “*Worship the Lord in the beauty of holiness; O all the earth, tremble before Him*” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="96" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 96:9</span>). Beyond corporate worship, the Church is called to live lives of obedience and sacrifice, presenting ourselves as “living sacrifices,” which is an act of spiritual worship (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:1-2</span>). This upward focus keeps the Church centered on God’s greatness and aligns its priorities with His will.

<!-- p44 -->
### Inward: Building up the Body

<!-- p45 -->
The Great Commission commands us to ‘teach them to obey’; this command goes far beyond formal educational methods such as this book. Discipleship (discussed further in the chapter on discipleship) is to shape the follower according to this training, like a parent raises a child. Good parents do not hand a five-year-old an encyclopedia and challenge them to figure it out alone! Rather, they model, show, correct, guide, nurture, love, care, and shepherd.

<!-- p46 -->
Parents sacrifice for their children because they love them, because they are parts of the same body, because they share the same blood. As members of the body and family of Christ we share one body; we share one blood. The Lord’s Supper, through its symbols, reminds us that we have been joined to one another through the sacrifice of Christ. The practice of the Lord’s Supper should produce unity, reminding us that we all need redemption, and that salvation is only from God. In this unity we see that no part of the body is greater than another, except our Head, Christ.

<!-- p47 -->
### Outward: Reaching the World

<!-- p48 -->
The most well-known part of the Church’s mission moves outward to reach the world with the Gospel. In Jesus’ Great Commission, His followers are invited to proclaim the Gospel to all nations and make disciples (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19-20</span>). Empowered by the Holy Spirit, the Church is to be His witness to the ends of the earth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>).

<!-- p49 -->
Jesus is the light of the world (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 8:12</span>). Light shines outward in every direction from its source. His light shines through His manifestations: the Word, His Spirit, and us, His body. We are the light of the world; in our lives we must spread His light into our dark world and display good works that glorify God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 5:14-16</span>). Practically, this means the Church is called to advocate for justice and mercy in both spiritual and physical ways. As James says, our pure religion is to care for the weak, the orphans, and the widows (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:27</span>).

<!-- p50 -->
## Church Leadership

<!-- p51 -->
Across all of Christianity there is great diversity of denominations, sees, congregations, and associations. There are very few universal principles for church leadership, but the principles we do have are extremely important:

<!-- p52 -->
1. Jesus is the sole Head of the Church. All serve Him (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 1:18</span>).

<!-- p53 -->
2. There are high standards for those who lead; they are worthy of double honor, because they will receive double accountability (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:17</span>).

<!-- p54 -->
2.1 They must be mature, not arrogant, responsible (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:1-13</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 1:5-9</span>).

<!-- p55 -->
2.2. They must be servants of all (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42-45" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 10:42-45</span>).

<!-- p56 -->
2.3. They must devote themselves to the Word and to prayer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:4</span>).

<!-- p57 -->
2.4. They are responsible for equipping the body for the work of service.

<!-- p58 -->
3. There is no greater or lesser member in the body. Supporting verses are:

<!-- p59 -->
3.1. There should be no partiality between rich and poor (James 2).

<!-- p60 -->
3.2.  There is no division between Jew and Gentile, slave or free, male or female (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 3:27-28</span>).

<!-- p61 -->
Apart from the rules of leadership, the structure of the church is important, but much of it is situational and a matter of tradition rather than universally ordained. “<u>We have always done it this way!</u>” is a popular phrase؛ if you go back far enough, there was usually a good reason that they did it that way. Generally, it is neither good nor bad, but a matter of taste, needs, and availability.

<!-- p62 -->
A useful example of structure being based on tradition is family mealtime. The most important part of family mealtime is that everyone gets food and the food is nourishing. The variations are countless: what is eaten، how it is eaten، how the food is seasoned، etc. Different combinations are generally acceptable and to a large extent are a matter of taste and preference.

<!-- p63 -->
The needs and capacities of a church with 10,000 members differ from a church of 50 people. The pattern established in the book of Acts and the corpus of the New Testament was that of elders and deacons. Although the leadership structure may look different among churches and denominations، ultimately this pattern represents the minimum viable structure of leadership for the church. Let us look at these leadership offices and the method of their selection.

<!-- p64 -->
### Elders (Overseers)

<!-- p65 -->
Roles and responsibilities:

<!-- p66 -->
- **Spiritual leadership** - provide oversight, guidance, and shepherding care for the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:2</span>).

<!-- p67 -->
- **Teaching and preaching** - responsible for teaching sound doctrine and protecting from false teaching (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:17</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 1:9</span>).

<!-- p68 -->
- **Shepherding the flock** - caring for the congregation’s spiritual needs، being an example of humility and service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:2-3</span>).

<!-- p69 -->
- **Protecting the church** - serving as guardians، protecting the church from false teaching and division (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29-31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:29-31</span>).

<!-- p70 -->
- **Equipping the saints** - training and preparing believers for works of service and discipleship (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-12</span>).

<!-- p71 -->
Method of selection:

<!-- p72 -->
- **Appointed in every church** - elders were appointed by the apostles or their representatives, often through prayer and fasting (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 14:23</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 1:5</span>).

<!-- p73 -->
- **With the confirmation of the Holy Spirit** - their appointment was understood as the Spirit’s guidance, recognizing God’s calling and gifting (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>).

<!-- p74 -->
- **Tested and approved** - candidates had to prove maturity and faithfulness before being entrusted with leadership (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:10</span>). They cannot be new converts! ((<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:6</span>)

<!-- p75 -->
### Deacons (servants)

<!-- p76 -->
Roles and responsibilities:

<!-- p77 -->
- **Practical service** - focusing on meeting physical needs in the church, so that the elders can devote themselves to prayer and teaching (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-4</span>).

<!-- p78 -->
- **Supportive service** - helping with hospitality, care for the poor, and administrative tasks (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:3</span>).

<!-- p79 -->
- **Promoting unity** - resolving issues of justice and care so that division does not arise (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-7</span>).

<!-- p80 -->
- **Examples of Faith** - Be an example in humility, faith, and service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:13</span>).

<!-- p81 -->
Selection process (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-6</span>):

<!-- p82 -->
- **Recognized by the church** - Chosen on the basis of their good reputation and spiritual character.

<!-- p83 -->
- **Approval of the leaders** - The apostles examined the selected servants with prayer and the laying on of hands and appointed them.

<!-- p84 -->
- **Confirmation in service** - Before formal appointment, servants were first tested to prove their faithfulness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:10</span>).

<!-- p85 -->
### Qualifications for Leadership

<!-- p86 -->
#### Qualifications for both elders and deacons:

<!-- p87 -->
- Moral character - Both should be blameless, dignified, and self-controlled (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:2</span>, 8).

<!-- p88 -->
- Family life - Both should be faithful in marriage (“husband of one wife”) and manage their households well (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:4-5</span>, 12).

<!-- p89 -->
- Spiritual maturity - Both should be tested and confirmed before appointment (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:6</span>, 10).

<!-- p90 -->
- Good reputation - Both should have a good reputation inside and outside the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:7</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
- Faith and integrity - Both should hold the faith with a clear conscience and live out their beliefs (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:9</span>).

<!-- p92 -->
### Model Structures

<!-- p93 -->
In light of what we have learned so far about leadership rules and structures, we will look at two examples of church structures found in the New Testament: Jerusalem and Antioch.

<!-- p94 -->
### The Jerusalem Church

<!-- p95 -->
The leadership of the Jerusalem church was primarily in the hands of the **apostles**, who focused on teaching, prayer, and spiritual care (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:42</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:4</span>). As the community grew, the need for an administrative structure became more evident, especially to handle practical matters such as caring for widows and distributing resources. This led to the appointment of **deacons** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-7</span>). These individuals, chosen for their spiritual maturity and good reputation, took on practical ministries so that the apostles could remain focused on their spiritual responsibilities. Over time, **elders** also emerged as leaders in the Jerusalem church. For example, during the Jerusalem council (Acts 15), both apostles and elders participated in decision-making, indicating a shared leadership structure.

<!-- p96 -->
### The Church in Antioch

<!-- p97 -->
The leadership structure of the church in Antioch was more decentralized and diverse compared to Jerusalem. Its leadership was in the hands of a group of **prophets and teachers**, including Barnabas, Saul (Paul), Simeon, Lucius, and Manaen (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 13:1</span>). The church in Antioch was more inclined toward reaching Gentiles, whereas the church in Jerusalem more prominently reflected its Jewish roots. Although the New Testament does not explicitly mention deacons in Antioch, given the church’s growth and its participation in sending aid to Jerusalem, it is likely that practical responsibilities were present (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29-30" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 11:29-30</span>). The church in Antioch became a model for the commissioning of missionaries, where the leaders prayed and fasted and, laying hands on Paul and Barnabas, sent them off (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 13:2-3</span>). This reflects a structure that balanced spiritual care (prophets and teachers) with mission advancement and practical relief.

<!-- p98 -->
In contrast to the corrections Paul made to the Corinthians and Galatians, the Scriptures do not criticize the leadership structures of Jerusalem or Antioch. This indicates that these churches, under the guidance of the Holy Spirit and within the bounds of sound doctrine and apostolic teaching, were free to shape their congregations according to their unique needs and circumstances (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:42</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>).

<!-- p99 -->
Some terms have been used in these references that we have not yet defined and studied: *apostle, prophet, evangelist*. These gifts are part of the fivefold ministry that Jesus gave to the church.

<!-- p100 -->
### Fivefold Ministry Gifts

<!-- p101 -->
Jesus established five distinct ministry gifts, often called the **Fivefold Ministry Gifts**, to equip believers for service and to build up the Body of Christ.

<!-- p102 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p103 -->
> **<u>Ephesians 4:11-13</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And He Himself gave some to be apostles, some prophets, some evangelists, and some pastors and teachers,
>
> for the equipping of the saints for the work of ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ,
>
> till we all come to the unity of the faith and of the knowledge of the Son of God, to a perfect man, to the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ;
>

<!-- p104 -->
</span>

<!-- p105 -->
The five gifts that were given to equip the saints for the work of ministry are:

<!-- p106 -->
1.  **Apostles** - trailblazers and visionaries who lay foundations, plant churches, and advance the Kingdom of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:20</span>). Apostles model boldness and faith, showing how to step into new territories and take spiritual initiative.

<!-- p107 -->
2.  **Prophets** - messengers and guides who declare God’s truth, bringing encouragement, edification, and guidance (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>). Prophets stir faith, invite others to walk according to God’s Word, and model reliance on hearing God’s voice.

<!-- p108 -->
3.  **Evangelists** - proclaimers of the gospel who focus on reaching the lost and mobilize others to share Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:5-8</span>). Evangelists teach disciples how to share their faith effectively, model boldness in witness, and cultivate compassion for the lost.

<!-- p109 -->
4.  **Pastors (shepherds)** - guardians and nurturers who provide protection, guidance, and spiritual oversight for the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>). Pastors teach disciples the importance of fellowship, caring for others, and accountability within the Body of Christ.

<!-- p110 -->
5.  **Teachers** - instructors and trainers who clarify doctrine and help believers grow in understanding and application of God’s Word (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 2:15</span>). Teachers enable disciples to study Scripture deeply, apply it to daily life, and teach others effectively.

<!-- p111 -->
## Leadership vs. Gifts

<!-- p112 -->
A commonly misunderstood aspect of leadership in the church is viewing leadership authority and responsibility as separate from specific gifts.

<!-- p113 -->
This confusion revolves around our use of words like teacher, pastor, prophet, apostle, evangelist. These words are gifts. In the New Testament church there is no **office** of ‘apostle’. Some do the work of an apostle, having an apostolic gift or calling. Possessing an apostolic gift does not give them authority or responsibility.

<!-- p114 -->
I am an accomplished musician; I have played the saxophone for more than twenty years. I played through school and college and even played semi-professionally for several summers while working with an evangelistic team. Even though I have the skill, gift, and ability to play the saxophone, I do not have the right to come to your church and stand on stage with the worship team.

<!-- p115 -->
Similarly, some people have the gift of being a pastor/shepherd, but that does not make them the local church’s ‘THE PASTOR,’ nor does it make them ‘your’ pastor. The fivefold ministry gifts do not lead the church; leaders (elders and deacons) do. It is very common for leaders to have specific fivefold ministry gifts, but the gift strengthens their leadership; it does not create it. Likewise, those whose fivefold gifts are still developing may serve and also aspire to leadership, but God is interested in character first, then gifts. We all must become mature, able to discern God’s Word, filled with the Spirit, and devoted to good works.

<!-- p116 -->
Every leader and every gift must first be submitted to being a disciple and to being part of the Body.

<!-- p117 -->
Jesus gave specific ministry gifts to equip the saints for the work of ministry. These are different from the motivational gifts (which will be discussed later in this chapter) or the manifestation gifts (which will be discussed in the chapter on baptisms). As disciples grow, the fivefold ministry gifts equip them for service, while elders and deacons provide oversight to ensure sound doctrine and practice.

<!-- p118 -->
## Your Role in the Church

<!-- p119 -->
So far in this chapter we have discussed church leadership and the foundational metaphors for the church. Let us move on to the specific metaphor of the *body* and consider a marathon runner.

<!-- p120 -->
### You are part of the whole

<!-- p121 -->
The church, as the body of Christ, depends on every member working together to fulfill its mission. Just as a body cannot function properly if any part is missing, likewise the church needs every believer to participate actively, grow, and serve. Every member of the body has a very important role. Being part of the body means staying connected. A body can live with limited capacity without a limb, but that limb cannot live once it has been separated from the body. We should be an active part of the church and not be like severed limbs. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:24</span> says that we should gather and not forsake the assembly.

<!-- p122 -->
To truly work together and be effective, you need fellowship! You need to know others, love others, and better yet, even like others! Establish strong relationships and friendships, and set them right when they come under pressure or are hurt. When a joint is injured or swollen, you should try to rest it and/or realign it, so that the joint that has slipped out of place does not break! A broken and unhealed member will eventually be separated, and you cannot remain a part of the body on your own.

<!-- p123 -->
We do not all have the same work. Some of us preach the gospel from the pulpit, some around the table. But we are all called to the ministry of reconciliation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 5:18-19</span>). We have all been included in the priesthood of believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:9</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 1:6</span>). This means that all of us should be able to do the work of ministry by the power of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p124 -->
Toes work with the foot, the foot with the ankles, and so on-each part contributes to the overall mission. Marathon runners must train if they want to finish the race. You must be ready-not just by learning but by putting into practice what you have learned. The church, like a marathon runner, flourishes when every member plays their role in harmony.

<!-- p125 -->
There are many people in your life whom possibly only you can reach. The pastor cannot be the only one to shepherd them, and the evangelist cannot be the only one trying to reach them. God wants to use you by the power of His Holy Spirit so that you reach unbelievers. We all do the work of ministry; the Holy Spirit helps us.

<!-- p126 -->
### You need training

<!-- p127 -->
Believers should train and prepare for their unique responsibilities in the church, like a runner strengthens specific muscles for the race. If you have not trained and jump straight into a marathon, you will not succeed, and not all training is the same; you must be properly prepared for the race. Jesus gave special ministry gifts to equip believers, so that they themselves would not do all the work but would enable each member to serve effectively in their unique role.

<!-- p128 -->
Marathon training brings transformation. You need strength and you need to be changed. It is our responsibility to submit ourselves to God and let Him transform us in renewal (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:1-2</span>). Transformation should lead you toward maturity, so that you can carry your own load and help others. You should be able to bear much fruit. This will be discussed in detail in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p129 -->
Training is difficult; sometimes it even seems unpleasant. At times you may prefer to skip the change you need to go through. But if you want to reach the goal, you must remain on the narrow path. Often you will need admonition, encouragement, or support-all given with patience- (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Thessalonians 5:14</span>).

<!-- p130 -->
Every great marathon runner has a coach who trains them; you too will need a trainer to help you. Certainly you have the Holy Spirit, but He often relies on His coaches, namely, mature men and women of God in the body of Christ, with whom you are called to work together.

<!-- p131 -->
As individuals we must first and foremost be subject to God and, secondly, in this context, subject to a body of believers. In the church, obedience means honoring godly leadership (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 13:17</span>) and submitting to one another in humility (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:21</span>).

<!-- p132 -->
Obedience can be difficult, especially when you have previously experienced broken trust or leadership failure. It does not mean blind obedience or control, but a spirit of reverence and cooperation. Worldly obedience is demanded by those in authority and can lead to humiliation and create resentment. Biblical obedience, which is founded on humility and discernment, strengthens the church and promotes accountability. Be willing to listen to guidance and constructive feedback.

<!-- p133 -->
Now that we have laid a strong foundation-you are a meaningful part of the body, but you need training-let us move on to finding where you fit.

<!-- p134 -->
### Finding your place

<!-- p135 -->
Finding your role in the church involves seeking God’s guidance, understanding your spiritual gifts, and staying actively connected with your church community. It is a journey of prayer, discovery, and growth, in which you discover how God has uniquely shaped you to contribute to His kingdom. It is very important to note that your role in the church will likely change over time and is unlikely to remain the same for your whole life.

<!-- p136 -->
Here is a practical process to help you find your place, presented as stages for clarity, but you will likely repeat the same stage multiple times during your journey with God.

<!-- p137 -->
#### Step 1 - Seek God’s guidance through prayer and the Word

<!-- p138 -->
Begin by **seeking God’s guidance through prayer**. God alone is the ultimate source of your purpose and calling (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="29" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 29:11</span>). Pray regularly about how you can serve in the church, and ask Him to reveal your spiritual gifts and passions. Trust that when you submit your plans to His will, He will direct your steps (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 3:5-6</span>).

<!-- p139 -->
**Study God’s Word** to deepen your understanding of the church’s mission and your role in it. Key passages like 1 Corinthians 12 and <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-12</span> highlight spiritual gifts and the diversity of roles in the body of Christ. Reflect on examples of people in Scripture who discovered and fulfilled their roles, such as Paul, Timothy, and Lydia.

<!-- p140 -->
#### Step 2 - Identify your gifts, passions, and skills

<!-- p141 -->
Identifying your **spiritual gifts** is an essential step. You may find it helpful to take a spiritual gifts assessment. These gifts are given by God for the building up of the Church. In the New Testament, several kinds of gifts are listed:

<!-- p142 -->
- **Motivational gifts** given by God: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:6-8</span>

<!-- p143 -->
- Described after this list.

<!-- p144 -->
- **Fivefold ministry gifts** given by Jesus: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-13</span>

<!-- p145 -->
- Explained earlier in this chapter.

<!-- p146 -->
- **Manifestation gifts** given by the Holy Spirit: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span>

<!-- p147 -->
- Explained in the chapter on baptism.

<!-- p148 -->
**Motivational gifts** show how a believer is shaped in terms of thinking, feeling, and acting. They motivate believers to serve in specific ways that build up the Church. Each gift complements the others so that the Church functions like a unified body with diverse roles (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:4-5</span>). They operate as grace-given abilities (Greek: ***charis** - khar'-ece - **G5463 -** unmerited gift*), meaning they are given by God, not earned or learned. Although the gifts are from God, the person who has a gift can become more skilled in its use.

<!-- p149 -->
The motivational gifts are:

<!-- p150 -->
1.  **Prophecy** - A God-given inclination to declare truth boldly, often emphasizing rebuke and righteousness, and guiding others to align with God's Word.\*

<!-- p151 -->
2.  **Service (Helps)** - Meets practical needs through acts of service and assistance to help others.

<!-- p152 -->
3.  **Teaching** - Clarifies and explains truth so that others understand God's Word and apply it.

<!-- p153 -->
4.  **Exhortation (Encouragement)** - Motivates, encourages, and comforts others to encourage growth and perseverance.

<!-- p154 -->
5.  **Giving** - Generously provides resources to meet needs and advance God’s work, without seeking fame or recognition.

<!-- p155 -->
6.  **Leadership (Administration)** - Organizes and guides others with vision and efficiency to accomplish goals.

<!-- p156 -->
7.  **Mercy** - Shows compassion and care to comfort and support the wounded or those in need.

<!-- p157 -->
\* Prophecy in the context of Romans is understood to be different from prophecy as a manifestation gift of the Holy Spirit (1 Corinthians). The Greek word is the same in both passages, but the context is different. For a deeper study of this distinction, I recommend this study:

<!-- p158 -->
- *The Gift of Prophecy* by Kenneth E. Hagin ([www.rhema.org](http://www.rhema.org))

<!-- p159 -->
<!-- -->

<!-- p160 -->
- *Gifts and Ministries of the Holy Spirit* by Lester Sumrall

<!-- p161 -->
When you identify your gifts, consider your **passions and skills**. God often calls us to serve in areas we are passionate about or in which we are skilled (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 31:2-6</span>). Consider what energizes you in serving God and others, and also think about how your professional skills, hobbies, and life experiences can contribute to service.

<!-- p162 -->
Seek guidance from **leaders and mentors** who can provide wisdom and insight (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 15:22</span>). Meet with a pastor or ministry leader to discuss your areas of interest and ask for honest feedback about where you fit best. Pray about the feedback you receive. Consider joining a discipleship group for training and ongoing support.

<!-- p163 -->
#### Step 3 - Now be faithful

<!-- p164 -->
The Parable of the Talents (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-30" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 25:14-30</span>) reminds us that to be entrusted with greater things, we must be faithful in small things (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 16:10</span>).

<!-- p165 -->
**Get involved and serve** in various ministries so that you can discover where God is calling you. Start with small tasks by volunteering in areas of need within your church and in the ministries it supports. For example:

<!-- p166 -->
- Join the hospitality team, children’s ministry, or outreach programs. Consider leading a discipleship-based group for new believers on this book, *Principles of Faith,* using the group discussion questions and the activities given at the end of the chapter to facilitate conversation.

<!-- p167 -->
- Be willing to step outside your comfort zone to discover new opportunities.

<!-- p168 -->
- Observe where God works through you and where you feel most effective.

<!-- p169 -->
Practice giving. We can give to the church in several important ways, and we are instructed to do so to accomplish the mission.

<!-- p170 -->
- Time - We can give our time and volunteer. When we give our time, we use our labor and our abilities. In this way we can also learn new skills and increase existing abilities.

<!-- p171 -->
- Resources - We can give our resources. These days this usually means giving financially to the church and ministries (not merely the “institution,” but also to those in need in the church). Giving money is useful, because the church can purchase needed resources with it, but people have also gone beyond money and given their material resources as well, for example, extra houses, clothing, vehicles, food, building materials, tools, etc. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="32-35" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 4:32-35</span> we see the example of the early believers’ generous giving, who shared the proceeds from the sale of their extra lands and houses so that ‘no one was in need’.

<!-- p172 -->
Working side by side with others in ministry can reveal your strengths and deepen your understanding of your character. Join small groups or church activities to build relationships. Listen to the burdens and needs of those around you and consider how you can support them. Building relationships within the church connects you to others and helps you understand the needs of the community.

<!-- p173 -->
#### Step 4 - Get help!

<!-- p174 -->
Jesus said that in life we will face trouble! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:33</span>) We are also encouraged not to carry the weight of our burdens alone but to cast them on God who cares for us! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:7</span>)

<!-- p175 -->
Sometimes on our journey we can get injured. Marathon runners, even when they are doing “everything right,” can push their bodies to the point of injury, or they can stumble and fall.

<!-- p176 -->
Not every ‘fall’ is related to sin, though some certainly are; not every weakness is ‘our own fault,’ sometimes others will sin against us. Whatever the cause of our weakness or injury, we need restoration and healing.

<!-- p177 -->
There is great freedom in seeking inner healing and restoration. We are called to cover the delicate parts of the body (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:23</span>) and to restore those who have been caught in transgressions (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:1</span>).

<!-- p178 -->
Much can be said on this subject, and we review the topics of healing in summary in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p179 -->
#### Step 5 - Be patient

<!-- p180 -->
Pay attention to confirmation from God and others. Often، God confirms your calling through fruitfulness, joy, and the affirmation of the people around you. Consider where your efforts have borne fruit and listen to feedback about the impact of your service. This can help identify where you are most effective in serving the body of Christ.

<!-- p181 -->
Be patient and trust God's timing. Discovering your role can take time and may involve trial and error (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 1:6</span>). Be assured that as you remain faithful and open to His guidance، God will lead you. In the meantime، commit to growth and training by attending workshops، Bible studies، and other resources that equip you for ministry،. Do this with the church، stay engaged with the body of Christ to discover your gifting.

<!-- p182 -->
Remember، the church is not a building or an institution؛ it is a living، breathing body made up of people like you. By stepping into your role، you participate in its mission and glorify Christ as its head.
