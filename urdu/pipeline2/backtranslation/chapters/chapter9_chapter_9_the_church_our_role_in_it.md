# Chapter 9 - The Church and Our Role in It

<!-- p1 -->
The Church is the way by which God reveals Himself on the earth-through His people, who do His work. We are called to be His body; we are His hands and feet so that we may fulfill His work and purpose. Without our efforts the harvest will remain in the field and will not be reaped.

<!-- p2 -->
## Who is the Church?

<!-- p3 -->
Church, ***Ekklesia** (eh-klee-SEE-ah - **G1577**),* is derived from two Greek words:

<!-- p4 -->
- **"Ek"**: "Out (of)."

<!-- p5 -->
- **"Kaleo"**: "To call."

<!-- p6 -->
Together, **Ekklesia** literally means "the called out" or "the called-out assembly." In ancient Greek city-states it referred to the assembly of citizens who were summoned for a particular purpose, such as a legislative or judicial meeting. It was a non-religious term for the gathering of people.

<!-- p7 -->
In the New Testament**,**, it is primarily used for the congregation of believers in Jesus Christ. It can refer to:

<!-- p8 -->
- **Local Church**: The specific gathering of believers in a particular place (e.g., the church of Corinth, Philippi, etc.).

<!-- p9 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="1" data-verses="2" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p10 -->
> **<u>1 Corinthians 1:2</u>** (NKJV) "*To the church of God which is in Corinth.*"

<!-- p11 -->
</span>

<!-- p12 -->
- **Universal Church**: The collective body of all believers in every time and place.

<!-- p13 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="fragment" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-verses="18" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p14 -->
> **<u>Matthew 16:18</u>** (NKJV) …*I \[Jesus\] will build My church, and the gates of Hades shall not prevail against it.*

<!-- p15 -->
</span>

<!-- p16 -->
The church is the people, not the building or the place where they meet. **The called-out people**: this reflects the concept that Christians are called out of the world and set apart for God’s purposes (e.g., <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-verses="9" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Peter 2:9</span>) for a specific mission and purpose (the Great Commission). Technically, the church is what you have when two or more believers gather together (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="18" data-verses="20" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 18:20</span>). Generally, the church organizes in the form of regular gatherings, possibly with a set place and time, but wherever two or more people gather in Jesus’ name, there is the church!

<!-- p17 -->
The Church was established by Jesus, and it grew in the first century AD through the labors of the disciples who followed Him. The church that is available to us today in our local congregations around the world, and which we experience, is a direct result of the original work of Christ and His apostles. As believers, we should be connected to a local church for fellowship, provision, equipping for service, and to do good works!

<!-- p18 -->
To help us understand what the church is, the Scriptures give us powerful images that show what its purpose is.

<!-- p19 -->
## Understanding the Church

<!-- p20 -->
Multiple fundamental metaphorical images are used to describe the characteristics of the Church:

<!-- p21 -->
1.  Body

<!-- p22 -->
2.  Bride

<!-- p23 -->
3.  Temple

<!-- p24 -->
4.  Family

<!-- p25 -->
5.  Flock

<!-- p26 -->
6.  Vine and Branches

<!-- p27 -->
Each analogy highlights a unique aspect of the Church; however, together they reveal the Church’s identity as a unified and diverse community under Christ’s leadership.

<!-- p28 -->
### Body

<!-- p29 -->
The Church is often called the **body of Christ**, which highlights both its unity and diversity. With Christ as the head, the Church functions like a living organism in which every believer plays an essential and potentially diverse role. Each member receives their unique gift and, according to their responsibility, contributes to the health and mission of the body. This image emphasizes the mutual interdependence of believers, especially that the body must be joined together in order to function, and that our practice should be cooperative rather than competitive. When each part works together, the body grows and flourishes in unity and love (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-verses="12-27" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 12:12-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-verses="15-16" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 4:15-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-verses="18" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Colossians 1:18</span>).

<!-- p30 -->
### Bride

<!-- p31 -->
The Church is also called the **bride of Christ**, which shows a deep and loving relationship with Jesus. Just as a bride is cherished and prepared for her husband, so the Church is sanctified and cleansed by Christ’s sacrificial love. This metaphor encapsulates the devotion and fidelity expected of the Church, as well as the profound love Christ has for his people. This imagery also points to the ultimate union of Christ and the Church, which will be celebrated at the marriage supper of the Lamb, where the Church is presented in glory without spot and without blemish (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-verses="25-27" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 5:25-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="REV" data-chapter="19" data-verses="7-9" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Revelation 19:7-9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="REV" data-chapter="21" data-verses="2" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Revelation 21:2</span>).

<!-- p32 -->
### Temple

<!-- p33 -->
The Church is called the **temple of God**, that is, the dwelling place of his Spirit. Believers are called living stones being built together on the foundation of the apostles and prophets, with Christ himself as the cornerstone. This holy building is a sign of God’s presence among his people and that they are a community of worship and prayer. This depiction of the temple underscores the importance of holiness and the Church’s corporate purpose of glorifying God (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="3" data-verses="16-17" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 3:16-17</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-verses="19-22" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 2:19-22</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-verses="4-5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Peter 2:4-5</span>).

<!-- p34 -->
### Family

<!-- p35 -->
The Church is called the **family of God**, which highlights relational unity and care among its members. Through the Spirit of adoption, believers are made children of God and members of his household. This family imagery underscores the love, commitment, and shared inheritance believers have in Christ. It also reflects the call that we treat one another as brothers and sisters of God’s family with compassion and humility (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-verses="10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Galatians 6:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-verses="19" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 2:19</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-verses="15-17" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 8:15-17</span>). Moreover, the family concept clarifies how members of the Church become mature: that they be taught and nurtured by spiritual fathers and mothers (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-verses="1-2" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Timothy 5:1-2</span>), so that we may grow from newborns who drink the pure milk of the word into those who, as mature, can eat the solid food of the word (Hebrews 5).

<!-- p36 -->
### Flock

<!-- p37 -->
The Church is also called a **flock**, that is, sheep under the care of Jesus the Good Shepherd. This image shows the Church’s dependence on Christ for guidance, provision, and protection. It also highlights the responsibility of church leaders to watch over God’s flock faithfully as under-shepherds, reflecting Christ’s example of love and care. This depiction of the flock points to trust, obedience, and the close relationship between the great Shepherd and his sheep (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-verses="14-16" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 10:14-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-verses="2" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Peter 5:2</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-verses="28" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 20:28</span>).

<!-- p38 -->
### Vine and Branches

<!-- p39 -->
The Church is also described as the **Vine and the branches**, which shows the essential connection between believers and Christ. Jesus is the true vine, and believers are the branches who abide in him to bear fruit. This metaphor emphasizes dependence on Christ for spiritual life, growth, and fruitfulness. Without him believers can do nothing, but by abiding in him they glorify God and fulfill their purpose. Jesus prunes us so that we may bear more fruit in our lives (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-verses="1-5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 15:1-5</span>).

<!-- p40 -->
## The Church's Roles

<!-- p41 -->
The mission of the Church, like the role of the Holy Spirit, can be understood through three dimensions: upward, inward, and outward, each reflecting a unique aspect of its calling in God's plan. These dimensions guide the Church in fulfilling its role as a worshiping, edifying, and missionary community.

<!-- p42 -->
### Upward: Worship and Glorifying God

<!-- p43 -->
The first purpose of the Church is to worship God and glorify Him. Corporate worship, where believers gather together to exalt God, is a visible reflection of His glory and a response to His majesty. As the psalmist proclaims, “*Worship the Lord in the beauty of holiness; let all the earth tremble before Him*” (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="96" data-verses="9" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Psalm 96:9</span>). Beyond corporate worship, the Church is called to live lives of obedience and sacrifice, presenting themselves as “living sacrifices,” which is a spiritual act of worship (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-verses="1-2" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 12:1-2</span>). This upward focus keeps the Church centered on the glory of God and aligns its priorities with His will.

<!-- p44 -->
### Inward: Building Up the Body

<!-- p45 -->
The Great Commission instructs us to ‘*teach them to obey*’; this command goes far beyond formal instructional methods such as this book. Discipleship (discussed further in the chapter on discipleship) is to shape the follower into likeness through training, like parents raising their child. Good parents do not hand an encyclopedia to a five-year-old and challenge them to figure it out on their own! Rather, they model, show, correct, guide, nurture, love, care, and shepherd.

<!-- p46 -->
Parents sacrifice for their children because they love them, because they are part of the same body, because they share the same blood. As members of Christ’s body and family, we share in the same body; we share in the same blood. The Lord’s Supper, through its symbols, reminds us that through Christ’s sacrifice we have been joined to one another. The practice of the Supper should be a point of unity, reminding us that we all need redemption, and that salvation is from God alone. In this unity we see that no part of the body is greater than another, except our head, Christ.

<!-- p47 -->
### Outward: Reaching the World

<!-- p48 -->
The most well-known part of the Church’s mission extends outward to reach the world with the gospel. Jesus’ Great Commission calls His followers to proclaim the good news and make disciples of all nations (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-verses="19-20" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 28:19-20</span>). Empowered by the Holy Spirit, the Church is to be His witness to the ends of the earth (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-verses="8" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 1:8</span>).

<!-- p49 -->
Jesus is the light of the world (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="8" data-verses="12" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 8:12</span>). Light shines from its source outward in every direction. His light shines through His expressions: the Word, His Spirit, and us, His body. We are the light of the world; in our lives we are to spread His light into our dark worlds and display such good works as glorify God (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="5" data-verses="14-16" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 5:14-16</span>). Practically, this means the Church is called to speak up for justice and mercy, both spiritually and physically. As James says, our pure religion is to care for the vulnerable, the orphans, and the widows (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-verses="27" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">James 1:27</span>).

<!-- p50 -->
## Church Leadership

<!-- p51 -->
Across Christianity there is a wide variety of denominations, meetings, gatherings, and associations. There are very few universal principles for church leadership, but those we have are extremely important:

<!-- p52 -->
1.  Jesus is the only head of the Church. All serve Him (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-verses="18" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Colossians 1:18</span>).

<!-- p53 -->
2.  Those who lead have high standards; they are worthy of double honor, because they will incur double judgment (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-verses="17" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Timothy 5:17</span>).

<!-- p54 -->
1.  They must be mature, free from arrogance, and accountable (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-verses="1-13" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Timothy 3:1-13</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="1" data-verses="5-9" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Titus 1:5-9</span>).

<!-- p55 -->
2.  They must be servants of all (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="10" data-verses="42-45" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Mark 10:42-45</span>).

<!-- p56 -->
3.  They must devote themselves to the word and to prayer (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-verses="4" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 6:4</span>).

<!-- p57 -->
4.  They are responsible for equipping the body for the work of ministry.

<!-- p58 -->
3.  There are no greater or lesser members of the body. The verses that support this are:

<!-- p59 -->
1.  There should be no favoritism between rich and poor (James 2).

<!-- p60 -->
2. There is no division between Jew and Gentile, slave or free, male or female (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="3" data-verses="27-28" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Galatians 3:27-28</span>).

<!-- p61 -->
Apart from leadership rules، the structure of the church is important، but much of it is situational and، rather than being universally prescribed، is a matter of tradition۔ “<u>We’ve always done it this way!</u>” is a popular phrase؛ if you go far enough back، there was usually a good reason that they adopted that method۔ It is usually neither good nor bad، but a matter of taste، needs، and availability۔

<!-- p62 -->
A helpful example of tradition being the basis of structure is family mealtime۔ The most important part of family mealtime is that everyone is fed and the food is nourishing۔ Variations are countless: what is eaten، how it is eaten، how you season your food، etc۔ Different combinations are generally acceptable and are mostly a matter of taste and preference۔

<!-- p63 -->
The needs and capacities of a church with 10,000 members differ from those of a church with 50 members۔ The pattern established in the book of Acts and the New Testament collection was the model of elders and deacons۔ Although leadership structures may appear different among churches and denominations، ultimately this model represents the minimally viable leadership structure for the church۔ Let’s look at these leadership positions and how they are selected۔

<!-- p64 -->
### Elders (Overseers)

<!-- p65 -->
Roles and responsibilities:

<!-- p66 -->
- **Spiritual leadership** - Provide oversight، guidance، and shepherding care for the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-verses="28" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 20:28</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-verses="2" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Peter 5:2</span>).

<!-- p67 -->
- **Teaching and preaching** - Responsible for teaching sound doctrine and guarding against false teaching (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-verses="17" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Timothy 5:17</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="1" data-verses="9" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Titus 1:9</span>).

<!-- p68 -->
- **Shepherding the flock** - Caring for the spiritual needs of the congregation، setting an example of humility and service (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-verses="2-3" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Peter 5:2-3</span>).

<!-- p69 -->
- **Protecting the church** - Serving as watchmen، guarding the church against false teaching and division (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-verses="29-31" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 20:29-31</span>).

<!-- p70 -->
- **Equipping the saints** - Training and outfitting believers for works of service and discipleship (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-verses="11-12" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 4:11-12</span>).

<!-- p71 -->
Selection process:

<!-- p72 -->
- **Appointed in every church** - Elders were often appointed by the apostles or their delegates through prayer and fasting (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="14" data-verses="23" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 14:23</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="1" data-verses="5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Titus 1:5</span>).

<!-- p73 -->
- **Confirmed by the Holy Spirit** - Their appointment was understood as the Spirit’s guidance، recognizing God’s call and gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-verses="28" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 20:28</span>).

<!-- p74 -->
- **Tested and approved** - Candidates had to demonstrate maturity and faithfulness before being entrusted with leadership responsibility (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-verses="10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Timothy 3:10</span>). They cannot be new converts! ((<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-verses="6" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Timothy 3:6</span>)

<!-- p75 -->
### Deacons (Servants)

<!-- p76 -->
Roles and responsibilities:

<!-- p77 -->
- **Practical service** - Focus on meeting physical needs in the church، so that the elders can devote themselves to prayer and teaching (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-verses="1-4" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 6:1-4</span>).

<!-- p78 -->
- **Supportive service** - Assist with hospitality، care for the needy، and administrative matters (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-verses="3" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 6:3</span>).

<!-- p79 -->
- **Promoting unity** - Address issues of fairness and care to prevent division (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-verses="1-7" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 6:1-7</span>).

<!-- p80 -->
- **Examples of faith** - They become examples in humility, faith, and service (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-verses="13" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Timothy 3:13</span>).

<!-- p81 -->
Selection process (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-verses="1-6" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 6:1-6</span>):

<!-- p82 -->
- **Recognized by the church** - Chosen on the basis of their reputation and spiritual character۔

<!-- p83 -->
- **Approved by the leaders** - The apostles examined the selected deacons through prayer and the laying on of hands and appointed them۔

<!-- p84 -->
- **Confirmed in service** - Before being formally appointed, the deacons were first tested to prove their faithfulness (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-verses="10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Timothy 3:10</span>).

<!-- p85 -->
### Qualifications for leadership

<!-- p86 -->
#### Qualifications for both elders and deacons:

<!-- p87 -->
- Moral character - Both should be blameless, dignified, and demonstrate self-control (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-verses="2" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Timothy 3:2</span>, 8).

<!-- p88 -->
- Family life - Both must be faithful in marriage (“husband of one wife”) and manage their households well (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-verses="4-5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Timothy 3:4-5</span>, 12).

<!-- p89 -->
- Spiritual maturity - Both should be tested and proven before appointment (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-verses="6" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Timothy 3:6</span>, 10).

<!-- p90 -->
- Reputation - Both need a good reputation inside and outside the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-type="ref" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-verses="7" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Timothy 3:7</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
- Faith and integrity - Both must hold the faith with a clear conscience and practice their beliefs (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-verses="9" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Timothy 3:9</span>).

<!-- p92 -->
### Model structures

<!-- p93 -->
In light of what we have learned so far about leadership rules and structures, we will look at two examples of church structure found in the New Testament, Jerusalem and Antioch۔

<!-- p94 -->
### The church in Jerusalem

<!-- p95 -->
The leadership of the church in Jerusalem was primarily in the hands of the **apostles**, who focused on teaching, prayer, and spiritual oversight (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-verses="42" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 2:42</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-verses="4" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 6:4</span>)۔ As the community grew, the need for administrative structure became evident, especially to handle practical matters such as care for widows and the distribution of resources۔ This led to the appointment of **deacons** (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-verses="1-7" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 6:1-7</span>)۔ These individuals, who were chosen for their spiritual maturity and good reputation, took on practical ministries so that the apostles could focus on their spiritual responsibilities۔ Over time, **elders** also emerged as leaders in the church in Jerusalem۔ For example, during the Jerusalem council (Acts 15), both apostles and elders participated in decision-making, which reflects a shared leadership structure۔

<!-- p96 -->
### The church in Antioch

<!-- p97 -->
The leadership structure of the church in Antioch was more decentralized and diverse compared to Jerusalem۔ Its leadership was in the hands of a group of **prophets and teachers**, including Barnabas, Saul (Paul), Simeon, Lucius, and Manaen (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="13" data-verses="1" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 13:1</span>)۔ The church in Antioch was much more inclined toward reaching Gentiles, whereas the church in Jerusalem reflected its Jewish roots more۔ Although the New Testament does not explicitly mention deacons in Antioch, in view of the church’s growing size and its involvement in sending relief to Jerusalem, it is likely that practical responsibilities were present (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="11" data-verses="29-30" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 11:29-30</span>)۔ The church in Antioch became a model for appointing missionaries, where the leaders prayed, fasted, and laid hands on Paul and Barnabas and sent them (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="13" data-verses="2-3" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 13:2-3</span>)۔ This reflects a structure that balanced spiritual oversight (prophets and teachers) with evangelistic outreach and practical support۔

<!-- p98 -->
In contrast to Paul’s corrections to the Corinthians and Galatians, the Scriptures do not criticize the leadership structures of Jerusalem or Antioch۔ This points to the fact that these churches, under the guidance of the Holy Spirit, were free to adapt their assemblies to their unique needs and circumstances while remaining within the scope of sound teaching and apostolic doctrine (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-verses="42" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 2:42</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-verses="16-17" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>)۔

<!-- p99 -->
In these references some terms are used that we have not yet defined and studied: apostle, prophet, evangelist۔ These gifts are part of the fivefold ministry that Jesus gave to the church۔

<!-- p100 -->
### Fivefold Ministry Gifts

<!-- p101 -->
Jesus established five distinct ministry gifts, often called the Fivefold Ministry Gifts, to equip believers for service and to strengthen the body of Christ.

<!-- p102 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-verses="11-13" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p103 -->
> **<u>Ephesians 4:11-13</u>** (NKJV) **11** *And He Himself gave some to be apostles, some prophets, some evangelists, and some pastors and teachers,* ***12** *for the equipping of the saints for the work of ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ,* ***13** *till we all come to the unity of the faith and of the knowledge of the Son of God, to a perfect man, to the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ;*

<!-- p104 -->
</span>

<!-- p105 -->
The five gifts given to equip the saints for the work of ministry are:

<!-- p106 -->
1.  **Apostles** - leaders and visionaries who lay foundations، plant churches، and advance the kingdom of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-verses="20" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 2:20</span>)۔ Apostles model boldness and faith by showing how to step into new territories and make spiritual advances.

<!-- p107 -->
2.  **Prophets** - messengers and guides who declare God’s truth، bringing encouragement، edification، and guidance (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-verses="3" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>)۔ Prophets stir faith، invite others to align with God’s word، and model reliance on hearing God’s voice.

<!-- p108 -->
3.  **Evangelists** - heralds of the gospel who focus on reaching the lost and mobilize others to share Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-verses="5-8" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 8:5-8</span>)۔ Evangelists train disciples to share their faith testimony effectively، exemplify boldness in witness، and awaken compassion for the lost.

<!-- p109 -->
4.  **Pastors (Shepherds)** - guardians and nurturers who protect، guide، and provide spiritual oversight for the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-verses="28" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 20:28</span>)۔ Pastors teach disciples the importance of community، caring for others، and accountability within the body of Christ.

<!-- p110 -->
5.  **Teachers** - instructors and trainers who clarify doctrine and help believers grow in understanding and applying God’s word (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="2" data-verses="15" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">2 Timothy 2:15</span>)۔ Teachers enable disciples to study the Scriptures deeply، apply them to daily life، and teach others effectively.

<!-- p111 -->
## Leadership versus Gift

<!-- p112 -->
A commonly misunderstood aspect of leadership in the church is separating the authority and responsibility of leadership from specific gifts.

<!-- p113 -->
This confusion revolves around our use of words like teacher، pastor، prophet، apostle، evangelist. These words are gifts. In the New Testament church there is no office/position of ‘apostle’. Some people do the work of an apostle، who have an apostolic gift or calling. Having the gift of apostle does not give them authority or responsibility.

<!-- p114 -->
I am an expert musician; I have been playing the saxophone for more than twenty years. I played throughout school and college and even played semi-professionally for several summers while working with an evangelistic team. Despite this skill، gift، and ability to play the saxophone، I do not have the right to come to your church and stand on stage with the worship team.

<!-- p115 -->
Similarly, some people excel in the gift of being a pastor/shepherd، but that does not make them the ‘actual pastor’ of a local church، nor does it make them ‘your’ pastor. Church leadership is not carried out by the Fivefold Ministry Gifts, but by leaders (elders and deacons). Often، leaders do have particular Fivefold Ministry Gifts، but that gift refines their leadership; it does not create it. Likewise, those who are still in development and who have Fivefold gifts do serve and aspire to leadership، but God is interested first in character, then in gifts. We all must become mature، be able to test God’s word، be filled with the Spirit، and do good works.

<!-- p116 -->
Every leader and every gift must first be subject to being a disciple, being part of the body.

<!-- p117 -->
Jesus gave specific ministry gifts to equip the saints for the work of ministry. These are different from motivational gifts (which will be discussed later in this chapter) or manifestation gifts (which will be discussed in the chapter on baptisms). As disciples grow، the Fivefold Ministry Gifts prepare them for service، while elders and deacons provide oversight to ensure sound teaching and practice.

<!-- p118 -->
## Your Role in the Church

<!-- p119 -->
Thus far in this chapter we have discussed church leadership and the core metaphors of the church. Let’s now consider the specific metaphor of the body and keep a marathon runner in view.

<!-- p120 -->
### You are part of the whole

<!-- p121 -->
The church, which is the body of Christ, depends on every member working together to accomplish its mission. Just as a body cannot function properly if any part is missing, likewise the church needs every believer to participate actively, keep growing, and serve. Every member of the body has a very important role. Being part of the body means being connected. The body can live in a weakened state without a limb, but that limb itself cannot live once separated from the body. We should remain an active part of the church and not be cut off and separated. <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-verses="24" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 10:24</span> says that we should gather and not forsake the assembly.

<!-- p122 -->
To truly work and walk together you need fellowship! You need to know others, love others, and as much as possible even like others! Build strong relationships and friendships, and set them right when they are under pressure or injured. When a joint is hurt or swollen, you have to do the hard work of resting it and setting it back in place; so that the joint that has slipped out of place does not break! A limb that is broken and does not heal eventually separates, and you cannot remain part of the body on your own.

<!-- p123 -->
We do not all have the same task. Some of us preach the gospel from the stage, and some around the dinner table. But we are all called to be ministers of reconciliation (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="5" data-verses="18-19" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">2 Corinthians 5:18-19</span>). We have all been included in the priesthood of believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-verses="9" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Peter 2:9</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-verses="6" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Revelation 1:6</span>). This means that all of us should be able to do works of service by the power of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p124 -->
Fingers with the feet, feet with the ankles, and so on - each part contributes to the overall mission. Marathon runners must train if they want to finish the race. You must be prepared - not only by learning, but by actually putting into practice what you have learned. The church, like a marathon runner, flourishes when every member plays their part in unity.

<!-- p125 -->
There are many people in your life whom perhaps only you can reach. The pastor alone cannot try to handle them, and the evangelist alone cannot try to reach them. God wants to use you by the power of His Holy Spirit so that you may reach the unbelievers. We all do the work of ministry; the Holy Spirit helps us.

<!-- p126 -->
### You need training

<!-- p127 -->
Believers should receive training and prepare for their unique roles in the church, just as a runner strengthens specific muscles for the race. If you jump into a marathon without training you will not succeed, and not all training is the same; you must be properly prepared for the race. Jesus has given special ministry gifts to equip believers, not so that they themselves do all the work, but so that each member may be enabled to serve effectively in their unique role.

<!-- p128 -->
Training for a marathon brings transformation. You need strength and change. It is our responsibility to surrender ourselves to God and allow Him to transform us in renewal (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-verses="1-2" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 12:1-2</span>). This transformation should lead you toward maturity, so that you can carry your own burdens and become a support to others. You should be able to bear much fruit. This will be discussed in detail in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p129 -->
Training is hard; sometimes it is uncomfortable. At times you may want to give up the very change you have to go through. But if you want to reach the goal you must stay on the narrow path. Sometimes you may need warning, encouragement, or help - these are all offered with patience (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1TH" data-chapter="5" data-verses="14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Thessalonians 5:14</span>).

<!-- p130 -->
Every great marathon runner has a coach to train them; you too will need a mentor to help you. Of course you have the Holy Spirit, but He often relies on His coaches—that is, God’s mature men and women in the body of Christ—with whom you are called to work together.

<!-- p131 -->
As individuals we should first surrender ourselves before God and then, in that context, be subject to a congregation of believers. Obedience in the church means honoring God-fearing leadership (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="13" data-verses="17" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 13:17</span>) and being submissive to one another in humility (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-verses="21" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 5:21</span>).

<!-- p132 -->
Obedience can be difficult, especially when you have previously experienced broken trust or failed leadership. It does not mean blind obedience or control, but rather the expression of a spirit of honor and cooperation. Worldly obedience is demanded by those in authority and can lead to humiliation and breed resentment. Biblical obedience, rooted in humility and discernment, strengthens the church and promotes accountability. Be ready to listen to guidance and constructive feedback.

<!-- p133 -->
Now that we have laid a solid foundation - you are a meaningful part of the body, but you need training - let’s see where you fit.

<!-- p134 -->
### Where you fit

<!-- p135 -->
Finding your role in the church involves seeking God’s guidance, understanding your spiritual gifts, and active engagement with your church community. It is a journey of prayer, seeking, and growth in which you discover how God has uniquely equipped you to contribute to His kingdom. It is very important to note that your role in the church will likely change over time and is unlikely to remain the same for your whole life.

<!-- p136 -->
Here is a practical process that will help you find your place; it is presented in stages for clarity, but in your journey with God you will likely repeat the same stage many times.

<!-- p137 -->
#### First step - Seek God’s guidance through prayer and the Word

<!-- p138 -->
Start by **seeking God’s guidance through prayer**. God is the ultimate source of your purpose and calling (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JER" data-chapter="29" data-verses="11" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Jeremiah 29:11</span>). Pray regularly for clarity about how you can serve in the church; ask Him to reveal your spiritual gifts and passions. Trust that when you commit your plans to His will, He will direct your steps (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="3" data-verses="5-6" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Proverbs 3:5-6</span>).

<!-- p139 -->
**Study God’s Word** to deepen your understanding of the church’s mission and your role in it. Key passages such as 1 Corinthians 12 and <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-verses="11-12" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 4:11-12</span> highlight spiritual gifts and the diversity of roles in the body of Christ. Reflect on the examples of people in Scripture who discovered and fulfilled their roles, such as Paul, Timothy, and Lydia.

<!-- p140 -->
#### Step 2 - Identify your gifts, passions, and skills

<!-- p141 -->
Identifying your **spiritual gifts** is an important step. Taking a spiritual gifts assessment may be helpful for you. These gifts are given by God for the building up of the church. In the New Testament, several kinds of gifts are listed:

<!-- p142 -->
- **Motivational gifts** given by God: <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-verses="6-8" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 12:6-8</span>

<!-- p143 -->
- This is discussed after this list.

<!-- p144 -->
- The fivefold ministry gifts that Jesus gave: <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-verses="11-13" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 4:11-13</span>

<!-- p145 -->
- This was discussed earlier in this chapter.

<!-- p146 -->
- Manifestation gifts given by the Holy Spirit: <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-verses="8-10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span>

<!-- p147 -->
- This is discussed in the baptism chapter.

<!-- p148 -->
**Motivational gifts** reflect how a believer is wired to think, feel, and act. They motivate believers to serve in specific ways for the building up of the church. Each gift complements the others, ensuring that the church functions as a unified body with diverse roles (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-verses="4-5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 12:4-5</span>). They function as grace-given abilities (Greek: ***charis** - khar'-ece - **G5463 -** undeserved gift), meaning they are given by God, not earned or taught. Although the gifts come from God, the person who has a gift can still become more skillful in its use.

<!-- p149 -->
The motivational gifts are:

<!-- p150 -->
1.  **Prophecy** - A God-given bent to boldly declare truth, often with a focus on reproof and righteousness, guiding others to align with God’s Word.\*

<!-- p151 -->
2.  **Serving (Helps)** - Meets practical needs through acts of service and assistance to help others.

<!-- p152 -->
3.  **Teaching** - Explains and interprets truth, helping others understand and apply God’s Word.

<!-- p153 -->
4.  **Exhortation (Encouragement)** - Motivates, encourages, and comforts others to promote growth and perseverance.

<!-- p154 -->
5.  **Giving** - Generously supplies resources to meet needs and advance God’s work, without seeking recognition.

<!-- p155 -->
6.  **Leadership (Administration)** - Organizes and guides others to achieve goals with insight and efficiency.

<!-- p156 -->
7.  **Mercy** - Shows compassion and care to comfort and help those who are hurting or in need.

<!-- p157 -->
\* Prophecy as used in the Romans context is understood differently from prophecy as a manifestation gift of the Holy Spirit (1 Corinthians). The Greek word is the same in both places, but the context is different. For a deeper study of this distinction, I recommend reading:

<!-- p158 -->
- *The Gift of Prophecy* by Kenneth E. Hagin ([www.rhema.org](http://www.rhema.org))

<!-- p159 -->
<!-- -->

<!-- p160 -->
- *Gifts and Ministries of the Holy Spirit* by Lester Sumrall

<!-- p161 -->
When you identify your gifts, consider your interests and skills. God often calls us to serve in areas we are passionate about or in which we are skilled (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="31" data-verses="2-6" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Exodus 31:2-6</span>). Consider what excites you in serving God and others, and think about how your professional skills, hobbies, and life experiences can be helpful in ministry.

<!-- p162 -->
Seek guidance from leaders and mentors who can provide wisdom and insight (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="15" data-verses="22" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Proverbs 15:22</span>). Meet with a pastor or ministry leader to discuss your interests and ask for honest feedback about where you are most well-suited. Pray about the feedback you receive. Consider joining a discipleship group for training and ongoing support.

<!-- p163 -->
#### Step 3 - Be faithful now

<!-- p164 -->
The Parable of the Talents (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-verses="14-30" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 25:14-30</span>) reminds us that to be trustworthy in greater things we must be faithful in small things (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="16" data-verses="10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Luke 16:10</span>).

<!-- p165 -->
**Get involved and serve** in different ministry areas to learn where God is calling you. Start small; volunteer in areas of need in your church and in the ministries it supports. For example:

<!-- p166 -->
- Join the hospitality team, children’s ministry, or outreach programs. Consider leading a discipleship-based group for new believers on this book *Principles of Faith,* using the group discussion questions and the activities at the end of the chapter to foster conversation.

<!-- p167 -->
- Be willing to step outside your comfort zone to discover new opportunities.

<!-- p168 -->
- Observe where God works through you and where you feel most effective.

<!-- p169 -->
Practice giving. We can give to the church in many important ways, and we are instructed to do so to fulfill the mission.

<!-- p170 -->
- Time - We can give our time and serve as volunteers. When we give our time, we use our labor and our abilities. This can also be an excellent way to learn new skills and increase existing ones.

<!-- p171 -->
- Resources - We can give our resources. Nowadays this generally means giving financially to the church and ministries (not just to the “institution,” but also to those in need in the church). Giving money is useful because the church can purchase needed resources with it, but people have given more than money—their material possessions as well, such as extra homes, clothing, vehicles, food, building materials, tools, etc. In <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="4" data-verses="32-35" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 4:32-35</span> we find an example of the early believers’ generous giving, who shared the proceeds from the sale of extra lands and houses and gave in such a way 'so that no one would be in need'.

<!-- p172 -->
Serving together with others can reveal your strengths and deepen your understanding of your character. Join small groups or church activities to build relationships. Listen attentively to the burdens and needs of those around you and consider how you can help them. Building relationships within the church connects you with others and helps you understand the needs of the community.

<!-- p173 -->
#### Step 4 - Get help!

<!-- p174 -->
Jesus said that in life we will have trouble! (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-verses="33" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 16:33</span>) We are encouraged not to carry the weight of our burdens alone but to cast it on the God who cares for us! (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-verses="7" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Peter 5:7</span>)

<!-- p175 -->
Sometimes on our journey we may also get injured. Marathon runners, even if they are doing 'everything right,' can put so much strain on their bodies that they get injured, or they may stumble and fall.

<!-- p176 -->
Not every 'fall' is related to sin, though some are; not every weakness is 'our fault'—sometimes others sin against us. Whatever the cause of our weakness or injury, we are to be restored and healed.

<!-- p177 -->
There is great freedom in seeking inner healing and restoration. We are called to cover the weaker parts of the body (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-verses="23" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 12:23</span>) and also to restore those who have been caught in transgressions (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-verses="1" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Galatians 6:1</span>).

<!-- p178 -->
There is much to be said on this subject, and we study the topics of healing, as an overview, in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p179 -->
#### Step 5 - Be patient

<!-- p180 -->
Pay attention to confirmation that comes from God and from others. Often, God confirms your calling through fruitfulness, joy, and the affirmation of the people around you. Consider where your efforts have borne fruit and listen to the feedback you receive about the impact of your ministry. This can help you recognize where you are most effective in serving the body of Christ.

<!-- p181 -->
Be patient and trust in God’s timing. It may take time to discover your role, and it may involve trial and error (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="1" data-verses="6" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Philippians 1:6</span>). Be confident that as you remain faithful and keep your heart open to His guidance, God will lead you. In the meantime, stay committed to growth and training by participating in workshops, Bible studies, and other resources that prepare you for ministry. Do this with the church; stay connected to the body of Christ to discover your gifts.

<!-- p182 -->
Remember, the church is not a building or an institution; it is a living, breathing body made up of people like you. By stepping into your role, you contribute to its mission and give glory to Christ, who is its head.
