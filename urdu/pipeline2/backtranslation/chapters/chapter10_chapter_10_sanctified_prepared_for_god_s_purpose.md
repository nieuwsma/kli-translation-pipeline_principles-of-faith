# Chapter 10 - Cleansed - Ready for God's Purpose

<!-- p1 -->
In the last two chapters we discussed several important topics. We examined the role and ministry of the Holy Spirit as well as the role and ministry of the Church.

<!-- p2 -->
We also have a role in the Church, and although we know we need training, we also need holiness. To truly be ready for God’s purposes we must be sanctified.

<!-- p3 -->
The definition of **sanctification** is to make something holy for a special purpose. This term is closely related to **dedication**, which means to set something apart for a special purpose.

<!-- p4 -->
Imagine a master sculptor preparing to work on a large block of marble. The master envisions what this raw and uncarved marble can be transformed into through his careful craftsmanship. With a very specific design in mind, he instructs his assistants to set that block of marble apart for a particular design (**dedication**). As he meticulously refines and works the marble, giving it texture, depth, and form, he sanctifies it for that particular design (**sanctifies it**). The act of dedicating the marble for his special project was swift, whereas shaping the marble to its final design requires considerable patience.

<!-- p5 -->
At salvation we were set apart, dedicated to God. Through the process of purifying our souls with the help of the Spirit we are transformed. This very process of purification is what sanctifies us.

<!-- p6 -->
When we received Jesus through salvation, we were born again. We were justified; our sins were forgiven. Our spirit was made new; we became alive in Christ. We were adopted into the family of God and became the true temple of the Holy Spirit. Such an amazing transformation that the Word says, “*There is joy in the presence of the angels of God over one sinner who repents.” (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 15:10</span>).

<!-- p7 -->
Receiving Jesus through salvation (**being justified**) saves our spirit, cleanses us, and makes us acceptable before God so that we may receive His Holy Spirit. However, despite such a great salvation, our souls (our mind, will, and emotions) are still in need of work!

<!-- p8 -->
Was the work of salvation incomplete? No! The work of salvation is effective, but it must continue in the life of the believer through the renewal brought by the Holy Spirit. This process of renewal and transformation is the work of **sanctification**.

<!-- p9 -->
We know there is still work to be done even after conversion, because our painful memories have not been resolved, many of our beliefs remained the same, and many of our habits or rituals did not disappear. Moreover, conversion did not separate our minds from their carnal (fleshly) nature.

<!-- p10 -->
The word ‘carnal’ comes from a Latin root that means ‘body’ or ‘flesh.’ The carnal nature is in each of us from birth; like animal instincts it is present for our survival. The way the carnal nature operates is that it is self-focused, self-inclined, self-centered. Children are the natural example of the carnal nature. Despite their innocence, their nature is fundamentally centered on their wants, desires, and needs; and ‘watch out!’ if you do not meet its every expectation.

<!-- p11 -->
Part of growing up is that we move beyond childish ways, but when pressure comes, we very easily revert to patterns of thinking and living that revolve around self. As James says:

<!-- p12 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p13 -->
> **<u>James 4:1</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Where do wars and fights come from among you? Do they not come from your desires for pleasure that wage war in your members?
>

<!-- p14 -->
</span>

<!-- p15 -->
Paul, in his epistle to the Romans (chapters 6–8), describes this carnal nature in greater detail. He says that this carnal nature keeps compelling him to do what he hates and inclines him to do evil. He describes this inner conflict as a war between his human nature and his God-created spirit.

<!-- p16 -->
This clash between flesh and spirit is present within each one of us, and it is our responsibility to do something about it. If we do not confront our carnal nature, our souls will remain entangled in patterns of spiritual decline, even though our spirits have been redeemed.

<!-- p17 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p18 -->
> **<u>Romans 8:6-8</u>** (NKJV)
>
> For to be carnally minded is death, but to be spiritually minded is life and peace.
>
> Because the carnal mind is enmity against God; for it is not subject to the law of God, nor indeed can be.
>
> So then, those who are in the flesh cannot please God.
>

<!-- p19 -->
</span>

<!-- p20 -->
Paul’s words are both a warning and a promise for us۔ Although we have been born again، we still have a fleshly mind that must be brought under the knowledge of Christ۔ When we do this، we walk in God’s life and peace۔

<!-- p21 -->
Take courage، there is hope for us! We are to walk according to the Spirit and remain under the guidance of the Spirit of God۔ By doing so we will not walk according to the flesh۔

<!-- p22 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="1,13,14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p23 -->
> **<u>Romans 8:1,13,14</u>** (NKJV)
>
> So now there is no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus, that is, those who do not walk according to the flesh but according to the Spirit۔
>
> For if you live according to the flesh you will die؛ but if by the Spirit you put to death the deeds of the body, you will live۔
>
> For as many as are led by the Spirit of God, these are the sons of God۔
>

<!-- p24 -->
</span>

<!-- p25 -->
How do we learn to walk in the Spirit؟ Is this *merely a conscious choice*؟ We learn to walk in the Spirit the same way we naturally learn to walk۔ We are prompted to grow and move forward، so we wrestle، struggle، crawl، and wobble until we can stand on our feet۔ Our earthly parents cannot walk in our place، but they can support us in our walk، encourage، help، and enable us۔

<!-- p26 -->
Likewise، God cannot put our faith into practice for us۔ He will teach us، but we must make the effort۔ As Paul said to the Philippians:

<!-- p27 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="12-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p28 -->
> **<u>Philippians 2:12-13</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Therefore, my beloved, just as you have always obeyed, not only in my presence but now much more in my absence، work out your salvation with fear and trembling؛
>
> for it is God who works in you both to will and to do according to His good pleasure۔
>

<!-- p29 -->
</span>

<!-- p30 -->
We must work out our own salvation۔ This does not mean that we climb onto a wooden cross and are physically crucified، but we must crucify our fleshly nature (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:24</span>)۔ God promises that He will work in us and help us، but we must choose to walk this path، and remain on it as well۔

<!-- p31 -->
It is correct to say that ’we‘ are dealing with our fleshly nature، but it is also correct to say that ’God‘ is dealing with the same۔ The work of the cross (righteousness) was hard for Jesus—He had to lay down His life for our sake— but easy for us، because it is a free gift۔ The work of the Spirit (sanctification) is easy for God—He walks in perfect truth and is willing and eager to dwell in us— but hard for us، because we must crucify our old nature، and our nature is not particularly interested in being crucified۔

<!-- p32 -->
This process is based on mutual cooperation۔ God helps، supports، guides، gives direction، and corrects۔ We listen، obey، follow، and respond۔ The result is growth and transformation۔ This is the pruning about which Jesus said that God will do in us۔ Jesus is the vine، His Father the gardener، and we are the branches:

<!-- p33 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p34 -->
> **<u>John 15:1-4</u>** (NKJV)
>
> "I am the true vine and My Father is the gardener۔
>
> Every branch in Me that does not bear fruit He cuts off؛ and every branch that bears fruit He prunes so that it may bear more fruit۔
>
> You are already clean because of the word which I have spoken to you۔
>
> Abide in Me and I in you۔ As the branch cannot bear fruit by itself unless it abides in the vine, so neither can you، unless you abide in Me۔
>

<!-- p35 -->
</span>

<!-- p36 -->
Pruning is extremely beneficial for a plant۔ Recalling our chapter on repentance، we are called to turn from dead works (that is, works that are no longer bearing fruit) and turn to God۔ The result of turning away from the dead parts of our lives and turning toward God is that the great gardener of our lives is given the authority to cut back branches، improve growth، and produce more spiritual fruit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:2</span>)۔

<!-- p37 -->
Although pruning may feel painful، it reflects God’s loving care as He molds us into vessels for His purpose (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 12:11</span>)۔ Some people may feel a sense of loss or even fear while going through the process of pruning۔ However، God is tenderhearted، and He will not break even a bruised reed (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="42" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 42:3</span>)۔ Grapevines are pruned in their dormant season so that the stress on the plant remains minimal۔ The gardener who tends growth cuts away that old, woody growth that no longer bears fruit as before؛ this allows the vine to focus its energy on the branches with the greatest potential for good fruit۔

<!-- p38 -->
Just as a gardener trims branches to direct growth، so God allows seasons of correction and training to refine faith and character، but His work is based on patience and kindness۔ God prunes those who abide in Him۔ Our focus should be more on abiding in Christ، and less on the work of pruning۔

<!-- p39 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 12:11</span> reminds us that pruning “*produces the peaceful fruit of righteousness*” for those who are trained by it۔

<!-- p40 -->
How are we trained in righteousness? By being transformed through the renewing of our minds.

<!-- p41 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p42 -->
> **<u>Romans 12:1-2</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Therefore, brothers! I plead with you by the mercies of God to present your bodies as a living sacrifice, holy and acceptable to God; this is your reasonable service.
>
> And do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind, that you may test and approve what is the good and acceptable and perfect will of God.
>

<!-- p43 -->
</span>

<!-- p44 -->
Paul lays out two simple conditions for transformation:

<!-- p45 -->
<u>1. Present your bodies before God as a living sacrifice</u>. A **living** sacrifice does not die physically in the act of sacrifice, but since a living sacrifice cannot die, you must keep bringing it to the altar again and again!

<!-- p46 -->
<u>2. Renew your mind!</u> Renewal, based on the Greek word: **Anakainōsis** (*An-ak-ah'-ee-no-sis -* **G342**), means renewal or renovation, which focuses on a continuous process of inner change and growth.

<!-- p47 -->
This renewal gives birth to transformation. The Greek word for transformation: **Metamorphoō** (*met-am-or-fo'-o -* **G3339**) means to change into another form; to transform or transfigure, which emphasizes complete change. From Metamorphoō comes the English word metamorphosis, the process by which caterpillars become butterflies.

<!-- p48 -->
God wants to fundamentally change our minds and patterns of thinking. His desire is that we not be conformed to the ways of the world, but that we know what God’s good (beneficial), acceptable (acceptable), and perfect (complete & without lack) will is.

<!-- p49 -->
The requirements for transformation really are that simple, **though it requires discipline**!

<!-- p50 -->
Our battle against the flesh will not be won by our own flesh, but by the preparation God provides for the battle of the mind. His Word (the sword of truth) has the power to correct every imagination and proud thought, and to take captive every thought that is against obedience to God.

<!-- p51 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="3-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p52 -->
> **<u>2 Corinthians 10:3-6</u>** (NKJV)
>
> For though we walk in the flesh, we do not wage war according to the flesh.
>
> For the weapons of our warfare are not carnal but mighty in God for pulling down strongholds,
>
> casting down arguments and every high thing that exalts itself against the knowledge of God, and bringing every thought into captivity to the obedience of Christ,
>
> and being ready to punish all disobedience when your obedience is fulfilled.
>

<!-- p53 -->
</span>

<!-- p54 -->
When authorities arrest someone, they put shackles on him and place him in a jail cell. If that person escapes custody, they do not look the other way; they deploy extraordinary resources and do not rest until they have brought that person back under control. We should approach our battle in the mind with the same zeal!

<!-- p55 -->
We are made new through obedience to and experience of the truth. Jesus said that if we abide in His word, we will be His disciples, we will experience (<u>know</u>) the truth, and it will set us free (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-32" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 8:31-32</span>)!

<!-- p56 -->
Experiencing the truth is different from knowledge of the truth. The Greek word for experience is **Ginōskō** (Ghin-oce'-ko **- G1097**), which means to know through personal experience or deep understanding; it carries the sense of gradual learning.

<!-- p57 -->
We are not transformed by our intellectual knowledge of the truth, nor are our minds renewed by it; rather, our personal experience of the truth changes us. We are sanctified by His truth! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="17" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 17:17</span>).

## How to renew your mind

<!-- p59 -->
Renewing our minds is not a one-size-fits-all process-it depends on which part of the mind we are talking about! God made our minds with astonishing complexity, including several different memory systems, each serving a specific purpose. Some operate automatically in the background, while others require focused, intentional thinking. These systems are gifts from God so that we can survive, grow, and flourish, but when they are misused or left unrenewed they can also lead us astray. Understanding how these systems work gives us insight into how we can cooperate with God’s process of transformation.

<!-- p60 -->
In the next section, I will describe each system of memory, their purposes and functions, some practical steps to refresh this aspect of your mind, as well as resources for ongoing practice and study.

<!-- p61 -->
### **Memory Systems and How to Renew Them**

<!-- p62 -->
#### **1. Explicit Memory (Declarative Memory)**

<!-- p63 -->
**Purpose:** Stores facts, events, and experiences that we can consciously recall. It is divided into two types-**Semantic** and **Episodic** memory.

<!-- p64 -->
**Semantic memory - facts and knowledge.** Semantic memory is where we store facts, definitions, and general information-such as historical dates, Bible verses, and practical information. It helps us predict outcomes, make decisions, and avoid danger (for example, recognizing poisonous plants). Although it gives us stability and order, it can also lead to rigidity-overreliance on rules instead of adapting to unexpected situations.

<!-- p65 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p66 -->
- **Study the Scriptures** to shape our worldview (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="119" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="105" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 119:105</span>).

<!-- p67 -->
- **Memorize truths** and creeds to stand firm in faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 3:16</span>).

<!-- p68 -->
- **Read Christian books** such as *"Renewing the Mind" by Casey Treat* or *"Battlefield of the Mind" by Joyce Meyer* to cultivate godly patterns of thinking.

<!-- p69 -->
**Episodic memory - personal experiences.** Episodic memory stores our personal experiences, linking them to specific times and places-such as a wedding day or a traumatic event. These memories provide context, helping us learn from the past and build relationships. However, episodic memory can be vulnerable to emotional bias and distortion. Past traumas can create fearful associations, making it difficult to move forward.

<!-- p70 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p71 -->
- **Reflect and confess** so that hidden pain comes into God’s light (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 1:9</span>).

<!-- p72 -->
- **Reframe past experiences** in the light of God’s grace (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:28</span>).

<!-- p73 -->
- **Seek inner healing** through prayer and counseling (*“Your Pain is Showing” by Larry F. Low, "The Bondage Breaker" by Neil T. Anderson*, *"Emotionally Healthy Spirituality" by Peter Scazzero*).

<!-- p74 -->
#### **2. Implicit Memory (Non-declarative Memory)**

<!-- p75 -->
**Purpose:** Unconscious memory used for habits, skills, and emotional associations. It operates automatically, helping us respond quickly to situations. It is divided into **procedural memory** and **emotional conditioning**.

<!-- p76 -->
**Procedural memory - skills and habits.** Procedural memory enables us to perform tasks without conscious thought-such as driving, typing, or playing a musical instrument. It is efficient, saving our mental energy and time. However, bad habits and negative patterns can also become deeply ingrained and are difficult to break.

<!-- p77 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p78 -->
- **Practice spiritual disciplines** such as prayer and fasting to form godly habits (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 5:14</span>).

<!-- p79 -->
- **Train yourself in godliness** through repetition and obedience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 4:7</span>).

<!-- p80 -->
- **Read books like** *"Atomic Habits" by James Clear* (practical) and *"Spiritual Disciplines for the Christian Life" by Donald Whitney* (biblical) to establish habits that honor God.

<!-- p81 -->
**Emotional conditioning - emotional association.** Emotional conditioning links sensory cues (such as smells or sounds) to feelings, and triggers responses even before we consciously understand them. This system protects us but can also produce irrational fears or emotional reactions when past experiences are negative.

<!-- p82 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p83 -->
- **Pray for healing from past wounds** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="41" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 41:10</span>).

<!-- p84 -->
- **Practice forgiveness** and let go of bitterness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-32" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:31-32</span>).

<!-- p85 -->
- **Replace fear with God’s promises** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 1:7</span>).

<!-- p86 -->
- **Seek guidance from books like** *“Your Pain is Showing” by Larry F. Low, "The Bondage Breaker" by Neil T. Anderson*, “*Soul Care" by Rob Reimer* to overcome emotional triggers.

<!-- p87 -->
#### **3. Working Memory (short-term memory)**

<!-- p88 -->
**Purpose:** Provides temporary storage for problem-solving and decision-making. It helps us stay focused, hold information for a short time, and process tasks—like remembering instructions or making quick decisions. Although it is powerful, it is also limited, vulnerable to distraction, and easily overloaded, especially under stress.

<!-- p89 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p90 -->
- **Focus on and meditate on God’s Word daily** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 1:2</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
- **Renew your thoughts through intentional thinking** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:2</span>).

<!-- p92 -->
- **Practice mindfulness** and reduce mental clutter (*"Habits of Grace" by David Mathis*).

<!-- p93 -->
- **Study** *"The Celebration of Discipline" by Richard Foster* to sharpen focus through spiritual disciplines.

<!-- p94 -->
## Conclusion

<!-- p95 -->
Renewing the mind is not a quick fix—it is a lifelong process of gradual transformation. Each memory system plays a role in how we think, feel, and act. By intentionally partnering with the Holy Spirit, applying Scripture, and cultivating godly habits, we can conform our minds to God’s truth. Whether dealing with past wounds, learning new habits, or staying present in the moment, God has given us both **spiritual tools** and **practical wisdom** so that we can walk in renewal and freedom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 10:5</span>).

<!-- p96 -->
Our world is filled with trouble, affliction, and trial. Through systematic renewal of the mind, our lives are transformed, and we can live a free life for God that is rich with the fruit of His Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-25</span>).
