# Chapter 10 - Sanctified - Prepared for God's Purpose

<!-- p1 -->
In the past two chapters we have discussed several important topics. We examined the role and ministry of the Holy Spirit as well as the role and ministry of the church.

<!-- p2 -->
We have a part in the church, and although we know we need training, we also need holiness. To be truly ready for God’s purposes, we must be holy.

<!-- p3 -->
The definition of **Sanctification** is to make something holy for a special purpose. The term **Consecration** is very close, meaning to set something apart for a special purpose.

<!-- p4 -->
Imagine a master sculptor preparing to work on a large block of marble. Through his careful craft, the master envisages what this raw and shapeless marble can be transformed into. With a very specific design in mind, he instructs his assistants to set that block of marble apart for a particular design (**Consecration**). As he refines and works the marble, giving it texture, depth, and form, he sanctifies it for that specific design (**sanctifies it**). Consecrating the marble for his special project was a quick act, whereas shaping the marble into its final design takes a great deal of painstaking labor.

<!-- p5 -->
At salvation we were set apart, consecrated to God. Through the process of purifying our souls with the help of the Spirit, we are transformed. This sanctifying process is what makes us holy.

<!-- p6 -->
When through salvation we received Jesus, we were born again. We were justified; our sins were forgiven. Our spirit was made alive again; we were made alive in Christ. We were adopted into God’s family and became a true temple of the Holy Spirit. Such a glorious transformation that the Scriptures say, “*There is joy in the presence of the angels of God over one sinner who repents.*” (*<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="15" data-verses="10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Luke 15:10</span>).

<!-- p7 -->
Receiving Jesus by means of salvation (**being justified**) saves our spirit, cleanses us, and makes us acceptable before God so that we may receive His Holy Spirit. However, despite such a great salvation, our souls (our mind, will, and emotions) still **need work**!

<!-- p8 -->
Was the work of salvation incomplete? No! The work of salvation is effective, but it must continue in the believer’s life through the renewal brought by the Holy Spirit. This process of renewal and transformation is the work of **sanctification**.

<!-- p9 -->
We know that even after conversion there is still work to be done because our painful memories were not resolved, many of our beliefs remained the same, and many of our habits or customs did not disappear. Furthermore, conversion did not detach our minds from their physical (carnal) nature.

<!-- p10 -->
The word ‘carnal’ comes from a Latin root meaning ‘flesh’ or ‘meat’. The carnal (fleshly) nature exists within each of us from birth; like an animal instinct, it is present to aid our survival. This nature operates in a way that is self-focused, self-inclined, self-centered. Babies are the natural example of the fleshly nature. Despite their innocence, their nature is primarily focused on their desires, wants, and needs; and “beware!” if you do not fulfill its every expectation.

<!-- p11 -->
Part of growing up is that we mature beyond childish ways, but under pressure we very easily revert to a way of thinking and living that revolves around self. As James says:

<!-- p12 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="4" data-verses="1" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p13 -->
> **<u>James 4:1</u>** (NKJV) *Where do wars and fights come from among you? Do they not come from your pleasures that war in your members?*

<!-- p14 -->
</span>

<!-- p15 -->
Paul, in his letter to the Romans (chapters 6 - 8), describes this fleshly nature in more detail. He says that this fleshly nature keeps compelling him to do what he hates and urges him to do evil. He describes this internal struggle as a war between his human nature and his God-made spirit.

<!-- p16 -->
This clash between the flesh and the spirit is present in all of us, and it is our task to do something about it. If we do not resist our carnal nature, our souls will remain trapped in patterns of spiritual decline, even though our spirits have been redeemed.

<!-- p17 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-verses="6-8" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p18 -->
> **<u>Romans 8:6-8</u>** (NKJV) **6** *For to be carnally minded is death, but to be spiritually minded is life and peace.* ***7** Because the carnal mind is enmity against God; for it is not subject to the law of God, nor indeed can be.* ***8** So then, those who are in the flesh cannot please God.*

<!-- p19 -->
</span>

<!-- p20 -->
Paul’s words are both a warning and a promise to us. Although we have been born again, there is still a fleshly mind within us that must be brought under the knowledge of Christ. When we do this, we walk in God’s life and peace.

<!-- p21 -->
Take heart, there is hope for us! We must walk according to the Spirit and remain under the guidance of God’s Spirit. By doing so we will not walk according to the flesh.

<!-- p22 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="fragment" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-verses="1,13,14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p23 -->
> **<u>Romans 8:1,13,14</u>** (NKJV)
>
>**1** *Therefore there is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus, who do not walk according to the flesh but according to the Spirit…*
>
>***13** For if you live according to the flesh you will die; but if by the Spirit you put to death the deeds of the body, you will live.*
>
>***14** For as many as are led by the Spirit of God, these are the sons of God.*

<!-- p24 -->
</span>

<!-- p25 -->
How do we learn to walk in the Spirit? Is it *just a conscious choice*? We learn to walk in the Spirit the same way we learn to walk naturally. There is an impulse in us to move and to grow, so we wrestle, struggle, crawl, and totter until we can stand on our feet. Our natural parents cannot walk in our place, but they can support our walk, encourage us, help us, and enable us.

<!-- p26 -->
Similarly, God cannot walk our faith on our behalf. He will teach us, but the effort is ours to make. As Paul said to the Philippians:

<!-- p27 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-verses="12-13" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p28 -->
> **<u>Philippians 2:12-13</u>** (NKJV) **12** Therefore, my beloved, just as you have always obeyed, not only in my presence but now much more in my absence, work out your own salvation with fear and trembling; **13** for it is God who works in you both to will and to do for His good pleasure.

<!-- p29 -->
</span>

<!-- p30 -->
We must bring our salvation to completion ourselves. This does not mean that we climb onto a wooden cross and are physically crucified, but we must indeed crucify our fleshly nature (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-verses="24" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Galatians 5:24</span>). God promises that He will work in us and help us, but we must choose to walk this path and remain on it.

<!-- p31 -->
It is correct to say that ‘we’ are dealing with our fleshly nature, but it is also correct to say that ‘God’ is dealing with it. The work of the cross (justification) was hard for Jesus—He had to lay down His life for our sake—but it is easy for us; it is a free gift. The work of the Spirit (sanctification) is easy for God—He walks in perfect truth and is willing and eager to dwell with us—but it is hard for us, because we have to crucify our old nature, and our nature has little interest in being crucified.

<!-- p32 -->
This process is cooperative. God helps, assists, guides, directs, and corrects. We listen, obey, follow, and respond. The result is growth and transformation. This is the pruning about which Jesus said that God will do in us. Jesus is the vine, His Father is the gardener, we are the branches:

<!-- p33 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-verses="1-4" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p34 -->
> **<u>John 15:1-4</u>** (NKJV)
>
>***1** "I am the true vine, and My Father is the vinedresser.*
>
>***2** Every branch in Me that does not bear fruit He takes away; and every branch that bears fruit He prunes, that it may bear more fruit.*
>
>***3** You are already clean because of the word which I have spoken to you.*
>
>***4** Abide in Me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself unless it abides in the vine, neither can you, unless you abide in Me.*

<!-- p35 -->
</span>

<!-- p36 -->
Pruning is exceedingly beneficial for a plant. Recalling our chapter on repentance, we are called to turn from dead works (those works that are no longer bearing fruit) and to turn toward God. Turning away from the dead parts of our lives and turning to God results in enabling the great Gardener of our lives to prune the branches, improve growth, and produce more spiritual fruit (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-verses="2" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 15:2</span>).

<!-- p37 -->
Although pruning can feel painful, it reveals God’s loving care as He molds us into vessels for His purpose (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-verses="11" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 12:11</span>). Some people, while going through the process of pruning, may feel lack and even fear. However, God is tenderhearted, and He will not break a bruised reed (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="42" data-verses="3" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Isaiah 42:3</span>). Grapevines are pruned during their dormant season so that the stress on the plant is minimal. The gardener who tends the growth cuts away the old, hardened, wood-like growth that no longer bears fruit as before; this allows the vine to redirect its energy to those branches that have greater potential for good fruit.

<!-- p38 -->
Just as a gardener cuts branches to direct growth, God also allows seasons of correction and training to refine our faith and character, yet His work is patient and kind. God prunes those who abide in Him. Our emphasis should be more on abiding in Christ and less on the work of pruning.

<!-- p39 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-verses="11" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 12:11</span> reminds us that pruning “produces the peaceable fruit of righteousness” for those who are trained by it.

<!-- p40 -->
How are we trained in righteousness? By being transformed through the renewal of our minds.

<!-- p41 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-verses="1-2" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p42 -->
> **<u>Romans 12:1-2</u>** (NKJV) ***1** Therefore, brothers, I beseech you by the mercies of God to present your bodies to God as a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable to God, which is your reasonable worship. **2** And do not be conformed to this world, but be <u>transformed</u> by the <u>renewing</u> of your mind, that you may prove what is the good, acceptable, and perfect will of God.* (emphasis added)

<!-- p43 -->
</span>

<!-- p44 -->
Paul states two simple conditions for transformation:

<!-- p45 -->
<u>1. Present your bodies before God as a living sacrifice</u>. A **living** sacrifice does not die physically during the sacrifice; but since a living sacrifice cannot die, you must keep bringing it to the altar continually!

<!-- p46 -->
<u>2. Renew your mind!</u> Renewal is based on the Greek word: **Anakainōsis** (*An-ak-ah'-ee-no-sis -* **G342**), which means to make new or to renovate, and emphasizes the ongoing process of inner change and growth.

<!-- p47 -->
This renewal brings transformation. Transformation is based on the Greek word: **Metamorphoō** (*met-am-or-fo'-o -* **G3339**), which means to change into another form; to be transformed or transfigured, emphasizing a complete change. From Metamorphoō comes the English word metamorphosis, which is the process that caterpillars go through to become butterflies.

<!-- p48 -->
God wants our minds and our way of thinking to be fundamentally changed. His desire is that we not conform to the way of the world, but that we know what God’s good (beneficial), pleasing (acceptable), and perfect (complete and without any lack) will is.

<!-- p49 -->
The conditions for transformation really are just that simple, **although they require discipline**!

<!-- p50 -->
Our battle against the flesh will not be won by our flesh, but by the means God has provided for the war of the mind. In His Word (the sword of truth) there is great power to correct every imagination, every arrogant thought, and to take every thought that goes against obedience to God captive (captivity).

<!-- p51 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="10" data-verses="3-6" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p52 -->
> **<u>2 Corinthians 10:3-6</u>** (NKJV)
>
> ***3** For though we walk in the flesh, we do not war according to the flesh.*
>
> ***4** For the weapons of our warfare are not carnal but mighty in God for pulling down strongholds,*
>
> ***5** casting down arguments and every high thing that exalts itself against the knowledge of God, bringing every thought into captivity to the obedience of Christ,*
>
> ***6** and being ready to punish all disobedience when your obedience is fulfilled.*

<!-- p53 -->
</span>

<!-- p54 -->
When authorities arrest someone, they shackle him and put him in a jail cell. If that person escapes custody, they do not overlook it; they will use extraordinary resources and will not rest until they bring him back under control. We too should apply the same zeal and fervor in the struggle of our minds!

<!-- p55 -->
We are made new through obedience and the experience of truth. Jesus said that if we abide in His word, we will be His disciples, we will (<u>know</u>) the truth, and it will set us free (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="8" data-verses="31-32" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 8:31-32</span>)!

<!-- p56 -->
The experience of truth is different from the knowledge of truth. For experience the Greek word **Ginōskō** (Ghin-oce'-ko **- G1097**) is used, which means to know through personal experience or deep understanding; it carries the sense of gradual learning.

<!-- p57 -->
What transforms us and renews our minds is not theoretical knowledge of the truth, but our personal experience of the truth. We are sanctified by His truth! (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="17" data-verses="17" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 17:17</span>).

## How to renew your mind

<!-- p59 -->
The renewal of our minds is not a one-size-fits-all process—it depends on which part of the mind we are talking about! God has designed our minds with extraordinary complexity, including several different memory systems, each serving a specific purpose. Some operate automatically in the background, while others require focused and intentional thought. These systems are God-given gifts that help us survive, grow, and thrive, but when they are misused or left unrenewed, they can also lead us astray. Understanding how these systems work gives us insight into how to cooperate with God’s process of transformation.

<!-- p60 -->
In the next section, I will describe each system of memory, their purpose and function, some practical steps to renew this aspect of your mind, as well as resources for ongoing practice and study.

<!-- p61 -->
### **Memory Systems and How to Renew Them**

<!-- p62 -->
#### **1. Explicit Memory (Declarative Memory)**

<!-- p63 -->
**Purpose:** Stores facts, events, and experiences that we can consciously recall. It is divided into two types-**Semantic** and **Episodic** memory.

<!-- p64 -->
**Semantic Memory - Facts and Knowledge.** Semantic memory is where we store facts, definitions, and general information-such as historical dates, Bible verses, and practical information. It helps us predict outcomes, make decisions, and avoid danger (e.g., recognizing poisonous plants). Although it gives us stability and order, it can also lead to rigidity-overreliance on rules instead of adapting to unexpected situations.

<!-- p65 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p66 -->
- **Study Scripture** to shape our thinking (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="119" data-verses="105" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Psalm 119:105</span>).

<!-- p67 -->
- **Memorize truths** and creeds to remain steadfast in faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="COL" data-chapter="3" data-verses="16" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Colossians 3:16</span>).

<!-- p68 -->
- **Read Christian books** such as *"Renewing the Mind" by Casey Treat* or *"Battlefield of the Mind" by Joyce Meyer* to cultivate a godly mindset.

<!-- p69 -->
**Episodic Memory - Personal Experiences.** Episodic memory stores our personal experiences, linking them to specific times and places-such as a wedding day or a traumatic event. These memories provide context, helping us learn from the past and form relationships. However, episodic memory is prone to emotional bias and distortion. Past traumas can create fear-based associations, making it difficult to move forward.

<!-- p70 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p71 -->
- **Reflect and confess** so that hidden pain comes into God's light (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="1" data-verses="9" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1- John 1:9</span>).

<!-- p72 -->
- **Reframe past experiences** through the lens of God's grace (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-verses="28" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 8:28</span>).

<!-- p73 -->
- **Seek inner healing** through prayer and counseling (*“Your Pain is Showing” by Larry F. Low, "The Bondage Breaker" by Neil T. Anderson*, *"Emotionally Healthy Spirituality" by Peter Scazzero*).

<!-- p74 -->
#### **2. Implicit Memory (Nondeclarative Memory)**

<!-- p75 -->
**Purpose:** Unconscious memory used for habits, skills, and emotional associations. It operates automatically, and helps us respond quickly to situations. It is divided into **Procedural Memory** and **Emotional Conditioning**.

<!-- p76 -->
**Procedural Memory - Skills and Habits.** Procedural memory allows us to perform tasks without thinking-such as driving, typing, or playing an instrument. It is efficient, and it saves our mental energy and time. However, bad habits and negative patterns can also become deeply ingrained and hard to break.

<!-- p77 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p78 -->
- **Practice spiritual disciplines** such as prayer and fasting to form godly habits (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="5" data-verses="14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 5:14</span>).

<!-- p79 -->
- **Train in godliness** through repetition and obedience (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="4" data-verses="7" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1- Timothy 4:7</span>).

<!-- p80 -->
- **Read books like** *"Atomic Habits" by James Clear* (practical) and *"Spiritual Disciplines for the Christian Life" by Donald Whitney* (biblical) to establish habits that honor God.

<!-- p81 -->
**Emotional Conditioning - Emotional Attachments.** Emotional conditioning links sensory cues (such as smells or sounds) to feelings, triggering reactions before they are consciously processed. This system protects us, but when past experiences are negative it can also produce undue fear or emotional reactions.

<!-- p82 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p83 -->
- **Pray for healing** from past wounds (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="41" data-verses="10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Isaiah 41:10</span>).

<!-- p84 -->
- **Practice forgiveness** and let go of bitterness (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-verses="31-32" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 4:31-32</span>).

<!-- p85 -->
- **Replace fear with God's promises** (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-verses="7" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">2 Timothy 1:7</span>).

<!-- p86 -->
- **Seek guidance from books like** *“Your Pain is Showing” by Larry F. Low, "The Bondage Breaker" by Neil T. Anderson*, “*Soul Care" by Rob Reimer* so that emotional triggers can be brought under control.

<!-- p87 -->
#### **3. Working Memory (Short-term Memory)**

<!-- p88 -->
**Purpose:** Provides temporary storage for problem-solving and decision-making. It helps us focus attention, hold information briefly, and process tasks, such as remembering instructions or making quick decisions. Although it is powerful, it is also limited, prone to distraction, and easily overloaded, especially under stress.

<!-- p89 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p90 -->
- **Pay attention and meditate** daily on God's Word (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="1" data-verses="2" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Psalm 1:2</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
- **Renew your thoughts** through intentional thinking (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-verses="2" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 12:2</span>).

<!-- p92 -->
- **Practice mental focus** and reduce mental clutter (*"Habits of Grace" by David Mathis*).

<!-- p93 -->
- **Read** *"The Celebration of Discipline" by Richard Foster* to sharpen focus through spiritual disciplines.

<!-- p94 -->
## Conclusion

<!-- p95 -->
The renewal of the mind is not a quick fix—it is a lifelong process of gradual transformation. Each memory system plays a role in shaping how we think, feel, and act. By working intentionally with the Holy Spirit, practicing Scripture, and developing God-honoring habits, we can train our minds according to God's truth. Whether dealing with past wounds, learning new habits, or staying focused in the moment, God has provided us with both **spiritual resources** and **practical wisdom** so that we may walk in renewal and freedom (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="10" data-verses="5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">2 Corinthians 10:5</span>).

<!-- p96 -->
Our world is filled with trouble, distress, and trial. Through the systematic renewal of the mind, our souls continue to be transformed, and we can live a life of freedom abounding with the fruit of His Spirit for God (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-verses="22-25" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Galatians 5:22-25</span>).
