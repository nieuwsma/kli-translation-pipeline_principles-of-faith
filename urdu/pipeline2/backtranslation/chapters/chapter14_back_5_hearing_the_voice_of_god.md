# Hearing the Voice of God

<!-- p1 -->
The following guest supplement by Pastor Larry Low ([<u>www.LarryLow.com</u>](http://www.larrylow.com)) completes our exploration of the role of the Holy Spirit, offering practical insights on hearing God's voice. The following guest writing is used with permission.

<!-- p2 -->
One morning during prayer, I asked **God** this question: “What is the most important thing I can teach Your people for the times that are coming toward us?” And He answered me very quickly, and I realized how vital it was when He said: “**Teach them to hear My voice.**”

<!-- p3 -->
When the Lord gives me this kind of direction, I always pick up a pad and pencil and begin to write what little I know—or what I think I know—and the verses that support it. I love to do biblical studies on the subjects the Lord leads me to teach. I like the anointing for preaching, but I truly love teaching the Word more. Finding those things that have always been there but have been overlooked is an adventure.

<!-- p4 -->
Surely David longed to discover God’s truths, for he prayed in Psalm 119:18: “Open my eyes, that I may behold wondrous things out of Your law.”

<!-- p5 -->
That is what I want to see-the wondrous things of His Word.

<!-- p6 -->
As I was studying and preparing to teach a second message on the subject of hearing and discerning God’s voice, He spoke to my heart as follows:

<!-- p7 -->
“*Teach them to hear My voice,  
teach them reverence and obedience.*

<!-- p8 -->
*Yes, teach them to hear My voice and I will always lead them away:  
away from calamities that would destroy them,*

<!-- p9 -->
*away from the trials they are in,  
to a place where there is peace, love, and joy, which very few have ever been in.*

<!-- p10 -->
*to a place of refreshing,  
to a place of divine delight.*

<!-- p11 -->
*Yes, son, teach them to hear My voice  
and these people will be free forever*.” 

<!-- p12 -->
05/27/00 9:10 A.M.

<!-- p13 -->
The eleven points below will help you understand how the Lord will speak to you:

<!-- p14 -->
1.  <u>Hearing God’s voice is not an audible voice but a knowing in your heart.</u>

<!-- p15 -->
There was one time when His word came so clearly and powerfully that it could have been an audible voice. I answered out loud; it was that real. However, there was no one there but the Lord and me, so I cannot say for certain that He spoke to me the way we speak to one another.

<!-- p16 -->
When Jesus was being baptized in the Jordan River, the Father said, “This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased,” but some of those present said it sounded like thunder. So I suppose He could have spoken to me in an audible voice, but for now I want you to understand that when I say God spoke to me, it is more a knowing in my heart than a voice in my ear.

<!-- p17 -->
2.  <u>This knowing is not formed by the logic of your mind; however, it is a complete thought or understanding.</u>

<!-- p18 -->
The Lord has never said anything to me that left me confused. He has never said anything that left me asking “why.” It is always a knowing with complete understanding. God is not the author of confusion (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:33</span>).

<!-- p19 -->
3.  <u>When He speaks, He speaks in complete thoughts.</u>

<!-- p20 -->
He never leaves us to fill in the blanks ourselves. In the blink of an eye, God can fill your heart with so much information that you could preach for a lifetime. One touch of God can fill you with a fire that will keep you burning for eternity. He is a perfect messenger.

<!-- p21 -->
4.  <u>When God speaks to us, He never drives; He guides.</u>

<!-- p22 -->
He is not a taskmaster; He is a shepherd. Herdsmen drive, but shepherds guide. Jesus is the great Shepherd of the sheep, and we are the sheep of His pasture.

<!-- p23 -->
5.  <u>When He speaks to His children, He never condemns; He convicts.</u>

<!-- p24 -->
His children are those who have been born again through their confession of faith in the shed blood of **Jesus** and His work on Calvary (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:3</span>, 5). Who would want to listen to someone who constantly condemns us for our mistakes? I believe that most of **God’s** children are afraid for **God** to speak to them, because they have been told that **God** will condemn them for this or that. See <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 3:19-20</span>. Whom does it say condemns us or declares us guilty? It is our own spirit or heart that condemns a child of **God**, but it is **God the Holy Spirit** who knows everything and is greater than our hearts. He is present for us as the Comforter. Read the following verses; this will help you understand that the Holy Spirit only convicts sinners. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33-34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:33-34</span> teaches us that nothing can come between us and the love of **God**. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:16</span>, 26 teaches us that the Holy Spirit is our Comforter, not our accuser; our Counselor, not our disciplinarian; our Advocate and Helper, not our hinderer. Also read <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 9:31</span> and 15:28 to see how the Holy Spirit (**God’s** voice) helps, comforts, and strengthens us.

<!-- p25 -->
6.  <u>The voice of God can be tested so that you may know that it is God and not your thoughts or the voice of familiar spirits.</u>

<!-- p26 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 4:1-3</span> instructs us to "test every spirit" so that we can be sure it is from God. When you think you are hearing from God, you have the right and the responsibility to question that voice (this is what it is to try or prove the spirit). The question to ask is: "Did Jesus Christ come in the flesh?" Familiar spirits will never admit one thing: that Jesus came to earth in the flesh. He was fully man and fully God. No false spirit or religion will believe that Jesus came to this earth in the flesh and redeemed human beings forever from all their sins. Test the voice you hear. You should also know that God will never lead you outside of His written Word, the Bible. If you are hearing something that is not supported in the Bible, you are not hearing from God. Drop it! Test the voice you hear to see whether it is from God or not.

<!-- p27 -->
7.  <u>The voice of God always glorifies Jesus Christ.</u>

<!-- p28 -->
The Holy Spirit is the voice of heaven. He is the One who is here on earth to work together with us, and Jesus said that He will always speak what He hears, and He will always glorify Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13-14</span>). He is not here to glorify your ministry, or your works, or the church’s programs. All these things are good, and most of them are even necessary to do the work we do, but the Holy Spirit will speak and glorify the person of Jesus. If the voice you are hearing is glorifying you, then you are not hearing from God. There is a difference between getting glory and being encouraged and strengthened. Whenever the Holy Spirit speaks to you, He will always encourage and strengthen you, even when He is correcting (guiding) you with the Word. But glory is when you begin to think that you yourself are some great person. Without Jesus you are nothing—He alone is truly the One; we are not.

<!-- p29 -->
8.  <u>The voice of God will always reveal something to us.</u>

<!-- p30 -->
See <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:14</span>. He always takes what belongs to **Jesus** and reveals it to us. The Amplified translation includes these words regarding the word "reveal": "declare, disclose, and transmit." **God** always first declares something to us, then unveils it to us, and then, through your faith, transmits it to us. Also study <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 2:10</span> and 12.

<!-- p31 -->
9.  <u>The voice of God will always bring peace.</u>

<!-- p32 -->
Read <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:26</span> and 27, and <span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 3:14</span> and 15. His voice never troubles us. It is a voice that brings perfect peace. Inner peace is there because there is a certainty inside that gives us bold confidence before God, and this turns into perfect peace. If the voice you are hearing makes you uneasy, then you are not hearing from God—turn it off. Jesus promised us peace, not as the world gives, but as He gives us through the comforting words and ever-abiding presence of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:27</span>).

<!-- p33 -->
10. <u>The voice of God always brings truth.</u>

<!-- p34 -->
Read <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:17</span>, 26; 16:13. In fact, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span> calls the Holy Spirit the "Spirit of Truth." God cannot lie! He is truth; therefore He will speak only truth. I have heard many people say, "I can’t believe what I’m hearing is from God. It’s too good to be true." And perhaps it really is too good to be true; that is why you must test the spirit, measure it against God’s Word to be sure that it is true. Some people simply cannot believe because what they are hearing is always positive, and they have been taught that God condemns us when we are wrong. God will tell only the truth, and it will always be good news. The sad truth is that most people hear only bad news from the voice they hear and never expect good news; therefore when good news comes they question it as possibly untrue. **Truth is always good news!** Since it is **the truth that** sets us free, how can **Truth** be anything but good news?

<!-- p35 -->
11. <u>The voice of **God** will always take us forward and upward.</u>

<!-- p36 -->
Deuteronomy 28:1-2, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="30" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 30:21</span>, and <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span>

<!-- p37 -->
God never takes you downward! He never brings you down; He only strengthens you. When God speaks to you, He speaks from the standpoint that everything the Word says about you has already been accomplished and you have received it. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="33" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 33:3</span> says: "Call to Me, and I will answer you, and show you great and mighty things which you do not know." Does that sound like bringing you down or like despair and darkness? There is absolutely no despair and darkness in God, therefore He cannot tell you anything ominous or dark. For despair and darkness you will have to contact the world’s news agency.

<!-- p38 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="29" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p39 -->
> **<u>Jeremiah 29:11</u>** (NKJV)
>
> For I know the thoughts that I think toward you, says the Lord, thoughts of peace and not of evil, to give you a future and a hope.

<!-- p40 -->
</span>

<!-- p41 -->
Now, if God thinks good thoughts about us—of peace, a future, and hope—why would He speak contrary to that? The answer is, He would not, and He does not! He only guides us upward and forward with great hope to provide us with an expected future.

<!-- p42 -->
I hope this encourages you to “call upon Him” and helps you learn **to hear His voice**. He has great things to tell you, but before all His goodness can benefit you, He needs your listening ear.
