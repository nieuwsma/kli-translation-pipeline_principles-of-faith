# Hearing God's Voice

<!-- p1 -->
Rev. Larry Low's ([<u>www.LarryLow.com</u>](http://www.larrylow.com)) guest addendum below completes our exploration of the role of the Holy Spirit, and offers practical insights on hearing God's voice. The guest writing below is used with permission.

<!-- p2 -->
One morning during prayer, I asked **God** this question: “What is the most important thing I can teach Your people for the times that are coming toward us?” And He answered me very quickly, and I sensed it was most urgent when He said: “**Teach them to hear My voice.**”

<!-- p3 -->
When the Lord gives me such guidance, I always pick up a pad and pencil and begin to write the little I know, or what I think I know, and the verses that prove it. I love to do scriptural study on the topics the Lord leads me to teach. I enjoy the anointing for preaching, but I truly prefer to teach the Word. Finding those things that have always been there but have been overlooked is an adventure.

<!-- p4 -->
Surely David was eager to discover God's truths, for he prayed in Psalm 119:18: “Open my eyes, that I may see wondrous things out of Your law.”

<!-- p5 -->
That is what I want to see—wondrous things from His Word.

<!-- p6 -->
As I was studying and preparing to teach a second message on the subject of hearing and recognizing God's voice, He said this to my heart:

<!-- p7 -->
“*Teach them to hear My voice,  
teach them to honor and obey.*”

<!-- p8 -->
“*Yes, teach them to hear My voice and I will always lead them away:  
away from the troubles that will destroy them,*”

<!-- p9 -->
“*away from the trials they are in,  
away to a place where there is peace, love, and joy, where very few have ever gone.*”

<!-- p10 -->
“*away to a place of refreshing,  
to a place of divine delight.*”

<!-- p11 -->
“*Oh yes, My son, teach them to hear My voice  
and these people will be free forever*.”

<!-- p12 -->
05/27/00 9:10 A.M.

<!-- p13 -->
The eleven points below will help you understand how the Lord will speak to you:

<!-- p14 -->
1.  <u>Hearing God's voice is not an audible voice but a knowing/assurance in your heart.</u>

<!-- p15 -->
There was a time when His word came so clear and strong that it might even have been an audible voice. I answered Him out loud; it was that real. However, there was no one there but the Lord and me, so I cannot say for sure whether He spoke to me in the same way we speak to each other.

<!-- p16 -->
There was also a time when Jesus was being baptized in the River Jordan and the Father said, “This is My beloved Son in whom I am well pleased,” but some who were there said it sounded like thunder, so I suppose He could also have spoken to me in an audible voice; but for now, I want you to understand that when I say that God spoke to me, it is more a knowing/assurance in my heart than a voice in my ear.

<!-- p17 -->
2.  <u>This knowing/assurance is not built by logic inside your mind; however, it is a complete thought or understanding.</u>

<!-- p18 -->
The Lord has never said anything to me that left me confused. He has never said anything to me that left me wondering “why.” It is always a knowing/assurance with complete understanding. God is not the author of confusion (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-verses="33" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 14:33</span>).

<!-- p19 -->
3.  <u>When He speaks, He speaks in complete thoughts.</u>

<!-- p20 -->
He never leaves us to fill in the blanks. In the blink of an eye, God can fill your heart with so much information that you could preach for a lifetime. One touch of God can fill you with a fire that will keep you burning eternally. He is a perfect messenger.

<!-- p21 -->
4.  <u>When God speaks to us, He never drives; He guides.</u>

<!-- p22 -->
He is not a taskmaster; He is a shepherd. Drovers drive, but shepherds guide. Jesus is the Great Shepherd of the sheep, and we are the sheep of His pasture.

<!-- p23 -->
5.  <u>When He speaks to His children, He never condemns; He convinces.</u>

<!-- p24 -->
His children are those who have been born again (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-verses="3" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 3:3</span>, 5) by their confession of faith in **Jesus’** shed blood and His work on Calvary. Who wants to listen to someone who always condemns us for our mistakes? I believe that most of **God’s** children are afraid for **God** to speak to them, because they have been told that **God** will condemn them for this or that. Take a look at <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="3" data-verses="19-20" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 John 3:19-20</span>. What does it say is it that condemns or declares us guilty? It is our own spirit or heart that declares the child of **God** guilty, but **God the Holy Spirit** is the One who knows everything and is greater than our hearts. He is present as our Comforter. Read the following verses; they will help you understand that the Holy Spirit only convicts sinners. <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-verses="33-34" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 8:33-34</span> teaches us that nothing can come between us and the love of **God**. <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-verses="16" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 14:16</span>, 26 teaches us that the Holy Spirit is our Comforter, not our condemner; our Counselor, not our disciplinarian; our Advocate and Helper, not a hinderer. Read also <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="9" data-verses="31" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 9:31</span> and 15:28 as examples of how the Holy Spirit (the voice of **God**) helps us, comforts us, and strengthens us.

<!-- p25 -->
6.  <u>The voice of God can be tested so that you may know that it is God, not your thoughts or the voice of familiar spirits.</u>

<!-- p26 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="4" data-verses="1-3" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 John 4:1-3</span> instructs us to “test every spirit” so that we can be certain it is from God. When you believe you are hearing from God, you have both the right and the responsibility to ask that voice a question (this is what it is to test or prove the spirit). The question to ask is: “Did Jesus Christ come in the flesh?” Familiar spirits will never acknowledge one thing—that Jesus came to the earth in the flesh. He was fully man and fully God. No false spirit or religion will admit that Jesus came to this earth in the flesh and forever redeemed humankind from all their sins. Test the voice you are hearing. Also know that God will never lead you outside His written Word, the Bible. If you are hearing something that is not supported in the Bible, you are not hearing from God. Drop it! Test the voice you are hearing to see whether it is from God or not.

<!-- p27 -->
7.  <u>The voice of God always glorifies Jesus Christ.</u>

<!-- p28 -->
The Holy Spirit is the voice of heaven. He is the One who is present on earth to work with us, and Jesus said that He will always speak what He hears, and He will always glorify Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-verses="13-14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 16:13-14</span>). He is not here to glorify your ministry, your works, or church programs. All these things are good, and most of them are necessary for doing what we do, but the Holy Spirit will speak and glorify the person of Jesus. If the voice you are hearing glorifies you, then you are not hearing the voice of God. There is a difference between being glorified and being encouraged and strengthened. Whenever the Holy Spirit speaks to you, He will always encourage and strengthen you, even if through the word He is correcting you (guiding you). But glorification is when you begin to think you are somebody. Without Jesus you are nothing-He is the main One, not us.

<!-- p29 -->
8.  <u>The voice of God will always reveal something to us.</u>

<!-- p30 -->
Take a look at <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-verses="14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 16:14</span>. He always takes what is **Jesus’** and reveals it to us. The Amplified adds these words with regard to the word “reveal”: announce, disclose, and transmit. **God** always tells us something, then unveils it to us, and then by your faith transmits it to us. Read further <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="2" data-verses="10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 2:10</span> and 12.

<!-- p31 -->
9.  <u>The voice of God will always bring peace.</u>

<!-- p32 -->
Read <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-verses="26" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 14:26</span> and 27, and <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="COL" data-chapter="3" data-verses="14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Colossians 3:14</span> and 15. His voice never makes us uneasy. It is a voice that brings perfect peace. There is inner peace because there is a certainty that gives us boldness before God, and this is what turns into perfect peace. If the voice you are hearing makes you uneasy, then you are not hearing from God—turn it off. Jesus has promised us peace, not as the world gives but as He gives us through the comforting words and continual indwelling of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-verses="27" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 14:27</span>).

<!-- p33 -->
10. <u>The voice of God always brings truth.</u>

<!-- p34 -->
Read <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-verses="17" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 14:17</span>, 26; 16:13. In fact, <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-verses="13" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 16:13</span> calls the Holy Spirit: “the Spirit of truth.” God cannot lie! He is truth, therefore He will speak only truth. People have said things like this to me: “I can’t believe what I’m hearing is from God. It’s too good to be true.” And perhaps it really is too good to be true; that’s why you must test the spirit, examining it according to God’s Word, to make certain it is true. Some people just can’t believe because what they hear is always positive, and they have been taught that God reproves us for our mistakes. God will tell only the truth, and it will always be good news. The sad reality is that most people only hear bad news from the voice they listen to and never expect to hear good news, so when good news comes they question it as possibly untrue. **Truth is always good news!** It is the **truth that** sets us free, so what else can **truth** be but good news?

<!-- p35 -->
11. <u>The voice of **God** always takes us forward and upward.</u>

<!-- p36 -->
Read Deuteronomy 28:1-2, <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="30" data-verses="21" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Isaiah 30:21</span>, and <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-verses="13" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 16:13</span>

<!-- p37 -->
God will never take you down! He never brings you down; He only builds you up. When God speaks to you, He speaks from the perspective that everything the Word says about you has already been accomplished and you have already received it. <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JER" data-chapter="33" data-verses="3" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Jeremiah 33:3</span> says: “Call to Me, and I will answer you, and show you **great and mighty** things which you do not know.” Does that sound like something that brings you down or like destruction and despair? There is no destruction and darkness in God, therefore He cannot tell you anything destructive or disheartening. For that you will have to turn your ears to the world’s news agency.

<!-- p38 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="JER" data-chapter="29" data-verses="11" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p39 -->
> **<u>Jeremiah 29:11</u>** (NKJV) *For I know the thoughts that I think toward you, says the Lord, thoughts of peace and not of evil, to give you a future and a hope.*

<!-- p40 -->
</span>

<!-- p41 -->
Now, if God thinks good thoughts of peace, a future, and hope for us, why would He speak the opposite? The answer is, He will not do that, and He does not! He only leads us upward and forward with great hope to provide us with an expected future.

<!-- p42 -->
I hope this will encourage you to “call to Him” and to learn how to hear His voice. He has great things to tell you, but before all His good things can be of any use to you, He needs your listening ear.
