# Chapter 11 - Baptisms - Water and Spirit

<!-- p1 -->
In our modern era, baptism, which is considered separate from Jewish roots, is generally understood only in terms of water baptism and the baptism of the Holy Spirit (Pentecost).

<!-- p2 -->
The book of Hebrews was written to a Jewish audience. The vast majority of Christians in our time do not have Jewish roots or heritage. Without Jewish roots and culture, most of us lack the cultural context that was shared among first-century Jewish Christians (those Jewish believers who confessed Jesus). Since we were not raised in the Jewish tradition, we generally do not know much about the feasts, fasts, prayers, washings, rites, and forms of worship that Jewish believers knew.

<!-- p3 -->
In Greek the word for baptisms or washings is: **Baptismos** (bap-tis-mos’ - **G909**) - the act of dipping or washing. It refers to ceremonial washing or cultic purification. It can point to immersion, purity rituals, or the washings practiced in Jewish and Christian contexts.

<!-- p4 -->
Baptisms are one of the foundations mentioned in Hebrews:

<!-- p5 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p6 -->
> **<u>Hebrews 6:1-2</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Therefore, leaving the elementary teachings of Christ, let us go on to perfection, not laying again the foundation of repentance from dead works and of faith toward God,
>
> and of the doctrine of baptisms, of laying on of hands, of resurrection of the dead, and of eternal judgment.
>

## Old Testament Washings - Historical Background

<!-- p9 -->
In the Old Testament, ceremonial washings played a central role in maintaining ritual purity before God and as a symbol of cleanliness. These washings were prescribed in the Law of Moses and applied both to the priests who served in the Tent of Meeting and to the people of Israel. For example, it was required of the priests to wash their hands and feet at the bronze laver before entering the Tent of Meeting or approaching the altar, which symbolized their preparation for service in the presence of a holy God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="30" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 30:18-21</span>). Likewise, rites for cleansing from uncleanness—such as contact with a dead body or skin diseases—included washing with water as a sign of restored purity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LEV" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Leviticus 14:8</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="NUM" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Numbers 19:17-19</span>). These practices were outward expressions of an inward desire for purity, highlighting the importance of being set apart for God.

<!-- p10 -->
Old Testament ceremonial washings had to be repeated to maintain ritual purity. Rather than being a permanent solution, these rites provided only temporary outward cleanliness and could not resolve the deeper problem of sin. The author of Hebrews makes this limitation clear, stating that these practices were “a symbol for the present time” and were unable to cleanse the conscience of the worshiper (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:9-10</span>). These washings remained a constant reminder of human failure and the need for something greater—an ultimate purification—that could cleanse not only the body but also the heart and soul.

<!-- p11 -->
The Old Testament ceremonial washings find their fulfillment in Christ, who through His sacrifice grants complete and eternal cleansing. In contrast to the temporary rites of the Law, the blood of Christ cleanses fully, purifying not only the body but also the conscience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:13-14</span>). Through His death and resurrection, Jesus accomplished what the Old Testament rites only pointed to—true reconciliation with God. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:22</span> calls believers to “draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, having our hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience and our bodies washed with pure water.” This language connects Christ’s work with the imagery of Old Testament washings while displaying its far greater effectiveness. His sacrifice does not merely cover sin but removes it completely (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 1:7</span>). Thus, the ceremonial washings were a shadow pointing to the deeper cleansing that only Christ could provide, fulfilling humanity’s need for sanctification through divine intervention.

<!-- p12 -->
To repeat the point, Christians are not obligated to perform any ceremonial washings. Washings did not provide salvation in the Old Testament, nor do they save in the New Testament. Ceremonial washings will neither increase our standing before God nor earn us favor in His sight. Paul strongly rebuked the Judaizers (read Galatians) who tried to add Jewish-context requirements (e.g., circumcision) to salvation. Salvation is only through faith in Jesus!

<!-- p13 -->
## John’s Baptism

<!-- p14 -->
The New Testament writings mention several baptisms. We have already discussed the baptisms and washings of Jewish tradition.

<!-- p15 -->
John the Baptist’s ministry was focused on preparing the way for the coming of Jesus Christ. His baptism, called a baptism of repentance for the forgiveness of sins (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 1:4</span>), invited people to confess their sins, turn their hearts to God, and prepare for the arrival of the Messiah. It was an outward sign of an inner desire for repentance, symbolizing spiritual cleansing and readiness for the kingdom of God. However, John’s baptism was temporary and preparatory—it pointed to the greater work that Christ would accomplish. Although John’s baptism emphasized repentance, it did not provide complete forgiveness or spiritual new birth because it did not include the indwelling and power of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:11-12</span>). In contrast, John openly declared that One greater than he was coming who would baptize not only with water but with the Holy Spirit and fire (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 3:16</span>).

<!-- p16 -->
John’s baptism was fulfilled and superseded through the death, burial, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. Christian baptism, which Jesus instituted and the apostles practiced, symbolizes a deeper reality—union with Christ, death to sin, and resurrection to new life (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 6:3-4</span>). Unlike John’s baptism, Christian baptism is directly tied to faith in Jesus’ atoning sacrifice and the indwelling of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:38</span>). The coming of the Holy Spirit at Pentecost marked the beginning of the new covenant and a life empowered by the Spirit, which was not possible through John’s baptism. For this reason, John’s baptism is no longer practiced, having been replaced by the baptism commanded by Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19</span>), which includes repentance, faith, and the sealing of believers by the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>).

<!-- p17 -->
## Christ’s Baptism

<!-- p18 -->
Christ’s baptism sets a strong example for His followers and lays the foundation for Christian water baptism. Although Jesus was sinless, He consented to be baptized by John “to fulfill all righteousness” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:15</span>). By doing so, He identified Himself with humanity’s need for repentance and pointed to His own death, burial, and resurrection. At His baptism the Holy Spirit descended like a dove, and the Father declared, “This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:16-17</span>). This moment marked the beginning of Jesus’ public ministry and revealed the unity of the Father, the Son, and the Spirit, foreshadowing the spiritual transformation believers would experience through faith in Christ.

<!-- p19 -->
Later, Jesus commanded His followers to be baptized and gave them this commission: “Therefore go and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19</span>). Water baptism, as instituted by Christ, became an act of obedience and a public declaration of faith for believers. It represents their identification with Jesus’ death, burial, and resurrection (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 6:3-4</span>). Going into the water signifies dying to sin, and coming out of the water portrays being raised to new life in Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:12</span>). However, it must be made very clear that this outward act is not the source of salvation but an outward sign of the inward work of grace that has already been accomplished through the blood of Jesus.

<!-- p20 -->
Water baptism is a sign of the cleansing power of Christ’s blood and the renewal of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:26</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>). It signifies repentance, faith, and a commitment to follow Jesus. It is an outward testimony of an inward transformation—a way by which believers publicly declare their allegiance to Christ and their inclusion in the body of Christ, that is, the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:41</span>). Although it is Jesus’ command and is exemplified in the early church, it does not grant anyone **salvation**. Salvation is by grace through faith alone (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>), and true cleansing occurs spiritually through the blood of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 1:5</span>). Water baptism is a response to that salvation; it is an act of obedience, not a condition for it.

<!-- p21 -->
In the New Testament, water baptism is consistently associated with **repentance and faith** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:38</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 16:16</span>), and both require a personal decision to follow Christ—something infants are not capable of. Baptism is a sign that a believer has consciously identified with Christ’s death, burial, and resurrection (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 6:3-4</span>), as well as a commitment to live in obedience to Him. Some denominations (e.g., Catholic, Lutheran) practice **infant baptism** as a covenantal sign akin to circumcision; however, since infants cannot express personal faith or repentance, the tradition of **infant baptism** is not aligned with the biblical pattern of **believer’s baptism**. In contrast, many churches hold **dedication ceremonies** for infants, following the examples of Hannah dedicating Samuel to the Lord (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1SA" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Samuel 1:27-28</span>) and Mary and Joseph presenting Jesus at the temple (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 2:22</span>). Dedication is not a substitute for baptism but the parents’ commitment to raise their child in the faith, teach God’s Word, and model a godly life until the child is able to decide to follow Christ and be baptized.

<!-- p22 -->
In contrast to the Old Covenant’s **ceremonial washings**, which required repeated rituals for purification, Christian baptism is a one-time event that reflects the finished work of grace. The washings of the Old Testament were signs of external purity and foreshadowed the deeper spiritual cleansing that comes through Christ. Conversely, New Testament baptism is a sign of the inward renewal and new birth that occurs through the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>). It points to the believer’s participation in the New Covenant, where cleansing from sin is permanent and based on Christ’s finished work.

<!-- p23 -->
Water baptism is closely connected with the **promise of the Holy Spirit**. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:38</span>, Peter invites people to repent, be baptized, and receive the gift of the Holy Spirit. Although water baptism publicly indicates the believer’s repentance and faith, the true baptism happens inwardly when the Holy Spirit washes, regenerates, and seals the believer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:13</span>). This spiritual washing makes us members of the body of Christ and empowers us to live a holy life. The **role of the Holy Spirit** in baptism reminds us that salvation is not through rituals but through God’s transforming power.

<!-- p24 -->
It is important to understand the difference between the **symbolic act of water baptism** and the spiritual reality of salvation through the blood of Christ. The Bible makes it clear that salvation is based on faith in Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 10:9-10</span>), not on works, including baptism (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>). However, water baptism is an **act of obedience** that publicly identifies the believer with Christ and the church. It expresses the outward commitment to live as a disciple of Jesus, but it neither adds to nor completes the work of salvation.

<!-- p25 -->
Christ’s water baptism set a followable example for believers, demonstrating humility, obedience, and readiness for service. For Christians today, water baptism is a public declaration of faith, symbolizing dying to sin and being raised to new life. Although it is Jesus’ command and an important step of obedience, it is not necessary for salvation. True cleansing comes through faith in the blood of Jesus, and the power for the Christian life comes through the work of the Holy Spirit within the believer. Baptism unites us with Christ and His church, and reminds us of the inner transformation and renewal that God has already accomplished.

<!-- p26 -->
Since we have established that water baptism is an outward sign of faith, we now turn to the baptism of the Holy Spirit, which empowers believers for service and ministry.

<!-- p27 -->
## Baptism of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p28 -->
Baptism in the Holy Spirit is a profound and transformative experience that God has promised to believers to empower them for service, ministry, and holy living. Every believer, when born again, receives the Holy Spirit—this is the way we are made spiritually alive. But afterward we should also receive baptism in the Holy Spirit, which grants us boldness, spiritual gifts, and power for effective ministry.

<!-- p29 -->
Water baptism is a public declaration of faith and a sign of the cleansing from sin through the blood of Christ; baptism in the Spirit represents God’s empowering presence in the believer’s life. Together, the two kinds of baptism form complementary aspects of the Christian way of life—one as an outward sign of obedience and the other as an inward source of power and renewal. The baptism of the Holy Spirit concerns the Spirit upon us rather than within. At salvation all believers receive the Spirit within them, but when God’s Spirit comes upon them, they receive power and boldness for service.

<!-- p30 -->
The baptism in the Holy Spirit was foretold by John the Baptist, who said,

<!-- p31 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p32 -->
> **<u>Matthew 3:11</u>** (NKJV)
>
> I indeed baptize you with water unto repentance, but He who comes after me is mightier than I; I am not worthy even to carry His sandals. He will baptize you with the Holy Spirit and fire.
>

<!-- p33 -->
</span>

<!-- p34 -->
This promise pointed to a greater work that Jesus would do after His resurrection—sending the Holy Spirit to indwell and empower His followers. Jesus confirmed this when He instructed His disciples to wait to receive the gift of the Spirit, explaining that they would receive power to be His witnesses (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>). The fulfillment of this promise came on the Day of Pentecost when the disciples were filled with the Holy Spirit, evidenced by speaking in tongues, prophesying, and boldly proclaiming the gospel (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:1-4</span>).

<!-- p35 -->
This outpouring marked the beginning of the church age and showed that the Spirit’s power is now available to all believers. The promise was not limited to the apostles but extends to all who believe, as Peter said, “This promise is for you and for your children and for all who are far off—that is, for all whom the Lord our God will call” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="39" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:39</span>).

<!-- p36 -->
### The Spirit within vs. the Spirit upon

<!-- p37 -->
When someone believes in Jesus, the Holy Spirit gives them new life within. This is what it means to be born again (**regeneration**). At that very moment of faith, the Holy Spirit comes to dwell within the believer, making them spiritually alive and incorporating them into God’s family (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:5-6</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>). This is the new birth or being born again, as Jesus described in John 3.

<!-- p38 -->
Biblical basis:

<!-- p39 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>* - “He saved us through the washing of regeneration and renewal by the Holy Spirit.”

<!-- p40 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>* - The Holy Spirit seals believers as a **guarantee** of their inheritance.

<!-- p41 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:9</span>* - "Whoever does not have the Spirit of Christ does not belong to Him."

<!-- p42 -->
This work of regeneration is common to all believers at the time of salvation and establishes the Spirit’s permanent indwelling. It is not dependent on any external act, such as water baptism, and guarantees salvation by grace through faith.

<!-- p43 -->
In contrast, the baptism in the Holy Spirit is a distinct and subsequent experience that empowers believers for service and witness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>). While regeneration relates to new life and identity, the baptism of the Spirit focuses on the descent of power **upon** and the release of spiritual gifts. This baptism often brings signs and manifestations of the gifts of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p44 -->
Scriptural basis:

<!-- p45 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p46 -->
> **<u>Acts 1:8</u>** (NKJV)
>
> But you shall receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you; and you shall be witnesses to Me in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the end of the earth."
>

<!-- p47 -->
</span>

<!-- p48 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:15-17</span>* - The Samaritans had believed and had been baptized, but later they received the Holy Spirit through prayer and the laying on of hands.

<!-- p49 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 19:6</span>* - The disciples at Ephesus, who had already believed, received the Holy Spirit when Paul laid his hands on them.

<!-- p50 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit does not replace regeneration but builds upon it, equipping the believer for spiritual ministry and boldness in service. The baptism in the Holy Spirit is a free gift for those who are saved, not a condition for being saved. It is also not a requirement that a person manifest any particular gift of the Holy Spirit to prove their 'baptism' in the Holy Spirit, but we should expect the baptism of the Holy Spirit to be manifested today just as it was in the book of Acts, with signs and boldness! Some people make the mistake of thinking that if someone does not speak in tongues they are not saved, or that they have not received the baptism in the Holy Spirit. Not everyone will receive every gift or manifestation of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29-31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:29-31</span>).

<!-- p51 -->
## How to receive the baptism of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p52 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit is described in Scripture as a gift promised to believers and received by faith. It is distinct from salvation and is primarily for power and witness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>). The baptism of the Holy Spirit is an empowering gift for believers, promised by Jesus and fulfilled on the Day of Pentecost. It is received through repentance, faith, and asking God. Although the laying on of hands is often used, ultimately God is the one who baptizes with the Spirit.

<!-- p53 -->
Scripture presents speaking in tongues and prophecy as a common sign, but the purpose of this baptism is much more - to provide boldness, power, and spiritual gifts for ministry. Every believer is encouraged to seek this experience as part of their spiritual journey, trusting that God will equip them for His work.

<!-- p54 -->
The Bible outlines a few basic principles for how this baptism is received:

<!-- p55 -->
### **1. The baptism of the Holy Spirit is a free gift from God**

<!-- p56 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit is not something believers can earn—it is a free gift given by God. God wants to give good gifts to His children and is eager to give the Holy Spirit to those who ask with faith and a desire to be empowered.

<!-- p57 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p58 -->
> **<u>Luke 11:13</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Therefore, if you, being evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to those who ask Him!"
>

<!-- p59 -->
</span>

<!-- p60 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="38-39" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p61 -->
> **<u>Acts 2:38-39</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Then Peter said to them, "Repent, and let every one of you be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of your sins؛ and you shall receive the gift of the Holy Spirit۔
>
> For the promise is to you and to your children and to all who are afar off، that is, as many as the Lord our God will call."

###  **2. By faith ask, seek, and find**

<!-- p64 -->
The Scriptures emphasize that baptism in the Holy Spirit is obtained through asking in prayer and expectant faith۔ Faith, together with a readiness to ask and receive, is the key to the experience of baptism in the Spirit۔ If you are not sure that God wants to give you the baptism of the Holy Spirit, then ask Him about it، He is a good Father! When He prompts you، it is possible that you will feel in your body a tangible presence of God that you have never felt before۔ It is okay if you do not feel it! God is a big God, and He does not need to be confined to one perfect formula۔ If you feel that God is drawing your heart, yield to Him!

## Gifts of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p79 -->
When the Holy Spirit was poured out، and people were baptized in the Holy Spirit، there were often manifestations of the Holy Spirit along with it۔

<!-- p80 -->
There are several lists of gifts in the New Testament:

<!-- p81 -->
- Motivational gifts given by God: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:6-8</span>

<!-- p82 -->
- This is discussed in the chapter on the Church.

<!-- p83 -->
- The fivefold ministry gifts given by Jesus: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-13</span>

<!-- p84 -->
- This is discussed in the chapter on the Church.

<!-- p85 -->
- The manifestation gifts given by the Holy Spirit: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span>

<!-- p86 -->
- It is discussed here.

<!-- p87 -->
Each set of these gifts has a different purpose and operates in a different way, but together they point to the **diversity** and **unity** of gifts within the **body of Christ**. Each gift plays a unique role in fulfilling God's purposes.

<!-- p88 -->
### Purposes &amp; Methods of the Gifts

<!-- p89 -->
The primary purpose of the gifts of the Holy Spirit is to build up the body of Christ and glorify God:

<!-- p90 -->
- **Edification** - strengthening and encouraging believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
- **Unity** - promoting harmony and mutual cooperation within the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:12-13</span>).

<!-- p92 -->
- **Evangelism** - displaying God's power as a testimony before unbelievers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="43" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:43</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:6-7</span>).

<!-- p93 -->
- **Service** - meeting others' needs through acts of mercy and generosity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:6-8</span>).

<!-- p94 -->
These gifts are not for personal glory but for the common good (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:7</span>). They function best when used in love and humility, and they reflect the character of Christ (1 Corinthians 13).

<!-- p95 -->
Paul emphasized that spiritual gifts should be used decently and in order (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:40</span>). This ensures that the use of the gifts promotes unity, peace, and understanding rather than disorder or division.

<!-- p96 -->
- Prophecies should be tested (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:29</span>).

<!-- p97 -->
- When a message in tongues is given to the church, pray that it may be interpreted (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:27-28</span>).

<!-- p98 -->
- Gifts should always result in **edification** for the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:12</span>).

<!-- p99 -->
This order does not quench the Spirit but ensures that the gifts operate in ways that honor God and benefit others.

<!-- p100 -->
The use of spiritual gifts must always be based on love. Paul, in an entire chapter ( <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 13</span> ), explains that even the greatest spiritual gifts are meaningless without love. Love provides the motivation and framework that guide the use of the gifts in such a way that they build up others rather than tearing them down.

<!-- p101 -->
Furthermore, the gifts operate **as the Spirit wills** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:11</span>). They can neither be produced nor controlled by human effort-they are given sovereignly and directed by the power of the Holy Spirit, often in moments of need or ministry. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 13:8</span> says that eventually prophecy and tongues will cease. In theology, those who believe that the gifts of the Holy Spirit have ceased are called ‘cessationists’. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 13:10</span> says “When the perfect \[Jesus\] comes, that wish is in part will be cause to cease”. Since Jesus has not yet returned for His church, the gifts are still at work on the earth.

<!-- p102 -->
It is very important to understand that the **gifts of the Spirit** should not be separated from the **fruit of the Spirit** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-23</span>). While the gifts display power, the fruit displays character—love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control.

<!-- p103 -->
- **Gifts without fruit**: A believer may operate in gifts but lack maturity or character, which can lead to pride, misuse, or harm.

<!-- p104 -->
- **Fruit without gifts**: A believer may have godly character but lack the enablement to carry out ministry effectively.

<!-- p105 -->
Both are necessary. The gifts equip believers for ministry. The fruit is a sign of their spiritual health (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 7:16</span>) and provides a ‘pleasant taste’ to those who use it.

<!-- p106 -->
### Explanation of the 9 manifest gifts of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p107 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span> lists the 9 manifest gifts of the Holy Spirit:

<!-- p108 -->
1.  **Word of Wisdom** - Supernatural insight into God's purposes or direction in a situation. It applies divine wisdom to complex problems or decisions (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:10</span>).

<!-- p109 -->
2.  **Word of Knowledge** - A supernatural disclosure of information about a person, event, or condition that could not have been known naturally (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 4:17-18</span>).

<!-- p110 -->
3.  **Faith** - The supernatural ability to trust God for miraculous things, even in impossible situations. This gift inspires boldness and confidence (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 3:6-7</span>).

<!-- p111 -->
4.  **Gifts of Healings** - Supernatural power to heal physical, emotional, or spiritual diseases and bring complete restoration (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 3:6-8</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 9:17-18</span>).

<!-- p112 -->
5.  **Working of Miracles** - The ability to perform supernatural acts that challenge natural laws, such as multiplying food or raising the dead (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40-41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 9:40-41</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 2:9-11</span>).

<!-- p113 -->
6.  **Prophecy** - Declaring God's truth or will under the prompting of the Holy Spirit. It is encouraging, edifying, and consoling (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>).

<!-- p114 -->
7.  **Discerning of Spirits (Discernment)** - The ability to recognize whether a spiritual influence is from God, from the enemy, or from a human source (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 16:16-18</span>).

<!-- p115 -->
8.  **Various Kinds of Tongues** - Speaking in languages unknown to the speaker, often as worship, prayer, or as a sign to unbelievers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:4</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:22</span>).

<!-- p116 -->
9.  **Interpretation of Tongues** - The supernatural ability to interpret a message spoken in tongues so that the church may be edified (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:27</span>).

<!-- p117 -->
Paul makes it clear that all the gifts are valuable and should work together for the common good of the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:7</span>). However, he also encourages believers to desire the greater gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:31</span>). The greater gifts are generally understood to be those that build up the church more directly, such as prophecy (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:1-5</span>).

<!-- p118 -->
- Particular emphasis is placed on prophecy because it strengthens, encourages, and comforts others (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>). Paul instructs believers to earnestly desire this gift because it promotes clarity and understanding in the church.

<!-- p119 -->
- Gifts like tongues are considered very valuable for personal edification and prayer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:4</span>), but Paul teaches that when a message in tongues is given to the church, one should pray for interpretation so that the church's edification is ensured (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:27-28</span>).

<!-- p120 -->
Although the Holy Spirit gives gifts، we should bow before the Holy Spirit and be subject to Him so that He may use them۔ Like muscles and abilities، when we practice submitting to the Holy Spirit in the use of His gifts, we become more confident and reliable in their use۔
