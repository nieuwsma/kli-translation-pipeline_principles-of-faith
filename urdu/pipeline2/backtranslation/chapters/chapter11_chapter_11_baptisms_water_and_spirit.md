# Chapter 11 - Baptisms-Water and Spirit

<!-- p1 -->
In our modern era, which has become detached from Jewish roots, baptism is generally understood only with reference to water baptism and the baptism of the Holy Spirit (Pentecost).

<!-- p2 -->
The Epistle to the Hebrews was written for a Jewish audience. Most Christians in our modern era do not have Jewish roots or heritage. Without Jewish roots and culture, many of us lack the cultural context that was common among first-century Messianic Jews (those Jewish believers who confessed Jesus). Since we were not raised in the Jewish tradition, we generally do not know much about the festivals, fasts, prayers, washings, rites, and customs with which Jewish believers were familiar.

<!-- p3 -->
The Greek word for baptisms or washings is: **Baptismos** (bap-tis-mos’ - **G909**) - the act of immersing or washing. It refers to religious washings or ritual purification. It can also denote immersion, rites of purification, or washings performed in Jewish and Christian contexts.

<!-- p4 -->
Baptisms are among the foundational teachings outlined in Hebrews:

<!-- p5 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="6" data-verses="1-2" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p6 -->
> **<u>Hebrews 6:1-2</u>** (NKJV) ***1** Therefore, leaving the discussion of the elementary principles of Christ, let us go on to perfection, not laying again the foundation of repentance from dead works and of faith toward God, **2** of the doctrine of baptisms, of laying on of hands, of resurrection of the dead, and of eternal judgment.*

<!-- p7 -->
</span>

<!-- p8 -->
## Old Covenant Washings - Historical Background

<!-- p9 -->
In the Old Covenant, ritual washings played a central role in maintaining outward purity before God and as a symbol of holiness. These washings were prescribed in the Law of Moses and applied both to the priests who served in the Tabernacle and to the children of Israel. For example, the priests were required to wash their hands and feet at the bronze laver before entering the Tabernacle or approaching the altar, which was a sign of their preparation for service before a holy God (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="30" data-verses="18-21" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Exodus 30:18-21</span>). Likewise, the rites for cleansing from impurity—such as touching a dead body or encountering skin diseases—involved washing with water as a sign of restoration to a state of purity (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="LEV" data-chapter="14" data-verses="8" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Leviticus 14:8</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="NUM" data-chapter="19" data-verses="17-19" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Numbers 19:17-19</span>). These practices were performed as outward expressions of an inward desire for purity, highlighting the importance of being set apart for God.

<!-- p10 -->
The ritual washings of the Old Testament had to be repeated in order to maintain purity. Unlike a permanent solution, these rites provided only temporary external cleansing and did not address the deeper problem of sin. The author of Hebrews highlights this deficiency, noting that these practices were “symbolic for the present time” and could not cleanse the worshiper’s mind and conscience (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-verses="9-10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 9:9-10</span>). These washings served as a continual reminder of human weakness and the need for something greater—a definitive purification that could cleanse not only the body but also the heart and soul.

<!-- p11 -->
The ritual washings of the Old Covenant find their fulfillment in Christ, who through His sacrifice provides complete and eternal cleansing. Unlike the temporary rites of the Law, the blood of Christ thoroughly purifies, cleansing not only the body but also the conscience (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-verses="13-14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 9:13-14</span>). Through His death and resurrection, Jesus accomplished what the Old Testament rites only pointed to—true reconciliation with God. <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-verses="22" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 10:22</span> calls believers to “draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, having our hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience and our bodies washed with pure water.” This language connects Christ’s work to the imagery of Old Covenant washings while also revealing its far superior efficacy. His sacrifice does not merely cover sin but removes it completely (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="1" data-verses="7" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 John 1:7</span>). Thus, the ritual washings were a shadow pointing to the deeper cleansing that only Christ could provide, fulfilling humanity’s need for sanctification through divine intervention.

<!-- p12 -->
To reiterate: Christians are under no obligation to perform any ritual washing. Washings were not a means of salvation in the Old Covenant, nor do they provide salvation in the New. A ritual washing will neither elevate our standing before God nor make us pleasing in His sight. Paul strongly condemns the Judaizers in Galatia (read Galatians) who tried to make the conditions of the Jewish framework mandatory for salvation (e.g., circumcision). Salvation is by faith in Jesus alone!

<!-- p13 -->
## John’s Baptism

<!-- p14 -->
The writings of the New Testament mention several baptisms. We have already discussed the washings and baptisms of Jewish tradition.

<!-- p15 -->
The ministry of John the Baptist centered on preparing the way for the coming of Jesus Christ. His baptism, called a baptism of repentance for the forgiveness of sins (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="1" data-verses="4" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Mark 1:4</span>), called people to confess their sins, turn their hearts to God, and prepare for the coming of the Messiah. It was an outward sign of inward repentance, symbolizing the desire for spiritual cleansing and readiness for the kingdom of God. However, John’s baptism was temporary and preparatory—it pointed to the greater work that Christ would accomplish. Although John’s baptism focused on repentance, it did not provide complete forgiveness or spiritual new birth, because it did not include the indwelling and empowering of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-verses="11-12" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 3:11-12</span>). Rather, John openly declared that One greater than he was coming who would baptize not only with water but with the Holy Spirit and fire (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="3" data-verses="16" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Luke 3:16</span>).

<!-- p16 -->
John’s baptism was fulfilled by and surpassed through the death, burial, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. Christian baptism, which Jesus instituted and the apostles administered, symbolizes a deeper reality—union with Christ, dying to sin, and rising to new life (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-verses="3-4" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 6:3-4</span>). Unlike John’s baptism, Christian baptism is directly connected to faith in Jesus’ atoning sacrifice and the indwelling of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-verses="38" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 2:38</span>). The outpouring of the Holy Spirit at Pentecost marked the sign of the New Covenant and the beginning of a life empowered by the Spirit, which John’s baptism could not impart. For this reason, John’s baptism is no longer in effect, as it has been replaced by the baptism commanded by Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-verses="19" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 28:19</span>) and which includes repentance, faith, and the sealing of believers by the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-verses="13-14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>).

<!-- p17 -->
## Christ’s Baptism

<!-- p18 -->
Christ’s baptism became a powerful example for His followers and established the foundation for Christian water baptism. Although Jesus was sinless, He consented to be baptized by John “to fulfill all righteousness” (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-verses="15" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 3:15</span>). In doing so, He identified Himself with humanity’s need for repentance and pointed ahead to His own death, burial, and resurrection. At His baptism the Holy Spirit descended like a dove, and the Father declared, “This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased” (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-verses="16-17" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 3:16-17</span>). This moment marked the beginning of Jesus’ public ministry and revealed the unity of the Father, the Son, and the Spirit, foreshadowing the spiritual transformation that believers would experience through faith in Christ.

<!-- p19 -->
Later Jesus commanded His followers to be baptized, commissioning them: “Go therefore and make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit” (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-verses="19" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 28:19</span>). Water baptism, as instituted by Christ, became an **act of obedience** and a **public declaration of faith** for believers. It represents their identification with Jesus’ death, burial, and resurrection (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-verses="3-4" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 6:3-4</span>). Going into the water symbolizes dying to sin, and coming out of the water depicts rising to new life in Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-verses="12" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Colossians 2:12</span>). Yet it is important to clarify that this outward act is not the source of salvation but the **outward sign** of the **inward work of grace** that has already been accomplished through the blood of Jesus.

<!-- p20 -->
Water baptism is a **sign** of the cleansing power of Christ’s blood and the renewal of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-verses="26" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 5:26</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-verses="5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Titus 3:5</span>). It is a sign of repentance, faith, and a pledge to follow Jesus. It is the outward testimony of an inward transformation—a way by which believers openly declare their fidelity to Christ and their inclusion in the body of Christ, that is, the Church (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-verses="41" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 2:41</span>). Although it is commanded by Jesus and modeled in the early Church, it does not give a person **salvation**. Salvation is only by grace through faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-verses="8-9" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>), and true cleansing occurs spiritually through the blood of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-verses="5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Revelation 1:5</span>). Water baptism is a response to that salvation, an act of obedience rather than a condition for it.

<!-- p21 -->
In the New Testament, water baptism is consistently associated with **repentance and faith** (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-verses="38" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 2:38</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-verses="16" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Mark 16:16</span>), and both require a personal decision to follow Christ—something infants cannot do. Baptism is a sign of a believer’s deliberate identification with Christ’s death, burial, and resurrection (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-verses="3-4" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 6:3-4</span>), as well as of his or her covenant to live in obedience before Him. Some denominations (for example, Catholic, Lutheran) liken infant baptism to circumcision as a covenant sign; however, since infants cannot express personal faith or repentance, the practice of **infant baptism** is not aligned with the biblical pattern of **believers’ baptism**. Instead, many churches hold **dedication ceremonies** for infants, following the examples of Hannah dedicating Samuel to the Lord (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1SA" data-chapter="1" data-verses="27-28" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Samuel 1:27-28</span>) and Mary and Joseph presenting Jesus in the temple (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="2" data-verses="22" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Luke 2:22</span>). Dedication is not a substitute for baptism but an expression of the parents’ covenant to raise their child in the faith, teach him or her God’s Word, and model a godly life until the child is able to decide for himself or herself to follow Christ and be baptized.

<!-- p22 -->
In contrast to the **ritual washings** of the Old Covenant, which required repeated rites for purification, Christian baptism is a one-time act that reflects the finished work of grace. The Old Testament washings were symbols of outward purity and pointed to the deeper spiritual cleansing that comes through Christ. By contrast, New Testament baptism is a sign of the inner renewal and new birth that is accomplished by the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-verses="5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Titus 3:5</span>). It points to the believer’s inclusion in the New Covenant, where cleansing from sin is permanent and based on Christ’s finished work.

<!-- p23 -->
Water baptism is closely connected with the **promise of the Holy Spirit**. In <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-verses="38" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 2:38</span> Peter invites the people to repent, be baptized, and receive the gift of the Holy Spirit. While water baptism publicly symbolizes a believer’s repentance and faith, the true baptism takes place inwardly when the Holy Spirit washes, regenerates, and seals the believer (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-verses="13" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 12:13</span>). This spiritual washing makes us members of the body of Christ and empowers us to live a holy life. The **role of the Holy Spirit** in baptism reminds us that salvation comes not through rituals but through God’s transforming power.

<!-- p24 -->
It is important to distinguish between the **symbolic act of water baptism** and the spiritual reality of salvation through the blood of Christ. The Bible clearly teaches that salvation is based on faith in Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="10" data-verses="9-10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 10:9-10</span>) and not on works, including baptism (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-verses="8-9" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>). However, water baptism is an **act of obedience** that publicly identifies the believer with Christ and the Church. It is an outward pledge to live as a disciple of Jesus, but it neither adds to nor completes the work of salvation.

<!-- p25 -->
Christ’s water baptism became a model for believers to follow, displaying humility, obedience, and readiness for service. For Christians today, water baptism serves as a public declaration of faith, symbolizing dying to sin and rising to new life. Although it is commanded by Jesus and is an important step of obedience, it is not necessary for salvation. True cleansing occurs through faith in the blood of Jesus, and the power for the Christian life comes from the work of the Holy Spirit within the believer. Baptism unites us with Christ and His Church, and reminds us of the inward transformation and renewal that God has already accomplished.

<!-- p26 -->
Since we have clarified water baptism as an outward sign of faith, we now turn to the baptism in the Holy Spirit, which empowers believers for ministry and service.

<!-- p27 -->
## Baptism in the Holy Spirit

<!-- p28 -->
Baptism in the Holy Spirit is a profound and transformative experience promised by God to empower believers for ministry, service, and holy living. Every believer receives the Holy Spirit when he or she is born again—that is how we are made spiritually alive. But afterward, we should also be baptized in the Holy Spirit, which grants us boldness, spiritual gifts, and power for effective service.

<!-- p29 -->
Water baptism is a public declaration of faith and a symbol of the cleansing of sin through the blood of Christ, whereas the baptism of the Spirit represents God’s empowering presence in a believer’s life. Together, these two forms of baptism constitute complementary aspects of the Christian way of life—one as an outward sign of obedience and the other as an inward source of power and renewal. The baptism in the Holy Spirit is not the Spirit within but the Spirit upon us. At salvation all believers have the Spirit within, but when God’s Spirit comes upon them, they receive power and boldness for service.

<!-- p30 -->
The baptism in the Holy Spirit was foretold by John the Baptist, who said,

<!-- p31 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="fragment" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-verses="11" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p32 -->
> **<u>Matthew 3:11</u>** *I baptize you with water for repentance, but the one who is coming after me… he will baptize you with the Holy Spirit and fire”*

<!-- p33 -->
</span>

<!-- p34 -->
This promise pointed to the greater work Jesus would accomplish after His resurrection—sending the Holy Spirit to indwell and empower His followers. Jesus confirmed this when He instructed His disciples to wait for the gift of the Spirit, making clear that they would receive power to be His witnesses (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-verses="8" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 1:8</span>). This promise was fulfilled on the day of Pentecost, when the disciples were filled with the Holy Spirit, evidenced by speaking in tongues, prophesying, and boldly proclaiming the Good News (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-verses="1-4" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 2:1-4</span>).

<!-- p35 -->
This outpouring marked the beginning of the **Church age** and demonstrated that the power of the Spirit is now available to all believers. This promise was not limited to the apostles but extends to all who believe, as Peter said, “The promise is for you and your children and for all who are far off - for all whom the Lord our God will call” (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-verses="39" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 2:39</span>).

<!-- p36 -->
### The Spirit within versus the Spirit upon us

<!-- p37 -->
When someone places faith in Jesus, the Holy Spirit gives him or her new life within. This is what it means to be born again (that is, new birth). At that moment of faith, the Holy Spirit takes up residence **within** the believer, making him or her spiritually alive and adopting him or her into God’s family (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-verses="5-6" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 3:5-6</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-verses="5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Titus 3:5</span>). This is new birth, or being born again, as Jesus described in John 3.

<!-- p38 -->
Biblical basis:

<!-- p39 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-verses="5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Titus 3:5</span>* - “He saved us through the washing of regeneration and renewal of the Holy Spirit.”

<!-- p40 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-verses="13-14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>* - The Holy Spirit seals believers as a **guarantee** of their inheritance.

<!-- p41 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-verses="9" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 8:9</span>* - "If anyone does not have the Spirit of Christ, he is not His."

<!-- p42 -->
This work of new birth is common to all believers at the time of salvation and establishes the Spirit’s permanent indwelling. It does not depend on any external act, such as water baptism, and guarantees salvation by grace through faith.

<!-- p43 -->
In contrast, the baptism in the Holy Spirit is a distinct and subsequent experience that empowers believers for ministry and witness (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-verses="8" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 1:8</span>). While new birth relates to new life and identity, the baptism of the Spirit focuses on empowerment from **above** and the release of spiritual gifts. This baptism is often accompanied by signs and manifestations of the Holy Spirit’s gifts.

<!-- p44 -->
Biblical foundation:

<!-- p45 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-verses="8" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p46 -->
> **<u>Acts 1:8</u>** *(NKJV) But when the Holy Spirit comes upon you, you will receive power؛ and you will be My witnesses in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the end of the earth.*

<!-- p47 -->
</span>

<!-- p48 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-verses="15-17" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 8:15-17</span>* - The Samaritans had believed and been baptized, but later received the Holy Spirit through prayer and the laying on of hands.

<!-- p49 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-verses="6" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 19:6</span>* - The disciples at Ephesus, who were already believers, received the Holy Spirit when Paul laid his hands on them.

<!-- p50 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit does not replace new birth but builds upon it, and equips the believer for spiritual ministry and boldness in service. Baptism in the Holy Spirit is a free gift for those who are saved, not a condition for being saved. It is not necessary that a person manifest some <u>particular</u> gift of the Holy Spirit to prove their 'baptism' in the Holy Spirit, but we should expect the baptism of the Holy Spirit to be manifested today as it was in the Book of Acts, with signs and boldness! Some err by thinking that if one does not speak in tongues, he or she is not saved, or has not received the baptism of the Holy Spirit. Not everyone will have every gift or every manifestation of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-verses="29-31" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 12:29-31</span>).

<!-- p51 -->
## How to receive the baptism of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p52 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit is described in Scripture as a gift promised to believers and received through faith. It is distinct from salvation and is primarily for empowerment and witness (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-verses="8" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 1:8</span>). The baptism of the Holy Spirit is a gift of empowerment for believers, promised by Jesus and fulfilled on the day of Pentecost. It is received through repentance, faith, and asking God. Although the laying on of hands is often used as a means, ultimately it is God who baptizes in the Spirit.

<!-- p53 -->
Scripture presents speaking in tongues and prophecy as a common sign, but the purpose of this baptism is much more than that - to provide boldness, power, and spiritual gifts for service. Every believer is encouraged to seek this experience as part of their spiritual journey, with the assurance that God will equip them for His service.

<!-- p54 -->
The Bible clarifies a few basic principles about how this baptism is received:

<!-- p55 -->
### **1. The baptism of the Holy Spirit is a free gift from God**

<!-- p56 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit is not something believers can earn-it is a free gift from God. God wants to give good gifts to His children and is eager to give the Holy Spirit to those who ask with faith and a desire for empowerment.

<!-- p57 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="11" data-verses="13" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p58 -->
> **<u>Luke 11:13</u>** (NKJV) *If you then, being evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to those who ask Him!"*

<!-- p59 -->
</span>

<!-- p60 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-verses="38-39" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p61 -->
> **<u>Acts 2:38-39</u>** (NKJV) **38** *Then Peter said to them, "Repent, and let every one of you be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of sins; and you will receive the gift of the Holy Spirit. **39** For the promise is to you and to your children, and to all who are afar off, as many as the Lord our God will call."*

<!-- p62 -->
</span>

<!-- p63 -->
- **2. Ask, seek, and receive by faith**

<!-- p64 -->
The Holy Scriptures emphasize that baptism in the Holy Spirit is received through asking in prayer and faith-filled expectation. Faith, combined with asking and a readiness to receive, is the key to experiencing the baptism of the Holy Spirit. If you are not sure that God wants to give you the baptism of the Holy Spirit, ask Him about it; He is a good Father! When He prompts you, you may feel in your body a tangible presence of God such as you have never felt before. If that does not happen, that’s okay too! God is a great God, and He does not need to be confined to a single perfect formula. If you feel that God is pulling on your heart, yield yourself to Him!

<!-- p65 -->
- **<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-verses="15-17" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 8:15-17</span>** - Peter and John prayed and laid their **hands** on the believers, and they **received the Holy Spirit**.

<!-- p66 -->
- **<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="10" data-verses="44-46" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 10:44-46</span>** - Cornelius and his household **received the Holy Spirit** while Peter was preaching - without hands being laid on them - showing that ultimately **God is the one who baptizes**.

<!-- p67 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="fragment" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="3" data-verses="2" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p68 -->
> **<u>Galatians 3:2</u>** (NKJV) *… Did you receive the Spirit by the works of the law, or by the hearing of faith?*

<!-- p69 -->
</span>

<!-- p70 -->
### **3. The practice of laying on of hands (optional)**

<!-- p71 -->
While some received the Holy Spirit spontaneously (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="10" data-verses="44" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 10:44</span>), others received through the laying on of hands by other believers. The laying on of hands is a biblical pattern, but not a requirement. The Holy Spirit ultimately responds to faith, not to rituals.

<!-- p72 -->

## Gifts of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p79 -->
When the Holy Spirit was poured out, and people were baptized in the Holy Spirit, it was often accompanied by outward manifestations of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p80 -->
There are several lists of gifts in the New Testament:

<!-- p81 -->
- Motivational gifts given by God: <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-verses="6-8" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 12:6-8</span>

<!-- p82 -->
- Discussed in the chapter on the Church.

<!-- p83 -->
- The fivefold ministry gifts given by Jesus: <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-verses="11-13" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 4:11-13</span>

<!-- p84 -->
- Discussed in the chapter on the Church.

<!-- p85 -->
- The manifestation gifts given by the Holy Spirit: <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-verses="8-10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span>

<!-- p86 -->
- Discussed here.

<!-- p87 -->
Each set of these gifts has been given for different purposes and operates in different ways, but together they point to the **diversity** and **unity** of gifts within the **Body of Christ**. Each gift has a unique role in fulfilling God's purposes.

<!-- p88 -->
### Purposes & Operation of the Gifts

<!-- p89 -->
The primary purpose of the gifts of the Holy Spirit is to build up the Body of Christ and to glorify God:

<!-- p90 -->
- **Edification** - strengthening and encouraging believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-verses="3" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
- **Unity** - promoting harmony and cooperation within the Church (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-verses="12-13" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 4:12-13</span>).

<!-- p92 -->
- **Evangelism** - demonstrating God's power as a witness to unbelievers (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-verses="43" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 2:43</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-verses="6-7" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 8:6-7</span>).

<!-- p93 -->
- **Service** - meeting others' needs through acts of mercy and generosity (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-verses="6-8" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 12:6-8</span>).

<!-- p94 -->
These gifts are not for personal glory but for the common good (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-verses="7" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 12:7</span>). They function best when used in love and humility, reflecting the character of Christ (1 Corinthians 13).

<!-- p95 -->
Paul emphasized that spiritual gifts should be used decently and in order (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-verses="40" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 14:40</span>). This ensures that the use of the gifts promotes unity, peace, and understanding rather than chaos or division.

<!-- p96 -->
- Prophecies should be tested (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-verses="29" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 14:29</span>).

<!-- p97 -->
- When a message in tongues is given for the Church, pray that it may be interpreted (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-verses="27-28" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 14:27-28</span>).

<!-- p98 -->
- The gifts should always result in the edification of the Church (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-verses="12" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 14:12</span>).

<!-- p99 -->
This order does not quench the Spirit, but ensures that the gifts operate in ways that honor God and benefit others.

<!-- p100 -->
The operation of spiritual gifts must always be rooted in love. Paul devotes an entire chapter (1 Corinthians 13) to explaining that without love, even the greatest spiritual gifts are meaningless. Love provides the motive and structure so that the gifts are used in ways that build others up rather than tear them down.

<!-- p101 -->
Additionally, the gifts operate **as the Spirit wills** (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-verses="11" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 12:11</span>). They can neither be manufactured nor controlled by human effort-they are sovereignly given and empowered by the Holy Spirit, often at moments of need or service. <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="13" data-verses="8" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 13:8</span> says that ultimately prophecy and tongues will cease. Theologically, those who believe that the gifts of the Holy Spirit have ceased are called ‘cessationists’. <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="13" data-verses="10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 13:10</span> says, “When the 'Perfect' \[Jesus\] comes, that which is partial will be done away.” Since Jesus has not yet returned for His Church, the gifts are still operative on earth.

<!-- p102 -->
It is critically important to recognize that the **gifts of the Spirit** must not be separated from the **fruit of the Spirit** (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-verses="22-23" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Galatians 5:22-23</span>). Where the gifts express power, the fruit displays character—love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control.

<!-- p103 -->
- **Gifts without fruit**: A believer may operate in gifts but lack maturity or character, which can give rise to pride, misuse, or harm.

<!-- p104 -->
- **Fruit without gifts**: A believer may have godly character but lack empowerment for effective ministry.

<!-- p105 -->
Both are necessary. The gifts equip believers for service. The fruit is a sign of their spiritual health (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="7" data-verses="16" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 7:16</span>) and provides a ‘pleasant taste’ to those who receive it.

<!-- p106 -->
### Explanation of the 9 manifestation gifts of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p107 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-verses="8-10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span> lists the 9 manifestation gifts of the Holy Spirit:

<!-- p108 -->
1.  **Word of wisdom** - Supernatural insight about God’s purposes or the direction of a situation. It applies divine wisdom to complex issues or decisions (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-verses="10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 6:10</span>).

<!-- p109 -->
2.  **Word of knowledge** - A supernatural disclosure of information about a person, event, or condition that could not have been known naturally (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="4" data-verses="17-18" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 4:17-18</span>).

<!-- p110 -->
3.  **Faith** - The supernatural capacity to trust God for miraculous things even in impossible situations. This gift sparks boldness and confidence (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="3" data-verses="6-7" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 3:6-7</span>).

<!-- p111 -->
4.  **Gifts of healings** - Supernatural power to heal physical, emotional, or spiritual ailments and to bring wholeness (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="3" data-verses="6-8" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 3:6-8</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="9" data-verses="17-18" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 9:17-18</span>).

<!-- p112 -->
5.  **Working of miracles** - The ability to perform supernatural acts that defy natural laws, such as the multiplication of food or raising the dead (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="9" data-verses="40-41" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 9:40-41</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="2" data-verses="9-11" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 2:9-11</span>).

<!-- p113 -->
6.  **Prophecy** - Declaring God’s truth or will under the prompting of the Holy Spirit. It should be encouraging, edifying, and comforting (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-verses="3" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>).

<!-- p114 -->
7.  **Discerning of spirits (discernment)** - The ability to recognize whether a spiritual influence is from God, from the enemy, or of human origin (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-verses="16-18" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 16:16-18</span>).

<!-- p115 -->
8.  **Different kinds of tongues** - Speaking in languages unknown to the speaker, often for worship, prayer, or as a sign to unbelievers (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-verses="4" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 2:4</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-verses="22" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 14:22</span>).

<!-- p116 -->
9.  **Interpretation of tongues** - The supernatural ability to interpret the message spoken in tongues so that the church is edified (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-verses="27" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 14:27</span>).

<!-- p117 -->
Paul makes it clear that all the gifts are valuable and should work together for the common good of the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-verses="7" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 12:7</span>). However, he also encourages believers to desire the greater gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-verses="31" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 12:31</span>). The greater gifts are often understood to be those that more directly edify the church, such as prophecy (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-verses="1-5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 14:1-5</span>).

<!-- p118 -->
- Prophecy is emphasized in particular because it strengthens, encourages, and comforts others (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-verses="3" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>). Paul instructs believers to eagerly desire this gift because it promotes clarity and understanding within the church.

<!-- p119 -->
- Gifts like tongues are very valuable for personal edification and prayer (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-verses="4" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 14:4</span>), but Paul teaches that when a message in tongues is given to the church, pray for interpretation so that the edification of the church is ensured (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-verses="27-28" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 14:27-28</span>).

<!-- p120 -->
While the Holy Spirit gives gifts, we must yield to the Holy Spirit and submit to Him in order to use them. Like muscles and abilities, as we practice yielding to the Holy Spirit to use His gifts, we become more confident and more reliable in their use.
