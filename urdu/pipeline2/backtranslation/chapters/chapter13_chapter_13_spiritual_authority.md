# Chapter 13 - Spiritual Authority

<!-- p1 -->
Jesus is the ultimate and complete source of spiritual authority. As the victorious Christ, He has final authority over everything in heaven and on earth.

<!-- p2 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p3 -->
> **<u>Matthew 28:18-20</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And Jesus came to them and said to them, "All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to Me.
>
> Therefore go and make disciples of all nations, and baptize them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit,
>
> and teach them to observe all things that I have commanded you; and behold, I am with you always, to the end of the world." Amen.
>

<!-- p4 -->
</span>

<!-- p5 -->
In this chapter we will study the authority and power of Christ, the authorities and powers that He has entrusted to us (His Church), and how to operate in the authority of Jesus' name. We will also study angelic and demonic beings and examine their authority, power, and natures.

<!-- p6 -->
By authority is meant having the right to act. By power is meant the ability to carry it out. In the spiritual life both are necessary. Authority is different from power. Let us look at the following table that compares authority &amp; power, and consider a few examples.

<!-- p7 -->
[See Table 7](../tables/table7.md)

<!-- p8 -->
Here are a few examples to help understand the difference between authority and power.

<!-- p9 -->
Example 1: In the workplace, an employee may have the abilities and skills (power) to sell the company's assets. This power is legitimate only when he also has the relevant authorization (authority) from the owners of the company. Without this authority, selling the company's assets is theft.

<!-- p10 -->
Example 2: A police officer receives specialized training in weapons and hand-to-hand combat so that he learns how to arrest and physically subdue a criminal (power). Police officers are given a legally backed duty of office (authority) to enforce criminal laws.

## The Source of Jesus' Authority

<!-- p74 -->
Now that we have seen what authority is, we need to know the source of this authority. Jesus’ authority is both divine and delegated. It proceeds from God the Father, is authenticated through His death and resurrection, and operates in perfect harmony with the Father’s will. Though absolute, His authority respects human free will, revealing God’s love rather than coercive control.

<!-- p75 -->
Jesus’ authority is rooted in His divine identity as the Son of God and is bestowed by the Father. The Father has entrusted all authority to Christ, including judgment and rule over heaven and earth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 11:27</span>). Jesus’ authority reflects the inner unity and love of the Trinity, wherein the Son perfectly carries out the Father’s plan (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="35" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:35</span>).

<!-- p76 -->
Although Jesus, as the Son of God, possessed inherent authority, His victory over sin, death, and hell was fully established through His death and resurrection. His obedience in the sacrifice led to His exaltation (seated at the Father’s right hand), and thus His authority over all creation was solidified. By defeating death, Christ gained absolute dominion over life and eternity.

## Laying on of Hands

<!-- p116 -->
The laying on of hands is an important biblical practice that has been used in both the Old and New Testaments as a sign of blessing, appointment, healing, and the transfer of spiritual authority or gifts۔ It is more than a physical act—it represents spiritual connection and impartation under the direction of God’s authority۔

<!-- p117 -->
In the Old Testament, the laying on of hands was often associated with blessing and consecration۔ For example, Jacob laid his hands on his grandsons Ephraim and Manasseh and blessed them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="48" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 48:14</span>)۔ Likewise, Moses laid hands on Joshua to appoint him as his successor and to transfer leadership authority and wisdom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="NUM" data-chapter="27" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Numbers 27:18-23</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="DEU" data-chapter="34" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Deuteronomy 34:9</span>)۔ This act publicly demonstrated God’s choice and empowerment for the task ahead۔

<!-- p118 -->
In the New Testament, the practice of laying on of hands is closely connected to the ministry of Jesus and the early church۔ Jesus often laid His hands on the sick to heal them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 6:5</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 4:40</span>)، showing both His compassion and His authority over sickness and disease۔ The apostles continued this practice, using it for the giving of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:17</span>), spiritual gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 4:14</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 1:6</span>), and to bless believers۔ It was also used to appoint leaders and missionaries for ministry (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 13:3</span>)۔

<!-- p119 -->
The laying on of hands is a sign of an act of faith and obedience to God’s word۔ It is not a mechanical ritual, but a spiritual practice rooted in dependence on the Holy Spirit۔ For example, in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:6</span> the apostles laid hands on those who were chosen for diaconal service, committing them to God’s service and publicly acknowledging their calling۔ In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 19:6</span> Paul laid hands on the believers, and they received the Holy Spirit, demonstrating its role in spiritual empowerment۔

<!-- p120 -->
However, the Bible also gives warnings about this practice:

<!-- p121 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p122 -->
> **<u>1 Timothy 5:22</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Do not lay hands on anyone hastily, nor share in other people's sins; keep yourself pure.
>

<!-- p123 -->
</span>

<!-- p124 -->
This highlights the importance that discernment and spiritual maturity are necessary when appointing to positions of leadership. As we studied in the chapter on the Church, leaders should be tested and proven so that unqualified or immature believers are not promoted who could damage the body of Christ.

<!-- p125 -->
A few further guidelines for the practice of laying on of hands:

<!-- p126 -->
1.  Contextually appropriate - the person on whom hands are being laid should be a willing participant.

<!-- p127 -->
2.  Physically appropriate - laying on of hands does not include sexual or erotic touch. Practically, placing a hand on the shoulder, hand, or forehead (with permission) are generally acceptable locations in the Western world.

<!-- p128 -->
3.  Culturally appropriate - in some cultures, touch between men and women is not considered appropriate unless they are related by blood or marriage.

<!-- p129 -->
Today the practice of laying on of hands remains a powerful expression of faith and obedience in the Church. It is a means of blessing, healing, commissioning, and empowerment, always pointing to the authority of Christ and the work of the Holy Spirit. Whether praying for the sick, sending out missionaries, or appointing leaders, it is a reminder that believers act not by their own strength but under the authority of Jesus Christ and in accordance with His purposes.

Now that we have studied the authority of Jesus and the authority of the believer, we will discuss other beings of the spiritual realm, namely angels and demons.

<!-- p131 -->
## Angels and Demons

<!-- p132 -->
### Angels

<!-- p133 -->
Angels are an important part of God’s creation, who, as His messengers and agents, fulfill His divine purposes. The Bible reveals a spiritual realm where these beings dwell, who are powerful, intelligent, and obedient to God’s commands. Although angels are often unseen, their presence and actions influence human history and spiritual warfare. Understanding their roles, kinds, and activities grants us a deeper grasp of God’s plan and the unseen realities around us.

<!-- p134 -->
The word "angel" itself means "messenger," which highlights their primary duty. Angels act in submission to God’s will, whether delivering messages, providing protection, executing judgment, or engaging in spiritual warfare. They are not to be worshiped but are examples of obedience and reverence, always directing toward the glory of God. This chapter examines the nature, kinds, and roles of angels and sheds light on their significance in Scripture and in our present lives.

<!-- p135 -->
In Scripture angels appear as **messengers**, **servants**, and **warriors**. Angels often serve as messengers, delivering important announcements, warnings, and encouragement. They reveal God’s plans and provide guidance in times of need (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26-38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 1:26-38</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 2:13</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 1:1</span>). Angels are active in protecting, strengthening, and delivering believers. They minister to God’s people in times of trouble and provide supernatural help (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="91" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 91:11</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 12:7-11</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 4:11</span>). Angels take part in spiritual warfare, contending with demonic forces to advance God’s kingdom and defend believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="DAN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Daniel 10:13</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:7-9</span>).

<!-- p136 -->
#### The Nature of Angels

<!-- p137 -->
Angels are created beings, brought into existence by God before the foundation of the world. They are distinct from humans and possess unique characteristics that equip them for their heavenly duties. The Bible teaches that angels are spiritual beings, not bound by physical limitations, yet they can take on visible form when interacting with humans.

<!-- p138 -->
Angels are described as immortal, meaning they neither die nor grow old. Their number is countless, vast hosts filling the heavenly realm. Although masculine pronouns are commonly used for them, Scripture makes clear that angels are sexless and neither marry nor reproduce. These attributes highlight their purpose as God’s servants, wholly devoted to His will and commands. Angels possess extraordinary qualities that reflect their divine purpose. They are obedient, reverent, wise, and powerful. These traits enable them to carry out God’s will effectively and faithfully.

<!-- p140 -->
Although the Bible highlights many roles and titles of angels, it does not provide a comprehensive classification system۔ However, some angels are identified according to specific roles or functions:

<!-- p141 -->
- **The Angel of the LORD** - Often understood as a pre-incarnate appearance of Christ, appearing with divine authority۔ (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="23" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 23:20-23</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JDG" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Judges 6:11-24</span>*)

<!-- p142 -->
- **Archangels** - High-ranking angels such as Michael, who leads the hosts, and Gabriel, who delivers divine messages۔ (*Jude 9; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:7</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 1:19-26</span>*)

<!-- p143 -->
- **Cherubim** - Guardians of God’s glory and presence, such as in Eden and on the Ark of the Covenant۔ (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 3:24</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 25:22</span>*)

<!-- p144 -->
- **Seraphim** - Worshipers of God, who continually proclaim His holiness۔ (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 6:1-6</span>*)

<!-- p145 -->
- **Elect angels** - Faithful angels who resisted Satan’s rebellion and remained faithful to God۔ (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:21</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 25:41</span>*)

<!-- p146 -->
These classifications highlight the diversity and order of the angelic realm، yet their primary focus is always the fulfillment of God’s purposes۔

<!-- p147 -->
#### Human interaction with angels

<!-- p148 -->
Humans often encounter angels in Scripture، but we are never instructed to command them or pray to them۔ Rather, angels act according to God’s direction۔ They minister to believers as **God’s servants** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 1:14</span>)۔ Their interventions include protection, guidance, and deliverance, and they always point us to God’s power and sovereignty۔

<!-- p149 -->
- **Warnings against angel-worship**: Scripture forbids worshiping angels or seeking encounters with them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:18</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 22:8-9</span>)۔

<!-- p150 -->
- **Prayer to God, not to angels**: Believers should pray to God, who sends angels according to His will (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="91" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 91:11</span>)۔

<!-- p151 -->
- **Encouragement and trust**: Angels remind us of God’s care، but our faith must rest wholly in God, not in angels۔

<!-- p152 -->
Angels are awe-inspiring beings created to serve God and His people۔ Although their work is often unseen, Scripture gives glimpses of their activity, revealing their faithfulness, power, and reverence۔ Angels remind us of God’s provision and protection, yet they always point to His glory۔ As believers, we are called to trust in God’s care, knowing that angels act as His servants to accomplish His divine purposes۔

<!-- p153 -->
### Demons

<!-- p154 -->
Just as the study of angels is necessary to understand the spiritual world، so is the study of demons۔ Scripture clearly describes their activity, influence, and their defeat through Jesus Christ۔ Recognizing their role in spiritual warfare helps believers stand firm in faith without fear۔

<!-- p155 -->
#### Origin and nature of demons

<!-- p156 -->
Demons are the fallen angels who followed Satan’s rebellion against God۔ *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:7-9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 25:41</span>).* Satan, formerly known as Lucifer (“light-bearer”), was a high-ranking angel; he sought to exalt himself above God and was cast out of heaven along with the other rebellious angels۔ *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 14:12-15</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EZK" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ezekiel 28:12-17</span>).*

<!-- p157 -->
Demons are spirits that do not have bodies but can harass and torment humans and animals۔ *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 5:11-13</span>).* They seek to deceive, tempt, oppress, and destroy۔ *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 11:14</span>). God, in contrast,* they are limited, and they are not omnipresent (everywhere), omniscient (all-knowing), omnipotent (all-powerful) نہیں ہیں*.* Their final defeat is certain, and they will face eternal punishment۔ *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 20:10</span>)*

<!-- p158 -->
Scripture describes Satan’s works and activities as follows:

<!-- p159 -->
- Opposes the work of God *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Thessalonians 2:18</span>)*

<!-- p160 -->
- Deceives the world *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 4:4</span>)*

<!-- p161 -->
- Tests and accuses believers *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 22:31</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:10</span>)*

<!-- p162 -->
- Afflicts and harms *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JOB" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Job 1:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 13:16</span>)*

<!-- p163 -->
Satan has many names and attributes، each of which describes a different aspect of his work۔

The Gospels and Epistles further clarify the enemy’s work۔ Demons، being disembodied، usually carry out their work through willing and open-hearted individuals۔ God works through people، Satan does the same۔ This oppression is often marked by torment، self-harm، and destructive behaviors *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 8:27-29</span>).* Their attacks produce fear، temptation، and discouragement *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 12:7</span>).* The most common source of their power (not authority) in a person’s life is through the influence of lies and emotional strongholds *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 10:3-5</span>).*

<!-- p166 -->
It is important to note that not every lie a person believes، every illness of the body، or every stronghold in life is directly the result of demonic influence، however these places of woundedness are those areas the enemy can use to exploit us and keep us in a state of vexation and oppression۔

#### Jesus’ authority over the works of the enemy

<!-- p168 -->
Do not fear، Jesus has complete and absolute authority over the works of the enemy۔ Jesus demonstrated power over demons during His ministry *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 1:34</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 5:1-20</span>).* The works of the enemy—and Satan himself—have been overcome through Jesus’ death and resurrection *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:15</span>).*

<!-- p169 -->
As believers، we have been given delegated authority by Jesus to cast out demons in His name:

<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

The pattern described in Scripture for dealing with an evil or unclean spirit is to take authority over it in the name of Jesus and command it to leave۔ If we ask، the Holy Spirit promises to give us wisdom and discernment! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:5</span>). We also do not need to make it a spectacle، or put on a movie-like drama، or converse with them؛ that is the devil’s ploy to distract us۔ The devil’s power is sustained and maintained only by the lies he can persuade us to believe۔

Two major precautions are stated in Scripture when dealing with demonic matters:

<!-- p180 -->
1.  **Only those who have a relationship with Jesus have authority over evil spirits**. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 19:11-20</span> there is mention of the sons of a Jewish chief priest named Sceva. They tried to intervene in the life of a man who was under Satan’s oppression and torment, but the evil spirit refused to come out because they had no relationship with Jesus. The person who was oppressed leaped on them, stripped off their clothes, and beat them. Just as you should not impersonate a police officer, **do not imitate someone who has a relationship with Jesus; rather, establish your own relationship with Jesus**!

<!-- p181 -->
2.  **When an evil spirit leaves, it goes away for a time and will try to return**. If it finds the place where it used to live empty, it will return in a worse state. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="43-45" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 12:43-45</span>. When we minister to someone who is under Satan’s oppression, that person must fill the void in their life that the evil spirit had occupied. This means at the very least establishing a personal relationship with Jesus and strengthening it through obedience to the Holy Spirit. Often a bad or unclean spirit is given permission to operate in someone’s life through decisions made on the basis of their beliefs. Anyone who is delivered from the devil should replace that old belief system with a new one and be transformed by the renewing of their minds (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:1-2</span>).

<!-- p182 -->
We serve a great and mighty God, and we are ‘more than conquerors’ over a defeated enemy (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="37" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:37</span>). We do not need to fear our defeated enemy, because Jesus has given us His authority to crush his head! Satan’s time is short, and in the end he, along with every realm and authority that rejects Christ’s rule on earth, will face judgment.
