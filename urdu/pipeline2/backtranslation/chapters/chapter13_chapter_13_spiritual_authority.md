# Chapter 13 - Spiritual Authority

<!-- p1 -->
Jesus is the ultimate and perfect source of spiritual authority. As the victorious Christ, He has the final authority over all things in heaven and on earth.

<!-- p2 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-verses="18-20" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p3 -->
> **<u>Matthew 28:18-20</u>** (NKJV) ***18** And Jesus came and spoke to them, "All authority has been given to Me in heaven and on earth.* ***19** Go therefore and make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit,* ***20** teaching them to observe all things that I have commanded you; and behold, I am with you always, at all times, to the end of the age." Amen.*

<!-- p4 -->
</span>

<!-- p5 -->
In this chapter we will study Christ’s authority and power, the authorities and powers that He has entrusted to us (His Church), and how to operate in the authority of Jesus’ name. We will also study angelic and demonic beings and examine their authority, power, and natures.

<!-- p6 -->
Authority means having the right to act. Power means the ability to carry it out. Both are necessary in the spiritual life. Authority is different from power. Let’s look at the following table that compares authority & power and also consider a few examples.

<!-- p7 -->
[See Table 7](../tables/table7.md)

<!-- p8 -->
Here are a few examples to help understand the difference between power and authority.

<!-- p9 -->
Example 1: At work, an employee may have the abilities and skills to sell the company’s assets (power). This power is legitimate when he also has the relevant permission (authority) from the company’s owners. Without this authority, selling the company’s assets is theft.

<!-- p10 -->
Example 2: A police officer receives specialized training in weapons and hand-to-hand combat to learn how to apprehend a criminal and physically subdue them (power). Police officers are given this responsibility (authority), with the backing of the law, to enforce criminal laws.

## The Source of Jesus' Authority

<!-- p74 -->
Now that we have carefully examined what authority actually is, we need to know the source of that authority. Jesus’ authority is both divine and delegated. It proceeds from God the Father, is validated through His death and resurrection, and operates in perfect harmony with the Father’s will. Although it is absolute, His authority still respects human free will, revealing God’s love rather than coercive control.

<!-- p75 -->
Jesus’ authority is rooted in His divine identity as the Son of God and is bestowed by the Father. The Father has entrusted all authority to Christ, which includes judgment and rule over heaven and earth (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="11" data-verses="27" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 11:27</span>). Jesus’ authority reflects the internal unity and love of the Trinity, where the Son perfectly fulfills the Father’s plan (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-verses="35" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 3:35</span>).

<!-- p76 -->
Although Jesus, as the Son of God, possessed inherent authority, His victory over sin, death, and hell was fully established through His death and resurrection. Christ’s obedience, displayed in His sacrifice, resulted in His exaltation (being seated at the right hand of the Father), and solidified His authority over all creation. By triumphing over death, Christ obtained absolute kingship over life and eternity.

## Laying on of Hands

<!-- p116 -->
Laying on of hands is an important biblical practice that has been used in both the Old Testament and the New Testament as a sign of blessing, commissioning, healing, and the impartation of spiritual authority or gifts. It is not merely a physical act—it represents spiritual connection and transfer under the direction of God's authority.

<!-- p117 -->
In the Old Testament, the laying on of hands is often associated with blessing and consecration. For example, Jacob laid his hands on his grandsons, Ephraim and Manasseh, and blessed them (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="48" data-verses="14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Genesis 48:14</span>). Likewise, Moses laid hands on Joshua to appoint him as his successor and to grant him leadership authority and wisdom (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="NUM" data-chapter="27" data-verses="18-23" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Numbers 27:18-23</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="DEU" data-chapter="34" data-verses="9" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Deuteronomy 34:9</span>). This act publicly signified God's choice and the empowerment for the work ahead.

<!-- p118 -->
In the New Testament, the practice of the laying on of hands is closely connected with the ministry of Jesus and the early church. Jesus often laid hands on the sick and healed them (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="6" data-verses="5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Mark 6:5</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="4" data-verses="40" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Luke 4:40</span>), expressing both compassion and authority over sickness and disease. The apostles continued this practice, using it for the impartation of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-verses="17" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 8:17</span}), spiritual gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="4" data-verses="14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Timothy 4:14</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-verses="6" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">2 Timothy 1:6</span>), and for blessing believers. It also played a role in appointing leaders and missionaries for ministry (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="13" data-verses="3" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 13:3</span>).

<!-- p119 -->
The laying on of hands is a sign of faith and an act of obedience to God's word. It is not a mechanical ritual, but a spiritual practice rooted in dependence on the Holy Spirit. For example, in <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-verses="6" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 6:6</span> the apostles laid hands on those who were chosen for the service of deacons, committing them to God's service and publicly acknowledging their calling. In <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-verses="6" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 19:6</span> Paul laid hands on the believers, and they received the Holy Spirit, demonstrating its role in spiritual empowerment.

<!-- p120 -->
However, the Bible also gives warnings about this practice:

<!-- p121 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-verses="22" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p122 -->
> **<u>1 Timothy 5:22</u>** (NKJV) *Do not lay hands on anyone hastily, nor share in other people’s sins; keep yourself pure.*

<!-- p123 -->
</span>

<!-- p124 -->
This highlights how important discernment and spiritual maturity are when appointing individuals to positions of leadership. As we studied in the chapter on the Church, leaders must be tested and proven in order to avoid promoting unqualified or immature believers who could harm the body of Christ.

<!-- p125 -->
A few further guidelines for the practice of laying on of hands:

<!-- p126 -->
1.  Contextually appropriate - the person on whom hands are being laid should participate voluntarily.

<!-- p127 -->
2.  Physically appropriate - laying on of hands does not involve sexual or erotic touch. In practice, placing a hand on the shoulder, hand, or forehead are generally acceptable locations in the Western world (with permission).

<!-- p128 -->
3.  Culturally appropriate - in some cultures it is not considered appropriate for men and women to touch one another unless they are related by blood or marriage.

<!-- p129 -->
Even today, the practice of laying on of hands in the church is a powerful expression of faith and obedience. It is a means of blessing, healing, appointment, and impartation, always pointing to the lordship of Christ and the work of the Holy Spirit. Whether praying for the sick, sending out evangelists, or appointing leaders, it is a reminder that believers act not in their own strength but under the authority of Jesus Christ and in accordance with His purposes.

## Angels and Demons

<!-- p132 -->
### Angels

<!-- p133 -->
Angels are an important part of God’s creation, fulfilling His divine purposes as His messengers and agents. The Bible reveals a spiritual realm inhabited by these beings, who are powerful, intelligent, and obedient to God’s commands. Though angels are often unseen, their presence and actions impact human history and spiritual warfare. Understanding their roles, kinds, and activities gives us deeper insight into God’s plan and the unseen realities that surround us.

<!-- p134 -->
The word "angel" itself means "messenger," highlighting their primary duty. Angels operate in obedience to God’s will, whether delivering messages, providing protection, executing judgment, or engaging in spiritual warfare. They are not to be worshiped but serve as examples of obedience and reverence, always pointing to the glory of God. This chapter examines the nature of angels, their kinds, and roles, in order to clarify their significance in Scripture and in our lives today.

<!-- p135 -->
In Scripture, angels are shown as **messengers**, **servants**, and **warriors**. Angels often function as messengers, delivering important announcements, warnings, and encouragements. They reveal God’s plans and provide guidance in times of need (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="1" data-verses="26-38" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Luke 1:26-38</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="2" data-verses="13" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 2:13</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-verses="1" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Revelation 1:1</span>). Angels are actively involved in the protection, strengthening, and deliverance of believers. They minister to God’s people in times of distress and provide supernatural help (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="91" data-verses="11" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Psalm 91:11</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="12" data-verses="7-11" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 12:7-11</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="4" data-verses="11" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 4:11</span>). Angels engage in spiritual warfare, fighting demonic forces to advance God’s kingdom and defend believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="DAN" data-chapter="10" data-verses="13" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Daniel 10:13</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-verses="7-9" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Revelation 12:7-9</span>).

#### The Nature of Angels

<!-- p137 -->
Angels are created beings whom God brought into existence before the foundation of the world. They are distinct from humans and possess unique attributes that make them suited to their heavenly duties. The Bible teaches that angels are spiritual beings, not bound by physical limitations, yet they can take on visible form when interacting with humans.

<!-- p138 -->
Angels are described as immortal, meaning they neither die nor grow old. They are innumerable, vast hosts filling the heavenly realms. Although masculine pronouns are often used for them, Scripture makes clear that angels are sexless and neither marry nor reproduce. These characteristics highlight that they are God’s servants, wholly subject to His will and commands. Angels possess extraordinary qualities that reflect their divine purpose. They are obedient, reverent, wise, and mighty. These traits enable them to carry out God’s will effectively and faithfully.

Although the Bible highlights several roles and titles of angels, it does not present a comprehensive classification system. However, some angels are identified by specific roles or duties:

<!-- p141 -->
- **The Angel of the Lord** - often understood as a pre-incarnate appearance of Christ, appearing with divine authority. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="23" data-verses="20-23" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Exodus 23:20-23</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JDG" data-chapter="6" data-verses="11-24" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Judges 6:11-24</span>*)

<!-- p142 -->
- **Archangels** - high-ranking angels such as Michael, who leads the hosts, and Gabriel, who delivers divine messages. (*Jude 9; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-verses="7" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Revelation 12:7</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="1" data-verses="19-26" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Luke 1:19-26</span>*)

<!-- p143 -->
- **Cherubim** - guardians of God’s glory and presence, such as in Eden and over the Ark of the Covenant. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="3" data-verses="24" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Genesis 3:24</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="25" data-verses="22" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Exodus 25:22</span>*)

<!-- p144 -->
- **Seraphim** - worshipers of God, continually proclaiming His holiness. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="6" data-verses="1-6" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Isaiah 6:1-6</span>*)

<!-- p145 -->
- **Elect angels** - faithful angels who resisted Satan’s rebellion and remained loyal to God. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-verses="21" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Timothy 5:21</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-verses="41" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 25:41</span>*)

<!-- p146 -->
These classifications highlight diversity and order in the angelic realm; however, their primary focus is always on fulfilling God’s purposes.

<!-- p147 -->
#### Human interaction with angels

<!-- p148 -->
People in Scripture often encounter angels, but we are never instructed to command them or pray to them. Instead, angels act according to God’s direction. They minister to believers as **God’s servants** (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="1" data-verses="14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 1:14</span>). Their interactions include protection, guidance, and deliverance, and they always point us to God’s power and sovereignty.

<!-- p149 -->
- **Warnings against the worship of angels**: Scripture forbids worshiping angels or seeking encounters with them (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-verses="18" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Colossians 2:18</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="REV" data-chapter="22" data-verses="8-9" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Revelation 22:8-9</span>).

<!-- p150 -->
- **Pray to God, not to angels**: Believers should pray to God, who sends angels according to His will (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="91" data-verses="11" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Psalm 91:11</span>).

<!-- p151 -->
- **Encouragement and trust**: Angels remind us of God’s care, but our faith must rest entirely in God, not in angels.

<!-- p152 -->
Angels are remarkable beings created to serve God and to serve His people. Though their work often remains unseen, Scripture offers glimpses of their activity that display their faithfulness, power, and reverence. Angels remind us of God’s provision and protection, yet they always point to His glory. As believers, we are to trust in God’s care, knowing that angels, as His servants, are at work to accomplish His divine purposes.

### Demons

<!-- p154 -->
Just as the study of angels is necessary to understand the spiritual world, so too is the study of demons. Scripture clearly describes their activity, influence, and their defeat through Jesus Christ. Recognizing their role in spiritual warfare helps believers stand firm in faith without fear.

<!-- p155 -->
#### The origin and nature of demons

<!-- p156 -->
Demons are the fallen angels who sided with Satan in his rebellion against God. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-verses="7-9" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Revelation 12:7-9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-verses="41" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 25:41</span>).* Satan, formerly called Lucifer ("light-bearer"), was an exalted angel who sought to exalt himself above God and was cast out of heaven along with other rebellious angels. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="14" data-verses="12-15" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Isaiah 14:12-15</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EZK" data-chapter="28" data-verses="12-17" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ezekiel 28:12-17</span>).* 

<!-- p157 -->
Demons are spirits without physical bodies but can torment and afflict humans and animals. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-type="ref" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="5" data-verses="11-13" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Mark 5:11-13</span>).* They seek to deceive, tempt, oppress, and destroy. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-verses="10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 10:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="11" data-verses="14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">2 Corinthians 11:14</span>). Unlike God,* they have limits, and they are not omnipresent (everywhere), omniscient (all-knowing), or omnipotent (all-powerful)*.* Their final defeat is certain, and they will face eternal punishment. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="REV" data-chapter="20" data-verses="10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Revelation 20:10</span>)*

<!-- p158 -->
Scripture describes Satan’s work and activity as follows:

<!-- p159 -->
- Opposes the work of God *(<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1TH" data-chapter="2" data-verses="18" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Thessalonians 2:18</span>)*

<!-- p160 -->
- Deceives the world *(<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-verses="9" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Revelation 12:9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="4" data-verses="4" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">2 Corinthians 4:4</span>)*

<!-- p161 -->
- Tempts and accuses believers *(<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="22" data-verses="31" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Luke 22:31</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-verses="10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Revelation 12:10</span>)*

<!-- p162 -->
- Torments and harms *(<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JOB" data-chapter="1" data-verses="12" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Job 1:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="13" data-verses="16" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Luke 13:16</span>)*

<!-- p163 -->
There are many names and attributes for Satan, and each describes a different aspect of his work.

The Gospels and the Epistles further clarify the work of the enemy. Evil spirits, being disembodied, generally carry out their work through willing and open vessels. God works through people; Satan does the same. This oppression is often marked by torment, self-harm, and destructive behaviors *(<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="8" data-verses="27-29" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Luke 8:27-29</span>).* Their attacks cause fear, temptation, and discouragement *(<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="12" data-verses="7" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">2 Corinthians 12:7</span>).* The most common source of their power (not authority) in someone’s life is through the influence of lies and emotional strongholds *(<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="10" data-verses="3-5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">2 Corinthians 10:3-5</span>).*

<!-- p166 -->
It is important to note that not every lie a person believes, illness in their body, or stronghold in their life is necessarily a direct result of demonic influence; but these places of woundedness are points where the enemy can use us and keep us in a state of vexation and oppression.

<!-- p167 -->
#### Jesus’ authority over the works of the enemy

<!-- p168 -->
Do not be afraid; Jesus has complete and absolute authority over the works of the enemy. Jesus demonstrated authority over evil spirits during His ministry *(<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="1" data-verses="34" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Mark 1:34</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="5" data-verses="1-20" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Mark 5:1-20</span>).* The works of the enemy, and Satan himself, have been defeated through Jesus’ death and resurrection *(<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-verses="15" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Colossians 2:15</span>).*

<!-- p169 -->
As believers, we have been delegated authority by Jesus to cast out evil spirits in His name:

<!-- p170 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="fragment" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-verses="17" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p171 -->
> **<u>Mark 16:17</u>** (NKJV) …in My name they will cast out demons…

<!-- p172 -->
</span>

<!-- p173 -->
Furthermore, as heirs of salvation we have the authority to submit ourselves to God, resist the devil, and he will flee from us! (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="4" data-verses="7" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">James 4:7</span>).

<!-- p174 -->
The method described in Scripture for dealing with an evil or unclean spirit is to take authority over it in the name of Jesus and command it to leave. The Holy Spirit promises that if we ask, He will give us wisdom and discernment! (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-verses="5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">James 1:5</span>). We do not need to turn it into a spectacle, or a movie-like drama, or talk with them; that is a trick of Satan to distract us. Satan’s power is established and maintained only through the lies he can persuade us to believe.

<!-- p175 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="18" data-verses="18" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p176 -->
> **<u>Matthew 18:18</u>** (NKJV) Whatever we bind on earth will be bound in heaven

<!-- p177 -->
</span>

<!-- p178 -->
We address them in Jesus’ name and command them to leave.

<!-- p179 -->
When dealing with demonic matters, Scripture sets out two major cautions:

<!-- p180 -->
1.  **Only those who have a relationship with Jesus have authority over demons.** <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-verses="11-20" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 19:11-20</span> mentions the sons of Sceva, a Jewish chief priest. They tried to intervene in the life of a man who was under Satan’s oppression and torment, and the evil spirit refused to come out because they had no relationship with Jesus. The oppressed man leaped on them, stripped them naked, and beat them. Just as you should not impersonate a police officer, **do not impersonate someone who belongs to Jesus; rather, establish a relationship with Jesus yourself!**

<!-- p181 -->
2.  **When an evil spirit leaves, it goes away for a time and tries to come back.** If it finds its former dwelling empty, it returns in a worse state. <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="12" data-verses="43-45" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 12:43-45</span>. When we minister to someone under Satan’s oppression, that person must fill the void in their life that the evil spirit had occupied. At the very least, this means establishing a personal relationship with Jesus and strengthening it through obedience to the Holy Spirit. Often an evil or unclean spirit gains the power to operate in someone’s life through choices made on the basis of their beliefs. Anyone delivered from the devil must change that old belief system and be transformed by the renewing of their mind (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-verses="1-2" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 12:1-2</span>).

<!-- p182 -->
We serve a great and mighty God, and we are ‘more than conquerors’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-verses="37" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 8:37</span>) over a defeated enemy. We have no need to fear our defeated enemy, because Jesus has given us His authority to crush his head! The devil’s time is short, and ultimately he will be brought to judgment along with every realm and power on earth that rejects the lordship of Christ.
