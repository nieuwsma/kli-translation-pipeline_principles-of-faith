# Chapter 5 - Repentance from Dead Works

<!-- p1 -->
Often in Christianity we consider repentance to be basic only for the beginning of our faith in Jesus. The spirit of repentance is much deeper than turning away from sin for salvation. Repentance from dead works means that we turn our hearts away from those things that harm us or hinder our growth-and turn toward the things of God.

<!-- p2 -->
The author of Hebrews establishes 'repentance from dead works' as the first item in the list of foundational doctrines after the elementary principles of Christ (Chapter 4).

### Dead Works

<!-- p9 -->
Everyone who has ever been employed knows what work is. Work is the set of tasks you perform to receive a paycheck so that you can earn a living (employment). Employment is a contract under which you give your skills and labor in exchange for something of value that helps sustain your survival. You do work because you get something from it.

<!-- p10 -->
Our work matters; it can bring a sense of purpose, and often a sense of identity. It is very common to be asked, 'What do you do for a living?' and the answer begins with an identity statement, 'I am.' Although work can be pleasant and meaningful, it can also be very possibly physically or mentally strenuous, difficult, challenging, and stressful. If your job is none of these things, you may find it very menial and boring.

<!-- p11 -->
If you work a 40-hour job in a week, and sleep 8 hours at night, you spend 36% of your waking hours at work, and that does not include the time you spend getting ready for work, commuting, maintaining the home, and all the other necessary tasks that are required to keep your life in order.

<!-- p12 -->
Why do we do all this work? It can vary, but by definition it is because we get something valuable from it.

<!-- p13 -->
If they stop paying you would you continue to work at the job? For most of us the easy and painfully obvious answer is a resounding, no!

<!-- p14 -->
Would you continue to work in a job if it were killing you? I think it depends on how quickly.

<!-- p15 -->
The context of ***nekros*** with ***erga*** is toil or work that is dead, that has no life, that cannot reproduce! The author of Hebrews is warning us about work that is dead.

<!-- p16 -->
In the study of biology there are several criteria by which it is determined whether a living organism is alive or not (for our purposes this is a partial list):

<!-- p17 -->
1.  **Metabolism**: Living organisms transform energy through metabolic processes in order to grow, develop, and sustain themselves.

<!-- p18 -->
2.  **Growth and Development**: Life involves increase and progress over time, with a specific life cycle and developmental changes.

<!-- p19 -->
3.  **Reproduction**: Living beings have the ability to reproduce, that is, to produce offspring or new entities.

<!-- p20 -->
4.  **Response to stimuli**: Life responds to external stimuli (such as light, heat, or sound) and can adapt itself to changes in the environment.

<!-- p21 -->
5.  **Homeostasis**: Organisms maintain internal stability, such as regulation of temperature or pH balance.

<!-- p22 -->
6.  **Adaptation**: Over time, organisms can adapt to their environment through genetic changes, which enables survival under different conditions.

<!-- p23 -->
If we apply this criterion of ‘life’ to our actions with a bit of creative license, then dead works are those that:

<!-- p24 -->
1.  do not allow personal growth or development.

<!-- p25 -->
2.  do not reproduce; cannot produce a positive or desired outcome.

<!-- p26 -->
3.  do not respond to external stimuli, to the light of God’s presence, or to the sound of His voice.

<!-- p27 -->
4.  do not maintain internal stability, become disordered, lack self-control.

<!-- p28 -->
5.  do not adapt (are extremely rigid) and no longer aid our survival.

<!-- p29 -->
Dead works are not God’s desire for us. God wants to give us life!

<!-- p30 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-verses="10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p31 -->
> **<u>John 10:10</u>** (NKJV) *The thief does not come except to steal, and to kill, and to destroy. I have come that they may have life, and that they may have it more abundantly.*

<!-- p32 -->
</span>

<!-- p33 -->
Although ‘dead’ versus ‘alive’ is a clear-cut distinction. Dying is a gradual process. Our dead works may not yet be completely dead in every category, but they will be! Dead works are like poisons; some have stronger effects.

<!-- p34 -->
Poison causes harm by preventing the body from functioning as it normally does. Some poisons have mild effects, like a headache. Others affect our ability to think, to see, or even to reproduce (to live a fruitful life). Dead works are the same. They may be merely a headache, or they may cause death.

<!-- p35 -->
Dead works are all the actions that, upon reflection, are not bringing the abundant life that Jesus promised. A non-marital relationship is a very powerful poison; it will very quickly have a total and negative effect. Being needlessly stubborn and spiteful will slowly poison a person until he drives everyone away from himself.

<!-- p36 -->
To be rid of dead works, we must repent!

<!-- p37 -->
### Repentance

<!-- p38 -->
‘Repenting from dead works’ is to turn to God and abandon everything that is harming our way with Him—beginning with a change of heart and mind.

<!-- p39 -->
Continuing with the analogy of poison for dead works, if you found out that your favorite snack was loaded with toxic heavy metals that, if eaten, would put you in a lethal condition, what would you do? We would unhesitatingly cast it away from ourselves and never enjoy it again, knowing that eating it is tantamount to our premature—and likely painful—death.

<!-- p40 -->
We should repent! We should turn away from those things in this life that are poisoning us and leading us toward both our physical and spiritual death.

<!-- p41 -->
To fully understand this, we need to look a bit deeper into the Greek language.

<!-- p42 -->
The word used in Hebrews is *repentance*. It is contrasted with the word *to repent,* for which an example is found

<!-- p43 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="4" data-verses="17" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p44 -->
> **<u>Matthew 4:17</u>** (NKJV) - “*From that time Jesus began to preach, and to say, Repent: for the kingdom of heaven is at hand.*”

<!-- p45 -->
</span>

<!-- p46 -->
To repent (***Metanoeō***، pronounced *meh-tah-NO-eh-oh* - **G3340**) is a verb; it is an action or event; it means to change one’s mind or purpose؛ to turn away from sin and turn to God.

<!-- p47 -->
Repentance (***Metanoia***، pronounced *meh-TAH-noi-ah* - **G3341**) is a noun; it is the state of having repented; it means a change of mind؛ the state of having turned away from sin and turned to God.

<!-- p48 -->
When God says in Hebrews ‘*repentance from dead works*’ He is speaking of being in a state of having repented! We are to live in a continuous state of being ‘turned away’ from dead works.

<!-- p49 -->
How many times do we need to repent? Trick question! We need to keep changing our minds until we stop doing dead works. Repenting is **not asking for forgiveness, or apologizing**, although that may be included. Repenting is turning away from the previous way of thinking.

<!-- p59 -->
### Systems of Change

<!-- p60 -->
There is a cognitive trap in changing our minds. If we do not even perceive the need to change our thoughts, why would we open our eyes to seek change? Returning to the poison example, if you cannot even sense that the poison is killing you, why would you stop using it?

<!-- p61 -->
Some people think that a person just has to reach ‘rock bottom’, then they will look at their lives and decide to change. Experience and psychology tell us that we humans are more complex. In the study of psychology there are multiple theories related to behavior change; one highly reputable theory is the **Transtheoretical Model of Change** which was formulated in the late 1970s by James Prochaska and Carlo DiClemente **\[1\].**

<!-- p62 -->
People may go through a cycle of six stages for change:

<!-- p63 -->
1.  Precontemplation: There is no intention to change.

<!-- p64 -->
2.  Contemplation: There is an intention to change in the near future.

<!-- p65 -->
3.  Preparation: Some steps have been taken toward change.

<!-- p66 -->
4.  Action: The behavior has been changed for a short period.

<!-- p67 -->
5.  Maintenance: The behavior has been changed and is being maintained.

<!-- p68 -->
6.  Termination: There remains no desire to return to the previous behavior.

<!-- p69 -->
It is not fixed how long any stage might take. Some people, with respect to certain problems in their lives, never meaningfully progress through these stages. In other cases, we may move very quickly from stage 1 to 6.

<!-- p70 -->
### Example - Emotional Self-Regulation

<!-- p71 -->
**Scenario:**, a father who suddenly feels a pang of conscience after yelling at his child.

<!-- p72 -->
1.  **Precontemplation**: He sees himself as a *strict* yet *loving* father. He believes raising his voice is necessary. He is not considering change. In fact, he can even justify it- “My father raised me the same way.”

<!-- p73 -->
2.  **Contemplation**: One day, after a long day at work, he loses his temper and yells at his young daughter louder and angrier than ever before. She is terrified. Her lips tremble. She quietly withdraws. In that moment… something **changes**.

<!-- p74 -->
3.  **Preparation**: He sits alone in the kitchen, stunned. The image of her hurt face plays in his mind over and over. His stomach twists. He says to himself, *“I don’t want to be who I just was.”* He knows he has to do something different-*now*.

<!-- p75 -->
4.  **Action**: He goes to her room, kneels, sincerely apologizes, and says, “Daddy was wrong. You didn’t deserve that.” She hugs him. Motivated to reinforce the change, he prays and talks to a friend for support.

<!-- p76 -->
5.  **Maintenance**: The next day he starts reading parenting books to work on building positive emotional self-regulation.

<!-- p77 -->
6.  **Termination**: Months pass. Yelling is no longer part of the routine. He is not perfect, but something really has changed. He no longer justifies the old behavior-and he has no desire to go back.

### Helping Others Change

<!-- p79 -->
Most of our discussion has focused on the process and need for repentance from the perspective of the person who needs to change. However, we will also encounter people who are in great need of change but are stuck in **Precontemplation**/**Contemplation** - unaware of their need to change. We will likely also meet people who are eager to change, but do not know what to do (**Preparation**).

<!-- p80 -->
When Jesus addressed the crowds, He often encountered people who did not know that they needed to change۔ We cannot forcibly change someone’s mind, nor can we repent on their behalf۔ That is not our job, nor is it the example of Jesus۔

<!-- p81 -->
Jesus deals with this slow process and the occasional inability to change by speaking in parables۔ Why did Jesus decide to speak in parables؟ He explains the reason for this in Matthew:

<!-- p82 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="fragment" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="13" data-verses="10,11,13,15" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p83 -->

>
>***10** And the disciples came to Him and said, "Why do You speak to them in parables?"*
>
>***11** He answered and said to them…*
>
>***13** … I speak to them in parables, because seeing they do not see، and hearing they do not hear، nor do they understand.*
>
>***15** … because the hearts of this people have become dull۔ Their ears are hard of hearing، and they have closed their eyes، lest they should see with their eyes and hear with their ears، lest they should understand with their hearts and turn، and I should heal them.'*

<!-- p84 -->
</span>

<!-- p85 -->
Jesus is saying that He is using parables to slowly open the minds and eyes of those who cannot see or understand the truth۔ Truth is like a dazzling light، at the same time its abundance forces us to shut our eyes، it is very painful۔ When we expose ourselves to a portion of that light, it produces illumination by which we come to know what darkness we are actually in۔ When we experience that illumination, we realize that the Word of God is a lamp to our path and a light to our feet (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="119" data-verses="110" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Psalm 119:110</span>) and that we can use words of truth to bring light to any aspect of our lives۔

<!-- p86 -->
If you find yourself dealing with someone whose mind is closed، my recommendation is that you pray and ask for God’s counsel to know how to proceed۔ From experience and from the example of Jesus it emerges that an encounter with truth filled with grace، sometimes gradually، can begin to open the eyes of the blind۔ Remember it is the work of the Spirit to bring conviction and illumination۔

<!-- p87 -->
When they begin to experience the transformative process of repentance it produces good fruit in their lives۔ If they have become sick from some poison, then after their eyes are opened، stopping the poison and taking the antidote will begin their healing۔

<!-- p88 -->
When we truly embrace this change, we can pray like King David:

### Godly Sorrow and the Goodness of God

<!-- p94 -->
Although hitting the lowest point is more of a story than any sure condition for change، God can use sorrow to influence our minds۔ God will use sorrow to bring us to repentance۔ He will convict us concerning our actions and the state of our lives and hearts so that our eyes may be opened and we may see our own perfect and complete need for Him۔

<!-- p95 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="7" data-verses="10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p96 -->
> **<u>2 Corinthians 7:10</u>** (NKJV) *For godly sorrow produces repentance leading to salvation, not to be regretted؛ but the sorrow of the world produces death.”*

<!-- p97 -->
</span>

<!-- p98 -->
Godly sorrow, or <u>the experience of sadness or distress that leads to a *metanoia*</u>, is not the same as worldly sorrow۔ Worldly sorrow is the same experience of sadness or distress, but the world gives no hope, it speaks only of comfort and the promise of death۔

<!-- p99 -->
Sorrow is also different from pain and mourning۔

<!-- p100 -->
Suffering is enduring pain—physical, emotional, mental, or spiritual—especially when it is prolonged or severe.

<!-- p101 -->
Grief is a multifaceted emotional response to a significant personal loss, especially relational or existential.

<!-- p102 -->
Sadness, suffering, and grief are closely related concepts. In the human condition we will experience all three; we can learn and grow through all three, but God will not bring death and destruction into our lives to teach us.

<!-- p103 -->
The Scriptures say that every good and perfect gift is from above, from God (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-verses="17" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">James 1:17</span>). He is a good Father who knows how to give good gifts to His children (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="7" data-verses="11" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 7:11</span>). Godly sorrow will produce discomfort, but His discipline is just and loving. Just as a good father would not deliberately break his child's arm ‘to teach him a lesson,’ God will not impose sickness or infirmity on His children to teach us.

<!-- p104 -->
### An example of worldly sorrow versus godly sorrow

<!-- p105 -->
A three-year-old boy was playing in the yard with his two-year-old sister. He was playing with a stick, and he deliberately hit her with it, which made her cry and left a red mark.

<!-- p106 -->
Their father heard the noise and investigated, and when he found out what his son had done, he took the stick, broke it in two, and threw it away. He sent his son inside to think about what he had done and comforted his daughter.

<!-- p107 -->
When the father came inside to deal with his son, he found him crying. When he asked why he was crying, the boy said, “Because you broke my stick.”

<!-- p108 -->
The father replied, “I’m sorry that you are more concerned about your stick than about your sister whom you hurt,” and he sent him back to his room for further reflection.

<!-- p109 -->
A few minutes later his son came out again and apologized. He said he was still sad because Dad broke his stick, and also because he hit his sister.

<!-- p110 -->
**Progress!**

<!-- p111 -->
In this example, the boy experienced both kinds of sorrow: worldly sorrow for his lost stick, and eventually godly sorrow for his injured sister.

<!-- p112 -->
Godly sorrow and God’s goodness are intertwined. God is a good Father who lets us experience the consequences of our actions, but He stands with us as we go through those consequences.

<!-- p113 -->
God’s unalloyed goodness—that if we come to Him He is ready to deliver and restore us—is a positive motivator on the path of our repentance. His goodness is not so that we remain engaged in sin, but that despite our sin He makes a way for our redemption and for us to experience His salvation.

<!-- p114 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="2" data-verses="4" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p115 -->
> **<u>Romans 2:4</u>** (NKJV) *Do you despise the riches of His goodness, forbearance, and longsuffering, not knowing that the goodness of God leads you to repentance?*

<!-- p116 -->
</span>

<!-- p117 -->
Despite God’s goodness, mercy, and grace, some people do not repent and do not change; they become trapped in a cycle of frustration and pain. In stark contrast, for some people simply the example of sin in someone else’s life and its consequence is enough to change their thinking.

<!-- p118 -->
Human beings have a great capacity for change; it is embedded in our nature. Humans are highly adaptable; we can learn and grow, and find better ways of doing things. However, the capacity for change is not the same as the willingness to change, or the resolve to embrace it.

<!-- p119 -->
I do not know why some people get stuck and others are so ready to change so quickly, but I can say that the entire process of change is in fact by the grace of God.
