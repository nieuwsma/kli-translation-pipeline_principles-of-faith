# Chapter 8 - The Holy Spirit-Comforter, Guide, and Power

<!-- p1 -->
The Holy Spirit is not a new spirit but is God's Spirit and has been with God from the beginning. In <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="1" data-verses="2" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Genesis 1:2</span> the Spirit of God was hovering over the waters during creation. In <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="NEH" data-chapter="9" data-verses="20" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Nehemiah 9:20</span>, the Word says that it was the Spirit who taught the people in the wilderness. Through this same Holy Spirit God anointed kings, judges, and prophets in the Old Testament. In <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JOL" data-chapter="2" data-verses="28-29" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Joel 2:28-29</span> there is this promise about the Spirit, that He will be poured out on all people. It was through this same Holy Spirit that God raised Jesus from the dead, and now He dwells in us (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-verses="11" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 8:11</span>). The Holy Spirit is one, and He has always been the same. The way He works with people has appeared different at different times, but His heart has never changed.

<!-- p2 -->
Jesus gave His disciples, and all of us, the powerful promise of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p3 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-verses="16-18" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p4 -->
> **<u>John 14:16-18</u>** (NKJV) **16** *And I will ask the Father, and He will give you another Helper, that He may be with you forever-**17** the Spirit of truth, whom the world cannot receive, because it neither sees Him nor knows Him; but you know Him, for He dwells with you and will be in you.* ***18** I will not leave you as orphans; I will come to you.*

<!-- p5 -->
</span>

<!-- p6 -->
When Jesus was walking on earth in His physical body, He was limited in the same way we are; His body needed the same things our bodies need: rest, water, food, sleep, etc. This limitation affected not only Jesus but also His followers, because they could only be in one place at a time. Often, when Jesus traveled, a large crowd surrounded Him; people would cry out to Him and try to touch Him. Jesus often needed to spend time with His heavenly Father to be refreshed and to pray.

<!-- p7 -->
For the increased impact of the Father's work, it was necessary for Jesus to prepare the disciples, and for them to continue that work. Jesus knew that a very important aspect of His ministry on earth depended on remaining connected to His Father and abiding in Him. In fact, Jesus said that He could do nothing unless His Father did it through Him and showed Him what to do (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="5" data-verses="19" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 5:19</span>). In His wisdom, God sent His Holy Spirit to dwell in us, to guide and lead us, and to empower us to do the Father's work.

<!-- p8 -->
## When was the Holy Spirit sent to all believers?

<!-- p9 -->
Jesus said:

<!-- p10 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-verses="7" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p11 -->
> **<u>John 16:7</u>** (NKJV) *Nevertheless I tell you the truth. It is to your advantage that I go away; for if I do not go away, the Helper will not come to you; but if I go, I will send Him to you.*

<!-- p12 -->
</span>

<!-- p13 -->
When Jesus was crucified, His death made it possible for God's Spirit to dwell within us. Until then, God's manifest Spirit was, for the most part, confined behind a thick veil in the temple. From time to time, God chose and anointed a few select individuals (consecrated and set apart for special service), and the Holy Spirit would come upon them. God's anointing and the Spirit being upon them was specific, temporary, and conditional. After his sin with Bathsheba, David prayed: 'Do not take Your Holy Spirit from me!' (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="51" data-verses="11" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Psalm 51:11</span>), because David had seen that the Spirit had departed from King Saul due to acts of disobedience.

<!-- p14 -->
We (humanity) could not receive the Holy Spirit within us because of our sinful nature, but when Jesus, through His sacrifice, dealt with the problem of sin, He opened the way by faith for us to become God's children and heirs (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-verses="17" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 8:17</span>).

<!-- p15 -->
In John 20, after the resurrection, Jesus appeared to His disciples in the middle of a locked room and breathed on them and said, 'Receive the Holy Spirit' (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="20" data-verses="22" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 20:22</span>). The exact theological understanding of what happened at that moment is somewhat debated, but most Pentecostal interpretations see it as the disciples receiving God's Spirit within them, which is a sign of new birth, being born again. This, however, was not the time when God's Spirit came upon them to empower them with boldness for their ministry. That was the time when they received the Holy Spirit within, enabling them to abide with God and have fellowship with Him. This will be discussed in greater depth later in this chapter and in the chapters on sanctification and baptism.

<!-- p16 -->
Later, at the end of the 40-day period between the resurrection and the ascension (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-verses="3" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 1:3</span>), Jesus gives the promise of the Holy Spirit:

<!-- p17 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-verses="4-9" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p18 -->
> **<u>Acts 1:4-9</u>** (NKJV)
>
> **4** *And being assembled together with them, He commanded them not to depart from Jerusalem, but to wait for the Promise of the Father, "which," He said, "you have heard from Me;*
>
> ***5** for John truly baptized with water, but you shall be baptized with the Holy Spirit not many days from now."*
>
> ***6** Therefore, when they had come together, they asked Him, saying, "Lord, will You at this time restore the kingdom to Israel?"*
>
> ***7** And He said to them, "It is not for you to know times or seasons which the Father has put in His own authority.*
>
> ***8** But you shall receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you; and you shall be witnesses to Me in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the end of the earth."*
>
> ***9** Now when He had spoken these things, while they watched, He was taken up, and a cloud received Him out of their sight.*

<!-- p19 -->
</span>

<!-- p20 -->
The disciples had to wait for the Spirit to be poured out according to the promise, and through this they would receive power to be witnesses to the ends of the earth. The Holy Spirit is an essential part of God’s plan for humanity, and is necessary for us as believers, for fulfilling our responsibility, and for the world.

<!-- p21 -->
## Knowing the Holy Spirit

<!-- p22 -->
The Holy Spirit is, above all, of God and belongs to God. The Scriptures call the Spirit the Spirit of God, or the Spirit of the Lord, or the Spirit of Christ; although these highlight different aspects, they are all the same. The Holy Spirit, since he is fully God, is also eternal and holy (blameless, innocent, pure, undefiled). Some translations also call the Holy Spirit the Holy Ghost.

<!-- p23 -->
In Greek, the word for Spirit is ***Pneuma** (NYOO-mah -* **G4151***),* which means breath, or wind. In Hebrew, the word for spirit carries the same meaning. Breath is important because we need it continually to live. It was God’s breath that he breathed into Adam and gave him life, and likewise it was Jesus’ own breath that he drew one last time before he died on the cross. Without breath there is no life. Breath, like wind, can be felt and experienced, but it is not as easily observed as the other ‘elements’—water, fire, earth.

<!-- p24 -->
There are many other fundamental attributes used to describe the Holy Spirit. Each of these is used with ‘Spirit of’, meaning these characteristics are vital for defining the Spirit. For example, *Spirit of Truth* or *Spirit of Life*.

<!-- p25 -->
Spirit of:

<!-- p26 -->
- **Truth** - <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-verses="17" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 14:17</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-verses="13" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 16:13</span> points to the Spirit’s role in guiding believers into all truth, revealing God’s will and the reality of Christ.

<!-- p27 -->
- **Life** - <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-verses="2" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 8:2</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-type="ref" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-verses="8" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Galatians 6:8</span> highlights the Spirit’s role in granting believers spiritual and eternal life, freeing them from the law of sin and death.

<!-- p28 -->
- **Grace** - <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-verses="29" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 10:29</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ZEC" data-chapter="12" data-verses="10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Zechariah 12:10</span> reflects the Spirit’s work in dispensing God’s grace, forgiveness, and unmerited favor to humanity.

<!-- p29 -->
- **Glory** - <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="4" data-verses="14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Peter 4:14</span> indicates the Spirit’s role in revealing and magnifying God’s glory, especially in times of suffering and persecution.

<!-- p30 -->
- **Sonship** - <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-verses="15" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 8:15</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="4" data-verses="5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Galatians 4:5</span> points to the Spirit’s work in making believers children of God, granting them the status of sons and daughters.

<!-- p31 -->
- **Holiness** - <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="1" data-verses="4" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 1:4</span> points to the sanctifying work of the Spirit, who sets believers apart for God’s purposes.

<!-- p32 -->
- **Power** - <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-verses="7" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">2 Timothy 1:7</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-verses="8" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 1:8</span> explains that the Spirit empowers believers with boldness, for service, and with the capacity to live out their faith effectively.

<!-- p33 -->
Among all these attributes, the Holy Spirit is rightly also acknowledged as the Helper or Comforter. Jesus refers to the Holy Spirit as the coming Helper, the Greek word ***Paraklētos** (*pah-RAH-klay-tos - **G3875**), which means advocate, helper, called alongside.

<!-- p34 -->
An advocate speaks on behalf of another, like a defense attorney. They intercede and assist the person to whom they have been entrusted.

<!-- p35 -->
A helper helps. They help strengthen the work, but their role is primarily to assist in it. A helper is not fully responsible for carrying out the task, but comes alongside to bring it to completion.

<!-- p36 -->
In the ministry of the Holy Spirit there are many unique purposes and roles. His work is inward (in the hearts and lives of believers), upward (in the service and recognition of God), and outward (toward our mission field, the world).

<!-- p37 -->
## The Roles of the Holy Spirit - Inward, Upward, Outward

<!-- p38 -->
### Inward - The Holy Spirit unites us with Jesus

<!-- p39 -->
Before Jesus performed any miracles or began his public ministry, he was baptized by the hand of John the Baptist, and God’s Spirit descended upon him, and a voice came from heaven, ‘This is my beloved Son, with whom I am well pleased’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-verses="17" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 3:17</span>).

<!-- p40 -->
When we accept Jesus by faith, we receive salvation and the seal of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p41 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-verses="13-14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p42 -->
> **<u>Ephesians 1:13-14</u>** (NKJV) ***13** In Him you also, when you heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation, and believed in Him, you were sealed in Him with the promised Holy Spirit, **14** who is the guarantee of our inheritance until the redemption of the purchased possession, to the praise of His glory.*

<!-- p43 -->
</span>

<!-- p44 -->
This seal is not metaphorical but real. It is the sign of our redemption and, as a down payment, the guarantee of the full inheritance that we receive as God’s children. Romans 8 states that through new birth we receive in our mortal bodies the very Spirit of God who raised Christ from the dead, and we become the true temple of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="6" data-verses="16" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">2 Corinthians 6:16</span>)۔

<!-- p45 -->
Receiving God’s Spirit transforms us, and it causes rivers of living water to flow from within us (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="7" data-verses="38" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 7:38</span>)۔ This renewal is immediate and perfect yet continues throughout our whole lives. The river of the Holy Spirit should never stop in our lives!

<!-- p46 -->
Through salvation and the indwelling of the Holy Spirit we can abide in Christ and He in us. God’s Spirit is no longer limited to being outside but can dwell with us deeply. From now into eternal life we can walk in fellowship and harmony with God.

<!-- p47 -->
Much could be said about the inner work of the Holy Spirit and His role in our sanctification, but these matters will be presented in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p48 -->
### Upward—the Holy Spirit testifies of Jesus

<!-- p49 -->
The Holy Spirit is from God and is God’s own. Just as Jesus said that He did nothing except what the Father showed Him (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="5" data-verses="19" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 5:19</span>), so the Holy Spirit does nothing unless directed by the Father. Since the Holy Spirit is the Helper, He does not act by His own will but works in perfect harmony with the Father.

<!-- p50 -->
The Spirit bears witness to Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-verses="26" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 15:26</span>), glorifies Jesus, and reveals and declares the things that Jesus has kept for us (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-verses="14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 16:14</span>)۔

<!-- p51 -->
The Holy Spirit will bear witness to Jesus, glorify Him, teach, and remind us of Jesus’ teaching (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-verses="26" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 14:26</span>), and will guide us into all truth (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-verses="13" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 16:13</span>)۔ Jesus told His disciples that He had many things to tell them but they could not bear them yet؛ but the Holy Spirit would lead them into all truth. This is another fine point in theology. The teachings that Jesus had to make known to His disciples were delivered in many ways. Jesus taught them personally, both before His cross and also after the resurrection and before His ascension.

<!-- p52 -->
Jesus also promised that the Spirit of truth, who was poured out on them on the day of Pentecost, would lead them into all truth through divine revelation. Is there still divine revelation today? **Certainly yes!** The gifts of the Holy Spirit, including prophecy and words of knowledge and wisdom, have not ceased! However, any prophecy or word that comes from the Spirit is not of private interpretation (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="2PE" data-chapter="1" data-verses="20" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">2 Peter 1:20</span>)۔ This is extremely important. Some people will mistakenly claim that they are listening to the Holy Spirit, but their actions and words do not bear witness to Jesus. <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="4" data-verses="3" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 John 4:3</span> warns about any spirit that does not bear witness to Jesus, that it is the spirit of the antichrist. This is worth repeating: If any spirit departs from the teaching of Christ, it is not the Holy Spirit but an unclean spirit!

<!-- p53 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="4" data-verses="1" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p54 -->
> **<u>1 John 4:1</u>** (NKJV) *Beloved, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits, whether they are of God, because many false prophets have gone out into the world.*

<!-- p55 -->
</span>

<!-- p56 -->
Throughout all of Scripture we see examples of false claims—often from false prophets or from well-meaning individuals who spoke against God’s will. For example, Jeremiah warned against prophets who spoke the visions of their own hearts, claiming peace when destruction was looming (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JER" data-chapter="23" data-verses="16-17" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Jeremiah 23:16-17</span>)۔ Likewise, in <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-verses="16-18" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 16:16-18</span> a slave girl’s words about Paul and Silas were accurate, but they were inspired not by the Holy Spirit but by an evil spirit. These examples remind us that things that appear true can also come from ungodly sources.

<!-- p57 -->
Every claim that ‘God told me!’ or any ‘new’ teaching must be tested by the biblical tests of truth. To discern rightly we must ask:

<!-- p58 -->
- Is it consistent with Scripture?

<!-- p59 -->
- Does it glorify Jesus Christ?

<!-- p60 -->
- Does it produce God-pleasing fruit?

<!-- p61 -->
By using these tests, we can distinguish the true guidance of the Holy Spirit from error or deception.

<!-- p62 -->
God is accustomed to giving us abundant & plentiful wisdom and guidance (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-verses="5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">James 1:5</span> & <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JER" data-chapter="33" data-verses="3" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Jeremiah 33:3</span>). God’s divine wisdom will not violate the Holy Scriptures, but it also extends beyond the scope of Scripture. His *entolē* *(*commands*)* are immediate instructions that must be obeyed; these are *rhema* words (as described in the chapter on faith).

<!-- p63 -->
This wisdom and understanding and guidance is not usually found word-for-word in Scripture. For example, the Scriptures give excellent general guidance about what qualities to look for in a spouse, but they do not give specific instructions about any particular person. God promises to guide our path, to lead us into all truth, and we can be comforted knowing that this wisdom and guidance of the Holy Spirit will never violate any teaching of the Holy Scriptures.

### Visible—the Holy Spirit Empowers the Mission

<!-- p65 -->
Jesus gave us His mission, that is, the Great Commission:

<!-- p66 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-verses="18-20" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p67 -->
> **<u>Matthew 28:18-20</u>** (NKJV) ***18** And Jesus came and spoke to them, "All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to Me.* ***19** Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit,* ***20** teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you; and behold, I am with you always, even to the end of the world." Amen.*

<!-- p68 -->
</span>

<!-- p69 -->
The Holy Spirit plays a central role in this mission, convicting the world concerning sin, righteousness, and judgment:

<!-- p70 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-verses="8-11" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p71 -->
> **<u>John 16:8-11</u>** (NKJV) **8** a*nd when He comes, He will convict the world concerning sin, and righteousness, and judgment:* ***9** concerning sin, because they do not believe in Me;* ***10** concerning righteousness, because I go to My Father and you see Me no more;* ***11** concerning judgment, because the ruler of this world has been judged.*

<!-- p72 -->
</span>

<!-- p73 -->
Going deeper, the Spirit convicts regarding these three realities:

<!-- p74 -->
1.  **Conviction of Sin**: Unveiling the reality of human rebellion against God, and drawing people's attention to the depravity of their own lives.

<!-- p75 -->
2.  **Convincing of Righteousness**: Revealing the perfect fulfillment of the Father’s will by Jesus, and that Jesus’ sacrifice has provided the once-for-all atonement required for the forgiveness of sins.

<!-- p76 -->
3.  **Convincing of Judgment**: Affirming the defeat of Satan, the certainty of God’s judgment, and that all will stand before judgment for their deeds and works.

<!-- p77 -->
The Holy Spirit also empowers the mission. The Holy Spirit gives believers the power to be bold in bearing witness to the gospel despite persecution. Boldness does not mean being loud. It means having courage in spite of fear. Power means strength, might, and ability. The Holy Spirit grants power and ability to testify to the gospel despite threats and the fear of punishment.

<!-- p78 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-verses="8" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p79 -->
> **<u>Acts 1:8</u>** *(NKJV) But when the Holy Spirit has come upon you, you will receive power؛ and you will be My witnesses in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, even to the end of the earth.*

<!-- p80 -->
</span>

<!-- p81 -->
The entire book of Acts is filled with stories and accounts in which this general sequence is seen:

<!-- p82 -->
1.  **Prayer**: Believers ask for boldness and for God’s intervention.

<!-- p83 -->
2.  **Empowerment**: The Holy Spirit fills them with courage, power, and guidance.

<!-- p84 -->
3.  **Proclamation**: They boldly proclaim the Good News, often with signs, wonders, and miracles.

<!-- p85 -->
4.  **Opposition**: Resistance arises, leading to persecution or various challenges. Sometimes believers were martyred, sometimes imprisoned and/or beaten.

<!-- p86 -->
5.  **Renewal**: The Church then turns back to prayer, asks for fresh empowerment, and the cycle continues.

<!-- p87 -->
The New Testament Church has, for the most part, grown in the face of steadfast opposition. Around the world the Church endures violence at the hands of oppressors and wicked rulers who intend to suppress the knowledge of the truth of Jesus.

<!-- p88 -->
God knew that this opposition would come; Jesus prophesied about it:

<!-- p89 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="12" data-verses="11-12" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p90 -->
> **<u>Luke 12:11-12</u>** (NKJV) **11** *"Now when they bring you to the synagogues and to rulers and authorities, do not worry about how you should answer or what you should say. **12** For the Holy Spirit will teach you in that very hour what you ought to say."*

<!-- p91 -->
</span>

<!-- p92 -->
Do not be afraid! Jesus promised us that:

<!-- p93 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="fragment" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-verses="18" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p94 -->
> **<u>Matthew 16:18</u>** (NKJV) *… I \[Jesus\] will build My Church, and the gates of Hades shall not prevail against it.*

<!-- p95 -->
</span>

<!-- p96 -->
This is an amazing and comforting promise. Gates are fixed, stationary defensive equipment. When Jesus says that the gates of Hades shall not prevail, He paints the picture of a conquering army that knocks down the gates and takes the city!

<!-- p97 -->
The Holy Spirit grants supernatural boldness to proclaim the witness of the gospel in the face of persecution. The result of this empowerment can be described, but it is best known through experience. It is a supernatural (beyond human) courage that stirs believers to share. Along with this boldness of proclamation there are often supernatural acts of the demonstration of the Spirit and of power (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="2" data-verses="1-5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 2:1-5</span>). People are healed physically and emotionally, the dead are raised, those oppressed by evil spirits are set free, hard-hearted people turn and repent.

<!-- p98 -->
Jesus said that these works, and even greater ones, we will do! (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-verses="12" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 14:12</span>). These collective acts are supernatural, divine, and desperately needed in a world that is tearing itself apart, filled with pain, and waiting for the mature sons of God to be revealed! (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-verses="22-23" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 8:22-23</span>)

<!-- p99 -->
Jesus’ chosen instrument for transformation on earth is His Church, empowered, emboldened, and led by the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p100 -->
## Establishing a Relationship with the Holy Spirit

<!-- p101 -->
Every relationship requires time and experience to deepen. The Holy Spirit is our advocate, our comforter, our helper, and our guide. He empowers us and sanctifies us.

<!-- p102 -->
Begin to think about the Holy Spirit the way you think about a person. In a relationship, both parties can either work to strengthen it or do things that weaken it. He can be ignored, quenched, insulted, or he can be listened to, honored, and obeyed.

<!-- p103 -->
Do not ignore the voice of the Holy Spirit. He is always speaking to us and to the Church. If you do not yet know how to recognize the voice of the Holy Spirit, it takes time, desire, and effort (as in every healthy and fruitful relationship). But don’t worry, you already have within you everything necessary to begin establishing a personal relationship with the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p104 -->
The quickest and most reliable ways to establish an effective relationship with the Holy Spirit are to

<!-- p105 -->
1.  Call out to him **individually** in prayer, pause, and wait for him to speak.

<!-- p106 -->
2.  With a **group** of people who know him (the Church), call out to him, pause, and wait for him to speak.

<!-- p107 -->
By the term 'call out' I mean literally speaking with your mouth, or however you speak (if you cannot speak aloud with your mouth, then with your hands). Why speak? A few reasons:

<!-- p108 -->
1.  We are speaking spirits made in the image of God. God speaks, and so do we.

<!-- p109 -->
2.  We primarily use speech for communication. You can also write what you want to say (journaling) and speak it out loud.

<!-- p110 -->
3.  Speaking is an intentional act, and it signals to the party you are addressing that you want to engage in conversation with them.

<!-- p111 -->
#### What should you say?

<!-- p112 -->
If you have never spoken to God, try introducing yourself. Certainly, it will feel a bit strange, but that discomfort is okay and temporary. Along with your introduction, tell God that you want to talk with him and that you want him to answer you.

<!-- p113 -->
Consider asking him a question. It does not have to be a deep theological question; it can start simple. Can you show me your love for me? Why do I feel a certain way? What would you like to tell me?

<!-- p114 -->
#### Then what?

<!-- p115 -->
Well, in every effective relationship you speak for a while, then pause and listen. God will speak.

<!-- p116 -->
God speaks in many ways, but he wants to do this personally and internally. It will probably not be an audible voice (like your best friend calling your name out loud) but an inner voice that speaks to your heart and mind. Elijah calls it a 'still small voice' (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1KI" data-chapter="19" data-verses="11-13" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Kings 19:11-13</span>).

<!-- p117 -->
The ways I have personally most commonly seen God speak to people, and I have been blessed to help dozens of people learn to speak directly with God, tend to be some combination of the following:

<!-- p118 -->
- An insight - the person who is speaking with God immediately receives understanding that helps them.

<!-- p119 -->
- An image - which is also called a vision or a dream in the Scriptures (a vision when you are awake, a dream when you are asleep); God will give you an image in your mind. Perhaps it is a memory, or a place you have been or that you know? Explore the image; what do you see? As a result, what do you feel? What is happening?

<!-- p120 -->
- An inner voice - like an audible voice, but one that only you can hear, and that is spoken in your heart and mind.

<!-- p121 -->
#### What will God say?

<!-- p122 -->
Not sure! But that’s the fun of it. I can say that it is not a spirit of fear but of perfect love, and it will never contradict the teaching of Scripture. For example, God will never tell you to have an extramarital affair.

<!-- p123 -->
Now what?

<!-- p124 -->
Keep the conversation going! It really is that simple.

<!-- p125 -->
Some good books I’ve read and would personally recommend to help you learn to talk with God are:

<!-- p126 -->
- *Authentic Christianity* by Larry Low ([<u>www.larrylow.com</u>](http://www.larrylow.com))

<!-- p127 -->
- *Good Morning, Holy Spirit* by Benny Hinn ([<u>www.bennyhinn.org</u>](http://www.bennyhinn.org))

<!-- p128 -->
### Why build a relationship?

<!-- p129 -->
The Holy Spirit is the Helper. The Helper helps us! If there is a common language and relationship between us and Him, the work goes much more smoothly. Can you imagine trying to work at your job with someone with whom you have no relationship, who does not speak your language, and with whom you cannot communicate?!

<!-- p130 -->
We serve a God who wants to communicate with us. Jesus said that His sheep know His voice (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-verses="27" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 10:27</span>), the innate abilities we need have already been placed in all of us, but we may lack practiced skill.

<!-- p131 -->
In my personal experience, a personal and active relationship with the Holy Spirit is one of the best things about being a Christian. His presence gives us peace, comfort, and guidance, but only when you know how to seek Him, listen, and obey Him.

<!-- p132 -->
Cultivate sensitivity to the Holy Spirit. As many married people, over time, can communicate with their spouse in a unique way, and sometimes with just a glance. That look alone tells you everything that just happened and what is about to happen. Develop that same kind of sensitivity and familiarity with the Holy Spirit. Involve Him in your whole life; this is a wonderful aspect of your relationship with Him that will add much depth and richness to your walk of faith.

<!-- p133 -->
The Holy Spirit came to lead and guide us, to empower us to be God’s people, and so that we would see the people of the earth completely transformed and freed from the kingdom of darkness into the kingdom of light. Knowing Him and building trust in Him is essential for everyday life and for completing the work Jesus has assigned us to do.

<!-- p134 -->
Our discussion of the work of the Holy Spirit does not end in this chapter. His work is discussed in detail in the remaining chapters of this book, especially in the chapters on sanctification and baptism.
