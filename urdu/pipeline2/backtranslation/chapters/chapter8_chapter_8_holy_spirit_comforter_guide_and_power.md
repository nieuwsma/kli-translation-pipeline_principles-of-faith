# Chapter 8 - The Holy Spirit—Comforter, Guide, and Power

<!-- p1 -->
The Holy Spirit is not a new spirit but is God’s and has been with God from the beginning. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 1:2</span> says that during creation God’s Spirit was hovering over the waters. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="NEH" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Nehemiah 9:20</span> it is written that it was the Spirit who guided the people in the wilderness. Through this same Holy Spirit God anointed kings, judges, and prophets in the Old Testament. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JOL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Joel 2:28-29</span> there is the promise of the Spirit being poured out upon all people. By this same Holy Spirit God raised Jesus from the dead, and now He dwells in us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:11</span>)۔ The Holy Spirit is one, and He has always been the same. The way He works with people has appeared different at different times, but His heart has never changed۔

<!-- p2 -->
Jesus gave his disciples and all of us powerful promises of the Holy Spirit۔

<!-- p3 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p4 -->
> **<u>John 14:16-18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And I will ask the Father, and He will give you another Helper, that He may be with you forever—
>
> the Spirit of truth, whom the world cannot receive because it neither sees Him nor knows Him; but you know Him, because He dwells with you and will be in you۔
>
> I will not leave you as orphans; I will come to you۔
>

<!-- p5 -->
</span>

<!-- p6 -->
When Jesus walked on earth in His body, He was limited like us; His body, like our bodies, needed rest, water, food, sleep, etc. This limitation affected not only Jesus but also His followers, because He could be in only one place at a time. Often when Jesus traveled, great crowds would surround Him, trying to call out to Him and touch Him. Jesus often needed to spend time with His heavenly Father for renewal of strength and for prayer۔

<!-- p7 -->
To multiply the Father’s work, Jesus had to prepare disciples to continue it. Jesus knew that a very important aspect of His ministry on earth depended on connecting with and remaining with His Father. In fact, Jesus said that He could do nothing unless the Father did it through Him and showed Him what to do (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 5:19</span>)۔ In His wisdom, God sent His Holy Spirit to dwell in us, so that He would guide and lead us, and enable us to do the Father’s work۔

<!-- p8 -->
## When was the Holy Spirit sent to all believers?

<!-- p9 -->
Jesus said:

<!-- p10 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p11 -->
> **<u>John 16:7</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Nevertheless I tell you the truth: it is to your advantage that I go away; for if I do not go, the Helper will not come to you; but if I go, I will send Him to you۔
>

<!-- p12 -->
</span>

<!-- p13 -->
When Jesus was crucified, His death made it possible for God’s Spirit to dwell within us. Before this, God’s felt Spirit was mostly confined behind a thick veil in the temple. From time to time some specially chosen individuals were anointed (made holy and set apart for unique service), and the Holy Spirit came upon them. God’s anointing and His Spirit upon them were specific, temporary, and conditional. After his sin with Bathsheba David prayed: ‘*Do not take Your Holy Spirit from me!*’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="51" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 51:11</span>)، because David had seen that because of disobedience the Spirit had departed from King Saul۔

<!-- p14 -->
We (humans), because of our sinful nature, could not receive the Holy Spirit within us, but when Jesus solved the problem of sin through His sacrifice, He made a way through faith for us to become God’s children and heirs (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:17</span>)۔

<!-- p15 -->
In John 20, after His resurrection, Jesus appeared in the midst of a locked room to His disciples and, breathing on them, said, ‘*Receive the Holy Spirit*’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 20:22</span>)۔ There is some debate in theology about exactly what happened at that moment, but most Pentecostal interpretations see it as the disciples receiving God’s Spirit within them, which is the sign of the new birth, that is, being born again. However, this was not the time when God’s Spirit came upon them, which empowers them with boldness for ministry. This was the time when they received the Holy Spirit within, by which they could remain with God and converse with Him. This will be explained in more detail later in this chapter, and also in the chapters on sanctification and baptism۔

<!-- p16 -->
Later, at the end of the forty-day period between the resurrection and the ascension (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:3</span>)، Jesus announced the promise of the Holy Spirit:

<!-- p17 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="4-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p18 -->
> **<u>Acts 1:4-9</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And being assembled together with them, He commanded them not to depart from Jerusalem, but to wait for the Promise of the Father, "which," He said, "you have heard from Me;
>
> for John baptized with water, but you shall be baptized with the Holy Spirit not many days from now."
>
> Therefore, when they had come together, they asked Him, "Lord, will You at this time restore the kingdom to Israel?"
>
> He said to them, "It is not for you to know the times or seasons which the Father has put in His own authority۔
>
> But you shall receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you; and you will be witnesses to Me in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and even to the end of the earth."
>
> And when He had said these things, while they watched, He was taken up, and a cloud hid Him from their eyes۔
>

<!-- p19 -->
</span>

<!-- p20 -->
The disciples were commanded to wait for the fulfillment of the promise of the Spirit that would be poured out upon them, and that it would give them power to be witnesses to the ends of the earth. The Holy Spirit is an essential part of God’s plan for humanity, and for us as believers, for the completion of our commission, and is indispensable for the world.

<!-- p21 -->
## Introduction to the Holy Spirit

<!-- p22 -->
Above all, the Holy Spirit is from God and belongs to God. The Holy Scriptures call the Spirit the Spirit of God, or the Spirit of the Lord, or the Spirit of Christ; although these highlight different aspects, they are all the same. The Holy Spirit, being fully God, is also eternal and holy (blameless, sinless, pure, not impure). Some translations also call the Holy Spirit the Holy Ghost.

<!-- p23 -->
In Greek the word for spirit is **Pneuma** (**NYOO-mah** - **G4151**), meaning breath or wind. In Hebrew, the word for spirit has the same sense as well. Breath is important because we continually need it to live. It was God’s own breath that He breathed into Adam and gave him life, and likewise, before dying on the cross Jesus took His last breath. Without breath there is no life. Breath, like wind, is felt and experienced but, unlike the other ‘elements’ water, fire, earth, it cannot be easily observed.

<!-- p24 -->
There are several other fundamental attributes used to describe the Holy Spirit. Each of these attributes is used with ‘Spirit of’, that is, these characteristics are essential for defining the Spirit. For example, *Spirit of Truth* or *Spirit of Life*.

<!-- p25 -->
Spirit of:

<!-- p26 -->
- **Truth** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:17</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span> points to this role of the Spirit that He guides believers into all truth, revealing God’s will and the reality of Christ.

<!-- p27 -->
- **Life** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:2</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:8</span> highlights the Spirit’s role in granting believers spiritual and eternal life, freeing them from the law of sin and death.

<!-- p28 -->
- **Grace** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:29</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ZEC" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Zechariah 12:10</span> reflects the Spirit’s work of distributing God’s grace, forgiveness, and unmerited favor to humanity.

<!-- p29 -->
- **Glory** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 4:14</span> shows that the Spirit reveals and highlights God’s glory, especially in times of suffering and persecution.

<!-- p30 -->
- **Adoption** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:15</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 4:5</span> refers to the Spirit’s work by which He makes believers the sons and daughters of God, granting them the status of adoption.

<!-- p31 -->
- **Holiness** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 1:4</span> points to the sanctifying work of the Spirit, who sets believers apart for God’s purposes.

<!-- p32 -->
- **Power** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 1:7</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span> states that the Spirit grants believers boldness, service, and the ability to live out their faith effectively.

<!-- p33 -->
In all these attributes the Holy Spirit is rightly recognized as the Helper or Comforter as well. Jesus calls the Holy Spirit the coming Helper; He uses the Greek word ***Paraklētos** (*pah-RAH-klay-tos - **G3875**), which means advocate, helper, one called alongside.

<!-- p34 -->
An advocate speaks on behalf of another, like a defense attorney. They intercede and help the person for whom they are appointed.

<!-- p35 -->
A helper helps. He helps to strengthen the work, but his primary task is to assist in the work. A helper does not take the full responsibility for accomplishing the work, but comes alongside and helps to accomplish it.

<!-- p36 -->
There are many unique purposes and roles in the ministry of the Holy Spirit. His work is inward (in the hearts and lives of believers), upward (in service to and recognition of God), and outward (toward the field of our commission, that is, the world).

<!-- p37 -->
## The Roles of the Holy Spirit - Inward, Upward, Outward

<!-- p38 -->
### Inward - The Holy Spirit unites us with Jesus

<!-- p39 -->
Before Jesus performed any miracle or began His public ministry, He was baptized by John the Baptist and the Spirit of God descended upon Him, and a voice came from heaven, ‘This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:17</span>).

<!-- p40 -->
When we receive Jesus by faith, we receive salvation and the seal of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p41 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p42 -->
> **<u>Ephesians 1:13-14</u>** (NKJV)
>
> In Him you also trusted, when you heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation; and having believed, you were sealed with the Holy Spirit of promise,
>
> who is the guarantee of our inheritance until the redemption of the purchased possession, to the praise of His glory.
>

<!-- p43 -->
</span>

<!-- p44 -->
This seal is not figurative but real. It is the sign of our redemption and the down payment of the full inheritance that we receive as children of God. Romans 8 explains that through new birth we receive in our mortal bodies the very Spirit of God who raised Christ from the dead, and we become a true temple of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 6:16</span>).

<!-- p45 -->
Receiving the Spirit of God transforms us, and from it rivers of living water begin to flow from within us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 7:38</span>). This renewal is immediate and complete yet continues throughout our whole life. The river of the Holy Spirit should never stop in our lives!

<!-- p46 -->
Through salvation and the indwelling of the Holy Spirit we can abide in Christ and He in us. The Spirit of God is no longer confined outside but can dwell with us deeply. From now into eternal life we can walk in fellowship and harmony with God.

<!-- p47 -->
There is much to say about the inward work of the Holy Spirit and His role in our sanctification, but these matters will be discussed in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p48 -->
### Upward—the Holy Spirit bears witness to Jesus

<!-- p49 -->
The Holy Spirit is from God and is God's own. Just as Jesus said that He did nothing except what the Father showed Him (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 5:19</span>), so the Holy Spirit does nothing without the Father's direction. Because the Holy Spirit is the Helper, He does not act on His own will but works in perfect harmony with the Father.

<!-- p50 -->
The Spirit bears witness to Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:26</span>), glorifies Jesus, and discloses and declares the things that Jesus has for us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:14</span>).

<!-- p51 -->
The Holy Spirit will testify of Jesus, glorify Him, teach, and bring Jesus' teaching to our remembrance (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:26</span>), and He will guide us into all truth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span>). Jesus told His disciples that He had much to teach them, but they could not bear it then; however, the Holy Spirit would lead them into all truth. This is another fine point in theology. The teachings that Jesus deemed necessary for His disciples to know were delivered in various ways. Jesus taught them in person, both before His crucifixion and after the resurrection and before His ascension.

<!-- p52 -->
Jesus also promised that the Spirit of truth, who was poured out on them on the Day of Pentecost, would lead them into all truth through divine revelation. Is there divine revelation today? Certainly yes! The gifts of the Holy Spirit, including prophecy and words of knowledge and wisdom, have not ceased! However, no prophecy or word from any spirit is subject to private interpretation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2PE" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Peter 1:20</span>). This is very important. Some people will mistakenly claim that they are hearing the Holy Spirit, but their deeds and words do not bear witness to Jesus. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 4:3</span> warns against every spirit that does not confess Jesus, saying that such a spirit is of the antichrist. This bears repeating: if a spirit departs from the teaching of Christ, that spirit is not the Holy Spirit but an unclean spirit!

<!-- p53 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p54 -->
> **<u>1 John 4:1</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Beloved, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits, whether they are of God; because many false prophets have gone out into the world.
>

<!-- p55 -->
</span>

<!-- p56 -->
Throughout Scripture we see examples of such claims that were in error, often from false prophets or well-meaning individuals who spoke contrary to God's will. For example, Jeremiah warned against prophets who spoke visions of their own heart, declaring peace when destruction was near (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="23" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 23:16-17</span>). Likewise, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 16:16-18</span> records that a slave girl’s words about Paul and Silas were accurate, yet they were inspired not by the Holy Spirit but by a demonic spirit. These examples remind us that things which appear correct can still originate from ungodly sources.

<!-- p57 -->
Every claim such as 'God told me!' or a 'new' teaching must be tested by the biblical standards of truth. For proper discernment we should ask:

<!-- p58 -->
- Is it consistent with Scripture?

<!-- p59 -->
- Does it glorify Jesus Christ?

<!-- p60 -->
- Does it produce divine fruit?

<!-- p61 -->
By applying these tests, we can distinguish the true guidance of the Holy Spirit from errors or deception.

<!-- p62 -->
God has the habit of giving us abundant &amp; plentiful wisdom and guidance (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:5</span> &amp; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="33" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 33:3</span>). God’s divine wisdom will not violate the Holy Scriptures, but it will go beyond the scope of the Scriptures. His *entolē* *(*commands*)* are such immediate orders to be obeyed; these are *rhema* words (as discussed in the chapter on faith).

<!-- p63 -->
This wisdom, understanding, and guidance will likely not be found word for word in the Scriptures. For example, the Scriptures give us excellent general guidance on what to look for in a spouse, but they do not give specific instructions about a particular person. God certainly promises to guide our path, to lead us into all truth, and we can be comforted knowing that this wisdom and this guidance of the Holy Spirit will never violate any teaching of Scripture.

<!-- p64 -->
### Outward-The Holy Spirit empowers the mission

<!-- p65 -->
Jesus gave us His mission, the Great Commission:

<!-- p66 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p67 -->
> **<u>Matthew 28:18-20</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And Jesus came to them and said, "All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to Me.
>
> Therefore go and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit,
>
> and teaching them to observe all things that I have commanded you; and behold, I am always with you, to the end of the age." Amen.
>

<!-- p68 -->
</span>

<!-- p69 -->
The Holy Spirit has a foundational part in this mission, convicting the world concerning sin, righteousness, and judgment:

<!-- p70 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="8-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p71 -->
> **<u>John 16:8-11</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And when He comes, He will convict the world of sin, and of righteousness, and of judgment:
>
> Of sin, because they do not believe in Me;
>
> Of righteousness, because I go to My Father and you see Me no more;
>
> Of judgment, because the ruler of this world is judged.
>

<!-- p72 -->
</span>

<!-- p73 -->
Going deeper, the Spirit brings conviction about these three truths:

<!-- p74 -->
1.  **Conviction of sin**: Revealing the reality of humankind’s rebellion against God, and drawing people’s attention to the depravity in their own lives.

<!-- p75 -->
2.  **Conviction of righteousness**: Displaying Jesus’ perfect obedience in fulfilling the Father’s will, and that Jesus’ sacrifice provided the once-for-all atonement required for the forgiveness of sins.

<!-- p76 -->
3.  **Conviction of judgment**: Affirming Satan’s defeat, the certainty of God’s judgment, and that all will stand before judgment for the works and deeds of their lives.

<!-- p77 -->
The Holy Spirit also empowers the mission. The Holy Spirit gives believers the power to be bold witnesses to share the gospel in the face of persecution. Boldness does not mean being loud. It means having courage despite fear. Power is might, strength, and ability. The Holy Spirit grants the strength and ability to testify to the gospel despite threats and fear of punishment.

<!-- p78 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p79 -->
> **<u>Acts 1:8</u>** (NKJV)
>
> "But you will receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you; and you will be My witnesses in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the ends of the earth."

<!-- p80 -->
</span>

<!-- p81 -->
The entire book of Acts is filled with stories and accounts in which this general cycle appears:

<!-- p82 -->
1.  **Prayer**: Believers ask for boldness and for God’s intervention.

<!-- p83 -->
2.  **Empowerment**: The Holy Spirit fills them with boldness, power, and guidance.

<!-- p84 -->
3.  **Proclamation**: They boldly proclaim the gospel, often with signs and wonders and miracles.

<!-- p85 -->
4.  **Opposition**: Resistance arises, leading to persecution or hardships. Sometimes believers were martyred, sometimes imprisoned and/or beaten.

<!-- p86 -->
5.  **Renewal**: The church returns to prayer, asks for fresh empowerment, and the cycle continues.

<!-- p87 -->
The New Testament church largely grew in the face of steadfast opposition. Around the world the church endures persecution at the hands of oppressors and wicked rulers who are intent on suppressing the knowledge of the truth of Jesus.

<!-- p88 -->
God knew that this opposition would come; Jesus prophesied about it:

<!-- p89 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p90 -->
> **<u>Luke 12:11-12</u>** (NKJV)
>
> "When they bring you to the synagogues and to rulers and authorities, do not worry about how or what you will answer, or what you will say.
>
> For the Holy Spirit will teach you in that very hour what you ought to say."
>

<!-- p91 -->
</span>

<!-- p92 -->
Do not be afraid! Jesus has promised us that:

<!-- p93 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p94 -->
> **<u>Matthew 16:18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And I also say to you that you are Peter, and on this rock I will build My church, and the gates of Hades shall not prevail against it.
>

<!-- p95 -->
</span>

<!-- p96 -->
This is an astonishing and comforting promise. Gates are fixed defensive installations. When Jesus says that *the gates of hell will not prevail* He is painting a picture of a conquering army that knocks down the doors and takes the city!

<!-- p97 -->
The Holy Spirit grants supernatural boldness to bear witness to the gospel in the face of persecution. The results of this empowerment can be described, but it is better to experience it. It is a supernatural (beyond human) boldness that prompts believers to share. This boldness in proclamation is often accompanied by supernatural acts of the manifestation of the Spirit and of power (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 2:1-5</span>). People are healed physically and emotionally, the dead are raised, those oppressed by evil spirits are set free, hard-hearted people turn and repent.

<!-- p98 -->
Jesus said that we will do these works and even greater! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:12</span>). These collective acts are supernatural, divine, and are desperately needed in a world that is tearing itself apart, is miserable, filled with pain, and is longing for the clear revealing of the mature sons of God! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:22-23</span>)

<!-- p99 -->
Jesus’ chosen instrument for transformation on earth is His church, which is empowered by the Holy Spirit, bold, and guided.

## Building a Relationship with the Holy Spirit

<!-- p101 -->
Every relationship takes time and experience to deepen. The Holy Spirit is our advocate, our comforter, our helper, and our guide. He strengthens us and sanctifies us.

<!-- p102 -->
Begin to think about the Holy Spirit as you think about a person. In our relationship, we both can work to strengthen it or do things that weaken our relationship. He can be ignored, he can be quenched, he can be blasphemed against, or he can be listened to, honored, and obeyed.

<!-- p103 -->
Do not ignore the voice of the Holy Spirit. He is always speaking to us and to the Church. If you do not yet know how to recognize the voice of the Holy Spirit, it takes time, willingness, and effort (as it does in all healthy and beneficial relationships). But do not worry; within you already are all the necessary things required to begin establishing a personal relationship with the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p104 -->
The fastest and most reliable ways to build an effective relationship with the Holy Spirit are to

<!-- p105 -->
1.  In prayer, call on him **individually**, pause, and wait for him to speak.

<!-- p106 -->
2.  As part of the **group** of people who know him (the Church), call on him, pause, and wait for him to speak.

<!-- p107 -->
By the phrase ‘call on,’ I mean quite literally to speak with your mouth, or however you speak (if you cannot speak aloud with your mouth, then with your hands). Why speak? A few reasons:

<!-- p108 -->
1.  We are speaking spirits made in the image of God. God speaks, and we speak too.

<!-- p109 -->
2.  We use speech as the primary way of communication. You can also write what you want to say (journaling) and speak it out loud.

<!-- p110 -->
3.  Speaking is a deliberate act, and it signals to the one you are speaking with that you want to engage in conversation with him.

<!-- p111 -->
#### What should you say?

<!-- p112 -->
If you have never spoken to God, try introducing yourself. Certainly, it will feel a bit strange, but that discomfort is okay and temporary. Along with your introduction, tell God that you want to talk with him and that he would answer you.

<!-- p113 -->
Consider asking him a question. It does not have to be a deep theological question; it can be simple. Can you show me your love? Why do I have a particular feeling? What do you want to say to me?

<!-- p114 -->
#### Then what?

<!-- p115 -->
Well, in every effective relationship you speak for a while, then pause and listen. God will speak.

<!-- p116 -->
God speaks in many ways, but he wants to do it personally and inwardly. It likely will not be an audible voice (like your best friend calling your name out loud), but an inner voice that speaks to your heart and mind. Elijah calls it ‘a still small voice’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1KI" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Kings 19:11-13</span>).

<!-- p117 -->
The ways I have personally most commonly seen God speak to people, and I have been blessed to help dozens of people learn to speak directly with God, are some combination of the following:

<!-- p118 -->
- An insight - the person who is speaking with God immediately receives an understanding that helps them.

<!-- p119 -->
- A picture - which is also called a vision or a dream in Scripture (a vision occurs when you are awake, a dream when you are asleep), God will give you a picture in your mind. Perhaps it is a memory, or a place you have been or that you know? Explore the picture: what do you see? As a result, what do you feel? What is happening?

<!-- p120 -->
- An inner voice - like an audible voice, but one that only you can hear, and that is spoken in your heart and mind.

<!-- p121 -->
#### What will God say?

<!-- p122 -->
I don't know! But that's the fun of it. I can say that it is not a spirit of fear at all, but of perfect love, and it will never violate the teachings of Scripture. For example, God will never tell you to commit an illicit relationship outside of marriage.

<!-- p123 -->
What now?

<!-- p124 -->
Keep the conversation going! It really is that simple.

<!-- p125 -->
A few good books I've read and would personally suggest to help you learn to talk with God are:

<!-- p126 -->
- *Genuine Christianity* by Larry Low ([<u>www.larrylow.com</u>](http://www.larrylow.com))

<!-- p127 -->
- *Good Morning Holy Spirit* by Benny Hinn ([<u>www.bennyhinn.org</u>](http://www.bennyhinn.org))

<!-- p128 -->
### Why build a relationship?

<!-- p129 -->
The Holy Spirit is the Helper. The Helper helps us! If we have a shared language and relationship between us and Him, things go much more smoothly. Can you imagine trying to work at your job with someone with whom you have no relationship, who doesn't speak the same language, and with whom you can't communicate?!

<!-- p130 -->
We serve a God who wants to communicate with us. Jesus said that His sheep know His voice (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:27</span>), the natural capacities we need are already within us, but we may lack practiced skill.

<!-- p131 -->
From personal experience, I would say that a personal and active relationship with the Holy Spirit is one of the best things about being a Christian. His presence gives us peace, comfort, and guidance, but only when you know how to seek Him, listen, and obey Him.

<!-- p132 -->
Develop sensitivity to the Holy Spirit. Just as many married people, over time, communicate with their spouse in a unique way—and sometimes with just a look. That look alone tells you everything about what just happened and what is about to happen. Develop the same kind of sensitivity and familiarity with the Holy Spirit. Involve Him in your whole life; this is a wonderful aspect of your relationship with Him that will bring great depth and richness to your faith journey.

<!-- p133 -->
The Holy Spirit has come to lead and guide us, to empower us so that we become God's people, and to see the people of the earth transformed radically, rescued from the kingdom of darkness and brought into the kingdom of light. Knowing Him and building trust in Him is essential for everyday life and for completing the work Jesus has given us to do.

<!-- p134 -->
Our discussion of the Holy Spirit's work does not end in this chapter. The remaining chapters of this book discuss His work in detail, especially in the chapters on sanctification and baptism.
