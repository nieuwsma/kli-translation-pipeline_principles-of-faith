# Chapter 4 - Jesus-Christ

<!-- p1 -->
Based on our study of sin, we concluded that sin exacts a severe price, upon those against whom sin is committed and who must be restored, and also upon those who commit sin and must make restitution. The price of sin must be paid. In the Old Testament, God appointed a series of sacrifices that were offered by priests, individuals, and communities in order to deal with sin, resolve it, and judge it.

<!-- p2 -->
Despite so many sacrifices for sin, they were still not sufficient—this was not because God failed, but because people continued to choose sin. Although animal sacrifices could provide limited atonement, no number of animal sacrifices could change the sinful nature of man.

<!-- p3 -->
God had a magnificent plan, namely the <u>Messiah</u> (in Hebrew ***Mashiach,*** pronounced *Mah-shee-ahkh -* **H4899**) also called <u>Christ</u> (in Greek ***Christos,*** pronounced *Khris-tos* - **G5547**) meaning “anointed”; someone set apart for a specific role (e.g., king, priest, or redeemer/savior).

<!-- p4 -->
To understand who Jesus is, it is helpful to see what people of His time hoped the Messiah would be like. These expectations shaped how they viewed Jesus and, in many cases, why they found it difficult to recognize Him as the fulfillment of God’s plan.

<!-- p5 -->
## Historical Background

<!-- p6 -->
The Jewish people had long awaited a coming Messiah. In the period just before Jesus, Jewish beliefs about the Messiah were shaped by Old Testament prophecies, which had varying interpretations and lacked universal agreement. Many Jews expected that the Messiah would be a political and military leader who would deliver Israel from foreign oppression (especially Roman rule) and restore the Davidic kingdom. Below is an overview of common messianic expectations and how they did (or did not) align with biblical prophecy:

<!-- p7 -->
### Common Jewish Expectations about the Messiah before Jesus

<!-- p8 -->
### **1. A political and military savior**

<!-- p9 -->
- **Belief:** The Messiah would be a king like David, who would lead a successful revolt against foreign powers and restore Israel’s independence۔

<!-- p10 -->
- **Reference verses:** <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 9:6-7</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="23" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 23:5-6</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ZEC" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Zechariah 9:9-10</span>۔

<!-- p11 -->
- **Historical context:** Under Roman rule, the Jews were oppressed and burdened under heavy taxes, so they hoped for a Messiah who would topple Rome and establish a powerful earthly kingdom۔

<!-- p12 -->
- **Truth:** Jesus is King, but His kingdom is not earthly (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="18" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="36" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 18:36</span>)۔ He established a spiritual kingdom at His first coming, but He will return as a conquering King to judge and rule in righteousness, as described in the Book of Revelation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 19:11-16</span>)۔

<!-- p13 -->
### **2. The restorer of the Davidic kingdom**

<!-- p14 -->
- **Belief:** The Messiah would reestablish the Davidic monarchy and bring a golden age of prosperity, justice, and peace۔

<!-- p15 -->
- **Reference verses:** <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2SA" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Samuel 7:12-16</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="AMO" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Amos 9:11</span>۔

- **Interpretation:** The focus often remained on national restoration rather than spiritual renewal۔

<!-- p17 -->
- **Truth:** Jesus, as the offspring of David, fulfills this promise by establishing an eternal kingdom. Although this kingdom is presently spiritual in nature, at His second coming it will reach its fulfillment as a visible reign (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="32-33" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 1:32-33</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 20:4-6</span>)۔

<!-- p18 -->
### **3. A prophet like Moses**

<!-- p19 -->
- **Belief:** Some Jews believed that the Messiah would be a prophet like Moses who would speak directly with God and speak on His behalf۔

<!-- p20 -->
- **Scriptures Referenced:** <span class="x-scripture" data-book="DEU" data-chapter="18" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Deuteronomy 18:15-19</span>.

<!-- p21 -->
- **Expectation:** Although many people were expecting a prophetic figure، they often did not see this role as the Messiah’s primary office but rather as an additional aspect۔

<!-- p22 -->
- **Truth:** Jesus fulfilled this prophecy as the final prophet، speaking God’s word and directly revealing the Father (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:18</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:10</span>). His prophetic role surpasses Moses، for he is the mediator of a new and better covenant (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:6-7</span>).

<!-- p23 -->
### **4. A leader who will bring justice**

<!-- p24 -->
- **Belief:** The Messiah will judge the wicked، uphold the righteous، and bring justice for the oppressed۔

<!-- p25 -->
- **Scriptures Referenced:** <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 11:3-5</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="72" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 72:1-4</span>.

<!-- p26 -->
- **Interpretation:** By justice was meant primarily the political and social aspect rather than the spiritual۔

<!-- p27 -->
- **Truth:** Jesus brings ultimate justice۔ At his first coming he granted spiritual salvation and justice through the cross (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:13-15</span>). At his second coming he will judge the wicked and establish perfect justice for all eternity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 20:11-15</span>).

<!-- p28 -->
### Diverse Messianic Beliefs

<!-- p29 -->
Just as Christianity today is divided into various denominations، sects, or doctrinal frameworks، so also the Jews of Jesus’ time were divided۔ In Jesus’ era, four major Jewish sects shaped Israel’s religious and political landscape، each of which had distinct beliefs and expectations about the Messiah۔ Understanding some background information about these different groups can provide meaningful context for the study of the New Testament books۔

<!-- p30 -->
**Pharisees** were the most influential among the common people، known for strict adherence to the Law (Torah) and oral traditions۔ They believed in the resurrection، the afterlife، and the existence of angels، and emphasized personal piety and obedience to God’s commandments۔ Their messianic expectations centered on a figure who would uphold the Law، bring justice، and restore Israel both politically and religiously۔

<!-- p31 -->
**Sadducees** by contrast were a wealthy and politically powerful group who controlled the Temple and the priesthood۔ They rejected oral traditions and accepted only the Torah as authoritative Scripture۔ The Sadducees denied the resurrection، the afterlife, and the existence of angels، and instead focused on cooperating with Rome to maintain their power and ensure political stability۔ Consequently، they placed less emphasis on the Messiah، since their primary concern was preserving the status quo۔

<!-- p32 -->
**Essenes** were a distinct group who separated from mainstream Jewish society and lived in secluded settlements such as Qumran। They placed strong emphasis on ritual purity، eschatological expectations، and an ordered way of life۔ The Essenes believed in two messianic figures: a priestly Messiah who would restore the sanctity of the Temple، and a royal Messiah who would lead Israel in righteousness۔ This dual-Messiah concept reflected their hope for spiritual and political renewal۔ The Essenes are not mentioned directly in Scripture۔

<!-- p33 -->
Finally، **Zealots** were militant nationalists who sought to overthrow Roman rule through violent revolt۔ They rejected Roman authority and were deeply committed to restoring Israel’s sovereignty۔ Their messianic expectation was of a warrior-king Messiah، like David، who would lead them in battle against their oppressors and establish a powerful earthly kingdom۔

<!-- p34 -->
Although these groups were focused on earthly expectations، Jesus’ mission a far greater plan-one that addresses not only political oppression but also humanity’s spiritual bondage to sin- reveals۔

<!-- p35 -->
## The Reality of the Messiah

<!-- p36 -->
### The anointing of Jesus

<!-- p37 -->
The Pharisees’ and the Zealots’ conception of the Messiah reflects a pattern repeatedly seen in human history-seeking salvation through earthly means while rejecting God’s greater plan۔ As we have seen throughout all of Scripture، people often turn away from God-despite the fact that he is always faithful۔ Even when God remains faithful and receives us، he is often forgotten and rejected، resulting in cycles of disobedience and decline that have destroyed earthly kingdoms۔

<!-- p38 -->
Although many were expecting a political Messiah، Jesus a far greater purpose of his anointing-one that addresses spiritual and eternal needs- revealed۔ If Jesus had come as a triumphant military leader، as the Jews had hoped and expected، it would have been only a matter of time before the kingdom fell into ruin due to human disobedience۔

<!-- p39 -->
Rather، Jesus established a kingdom that cannot be shaken۔ His mission was not rooted in military might or political dominance but in divine purpose۔ As the Messiah، Jesus made clear what he was anointed to do and how he would accomplish it:

<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="16-21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p43 -->
Jesus was anointed—set apart by God—and sent with a divine mission, full of purpose and power.

<!-- p44 -->
Jesus proclaimed:

<!-- p45 -->
- The good news of the Kingdom (Gospel).

<!-- p46 -->
- Release - pardon, forgiveness of sin, freedom.

<!-- p47 -->
- The acceptable year (season) of the Lord.

<!-- p48 -->
Those to whom He came were these:

<!-- p49 -->
- Brokenhearted — emotionally and spiritually shattered.

<!-- p50 -->
- Blind — spiritually, and at times physically as well.

<!-- p51 -->
- Poor — needy, in destitution (spiritual and/or physical).

<!-- p52 -->
- Captives — meaning 'taken with a spear,' that is, those who are held in imprisonment against their will.

<!-- p53 -->
Jesus transformed these broken ones thus:

<!-- p54 -->
- Restoring sight

<!-- p55 -->
- Establishing them in a state of freedom

<!-- p56 -->
- Binding up, healing, restoring

<!-- p57 -->
The purpose of Jesus’ service and ministry was both individual and global, immediate and enduring. His purpose is not confined to a single aspect but is part of that divine plan of God by which humanity is reconciled to God. When sin entered the world, it brought separation between God and man. God had to expel man from the garden and from His presence. Throughout the Old Testament, man could live with God, but only partially. It was very rare for the Spirit of God to be given to humans, and most people lived quite cut off from God. God chose for Himself a people among the Jews, but He desired a permanent, certain, and perfect way to restore His relationship with man.

<!-- p58 -->
In Jesus’ time, the presence of God on earth was largely confined to the innermost part of the temple, the Holy of Holies. Here, behind the thick veil that separated the inner part from the holy place, the presence of God rested with the Ark of the Covenant. Man was separated from God, and God was apart from His creation.

<!-- p59 -->
God sent Jesus to repair, rebuild, restore humanity, and bring it into reconciliation with Himself. (2 Corinthians 5:17-21)

<!-- p60 -->
### The Essential Work of Jesus

<!-- p61 -->
In order to reconcile humanity fully to God, the Messiah’s mission included not only proclamation and healing but also the final act of atonement. Jesus was to become the perfect and complete sacrifice through his death on the cross۔

<!-- p62 -->
The Book of Hebrews, in its 13 chapters, explains in detail how Jesus became the perfect sacrifice, and that he himself was both the sin offering and the High Priest who offered it (Hebrews 3 & 4)۔ Jesus’s priesthood is not of the tribe of Levi (the line of Levi), but according to the order of Melchizedek, of whom it is said that he has neither beginning nor end, meaning he is not bound by human ways and customs (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 7:3</span>)۔ The limitation of the Levitical priesthood is that it depends on the blood of bulls and goats (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:12-13</span>) whose sacrifices are insufficient for the permanent and enduring forgiveness of sins۔ From our study of sin and sacrifice we know that the consequences of people’s sins could be deferred for a year on the Day of Atonement (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:3</span>)، but this sacrifice had to be renewed again and again۔

<!-- p63 -->
By dying on the cross (accounts of which are in Matthew 27، Mark 15، John 19 and Luke 23) Jesus shed his blood and paid the price for the sins of all humanity—past, present, and future۔ Isaiah 53 portrays Jesus as the Suffering Servant who took upon himself the full weight of punishment and anguish۔ His once-for-all death became the final, ultimate sacrifice۔ Jesus is the Lamb of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:29</span>)، without spot or wrinkle (sin); because he was perfect, complete, and holy, the shedding of his innocent blood made atonement possible۔ **No further sacrifices are needed or accepted. The old sacrifices were only temporary-but Jesus’s was perfect and final.**

<!-- p64 -->
Through death and resurrection, we have received a new and better covenant (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:6</span>)، established by the blood of God’s own Son; Jesus is the Mediator of this covenant, the one who stands in between۔ Through Christ’s sacrifice we are saved, and he is ‘the author of eternal salvation to all who obey him’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 5:9</span>)۔

<!-- p65 -->
In Greek the word for salvation is **Sōtēria** (*so-tay-REE-ah - **G4991)**.* It basically means **salvation, deliverance, or protection**۔ It encompasses the act of being saved, both in a spiritual sense (eternal salvation through Christ) and in physical circumstances (deliverance from danger or harm)۔ The term conveys the idea of safety, wholeness, and restoration۔

<!-- p66 -->
**Salvation or deliverance:**

<!-- p67 -->
- Points to God’s deliverance of humanity from sin and its consequences through Jesus Christ۔

<!-- p68 -->
- Used in the context of eternal salvation (justification, sanctification, and glorification)۔

<!-- p69 -->
**Protection:**

<!-- p70 -->
- Conveys the sense of being kept safe, rescued, or protected from danger۔

<!-- p71 -->
**Wholeness or healing:**

<!-- p72 -->
- Includes restoration to a state in which wholeness or soundness is restored۔

<!-- p73 -->
Jesus’s sacrifice paid the price for our forgiveness and made a way for us to have a relationship with God, and set a path for our remaining in him that depends on our obedience, not on our perfection or our ability to abstain from sin۔

<!-- p74 -->
When we receive Jesus by faith (to be explained later), we receive his salvation۔ This salvation connects us to God and records our name in ***the Lamb’s Book of Life*** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 20:11-15</span>)۔ The Gospel of John says:

<!-- p75 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p76 -->
> **<u>John 3:16</u>** (NKJV)
>
> For God so loved the world that He gave His only begotten Son, that whoever believes in Him should not perish but have everlasting life۔
>

<!-- p77 -->
</span>

<!-- p78 -->
To perish means to be ruined—like fruit that spoils before it can be enjoyed۔ It is a life that misses its purpose۔ Jesus came to give us eternal, that is, everlasting life, and to save us from perishing۔ Eternal life is not a life that begins after death; it begins the moment we receive Jesus as our salvation, when we are born-again۔

### New Birth

<!-- p80 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

When we receive Jesus, we become ‘born again’. Jesus used this phrase to describe a transitional place where we are born of the Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:1-15</span>). This happens through faith - choosing to trust Him completely, like entering into a marriage covenant, with both heart and words.

<!-- p84 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="9-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

When we were born on earth (born of flesh and water) many things took place: we took our first breaths and were declared alive, we were received by our parents, we were given a name, we became heirs, we received food and protection, and we kept growing.

<!-- p88 -->
When we place our trust in Jesus and receive Him, we are born again, not of the flesh but of the Spirit. We become spiritually alive! Just as many things happen with physical birth, so also with spiritual birth:

<!-- p89 -->
1.  When we received Jesus, God wiped away our sins and declared us forgiven (**justified**). He gave us a new start. The dividing wall between us and Him was removed. (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>)

3.  We were **adopted** into God’s family and made heirs. (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:5</span>)

<!-- p92 -->
4.  We receive a source of spiritual nourishment, and eternal protection. (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:2</span>)
