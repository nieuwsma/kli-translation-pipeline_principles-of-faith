# Chapter 7 - Jesus-The Foundation of Our Faith

<!-- p1 -->
Building on our study of Jesus as the Christ, in this chapter we continue to look at the life, person, and work of Jesus. We will see that Jesus is the mediator of a new and better covenant—and that he perfectly fulfills the roles of prophet, priest, and king. In this new covenant we, as believers, have a right relationship with God and move from a works-based covenant to a trust-based (faith-based) covenant. Finally, to complete our study of Jesus, we will read and study the *Apostles' Creed*.

<!-- p2 -->
## The Mediator of the New Covenant

<!-- p3 -->
The concept of a mediator is of one who stands between two parties to bridge the gap and help establish a relationship or agreement. In the story of the Exodus, Moses is the initial mediator who represented the covenant that God established with his people. Moses received the Law, offered sacrifice (priest), called God’s people to obedience (prophet), and judged them (judge / king). After Moses, throughout the rest of the Old Testament the mediating role was carried out through the prophets, the Levitical priesthood, and the judges and later the kings of the nation.

<!-- p4 -->
The roles of mediators in the Old Testament:

<!-- p5 -->
1.  **Prophet:** Prophets were God’s messengers, sent to speak his heart to the people and to call them back when they strayed. Moses, for example, served as a prophet who mediated the old covenant by receiving the Law from God and teaching it to Israel. The prophets also foretold the coming of the Messiah and the final salvation of humanity. Other notable examples of prophets include: Isaiah, Jeremiah, Elijah, Elisha, Samuel.

<!-- p6 -->
2.  **Levitical priesthood:** Priests served as mediators by offering sacrifices for the atonement of sins and interceding for the people before God. The high priest in particular played a key role on the Day of Atonement, when he entered the Most Holy Place and sprinkled blood on the mercy seat, symbolizing reconciliation with God. The ministry of the priest is to intercede for the people.

<!-- p7 -->
3.  **Judge/King:** Judges, and later kings, were responsible for governing God’s people, enforcing his laws, judging among the people, delivering them from enemies, and guiding them in righteousness. The book of Judges tells the stories of these leaders - such as Deborah, Samson, and Gideon - whom God raised up to save and guide Israel. Later, there was a line of kings in Israel, many evil, some good (see 1<sup>st</sup> and 2<sup>nd</sup> Kings, 1<sup>st</sup> and 2<sup>nd</sup> Samuel, and 1<sup>st</sup> and 2<sup>nd</sup> Chronicles). A few notable examples are Saul, David, and Solomon.

<!-- p8 -->
It is noteworthy that all the mediators of the old covenant were entirely human and not divine. Being human, they could sin, and often did fall into sin. Some mediators were sinful and led the people away from God and into impure worship: false prophets, lying and corrupt priests, and wicked kings.

<!-- p9 -->
### Christ as Mediator of the New Covenant

<!-- p10 -->
Jesus’ death, resurrection, and ascension fulfill the old covenant and establish the new covenant.

<!-- p11 -->
The New Covenant is a transformative relationship between God and his people, established through the life, death, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. Unlike the old covenant, which was centered on external laws, the new covenant emphasizes inner transformation and grace. It was inaugurated by Jesus at the Last Supper, where he declared that his blood would establish this covenant, and that his once-for-all atonement would replace the repeated animal sacrifices of the old covenant (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="22" data-verses="20" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Luke 22:20</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-verses="12" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 9:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-verses="10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 10:10</span>). Through this sacrifice, the new covenant provides perfect forgiveness and cleansing from sin, fulfilling the condition for perfect and eternal reconciliation with God (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-verses="34" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Jeremiah 31:34</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-verses="12" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 8:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-verses="12-18" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 10:12-18</span>).

<!-- p12 -->
One of the central features of the new covenant is its inward nature, as God writes his law on the hearts of his people and, through the Holy Spirit, nurtures a relationship of the heart with him (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-verses="33" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Jeremiah 31:33</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-verses="10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 8:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="3" data-verses="3" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">2 Corinthians 3:3</span>). This transformation enables believers to obey God’s will not merely by conforming to external commands but by the power of the Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-verses="1-4" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 8:1-4</span>). The new covenant also grants direct access to God, eliminating the need for an earthly priesthood. Jesus serves as the eternal High Priest, and believers are called a royal priesthood, able to come boldly to God through Christ’s sacrifice (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-verses="24-25" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 7:24-25</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-verses="19-20" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 10:19-20</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-verses="9" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Peter 2:9</span>).

<!-- p13 -->
In contrast to the old covenant, which was limited to Israel, the new covenant is universal and extends to everyone who believes in Christ, regardless of race or background (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-verses="16" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 3:16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="3" data-verses="28" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Galatians 3:28</span>). This covenant is entirely grace-based, freely providing salvation and forgiveness through faith in Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-verses="8-9" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>). It is not sustained by human effort but by the empowering work of the Holy Spirit, who transforms believers and enables them to live in obedience and reflect God’s character (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EZK" data-chapter="36" data-verses="26-27" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ezekiel 36:26-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-verses="9-16" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Romans 8:9-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-verses="22-23" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Galatians 5:22-23</span>). Moreover, the new covenant is eternal, surpassing the temporary and conditional nature of the old covenant (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JER" data-chapter="32" data-verses="40" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Jeremiah 32:40</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-verses="13" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 8:13</span>). It represents the final fulfillment of God’s redemptive plan, bringing humanity into reconciliation with himself and enabling believers to live as his people under his kingdom forever.

<!-- p14 -->
Jesus is explicitly identified as the mediator of the new covenant in

<!-- p15 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-type="quote" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-verses="15" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">

<!-- p16 -->
> **<u>Hebrews 9:15</u>** (NKJV) - *For this reason He is the Mediator of the new covenant, that those who are called may receive the promise of the eternal inheritance*.

<!-- p17 -->
</span>

<!-- p18 -->
Through his atoning death and resurrection, Christ inaugurates this new covenant, replacing the old system of sacrifices and external ordinances with a covenant written on believers’ hearts (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-verses="31-34" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Jeremiah 31:31-34</span>). He stands between God and humanity, not only interceding on behalf of his people but also providing the means of reconciliation through his own blood (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-verses="12" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 9:12</span>). Unlike the mediators of the old covenant, Jesus is sinless, eternal, and fully able to meet the divine requirements of justice and mercy, ensuring a lasting relationship between God and believers.

<!-- p19 -->
Jesus fulfills and perfects these roles

<!-- p20 -->
1.  **Prophet:** Jesus is the ultimate Prophet, not merely a messenger of God’s Word but the Word made flesh (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-verses="14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 1:14</span>). He fully and perfectly reveals God’s character, will, and plan of salvation (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="1" data-verses="1-2" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 1:1-2</span>). His teachings, miracles, and prophetic statements fulfill the prophecies of the Old Testament and provide the foundation for the New Covenant.

<!-- p21 -->
2.  **Priest:** Jesus is the perfect High Priest, who offered Himself once for all as a sacrifice for sin (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-verses="27" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 7:27</span>). Unlike the priests of the Old Covenant who had to offer sacrifices repeatedly for their own sins and for the people, Jesus, since He was sinless, offered His own blood to secure eternal redemption (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-verses="14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 9:14</span>).

<!-- p22 -->
3.  **King & Judge:** Jesus is the eternal King, who reigns in righteousness and justice. As the Son of David, He fulfills the promise of an everlasting kingdom (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="2SA" data-chapter="7" data-verses="16" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">2 Samuel 7:16</span>). His kingdom is not merely earthly but cosmic, ruling over all creation and establishing peace through His victory over sin and death (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-verses="9-11" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Philippians 2:9-11</span>). Jesus will return as King and Judge to judge ‘both the living and the dead’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="10" data-verses="42" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 10:42</span>).

<!-- p23 -->
In Christ, the offices of Prophet, Priest, and King are united and perfectly fulfilled. As the Mediator of the New Covenant, He bridges the gap between a holy God and sinful humanity, enabling believers to come boldly before God (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="4" data-verses="16" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 4:16</span>) and securing the eternal inheritance promised in Him.

## The Apostles’ Creed

<!-- p25 -->
*The Apostles’ Creed* is not found in Scripture; it was developed and also revised on the basis of the church’s earliest teachings. A creed is a formal statement of a confession of faith; think of it as a blend of a covenant or oath with a doctrinal statement. Creeds are important because they bring unity among those who believe in a particular way and distinguish them from those who do not. In the United States, participants in civic ceremonies such as city council meetings or legislative assemblies recite the Pledge of Allegiance. This pledge is widely recited in the United States, but not in other countries, because the citizens of those countries are not loyal to the United States. The pledge does not make a person “American,” but it represents an important foundational conviction for those citizens.

<!-- p26 -->
The earliest recognizable forms of this creed were written in the fourth century AD. Over the centuries it has undergone minor changes, and slightly different versions are found depending on the denomination. Not every Christian denomination uses this creed, but to a great extent it has been widely accepted throughout Christianity in one form or another.

<!-- p27 -->
The Apostles’ Creed has been used for centuries as a means of teaching and catechism and expresses the most fundamental truths of the Christian faith—the basic doctrines that must be believed and held in order to be a Christian. In some denominations there is a formal process of catechism through which a new believer is received into that particular denomination and trained in its beliefs.

<!-- p28 -->
It is not necessary for a person to fully understand these doctrines in order to come to God and be saved; salvation is by faith, not by a doctrinal exam! However, as we go on in that faith, we should all come to understand these foundational truths.

## A Brief Explanation of the Apostles’ Creed

<!-- p31 -->
The core truths of the Apostles’ Creed are many and quite profound; a complete explanation would certainly require several hefty volumes. Although this chapter is about Jesus, many subjects also relate to the other persons of the Godhead. This will be a brief explanation of the creed, focusing only on the most central themes. Many of these topics are explored in greater depth in the chapters of this book.

<!-- p32 -->
#### *I believe in God, the Father Almighty, Creator of heaven and earth.*

<!-- p33 -->
This is an affirmation of faith in God as the Almighty, sovereign Creator and Father. The title “Father” highlights God’s loving relationship with His creation, especially with His children through faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-verses="12" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 1:12</span>). As Creator, He brought the universe into existence by command and brought order out of chaos (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="1" data-verses="1-3" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Genesis 1:1-3</span>). His omnipotent power is displayed in the vastness of creation, while His fatherly care is seen in His continual nurture and providence. This foundational statement establishes God as the source and ruler of all life, worthy of worship and trust.

<!-- p34 -->
#### *I believe in Jesus Christ, his only Son, our Lord.*

<!-- p35 -->
Jesus Christ is God’s only Son, begotten from eternity, not created, and sharing in the divine nature (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-verses="1-2" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 1:1-2</span>, 14). Jesus is the “Word” from the beginning, and He became flesh and dwelt among us. As the “Christ,” He is the Anointed Savior who fulfills the promises of the Old Testament. The title “Lord” acknowledges His authority over all creation and His status as ruler and king (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-verses="10-11" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Philippians 2:10-11</span>). He is not only Lord, but He is the Lord who saves (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-verses="31" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Acts 16:31</span>).

<!-- p36 -->
Jesus was not merely sent by God; He is of God. Jesus abides in the Father, and the Father in Jesus. The Gospel of John repeatedly proves that Jesus and the Father are one (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-verses="30" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 10:30</span>), that Jesus did nothing of Himself (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="5" data-verses="19" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 5:19</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="12" data-verses="49-50" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 12:49-50</span>), and that whatever He did was to glorify His Father (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="17" data-verses="4" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 17:4</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-verses="13" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 14:13</span>), and that all of it was by His Father’s authority and power (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-verses="10" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 14:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="8" data-verses="28-29" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 8:28-29</span>).

<!-- p37 -->
#### *He was conceived by the power of the Holy Spirit and born of the Virgin Mary.*

<!-- p38 -->
This affirms the miraculous birth of Jesus through the Holy Spirit and highlights His divine origin. By being born of the Virgin Mary, Jesus fulfills the prophecy of <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="7" data-verses="14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Isaiah 7:14</span>, which foretold the Messiah’s birth from a virgin. This event signifies the Incarnation, when God’s eternal Word took on human flesh (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-verses="14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 1:14</span>). As fully God and fully man, Jesus bridges the gap between God and humanity, making Him the perfect Mediator and Savior.

<!-- p39 -->
Jesus was conceived by the seed of the Holy Spirit, and He did not inherit the sinful nature that we, who are born of the seed of men, possess. Jesus was tempted and tried in every way, yet He was perfect and without sin. <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="4" data-verses="15" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 4:15</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="5" data-verses="21" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">2 Corinthians 5:21</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-verses="22" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Peter 2:22</span>.

<!-- p40 -->
#### *He suffered under Pontius Pilate، was crucified، died، and was buried۔*

<!-- p41 -->
Jesus’ suffering and death were real، historical events that occurred during the tenure of the Roman governor Pontius Pilate، showing the Christian faith to be rooted in verifiable history۔ His crucifixion was the fulfillment of Old Testament prophecies، such as <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="53" data-verses="5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Isaiah 53:5</span>، which speaks of the suffering Servant who would bear the sins of many۔ Jesus’ death was a sacrifice، taking upon himself the penalty for sin on behalf of humanity (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-verses="24" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Peter 2:24</span>)۔ His burial confirms the reality of his death and paves the way for the significance of the resurrection۔

<!-- p42 -->
#### *He descended to the dead۔*

<!-- p43 -->
After his death، Jesus descended to the realm of the dead، commonly referred to in Scripture as *Hades* or *Sheol*۔ This act demonstrates his full solidarity with humanity، even in death، and signifies his victory over sin، death، and the devil (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="3" data-verses="18-20" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Peter 3:18-20</span>)۔ By descending، he proclaimed his triumph and the completion of his saving work۔

<!-- p44 -->
#### *On the third day he rose again۔*

<!-- p45 -->
Jesus’ resurrection is the cornerstone of our Christian faith، confirming his divinity and the truth of his teachings۔ By rising from the dead، he defeated sin and death، and provided the assurance of eternal life for believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="15" data-verses="17-22" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 15:17-22</span>)۔ The resurrection also fulfills Scripture، including Jesus’ own predictions (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-verses="21" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 16:21</span>)۔ This miraculous event transformed the disciples’ despair into bold faith، and laid the foundation for the testimony of the early church۔

<!-- p46 -->
#### *He ascended into heaven، and is seated at the right hand of the Father۔*

<!-- p47 -->
Jesus’ ascension signifies his return in glory to the Father’s presence۔ Seated at the right hand of the Father (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-verses="20" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 1:20</span>)، he is our eternal High Priest، interceding for believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-verses="25" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Hebrews 7:25</span>)۔ His place of authority also signifies his enthronement as King، who rules over all creation and guides the church’s mission (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-verses="22" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 1:22</span>) so that it may do greater works (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-verses="12" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 14:12</span>) and fulfill the Great Commission he gave us (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-verses="18-20" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 28:18-20</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-verses="15-18" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Mark 16:15-18</span>)۔ The ascension assures believers of his ongoing presence and power through the Holy Spirit۔

<!-- p48 -->
#### *He will come again to judge the living and the dead۔*

<!-- p49 -->
This points to Christ’s future return when he will bring final judgment and restoration۔ Jesus will judge all people، reward the faithful and execute justice upon the unrepentant (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="4" data-verses="1" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">2 Timothy 4:1</span>)۔ His return will fulfill God’s ultimate plan for creation، establishing his eternal kingdom of righteousness and peace۔ This hope encourages believers to live with faithfulness and expectation، knowing that their labor in the Lord is not in vain (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="15" data-verses="58" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 15:58</span>)۔

<!-- p50 -->
#### *I believe in the Holy Spirit۔*

<!-- p51 -->
The Holy Spirit، the third person of the Trinity، is God’s active presence and power in the world today۔ He convicts concerning sin، gives new life to hearts، and empowers believers to live holy lives (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-verses="8-13" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 16:8-13</span>)۔ The Spirit unites Christians as one body، that is، the church، and grants them spiritual gifts for service (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-verses="4-11" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 12:4-11</span>)۔ He is the Comforter، Advocate، and Guide، who indwells believers as the seal of their eternal inheritance (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-verses="13-14" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>)۔

<!-- p52 -->
#### *"The holy catholic church"، the communion of saints۔*

<!-- p53 -->
"By 'holy catholic church' is meant the universal body of Christ، which consists of all believers of all times and places۔ The church is set apart for God’s purposes، to reflect his holiness and to proclaim the gospel۔ "The communion of saints" highlights the unity of all Christians، living and dead، who are members of Christ’s body (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-verses="4-6" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 4:4-6</span>)۔ This fellowship transcends earthly divisions، and reveals the shared hope and mission of believers۔

<!-- p54 -->
Jesus is the head of the church۔ The church is called both the Bride of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-verses="25-27" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 5:25-27</span>) and the Body of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-verses="18" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Colossians 1:18</span>)۔

<!-- p55 -->
#### *The forgiveness of sins۔*

<!-- p56 -->
This highlights the heart of the gospel: through the sacrificial death of Jesus، God offers full forgiveness of sins to all who repent and believe (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-verses="7" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Ephesians 1:7</span>)۔ This forgiveness restores the broken relationship between humanity and God، and removes guilt and shame۔ It calls believers to forgive others as well، so that they reflect the grace they have received (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="6" data-verses="14-15" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Matthew 6:14-15</span>)۔

<!-- p57 -->
#### *The resurrection of the body۔*

<!-- p58 -->
Like Jesus، we too will experience bodily resurrection at the end of the age۔ This resurrection will transform mortal and perishable bodies into glorious، imperishable bodies fit for eternal life (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="15" data-verses="42-44" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">1 Corinthians 15:42-44</span>)۔ This highlights the Christian hope of restoration not only of the individual but of all creation، for God is making all things new (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="REV" data-chapter="21" data-verses="4-5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Revelation 21:4-5</span>)۔

<!-- p59 -->
#### *And the life everlasting۔ Amen۔*

<!-- p60 -->
Eternal life with God is the ultimate promise of the Christian faith۔ This life begins even now through a relationship with Christ and will be fully revealed in the new heavens and the new earth، where believers will dwell with God forever (<span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-verses="16" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">John 3:16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-type="ref" data-book="REV" data-chapter="22" data-verses="1-5" data-lang="en" data-version="NKJV">Revelation 22:1-5</span>)۔ This concluding confession reflects the joy and confidence of salvation، which is an echo of the trust and hope expressed throughout the creed۔

<!-- p61 -->
## Conclusion

<!-- p62 -->
In the foundational truths articulated in the Apostles’ Creed we see that the life and work of Jesus are the central point of God’s plan for humanity۔ In each statement of the creed the key aspects of our faith in God and of who Jesus is-his deity، humanity، mission، and final victory-are preserved۔ These truths point to his unique role as the mediator of the new covenant۔ As mediator he invites us to come boldly to God، trusting in his perfect sacrifice and continual intercession۔ It calls us to worship him as rightful King and to follow his teachings as the ultimate Prophet۔ Ultimately، the study of Jesus leads us into a deeper relationship with him، shaping us into people who live in the light of his grace and truth۔

<!-- p63 -->
Understanding who Jesus is and what he has accomplished is the foundation of the Christian faith۔ Jesus’ many names، titles، and roles illuminate different aspects of his person and mission، providing a more complete picture of his love and authority۔

<!-- p64 -->
As with our study about God، likewise the Scriptures are filled with various names for Jesus۔ Each name represents a particular aspect of his complete person، ministry، and character۔ These names are given to reveal to us one aspect of Jesus۔ For your personal study a reference table is included in the appendix: *Table 12 - Names of Jesus.* Read these names and study the reference verses۔
