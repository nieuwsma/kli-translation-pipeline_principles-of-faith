# Chapter 7 - Jesus-The Foundation of Our Faith

<!-- p1 -->
Advancing our teaching that Jesus is the Christ, in this chapter we will continue to look at Jesus’s life, person, and work. We will see that Jesus is the mediator of a new and better covenant—and that he has perfectly filled the roles of prophet, priest, and king. In this new covenant we, as believers, are set in a right relationship with God and move from a works-based covenant to a trust-based (faith-based) covenant. Finally, to complete our teaching on Jesus, we will read and study the *Apostles’ Creed*.

<!-- p2 -->
## The Mediator of the New Covenant

<!-- p3 -->
The concept of a mediator is of a go-between who bridges the gap between two parties and makes a relationship or agreement possible. In the story of Exodus, Moses is the initial mediator of the covenant that God established with his people. Moses received the Law, offered sacrifice (priest), called God’s people to obedience (prophet), and judged them (judge / king). After Moses, throughout the rest of the Old Testament this mediating role continued to be carried out through the office of the prophet, the Levitical priesthood, and the nation’s judges and later kings.

<!-- p4 -->
Mediatorial roles in the Old Testament:

<!-- p5 -->
1.  **Prophet:** Prophets were God’s messengers, sent to convey his heart to the people and to call them back when they strayed. For example, Moses served as a prophet who received the Law from God and, by teaching it to Israel, mediated the old covenant. The prophets also foretold the coming of the Messiah and the final salvation of humanity. Other notable examples of prophets include: Isaiah, Jeremiah, Elijah, Elisha, Samuel.

<!-- p6 -->
2.  **Levitical priesthood:** Priests became mediators by offering sacrifices for the atonement of the people’s sins and by interceding for the people before God. The high priest in particular played a great role on the Day of Atonement when he would enter the Holy of Holies and sprinkle blood on the mercy seat, which was a sign of reconciliation with God. The ministry of the priest is to intercede for the people.

<!-- p7 -->
3.  **Judge/King:** Judges, and later kings, were responsible to rule over God’s people, enforce his laws, judge between the people, deliver them from enemies, and lead them in righteousness. The book of Judges tells the stories of these leaders—leaders-people like Deborah, Samson, and Gideon-whom God raised up to save and guide Israel. Later a line of kings came in Israel, often wicked, some good (see 1<sup>st</sup> and 2<sup>nd</sup> Kings، 1<sup>st</sup> and 2<sup>nd</sup> Samuel، and 1<sup>st</sup> and 2<sup>nd</sup> Chronicles). Some notable examples are Saul، David، and Solomon.

<!-- p8 -->
It is noteworthy that all the mediators of the Old Testament were entirely human and not divine. Being human, they could sin, and often fell prey to sin. Some mediators were sinful and led the people away from God into impure worship: false prophets, false and corrupt priests, and wicked kings.

<!-- p9 -->
### Christ as the Mediator of the New Covenant

<!-- p10 -->
The death, resurrection, and ascension of Jesus fulfilled the old covenant and established the new covenant.

<!-- p11 -->
The new covenant is a transformative relationship between God and his people established through the life, death, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. Unlike the old covenant, which focused on external laws, the new covenant emphasizes inward transformation and grace. It was inaugurated by Jesus at the Last Supper, when he declared that his blood would establish this covenant, replacing the old covenant’s repeated animal sacrifices with his once-for-all sacrifice (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 22:20</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:10</span>). Through this sacrifice the new covenant provides complete forgiveness and cleansing from sin, fulfilling the requirement for perfect and everlasting peace with God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 31:34</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:12-18</span>).

<!-- p12 -->
One of the central features of the new covenant is its internal nature, as God writes his law on the hearts of his people, and through the Holy Spirit establishes a relationship of the heart with him (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 31:33</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 3:3</span>). This transformation enables believers to do God’s will not merely by adhering to external commandments but by the power of the Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:1-4</span>). The new covenant also grants direct access to God, eliminating the need for an earthly priesthood. Jesus serves as the eternal High Priest, and believers are called a royal priesthood, who can come boldly before God through the sacrifice of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 7:24-25</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:19-20</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:9</span>).

<!-- p13 -->
Unlike the old covenant, which was limited to Israel, the new covenant is universal, extending to all who believe in Christ, whatever their race or background (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 3:28</span>). This covenant is entirely grace-based, and offers salvation and forgiveness freely through faith in Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>). It is not sustained by human effort but by the power of the Holy Spirit, who transforms believers and enables them to live in obedience and reflect God’s character (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EZK" data-chapter="36" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ezekiel 36:26-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:9-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-23</span>). Moreover, the new covenant is eternal, surpassing the temporary and conditional nature of the old covenant (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="32" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 32:40</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:13</span>). It represents the final fulfillment of God’s redemptive plan, reconciling humanity to himself and enabling believers to live forever as his people in his kingdom.

<!-- p14 -->
Jesus is explicitly identified as the Mediator of the New Covenant

<!-- p15 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

>
> And for this reason He is the Mediator of the new covenant, by means of death, for the redemption of the transgressions under the first covenant, that those who are called may receive the promise of the eternal inheritance.
>

<!-- p17 -->
</span>

<!-- p18 -->
Through his atoning death and resurrection, Christ inaugurates this new covenant, replacing the old system of sacrifices and external laws with a covenant that is written on believers’ hearts (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 31:31-34</span>). He stands between God and humanity, not only interceding on behalf of his people but also providing the means of reconciliation through his own blood (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:12</span>). Unlike the mediators of the old covenant, Jesus is sinless, eternal, and fully able to fulfill the divine demands of justice and mercy, ensuring a lasting relationship between God and believers.

<!-- p19 -->
Jesus fulfills and perfects these roles

<!-- p20 -->
1.  **Prophet:** Jesus is the ultimate Prophet، not merely a messenger of God’s Word but the Word made flesh (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:14</span>)۔ He reveals God’s being, will, and plan of salvation in full and perfect form (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 1:1-2</span>)۔ His teachings, miracles, and prophetic statements fulfill the Old Testament prophecies and provide the foundation for the New Covenant۔

<!-- p21 -->
2.  **Priest:** Jesus is the perfect High Priest، who offers Himself as a once-for-all sacrifice for sin (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 7:27</span>)۔ Unlike the priests of the Old Covenant, who had to offer sacrifices repeatedly for their own sins and for the people, Jesus, who is sinless, offered His own blood to secure eternal redemption (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:14</span>)۔

<!-- p22 -->
3.  **King and Judge:** Jesus is the eternal King، who reigns in righteousness and justice. As the Son of David, He fulfills the promise of an everlasting kingdom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2SA" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Samuel 7:16</span>)۔ His kingship is not merely earthly but cosmic; He rules over all creation and establishes peace through His victory over sin and death (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 2:9-11</span>)۔ Jesus will return as King and Judge, and will judge ‘both the living and the dead’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 10:42</span>)۔

<!-- p23 -->
In Christ the roles of Prophet, Priest, and King are united and perfectly fulfilled۔ As the Mediator of the New Covenant, He bridges the gap between a holy God and sinful humanity, enabling believers to come before God with confidence (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 4:16</span>) and securing the eternal inheritance promised in Him۔

## The Apostles’ Creed

<!-- p25 -->
The Apostles’ Creed is not found in Scripture؛ it was formulated on the basis of the early teachings of the Church and revised over time. A creed is a formal statement of confession of faith؛ think of it as a combination of a statement of belief with a covenant or oath. Creeds are important because through them those who believe in a particular way and those who do not are both united and distinguished. In the United States, in civic ceremonies such as city council meetings or legislative assemblies, it is common for participants to recite the Pledge of Allegiance. This pledge is widely used in the United States, but not in other countries, because the citizens of those countries are not loyal to the United States. This pledge does not make a person ‘American’, but for those who are citizens it represents a significant and foundational conviction۔

<!-- p26 -->
The earliest recognizable forms of the creed were written in the fourth century A.D. Over the centuries this creed underwent minor changes, and some partial differences are found on a denominational basis. Not every Christian denomination uses this creed, but to a large extent it is widely accepted in some form throughout Christianity۔

<!-- p27 -->
For centuries the Apostles’ Creed has been used as a tool for instruction and Christian teaching (catechism) and sets forth the most basic truths of the Christian faith—the foundational doctrines that must be acknowledged and held in order to be Christian. In some denominations there is a formal process of catechism through which a new believer is received into that particular denomination and trained in their beliefs۔

<!-- p28 -->
It is not necessary for a person to fully understand these doctrines in order to come to God and be saved؛ salvation is by faith, not by a doctrinal exam! However, as we continue in this faith, we should all come to understand these foundational truths۔

## A brief explanation of the Apostles’ Creed

<!-- p31 -->
The foundational truths in the Apostles’ Creed are many and exceedingly deep؛ a complete exposition would certainly require several volumes. Although this chapter is about Jesus, many topics extend to the other Persons of the Godhead. This will be a brief explanation of the creed, focusing only on the central themes. Many of these topics are explored in greater depth in various chapters of this book۔

<!-- p32 -->
#### *I believe in God, the Father Almighty, Maker of heaven and earth.*

<!-- p33 -->
This affirms faith in God as the Almighty, sovereign Creator and Father. The title “Father” highlights God’s loving relationship with His creation, especially with those children who are His through faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:12</span>)۔ As Creator, He spoke the universe into existence and brought order out of chaos (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 1:1-3</span>)۔ His omnipotent power is revealed in the vastness of creation, while His fatherly care is seen in His continual sustaining and provision. This foundational statement establishes God as the source and ruler of all life, worthy of worship and trust۔

<!-- p34 -->
#### *I believe in Jesus Christ, who is His only Son, our Lord.*

<!-- p35 -->
Jesus Christ is God’s only Son, eternally begotten, not created, and partaking in the divine nature (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:1-2</span>, 14)۔ Jesus is the ‘Word’ from the beginning, and He became flesh and dwelt among us. As the “Christ” He is the anointed Messiah, fulfilling the Old Testament promises about the Savior. The title “Lord” acknowledges His authority over all creation and His rightful place as Ruler and King (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 2:10-11</span>)۔ He is not only Lord but the saving Lord (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 16:31</span>)۔

<!-- p36 -->
Jesus was not merely sent by God؛ He is of God. Jesus abides in the Father and the Father abides in Jesus. The Gospel of John repeatedly proves that Jesus and the Father are one (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="30" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:30</span>), that Jesus did nothing of Himself (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 5:19</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="49-50" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 12:49-50</span>), and that whatever He did was for His Father’s glory (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="17" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 17:4</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:13</span>), and that all of it was done by His Father’s authority and power (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 8:28-29</span>)۔

<!-- p37 -->
#### *He was conceived by the power of the Holy Spirit and born of the Virgin Mary.*

<!-- p38 -->
This affirms Jesus’ miraculous conception by the Holy Spirit, which highlights His divine origin. By being born of the Virgin Mary, Jesus fulfilled the prophecy of <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 7:14</span>, which foretold the Messiah’s birth from a virgin. This event is the sign of the incarnation, when God’s eternal Word took on human flesh (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:14</span>)۔ As fully God and fully man, Jesus bridges the gap between God and humanity, making Him the perfect Mediator and Savior۔

<!-- p39 -->
Jesus was conceived by the seed of the Holy Spirit, and He did not inherit the sinful nature that we, who are born of the seed of man, possess. Jesus was tempted and tested in every way, but He was perfect and sinless. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 4:15</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 5:21</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:22</span>.

<!-- p40 -->
#### *He suffered under Pontius Pilate, was crucified, died, and was buried.*

<!-- p41 -->
Jesus’ suffering and death were real, historical events that occurred during the time of the Roman governor Pontius Pilate, which roots the Christian faith in verifiable history. His crucifixion was the fulfillment of Old Testament prophecies, such as <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="53" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 53:5</span>, which speaks of the suffering Servant who would bear the sins of many. Jesus’ death was a sacrifice, taking upon himself the penalty of sin on behalf of humanity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:24</span>). His burial confirms the reality of his death and sets the stage for the significance of the resurrection.

<!-- p42 -->
#### *He descended to the dead.*

<!-- p43 -->
After his death, Jesus descended to the realm of the dead, often called *Hades* or *Sheol* in Scripture. This act reveals his complete identification with humanity, even in death, and is a sign of his victory over sin, death, and the devil (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 3:18-20</span>). By descending he proclaimed his triumph and the completion of his redemptive work.

<!-- p44 -->
#### *On the third day he rose again.*

<!-- p45 -->
Jesus’ resurrection is the cornerstone of our Christian faith, confirming his divinity and the truth of his teachings. By rising from the dead he defeated sin and death, giving believers the assurance of eternal life (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 15:17-22</span>). The resurrection also fulfills Scripture, including Jesus’ own predictions (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 16:21</span>). This miraculous event turned the disciples’ despair into bold faith and laid the foundation for the testimony of the early church.

<!-- p46 -->
#### *He ascended into heaven and is seated at the right hand of the Father.*

<!-- p47 -->
Jesus’ ascension marks his glorious return to the presence of the Father. Seated at the right hand of the Father (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:20</span>), he assumes the role of our eternal High Priest, interceding for believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 7:25</span>). His position of authority also signifies his exaltation as King, ruling over all creation and guiding the mission of the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:22</span>) so that it may do greater works (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:12</span>) and fulfill the Great Commission he gave us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:18-20</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 16:15-18</span>). The ascension assures believers of his continuing presence and power through the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p48 -->
#### *He will come again to judge the living and the dead.*

<!-- p49 -->
This points to Christ’s future return when he will bring final judgment and restoration. Jesus will judge all people, rewarding the faithful and bringing judgment upon the unrepentant (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 4:1</span>). His return will accomplish God’s ultimate plan for creation, inaugurating his eternal kingdom of righteousness and peace. This hope encourages believers to live with faithfulness and expectation, knowing that their labor in the Lord is not in vain (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="58" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 15:58</span>).

<!-- p50 -->
#### *I believe in the Holy Spirit.*

<!-- p51 -->
The Holy Spirit, the third person of the Trinity, is God’s presence and power at work in the world today. He convicts of sin, gives new life to hearts, and empowers believers to live holy lives (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:8-13</span>). The Spirit unites believers into one body, the church, and equips them with spiritual gifts for service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:4-11</span>). He is the Comforter, Advocate, and Guide, who dwells within believers to seal them for their eternal inheritance (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>).

<!-- p52 -->
#### *The holy universal Church, the communion of saints.*

<!-- p53 -->
The "holy universal Church" refers to the universal body of Christ, comprising all believers across time and space. The Church is set apart for God’s purposes, to display his holiness and share the gospel. The "communion of saints" emphasizes the unity of all Christians, living and dead, as members of Christ’s body (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:4-6</span>). This fellowship transcends earthly divisions and expresses the shared hope and mission of believers.

<!-- p54 -->
Jesus is the head of the Church. The Church is called both the Bride of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="25-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:25-27</span>) and the Body of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 1:18</span>).

<!-- p55 -->
#### *The forgiveness of sins.*

<!-- p56 -->
This highlights the heart of the gospel: through Jesus’ sacrificial death, God offers full forgiveness of sins to all who repent and believe (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:7</span>). This forgiveness restores the broken relationship between humanity and God and removes the burden of guilt and shame. It also calls believers to forgive others, reflecting the grace they have received (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 6:14-15</span>).

<!-- p57 -->
#### *The resurrection of the body.*

<!-- p58 -->
Like Jesus, at the end of the age we will experience a bodily resurrection. This resurrection will transform mortal, perishable bodies into glorious, incorruptible bodies fit for eternal life (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42-44" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 15:42-44</span>). This highlights the Christian hope of restoration, not only for individuals but for all creation, when God makes all things new (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="21" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 21:4-5</span>).

<!-- p59 -->
#### *And the life everlasting. Amen.*

<!-- p60 -->
Eternal life with God is the ultimate promise of the Christian faith. This life begins even now through a relationship with Christ and will be fully revealed in the new heavens and the new earth, where believers will dwell with God forever (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 22:1-5</span>). This concluding affirmation reflects the joy and confidence of salvation, and echoes the trust and hope expressed throughout the creed.

<!-- p61 -->
## Conclusion

<!-- p62 -->
In the fundamental truths stated in the Apostles’ Creed, we see that the life and work of Jesus are at the center of God’s plan for humanity. In each statement of the creed, the key aspects of faith in God and who Jesus is—His divinity, humanity, mission, and ultimate victory—are encompassed. These truths point to His unique role as Mediator of the New Covenant. As Mediator He invites us to come before God with confidence, trusting in His perfect sacrifice and ongoing intercession. It calls us to worship Him as the righteous King and to follow His teachings as the ultimate Prophet. Ultimately, the study of Jesus leads us into a deeper relationship with Him, and shapes us into people who live in the light of His grace and truth.

<!-- p63 -->
Understanding who Jesus is and what He has accomplished is the foundation of the Christian faith. The many names, titles, and roles of Jesus illuminate different aspects of His person and mission, and present a more complete picture of His love and authority.

<!-- p64 -->
As in the study of God, so too Scripture is filled with various names with reference to Jesus. Each name represents a particular aspect of His complete character, ministry, and office. These names are given to reveal to us one aspect of Jesus. For your personal study, a reference table is included in the appendix: *Table 12 - Names of Jesus.* Read these names and study the Scripture references.
