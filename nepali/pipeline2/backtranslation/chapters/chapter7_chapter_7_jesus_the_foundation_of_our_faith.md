# Chapter 7 - Jesus—The Foundation of Our Faith

<!-- p1 -->
After studying Jesus as the Messiah, in this chapter we continue to look at Jesus’s life, character, and work. We will see that Jesus is the mediator of a new and better covenant—and that He perfectly fulfilled the roles of prophet, priest, and king. In this new covenant we believers remain in right relationship with God, and there is a transition from a works-based covenant to a trust-based (faith-based) covenant. Finally, we will read and study the Apostles’ Creed as a way to complete our study of Jesus.

<!-- p2 -->
## Mediator of the New Covenant

<!-- p3 -->
The concept of a mediator refers to a go-between who bridges the gap between two parties to bring about a relationship or agreement. In the Exodus narrative, Moses is the initial mediator of the covenant that God established with His people. Moses received the law, offered sacrifices (priest), called God’s people to obedience (prophet), and judged them (judge/king). After Moses, throughout the rest of the Old Testament, the role of mediator was carried out through the roles of prophets, the Levitical priesthood, and later the nation’s judges and kings.

<!-- p4 -->
The roles of mediators of the old covenant:

<!-- p5 -->
1.  **Prophet:** Prophets were God’s messengers, sent to speak God’s heart to the people and to call them back when they strayed. For example, Moses, as a prophet mediating the old covenant, received the law from God and taught Israel. The prophets also foretold the coming Messiah and the ultimate redemption of humanity. Other major prophets include Isaiah, Jeremiah, Elijah, Elisha, and Samuel.

<!-- p6 -->
2.  **Levitical priesthood:** Priests carried out the work of mediation by offering sacrifices for the atonement of sin and by pleading with God on behalf of the people. In particular, the high priest would enter the Most Holy Place on the Day of Atonement and sprinkle blood on the mercy seat, symbolizing reconciliation with God. The ministry of priests is to intercede for the people.

<!-- p7 -->
3.  **Judge/King:** Judges, and later kings, were given the task of ruling over God’s people, enforcing His laws, administering justice among the people, delivering them from enemies, and leading in righteousness. The book of Judges tells the stories of these leaders—figures like Deborah, Samson, and Gideon—whom God raised up to save and guide Israel. Later in Israel there were many evil and a few good kings (see 1st and 2nd Kings, 1st and 2nd Samuel, and 1<sup>st</sup> and 2<sup>nd</sup> Chronicles). Some key examples are Saul, David, and Solomon.

<!-- p8 -->
What is worth noting is that all the mediators of the old covenant were fully human and not divine. Because they were human, they were capable of sin, and many times inclined toward it. Some mediators became entangled in sin and led the people away from God and into profane worship: false prophets, lying and corrupt priests, and wicked kings.

<!-- p9 -->
### Christ as Mediator of the New Covenant

<!-- p10 -->
Jesus’s death, resurrection, and ascension fulfill the old covenant and establish the new covenant.

<!-- p11 -->
The new covenant is the transformative relationship between God and His people, established through the life, death, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. In contrast to the old covenant, whose center was external law, the new covenant emphasizes inner transformation and grace. Jesus inaugurated this covenant at the Last Supper, where He declared that His blood would establish it, placing His once-for-all atonement in the place of the repeated animal sacrifices of the old covenant (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 22:20</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:10</span>). Through this sacrifice, the new covenant grants full forgiveness and purification from sin, fulfilling the need for perfect and lasting reconciliation with God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 31:34</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:12-18</span>).

<!-- p12 -->
A central feature of the new covenant is its internal nature, according to which God writes His law on the hearts of His people, developing a heart-level relationship with Him through the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 31:33</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 3:3</span>). This transformation enables believers to live out God’s will not merely by keeping external commands, but being empowered by the Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:1-4</span>). The new covenant also provides direct access to God, removing the need for an earthly priesthood. Jesus is the eternal High Priest, and believers are called a royal priesthood, who are able to approach God with confidence through Christ’s sacrifice (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 7:24-25</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:19-20</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:9</span>).

<!-- p13 -->
In contrast to the old covenant, which encompassed only Israel, the new covenant is universal, extended to all who believe in Christ regardless of ethnicity or background (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 3:28</span>). This covenant is entirely based on grace, offering salvation and forgiveness freely through faith in Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>). It is not sustained by human effort but by the empowering work of the Holy Spirit, who transforms believers and enables them to live in obedience and reflect God’s character (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EZK" data-chapter="36" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ezekiel 36:26-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:9-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-23</span>). Moreover, the new covenant will endure forever, surpassing the temporary and conditional nature of the old covenant (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="32" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 32:40</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:13</span>). It represents the ultimate fulfillment of God’s plan of salvation, reconciling humanity to Him and enabling believers to live as His people under His rule forever.

<!-- p14 -->
Jesus is explicitly identified as the mediator of the new covenant in

<!-- p15 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p16 -->
> **<u>Hebrews 9:15</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And for this reason He is the Mediator of the new covenant, by means of death, for the redemption of the transgressions under the first covenant, that those who are called may receive the promise of the eternal inheritance.
>

<!-- p17 -->
</span>

<!-- p18 -->
By His atoning death and resurrection, Christ inaugurates this new covenant, replacing the old system of sacrifices and external law with a covenant written on the hearts of believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 31:31-34</span>). He stands between God and humanity, not only interceding for His people but also providing the very means of reconciliation through His own blood (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:12</span>). Unlike the mediators of the old covenant, Jesus is sinless, eternal, and fully able to meet the divine demands of justice and grace, ensuring a lasting relationship between God and believers.

<!-- p19 -->
Jesus fulfills and perfects these roles

<!-- p20 -->
1.  **Prophet:** Jesus is the supreme Prophet; he is not merely a messenger of God’s Word, but is himself the Word made flesh (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:14</span>). He fully and perfectly reveals God’s nature, will, and plan of salvation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 1:1-2</span>). His teachings, miracles, and prophetic declarations fulfill Old Testament prophecies and provide the foundation of the New Testament.

<!-- p21 -->
2.  **Priest:** Jesus is the perfect High Priest, who offered himself once for all as a sacrifice for sin (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 7:27</span>). Unlike the Old Testament priests who had to offer sacrifices repeatedly for their own sins and for the people, the sinless Jesus offered his own blood to secure eternal redemption (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:14</span>).

<!-- p22 -->
3.  **King &amp; Judge:** Jesus is the eternal King, who reigns in righteousness and justice. As a descendant of David, he fulfills the promise of an everlasting kingdom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2SA" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Samuel 7:16</span>). His kingship is not merely earthly but universal; he reigns over all creation and establishes peace through his victory over sin and death (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 2:9-11</span>). Jesus will come again as King and Judge and will judge ‘the living and the dead’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 10:42</span>).

<!-- p23 -->
In Christ, the roles of prophet, priest, and king are unified and perfectly fulfilled. As the mediator of the new covenant, he bridges the gap between a holy God and sinful humanity, enabling believers to approach God with confidence (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 4:16</span>) and securing the eternal inheritance promised in him.

## The Apostles’ Creed

<!-- p25 -->
*The Apostles’ Creed* is not found in Scripture; it was composed and refined on the basis of the early teachings of the church. A creed is a formal confession of beliefs; think of it like a pledge or oath associated with a statement of faith. Creeds are important because they are used to unite those who believe in a particular way and to distinguish them from those who do not. In the United States, participants in civic ceremonies such as a city council meeting or a legislative assembly often recite the Pledge of Allegiance. This pledge is used widely throughout the United States, but not in other countries, because citizens of those countries are not pledged to the United States. This pledge does not make anyone “an American,” but for citizens it represents an important foundational belief.

<!-- p26 -->
The earliest identifiable versions of the creed were written in the fourth century AD. The creed has changed slightly over time, and there are small variations depending on denomination. Not every Christian denomination uses this creed, but it is largely accepted in some form throughout Christianity.

<!-- p27 -->
The Apostles’ Creed has been used for centuries as a tool for teaching and catechesis, and it communicates the most fundamental truths of the Christian faith—that is, the core beliefs to be accepted and held in order to be Christian. Some denominations have a formal catechesis process, by which new believers are incorporated into that specific denomination and initiated in their faith.

<!-- p28 -->
It is not necessary to fully understand these beliefs in order to come to God and be saved; salvation is by faith, not by a doctrinal test! However, as we go on in that same faith, we should all come to understand these foundational truths.

## A Brief Explanation of the Apostles’ Creed

<!-- p31 -->
The foundational truths of the Apostles’ Creed are many and profound; it would take many volumes to expound it fully. Although this chapter is about Jesus, many topics also apply to the other members of the Godhead. This will be a concise explanation of the creed, focusing only on central themes. Some of these subjects are studied in greater detail in various chapters of this book.

<!-- p32 -->
#### *I believe in God, the Father almighty, Creator of heaven and earth.*

<!-- p33 -->
This affirms belief in God as the almighty, sovereign Creator and as Father. The title “Father” especially emphasizes his loving relationship with his children by faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:12</span>). As Creator, he spoke the universe into existence, bringing order out of chaos (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 1:1-3</span>). His omnipotent power is displayed in the vastness of creation, and his fatherly care is seen in his continual sustenance and provision. This foundational statement establishes God as the origin and ruler of all life, who is worthy of worship and trust.

<!-- p34 -->
#### *I believe in Jesus Christ, his only Son, our Lord.*

<!-- p35 -->
Jesus Christ is the unique Son of God, eternally begotten, not created, and shares the divine nature (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:1-2</span>, 14). Jesus is the “Word” from the beginning, and he dwelt among us by taking on flesh. As the “Christ,” he is the anointed Messiah who fulfills the Old Testament promises concerning the Savior. The title “Lord” acknowledges his authority over all creation and his rightful place as ruler and king (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 2:10-11</span>). He is not only Lord but also the Lord who saves (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 16:31</span>).

<!-- p36 -->
Jesus was not only sent by God; he is of the very being of God. Jesus dwells in the Father and the Father dwells in Jesus. The Gospel of John repeatedly affirms that Jesus and the Father are one (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="30" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:30</span>), that Jesus did not act according to his own will (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 5:19</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="49-50" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 12:49-50</span>), and that everything he did was to glorify his Father (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="17" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 17:4</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:13</span>), and that all of it was by his Father’s authority and power (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 8:28-29</span>).

<!-- p37 -->
#### *He was conceived by the power of the Holy Spirit and born of the virgin Mary.*

<!-- p38 -->
This affirms the miraculous conception of Jesus by the Holy Spirit, emphasizing his divine origin. By being born of the virgin Mary, Jesus fulfills the prophecy of <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 7:14</span>, which foretold the virgin birth of the Messiah. This event marks the incarnation, in which the eternal Word of God took on human flesh (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:14</span>). Being fully God and fully man, Jesus bridges the distance between God and humanity, making him the perfect mediator and Savior.

<!-- p39 -->
Jesus was conceived by the seed of the Holy Spirit, and since we are born of the seed of man, by which we receive a sinful nature, he did not receive that. Jesus was tempted and tested in every way, yet he was perfect and sinless. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 4:15</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 5:21</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:22</span>.

<!-- p40 -->
#### *He suffered under Pontius Pilate, was crucified, died, and was buried.*

<!-- p41 -->
Jesus’ suffering and death were real, historical events that occurred under the Roman governor Pontius Pilate, providing the Christian faith with a basis in verifiable history. His crucifixion fulfilled Old Testament prophecies, such as <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="53" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 53:5</span>, which speaks of the suffering servant who bears the sins of many. Jesus’ death was sacrificial, bearing the penalty for sin on behalf of humanity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:24</span>). His burial confirms the reality of His death and prepares for the significance of the resurrection.

<!-- p42 -->
#### *He descended to the dead.*

<!-- p43 -->
After His death, Jesus descended to the realm of the dead, which Scripture often calls *Hades* or *Sheol*. This act shows His complete identification with humanity even in death, and signifies His victory over sin, death, and Satan (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 3:18-20</span>). In His descent, He proclaimed His victory and the completion of His saving work.

<!-- p44 -->
#### *On the third day He rose again.*

<!-- p45 -->
Jesus’ resurrection is the cornerstone of our Christian faith, confirming His divinity and the truth of His teachings. By rising from the dead, He defeated sin and death and gave believers the assurance of eternal life (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 15:17-22</span>). The resurrection also fulfills Scripture, including Jesus’ own prophecies (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 16:21</span>). This wondrous event transformed the disciples’ despair into courageous faith and became the foundation of the early church’s witness.

<!-- p46 -->
#### *He ascended into heaven, and is seated at the right hand of the Father.*

<!-- p47 -->
Jesus’ ascension signifies His return to the Father’s presence in glory. Seated at the Father’s right hand (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:20</span>), He assumes the role of our eternal High Priest, interceding for believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 7:25</span>). His position of authority also signifies His exaltation as King, reigning over all creation and directing the mission of the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:22</span>), so that greater works may be done (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:12</span>) and the Great Commission He has given us may be fulfilled (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:18-20</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 16:15-18</span>). The ascension assures believers of His continued presence and power through the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p48 -->
#### *He will come again to judge the living and the dead.*

<!-- p49 -->
This points to Christ’s future return, when He will bring final judgment and restoration. Jesus will judge all people, rewarding the faithful and bringing judgment upon those who do not repent (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 4:1</span>). His return will accomplish God’s ultimate plan for creation, inaugurating His eternal kingdom of righteousness and peace. This hope encourages believers to live faithfully and expectantly, knowing that their labor in the Lord is not in vain (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="58" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 15:58</span>).

<!-- p50 -->
#### *I believe in the Holy Spirit.*

<!-- p51 -->
The Holy Spirit, the third person of the Trinity, is God’s active presence and power in the world today. He convicts of sin, brings new birth to hearts, and empowers believers to live holy lives (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:8-13</span>). The Spirit forms Christians into one body, namely the church, and equips them for service through spiritual gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:4-11</span>). He is the Comforter, Advocate, and Guide, dwelling within believers and serving as the seal of their eternal inheritance (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>).

<!-- p52 -->
#### *The holy universal church, the communion of saints.*

<!-- p53 -->
"The holy universal church" refers to the worldwide body of Christ, encompassing all believers across time and place. The church is set apart for God’s purposes, called to reflect His holiness and to share the gospel. "The communion of saints" emphasizes the unity of all Christians, living and dead, as members of Christ’s body (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:4-6</span>). This fellowship transcends worldly divisions, displaying believers’ shared hope and mission.

<!-- p54 -->
Jesus is the head of the church. The church is addressed as both the bride of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="25-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:25-27</span>) and the body of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 1:18</span>).

<!-- p55 -->
#### *The forgiveness of sins.*

<!-- p56 -->
This highlights the very heart of the gospel: through Jesus’ sacrificial death, God grants full forgiveness of sins to all who repent and believe (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:7</span>). This forgiveness restores the broken relationship between humanity and God, removing guilt and shame. It also calls believers to extend forgiveness to others, reflecting the grace they have received (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 6:14-15</span>).

<!-- p57 -->
#### *The resurrection of the body.*

<!-- p58 -->
Like Jesus, at the end of time we will experience bodily resurrection. This resurrection will transform mortal, perishable bodies into glorified, imperishable bodies fit for eternal life (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42-44" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 15:42-44</span>). It underscores the Christian hope of restoration not only for individuals but for all creation, as God makes all things new (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="21" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 21:4-5</span>).

<!-- p59 -->
#### *And the life everlasting. Amen.*

<!-- p60 -->
Eternal life with God is the supreme promise of the Christian faith। This life begins even now through a relationship with Christ and will be fully realized in the new heavens and the new earth, where believers will dwell with God forever (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 22:1-5</span>)। This concluding affirmation reflects the joy and confidence of salvation, echoing the trust and hope expressed throughout the Creed।

<!-- p61 -->
## Conclusion

<!-- p62 -->
In the fundamental truths expressed in the Apostles' Creed, we see the life and work of Jesus as the center of God's plan for humanity। Each statement of the Creed encompasses the key aspects of our faith in God and who Jesus is - his divinity, humanity, mission, and ultimate victory। These truths point to his unique role as the mediator of the New Covenant। As mediator, he invites us to come before God with confidence, relying on his perfect sacrifice and ongoing intercession। This calls us to worship him as the rightful King and to follow his teachings as the supreme Prophet। Ultimately, the study of Jesus leads us into a deeper relationship with him, transforming us into people who live in the light of his grace and truth।

<!-- p63 -->
Understanding who Jesus is and what he accomplished is the foundation of the Christian faith। The many names, titles, and roles of Jesus highlight different aspects of his character and mission, giving a fuller picture of his love and authority।

<!-- p64 -->
As with the study of God, the Scriptures are filled with various names that refer to Jesus। Each name represents a specific aspect of his overall character, ministry, and role। These names are given to reveal to us an aspect of Jesus। For your personal study, a reference table is included in the appendix: *Table 12 - Names of Jesus.* Read these names and study the corresponding Scripture references।
