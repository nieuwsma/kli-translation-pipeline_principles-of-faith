# Chapter 6 - Faith Toward God

<!-- p1 -->
Faith is a cornerstone-like concept of the Christian faith. In Christianity the word faith is used so commonly that sometimes its meaning can be displaced by subtle misconceptions and misunderstandings. But it is not something you can hold in your hand—it is something formed in the heart as one trusts in God. Persuasion itself is faith.

<!-- p2 -->
The author of Hebrews instructs us to have ‘faith toward God’.

### Definition of Faith

<!-- p10 -->
Faith is one of the most misunderstood and misrepresented aspects of the Christian faith. As the saying goes, "There are ditches on both sides of the road"! In modern Christianity, the word 'faith' is so often used without proper explanation that it can take on faulty definitions amplified by misunderstandings. Faith is often portrayed either as a general statement of vague belief, or as an almost mystical power or "magic"—something you supposedly carry in a measured and limited amount.

<!-- p11 -->
#### What Faith Is Not

<!-- p12 -->
Faith that is misrepresented as abstract has no specific conviction or persuasion. This becomes clear with a little probing, because such a kind of faith cannot be precisely specified. If you ask, 'What do you believe?' or 'Why do you believe?', a person with such an understanding of faith usually cannot clarify beyond 'I believe.' <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 3:15</span> says we must be ready to give an answer for the reason for the hope within us.

<!-- p13 -->
Sometimes people think of faith as a magical power—but Scripture does not teach that. Faith is about a real relationship built on trust. In Scripture, faith is highly dynamic and relational—it is deeply rooted in persuasion, trust, and confidence in the nature and promises of God. It is not an impersonal force, but an engaged response toward God that shapes thoughts, emotions, and actions.

<!-- p14 -->
Why is faith related to persuasion?

<!-- p15 -->
Our beliefs shape how we see and interact with the world around us. Belief forms perception and energizes our actions. Our brains are wired this way—we live on the basis of what we believe is possible.

<!-- p16 -->
We act according to what we perceive, understand, and believe is possible. When we walk down a corridor, our brain recognizes the boundaries of the floor and walls and automatically tells our feet where to step and what to avoid. This truth extends far beyond physically navigating the world around us.

<!-- p17 -->
Suppose you believe it is impossible for you to be financially free or to live a healthy and active lifestyle. Then your actions will follow that belief, and you, staying true to your own belief, will likely not even want to do or attempt what you have deemed impossible.

<!-- p18 -->
If you believe that God is unloving and punitive in nature, that perception of Him will itself keep you from walking with Him as His child.

<!-- p19 -->
#### Faith Is Persuasion

<!-- p20 -->
So then, what is faith? Persuasion! The Greek word ***pistis*** means persuasion, or the result of being persuaded. We must have persuasion toward God. In English we say faith (persuasion) ‘**in** God’. If you are married, it is hoped that you have ‘**in’** trust toward your spouse. You believe that they do the things they say, and you also believe that they are real and faithful. Such trust should extend beyond the things they have said to a general trust in them.

<!-- p21 -->
In a relationship, it takes time and experience to develop faith, persuasion, and trust. Hopefully, in life you have learned how to develop a trust-filled relationship with another person. The same principles you have learned that help you grow trust in someone you know also help increase your faith toward God. In a relationship, trust grows over time, through experience, through consistency, and through small and large actions that build your confidence in the other person’s character and nature.

<!-- p22 -->
‘Faith toward God’ is relational. Relationships are built by two parties, not one. The good news about our faith in God is that God takes responsibility for His side of the relationship (made possible by Jesus’ atoning death, and by His Spirit filling us in new birth), and He also helps on our side (through the work of the Comforter—the Holy Spirit). God is doing almost all the work; our part (which we will discuss later in this chapter) is to walk *with* Him in obedience.

<!-- p23 -->
### Jesus matures our faith

<!-- p24 -->
Jesus is the author and finisher of our faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 12:2</span>). Our faith is designed to keep growing and expanding. We do not reach a single point where we say there is now enough ‘faith’; rather our faith keeps increasing. Consider a deep and personal relationship in your life today. As in any relationship, we do not reach a certain milestone and then say we now ‘know’ that person. We keep on knowing them, increasing our knowledge about them, and this refines, shapes, and develops our relationship.

<!-- p25 -->
How do we grow our faith in God? By continually experiencing Him across His many revelations, manifestations, and attributes. Again, consider a deep personal relationship in your life. You engage with them in different ways, different patterns, different mediums, in different environments, deepening the relationship. You talk with them, send messages, perhaps write. You see them personally, and also in group settings. You talk about your values, and perhaps even challenge one another. There are countless such ways. Through these many expressions you come to know that person more deeply.

<!-- p26 -->
We all must come to know God as our Savior through Jesus. Remember the names of God (*see Appendix),* knowing God as ‘Savior’ alone does not mean we have already experienced Him as ‘Redeemer’, ‘Healer’, ‘Counselor’, ‘Lord’.

<!-- p27 -->
If you are at a place where, being persuaded (having faith), you have come to know God as your Savior, as this relationship is built your trust in His character grows in such a way that it enables you to know Him in other ways as well.

<!-- p28 -->
In my life I am blessed to have some very deep relationships. One of those relationships is with my wife. In our relationship, because of the level of trust (faith) between us, my wife can tell me things she will do, which I have not yet seen tangibly, and because of her proven character I have the confidence to trust her.

<!-- p29 -->
This example shows how we can experience a deep relationship with God with trust and confidence! Perhaps we have not yet fully experienced Him (in fact, in this life we will never fully experience Him), but as our relationship matures, we can trust Him beyond the limits of what I have personally known and already seen happen.

<!-- p30 -->
Jesus grows our faith through our experiences with Him. One important way He can and should be experienced is through His Word.

<!-- p36 -->
When we substitute the word *faith* with *persuasion*, it reads like this:

<!-- p37 -->
*‘Then persuasion comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God’*. This truth is by nature self-evident. One primary way persuasion arises in someone is that they are persuaded (made convinced), and we are convinced when someone presents an ‘argument’—that is, a rational and clearly expressed explanation.

<!-- p38 -->
In the Bible there are two Greek words for ‘Word’—**logos** and **rhema**. Understanding the difference helps show how faith grows.

<!-- p39 -->
**Logos**: Generally refers to the **concept, idea, logic, or broad expression** of God’s Word. It encompasses universal, foundational truth or message and is often used to describe God’s eternal wisdom and Christ Himself as the living Word.

<!-- p40 -->
**Rhema (G4487)**: emphasizes a **specific, spoken word or utterance**. It generally refers to a specific word or message given at a particular moment, especially a word spoken or revealed in a way directly applicable to a person’s situation।

<!-- p41 -->
*Logos* is like the entire message of a book, whereas *rhema* is like a particular passage or sentence that speaks directly to someone at a moment. While the *logos* is God’s universal truth, the *rhema* is a portion of that truth spoken to an individual in a personal and applicable way. Together, these depict the fullness of God’s communication: the *logos* as the broad, foundational truths of God’s nature and will, and the *rhema* as the specific, applied words that make that truth living in a person’s life and circumstances।

<!-- p42 -->
### An example of the Rhema word in action

<!-- p43 -->
God has promised to give light and wisdom to all who ask. When He speaks to us, that utterance is a *rhema* word. Rhema words are from God and serve to confirm, lead, and guide us. They are not a substitute for the *logos* word, nor do they carry the authority of Scripture. *Rhema* words must be tested by the same standard as prophecy or interpretation of tongues (see the chapter on the Holy Spirit for a detailed study of this topic)।

<!-- p44 -->
At many stages of my career, I have prayed for divine guidance. A few years ago, I was thinking about the process of beginning to look for new opportunities, and during my prayer time, I heard God say to me, ‘*Wait for Me, I have an opportunity for you.*’ I was starting to look for a new job because I was dissatisfied with my current role and felt discouraged. The word of God’s direction, ‘*Wait for Me’*, helped give me peace and confidence in an otherwise turbulent season।

<!-- p45 -->
Three or four months passed, I kept working hard and faithfully, keeping my attitude positive. God would remind me, *‘Wait for Me’*, then His promise was fulfilled. I received an opportunity for a promotion and I received a generous pay increase।

<!-- p46 -->
I could have continued my job search; I could have quit. I could have started somewhere new. Without a doubt, someone else would have gotten that promotion! But because I sought the Lord’s counsel and trusted His **rhema word**, I remained obedient, and I benefited. God’s words are **living and personal**, and He has promised to give us wisdom in many ways and generously (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:5</span>)।

<!-- p47 -->
I have had this happen dozens, if not hundreds, of times, where I prayed and asked God what to do and He gave me a rhema word. The outcome of each was not equally amazing, but all of them brought me a sense of peace and confidence that I was obeying God and that He would work everything together. Those were the words I needed for that moment! Many times, when God gives a rhema word, it is from the logos. He brings to my remembrance a passage of Scripture, “*Do not fear, for I am with you!*” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="41" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 41:10</span>), “*By His stripes we are healed”* (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="53" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 53:5</span>), “*Do not grow weary in doing good!*” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:9</span>), and many such examples go on!

<!-- p48 -->
Receiving a rhema word not only strengthens our faith, but also deepens our relationship with God as we come to trust Him more fully in our daily decisions. We will continue our study on how to hear from God in the chapter about the Holy Spirit।

<!-- p49 -->
Our faith is also strengthened when we see the works God has done, but sometimes the things we see in the natural world challenge what we have heard from God. Reconciling the discrepancy between what we have seen and what we have heard is an important ministry of the Holy Spirit, who has promised to lead us into all truth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span>)।

### A faith greater than what we can see

<!-- p51 -->
After His resurrection, Jesus appeared many times to many of His disciples. Thomas (one of the Twelve) was not with the other disciples, so he missed the vision of the risen Savior. Thomas believed in Jesus, and he also trusted his fellow disciples, but he did not believe that Jesus had risen physically; he insisted that he could not actively believe it until he himself personally experienced it।

<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="24-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

When Jesus appeared to His disciples and calmed them (I imagine it is quite startling for someone to appear in your midst when the door is locked), He spoke to Thomas. He did not criticize or blame him, nor did He accuse him of being part of an evil and perverse generation that seeks a sign (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 16:4</span>) but revealed Himself to Thomas।

<!-- p60 -->
Jesus goes on to say, ‘*Blessed are those who have not seen and yet have believed*’. This is not encouraging a ‘blind or ignorant faith’ or rejecting the use of empirical evidence. Rather, He is saying that when we can believe without the need for empirical evidence, then we are more blessed. Why? If our faith, our trust, were limited only to the empirically demonstrated things we had seen, we would never be able to have confidence about future works. We would have great confidence in what has already happened, but we would not walk in God’s peace for our tomorrows and our future।

<!-- p61 -->
Using a relational example, how challenging would my relationship with my wife be if my attitude were always like ‘<u>Only after I see it will I believe it!</u>’? Instead, a trust-based relationship says ‘I know you are a dependable person’।

3.  The act of our believing is manifested in obedience.

<!-- p91 -->
The word for ‘obey’ is **hupakouō** (**hoop-ak-oo'-o**- G5219): **hupakouō** means *to hear under* (as *a subordinate*), that is, *to listen attentively*; by implication, *to heed* or *to conform* to a command or authority: - to listen attentively, be obedient, obey.

<!-- p92 -->
In a work environment, when a boss or supervisor is giving instructions, they are not “speaking just to speak”; rather, they are giving instructions they want their employees to follow.

<!-- p93 -->
When we truly listen to God, it should lead us to action—to trust in Him and obey. The Scriptures say it this way:

<!-- p94 -->

The counselor will teach them the principles of financial freedom and help to make a budget. This counseling will give them (confidence / faith) about what they must do to become debt-free... But confidence is only the first step; to become debt-free and receive good results and blessing, they must follow the principles they have learned through disciplined practice.

It is better for us to follow what we are to do correctly than to end up in a situation where a sacrifice is needed to correct what we did wrong. Fortunately, when we stray from the path, miss the mark (sin), and need to turn back to God, there is grace from God.

<!-- p108 -->
As we build our faith in God, it naturally produces obedience in us. This obedience is for our good and it yields a good harvest. Returning to my personal example from this chapter, if I had not obeyed the word God had given me—wait for Him—I would not have been able to experience the blessing of peace and promotion in that season!

### Practical steps to grow your faith

<!-- p153 -->
Walking with God is relational, but if you want a good relationship, you must learn some skills. In human relationships, the skills that produce strong and secure relationships are based on:

<!-- p154 -->
- Open and unhindered dialogue and communication

<!-- p155 -->
- Healthy conflict resolution

<!-- p156 -->
- Commitment

<!-- p157 -->
- Consistency

<!-- p158 -->
If you want a strong, vibrant relationship, you must show up consistently, reveal yourself (communicate) — our thoughts and feelings —, receive the other person (listen), and walk together with them! All these skills also apply to our relationship with God.

<!-- p159 -->
Beyond basic relational skills, there are specific practices and disciplines that strengthen your relationship with God:

<!-- p160 -->
- **Build and maintain a life of prayer; listen to and communicate with God.** Regular and heartfelt prayer is essential for a close relationship with God. Through prayer, sharing your concerns, joys, and gratitude, you can seek His guidance and strength. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 4:6-7</span>*) Being sensitive to the voice of the Holy Spirit helps to discern God’s will, gain wisdom in decisions, and grow in spiritual maturity. Make time for quiet reflection to listen to His leading. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:14</span>*)

<!-- p161 -->
- **Spend time in worship.** Worship is a powerful way to draw near to God by expressing love and reverence. Through praise, song, or quiet contemplation, worship deepens the awareness of His presence. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="100" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalms 100:2-4</span>*)

<!-- p162 -->
- **Read, study, and meditate on Scripture.** God’s Word reveals His character, promises, and directions for life. Dedicating time to the study of Scripture helps you grow in faith, understanding, and spiritual resilience. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>*)

<!-- p163 -->
- **Obey His commandments.** Obeying God’s instructions shows your love, reverence, and trust toward Him. Walking in obedience aligns your life with His purpose and brings blessings. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:15</span>*)

<!-- p164 -->
- **Be actively involved in and serve in a local church.** Engaging in the church community provides spiritual support, encouragement, and opportunities to serve others. Getting involved helps build a sense of belonging and strengthens your commitment to God’s mission. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:24-25</span>*)

<!-- p165 -->
- **Build relationships with other believers.** Fellowship with other Christians provides accountability, encouragement, and shared wisdom. These relationships enrich your faith-journey and help you grow through mutual support. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="27" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 27:17</span>*)

<!-- p166 -->
Our ‘faith toward God’ is not a one-time event for salvation, but a continuous practice and relationship that we must cultivate throughout life. The benefits of a trusting and beneficial relationship are truly countless, but to see it flourish we must do the good works that foster that trust and growth.
