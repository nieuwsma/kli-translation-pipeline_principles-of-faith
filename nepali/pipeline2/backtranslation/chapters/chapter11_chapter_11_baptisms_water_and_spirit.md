# Chapter 11 - Baptisms—Water and Spirit

<!-- p1 -->
In the modern era, apart from its Jewish roots, baptism is often thought of only as water baptism and the baptism of the Holy Spirit (Pentecost).

<!-- p2 -->
The book of Hebrews was written to a Jewish audience. The vast majority of Christians in our modern era do not have Jewish roots or heritage. Without Jewish roots and culture, many of us lack the cultural context that first-century Messianic Jews (Jewish believers who accepted Jesus) shared. Because we were not raised in Jewish tradition, we generally do not know much about the feasts, fasts, prayers, washings, rites, and rituals that would have been known to Jewish believers.

<!-- p3 -->
In Greek, the word for baptism, or washing, is: **Baptismos** (bap-tis-mos’ - **G909**) - the act of dipping or washing. It refers to religious washing or ceremonial purification. It can denote immersions, purification rites, or washings practiced in Jewish and Christian contexts.

<!-- p4 -->
Baptisms are one of the foundational teachings mentioned in Hebrews:

<!-- p5 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p6 -->
> **<u>Hebrews 6:1-2</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Therefore, leaving aside the discussion of the elementary doctrines of Christ, let us go on to perfection, and let us not lay again the foundation of repentance from dead works and of faith toward God,
>
> the teaching of baptisms, of laying on of hands, of the resurrection of the dead, and of eternal judgment as well.
>

</span>

<!-- p8 -->
## Washings in the Old Covenant - Historical Context

<!-- p9 -->
In the Old Covenant, ritual washings played a central role, symbolically displaying holiness before God and maintaining ceremonial purity. These washings were commanded in the Mosaic Law and applied to the priests who served in the tabernacle and to the people of Israel. For example, before entering the tabernacle or approaching the altar, the priests had to wash their hands and feet at the bronze basin, which symbolized being prepared to serve in the presence of the holy God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="30" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 30:18-21</span>). Similarly, the procedures for cleansing a person from impurities such as having touched a dead body or from skin diseases also required washing with water, which signified restoration to purity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LEV" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Leviticus 14:8</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="NUM" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Numbers 19:17-19</span>). These acts outwardly displayed the desire for inner holiness and emphasized the importance of living a life set apart for God.

<!-- p10 -->
The ritual washings of the Old Covenant had to be repeated regularly to maintain ceremonial purity. Because they were not a permanent solution, these rites provided only temporary external purity, but could not address the deeper problem of sin. The author of Hebrews points out this limitation, saying that these practices were “symbolic for the present time” and unable to purify the worshiper’s conscience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:9-10</span>). These washings were a continual reminder of human imperfection and a sign of the need for something greater—ultimate purification—that could cleanse not only the body, but also the heart and spirit.

<!-- p11 -->
The ritual washings of the Old Covenant are fulfilled in Christ, who by His own sacrifice provides complete and eternal cleansing. Unlike the temporary rites of the Law, the blood of Christ cleanses completely—not only the flesh, but even the conscience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:13-14</span>). Through His death and resurrection, Jesus accomplished what the rites of the Old Covenant only foreshadowed—true reconciliation with God. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:22</span> calls believers to “draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, having our hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience and our bodies washed with pure water.” This language connects Christ’s work to the imagery of the Old Covenant washings and shows its surpassing effectiveness. His sacrifice does not merely cover sin, but removes it completely (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 1:7</span>). Thus, the ritual washings were only shadows, pointing toward the deeper cleansing that only Christ could give—the fulfillment of humanity’s need for holiness through divine intervention.

<!-- p12 -->
To emphasize this again, Christians are not obligated to perform any ritual washings. Washings did not grant salvation in the Old Covenant, nor do they grant salvation in the New. A ritual washing neither improves our standing with God nor earns His favor. Paul strongly rebukes the Judaizers in Galatia (read Galatians), who tried to add Jewish requirements (such as circumcision) to salvation. Salvation is only by faith in Jesus!

<!-- p13 -->
## John’s Baptism

<!-- p14 -->
Multiple baptisms are mentioned in the New Testament canon. We have already discussed the baptisms and washings of Jewish tradition.

<!-- p15 -->
The focus of John the Baptist’s ministry was preparing for the coming of Jesus Christ. His baptism, described as a baptism of repentance for the forgiveness of sins (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 1:4</span>), called people to acknowledge their sins, turn their hearts toward God, and prepare for the coming of the Messiah. It was an outward sign of inward repentance, expressing a desire for spiritual cleansing and readiness for the kingdom of God. But John’s baptism was temporary and preparatory—it pointed toward the greater work fulfilled by Christ. Although John’s baptism focused on repentance, because it lacked the indwelling and power of the Holy Spirit it provided neither full forgiveness nor spiritual rebirth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:11-12</span>). Rather, John openly declared that someone greater than he was coming, who would baptize not only with water but also with the Holy Spirit and fire (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 3:16</span>).

<!-- p16 -->
John’s baptism was fulfilled and superseded by the death, burial, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. The Christian baptism that Jesus instituted and the apostles practiced signifies a deeper reality—union with Christ, dying to sin, and rising to new life (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 6:3-4</span>). Unlike John’s baptism, Christian baptism is directly connected to faith in Jesus’ atoning sacrifice and the indwelling of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:38</span>). The coming of the Holy Spirit at Pentecost marked the beginning of the New Covenant and the start of a Spirit-empowered life, which John’s baptism could not provide. For this reason, John’s baptism is no longer practiced, because it has been replaced by the baptism commanded by Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19</span}), which includes repentance, faith, and the sealing of believers by the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>).

<!-- p17 -->
## Christ’s Baptism

<!-- p18 -->
Christ’s baptism established a powerful example for His followers and became the basis for the Christian practice of water baptism. Though Jesus was sinless, He was baptized by John “to fulfill all righteousness” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:15</span>). In doing so, He identified Himself with humanity’s need for repentance and pointed forward to His death, burial, and resurrection. At His baptism, the Holy Spirit descended like a dove, and the Father declared, “This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:16-17</span>). This moment marked the beginning of Jesus’ public ministry and revealed the unity of the Father, the Son, and the Spirit, foreshadowing the spiritual transformation believers would experience through faith in Christ.

<!-- p19 -->
Jesus later commanded His followers to baptize, commissioning them this way, “Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19</span>). The water baptism instituted by Christ became for believers an **act of obedience** and a **public confession of faith**. It signifies their identification with Jesus’ death, burial, and resurrection (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 6:3-4</span>). Going under the water signifies dying to sin, and coming up out of the water is a picture of being raised to new life in Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:12</span>). However, it is essential to make clear that this outward act is not the source of salvation; rather, it is the **visible sign** of the **inward work of grace** already accomplished by the blood of Jesus.

<!-- p20 -->
Water baptism is a **symbol** of the cleansing power of Christ’s blood and the renewal of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:26</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>). It signifies repentance, faith, and the commitment to follow Jesus. It is an outward witness of inward transformation - a way for believers to publicly declare their allegiance to Christ and their participation in the body of Christ, the Church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:41</span>). Although it is commanded by Jesus and modeled in the early church, it **does not save** anyone. Salvation is by grace through faith alone (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>), and true cleansing is spiritual through the blood of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 1:5</span>). Water baptism is a response to that salvation, not a necessary condition for it, but an act of obedience.

<!-- p21 -->
In the New Testament, water baptism is consistently associated with **repentance and faith** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:38</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 16:16</span>), both of which require a personal decision to follow Christ - something infants cannot do. Baptism symbolizes not only the believer’s deliberate identification with Christ’s death, burial, and resurrection (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 6:3-4</span>), but also a commitment to live in obedience to Him. Some denominations (for example, Catholic, Lutheran) practice infant baptism as a sign of the covenant, akin to circumcision; but since infants cannot express personal faith or repentance, the practice of **infant baptism** does not align with the biblical pattern of **believer’s baptism**. Instead, many churches hold **dedication ceremonies** for infants, following the example of Hannah dedicating Samuel to the Lord (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1SA" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Samuel 1:27-28</span>) and of Mary and Joseph presenting Jesus at the temple (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 2:22</span>). Dedication is not a substitute for baptism; rather, it is a commitment by parents to raise their child in the faith, teach the Word of God, and model a devout life until the child is able to make his or her own decision to follow Christ and be baptized.

<!-- p22 -->
Like the **ceremonial washings** of the Old Covenant, which required repeated rituals for purification, Christian baptism is a one-time event that signifies the completed work of grace. The washings of the Old Testament symbolized external cleansing and foreshadowed the deeper spiritual cleansing that would come from Christ. In contrast, New Testament baptism symbolizes the inner renewal and regeneration accomplished by the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>). It points to the believer’s participation in the New Covenant, where cleansing from sin is permanent and based on Christ’s finished work.

<!-- p23 -->
Water baptism is closely related to the **promise of the Holy Spirit**. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:38</span>, Peter calls people to repent, be baptized, and receive the gift of the Holy Spirit. Water baptism publicly signifies the believer’s repentance and faith, but the true baptism happens within, when the Holy Spirit washes, regenerates, and seals the believer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:13</span>). This spiritual washing makes us members of the body of Christ and empowers us to live a holy life. The **role of the Holy Spirit** in baptism reminds us that salvation is accomplished not by rituals, but by God’s transforming power.

<!-- p24 -->
It is important to distinguish between the **symbolic act of water baptism** and the spiritual reality of salvation through Christ’s blood. The Bible clearly teaches that salvation is based on faith in Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 10:9-10</span>) and not on works, including baptism (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>). Even so, water baptism is an **act of obedience** that publicly identifies the believer with Christ and the Church. It shows an outward commitment to live as a disciple of Jesus, but it adds nothing to, nor completes, the work of salvation.

<!-- p25 -->
Christ’s water baptism established an example believers should follow, demonstrating humility, obedience, and readiness for ministry. For Christians today, water baptism is a public declaration of faith, symbolizing death to sin and resurrection to new life. Though it is a command of Jesus and an important step of obedience, it is not necessary for salvation. True cleansing comes through faith in the blood of Jesus, and the empowerment for the Christian life comes from the work of the Holy Spirit within the believer. Baptism unites us with Christ and His Church, and reminds us of the inner transformation and renewal that God has already accomplished.

<!-- p26 -->
Having established water baptism as the outward sign of faith, we now turn to the baptism of the Holy Spirit, which empowers believers for ministry and service.

<!-- p27 -->
## Baptism of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p28 -->
Baptism in the Holy Spirit is a profound and transformative experience that God has promised to empower believers for ministry, service, and holy living. Every believer receives the Holy Spirit at new birth - this is how we are made spiritually alive. But afterward, we also need to be baptized in the Holy Spirit, which empowers us with courage, spiritual gifts, and effective ministry.

<!-- p29 -->
Water baptism is a public declaration of faith and symbolizes the cleansing of sin by the blood of Christ, while Spirit baptism represents God’s empowering presence in the believer’s life. Together, these two kinds of baptism form complementary aspects of the Christian journey - one as an outward sign of obedience and the other as an inward source of power and renewal. The baptism of the Holy Spirit is the Spirit coming upon us, not within. At salvation all believers receive the Spirit within themselves, but when God’s Spirit comes upon them, they receive power and courage for ministry.

<!-- p30 -->
The prophecy of baptism in the Holy Spirit was made by John the Baptist, who said,

<!-- p31 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p32 -->
> **<u>Matthew 3:11</u>** (NKJV)
>
> I indeed baptize you with water unto repentance; but He who is coming after me is mightier than I, whose sandals I am not worthy to carry. He will baptize you with the Holy Spirit and fire.
>

<!-- p33 -->
</span>

<!-- p34 -->
This promise pointed to a great work Jesus accomplished after His resurrection - sending the Holy Spirit to indwell His followers and empower them. Jesus confirmed this when He told His disciples to wait for the gift of the Spirit, making it clear that they would receive power to be His witnesses (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>). The fulfillment of this promise came on the day of Pentecost, when the disciples were filled with the Holy Spirit, and its evidence was seen in speaking in tongues, prophesying, and bold proclamation of the gospel (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:1-4</span>).

<!-- p35 -->
This outpouring marked the beginning of the **Church Age** and showed that the power of the Spirit is now available to all believers. This promise was not limited to the apostles, but extends to all who believe, as Peter declared, *“The promise is for you and your children, and for all who are far off - for all whom the Lord our God will call.”* (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="39" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:39</span>).

<!-- p36 -->
### The Spirit within vs. the Spirit coming upon

<!-- p37 -->
When someone places their faith in Jesus, the Holy Spirit gives new life to their inner being. This is what it means to be born again (**regenerated**). At that very moment of faith, the Holy Spirit dwells **within** the believer, making them spiritually alive and adopting them into the family of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:5-6</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>). This is the new birth, or being born again, as Jesus described in John 3.

<!-- p38 -->
Biblical basis:

<!-- p39 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>* - “He saved us through the washing of regeneration and renewal by the Holy Spirit.”

<!-- p40 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>* - The Holy Spirit seals believers as the **guarantee** of their inheritance.

<!-- p41 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:9</span>* - “Anyone who does not have the Spirit of Christ does not belong to Him.”

<!-- p42 -->
This regenerating work is universal for all believers at the time of salvation and establishes the Spirit’s permanent indwelling. It does not depend on any external act such as water baptism, and it secures salvation by grace through faith.

<!-- p43 -->
In contrast, baptism in the Holy Spirit is a separate and subsequent experience that equips believers with power for service and witness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>). While regeneration relates to new life and identity, baptism in the Spirit focuses on empowerment **upon** and the release of spiritual gifts. This baptism is often accompanied by signs and manifestations of the gifts of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p44 -->
Scriptural basis:

<!-- p45 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p46 -->
> **<u>Acts 1:8</u>** (NKJV)
>
> But you shall receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you; and you shall be witnesses to Me in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the end of the earth."
>

<!-- p47 -->
</span>

<!-- p48 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:15-17</span>* - The Samaritans had already believed and had been baptized, but later received the Holy Spirit through prayer and the laying on of hands.

<!-- p49 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 19:6</span>* - The disciples in Ephesus, already believers, received the Holy Spirit when Paul laid hands on them.

<!-- p50 -->
Baptism in the Holy Spirit does not replace regeneration, but builds on it, equipping the believer for spiritual service and boldness in ministry. Baptism in the Holy Spirit is a free gift given to those who are saved, not a mandatory condition for obtaining salvation. There is no requirement that someone prove their ‘baptism’ in the Holy Spirit only by displaying a <u>specific</u> gift of the Spirit; rather, we should expect the baptism in the Holy Spirit to be manifested today, just as in the book of Acts, with signs and boldness! Some go wrong in saying that if someone does not speak in tongues, he is not saved, or he does not have the baptism of the Holy Spirit. Not all receive every gift or manifestation of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29-31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:29-31</span>).

<!-- p51 -->
## How to receive the baptism of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p52 -->
Scripture describes the baptism of the Holy Spirit as a gift promised to believers and received by faith. It is distinct from salvation and is primarily for empowerment and witness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>). The baptism of the Holy Spirit is a gift of empowerment for believers, promised by Jesus and fulfilled at Pentecost. It is received through repentance, faith, and asking God. Though the laying on of hands is often used, ultimately it is God who baptizes in the Spirit.

<!-- p53 -->
Scripture presents speaking in tongues and prophecy as common signs, but the purpose of this baptism is much greater—to provide boldness, power, and spiritual gifts for service. Every believer is encouraged to seek this experience as part of their spiritual journey, trusting that God will equip them for His work.

<!-- p54 -->
The Bible presents a few basic principles on how this baptism is received:

<!-- p55 -->
### **1. The baptism of the Holy Spirit is God's free gift**

<!-- p56 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit is not something believers can earn—it is a free gift God gives. God desires to give good gifts to His children and is ready to give the Holy Spirit to those who ask with faith and a desire for empowerment.

<!-- p57 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p58 -->
> **<u>Luke 11:13</u>** (NKJV)
>
> If you then, being evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to those who ask Him!"
>

<!-- p59 -->
</span>

<!-- p60 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="38-39" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p61 -->
> **<u>Acts 2:38-39</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Then Peter said to them, "Repent, and let every one of you be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of sins; and you shall receive the gift of the Holy Spirit.
>

###  **2. Ask, Seek, and Receive by Faith**

<!-- p64 -->
Scripture emphasizes that baptism in the Holy Spirit is received by asking in prayer and with faith-filled expectation. The faith joined with a willingness to ask and to receive is the key to experiencing the baptism of the Spirit. If you’re not sure whether God wants to give you the baptism in the Holy Spirit, ask Him; He is a good Father! As He prompts you, you may sense a tangible presence of God in your own body that you have never experienced before. If not, that’s okay! God is great, and He does not need to be confined within any one perfect formula. If you feel that God is drawing your heart, surrender to Him!

<!-- p65 -->
- **<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:15-17</span>** - Peter and John prayed and **laid hands** on the believers, and they **received the Holy Spirit**.

## Gifts of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p79 -->
When the Holy Spirit was poured out, and people received baptism in the Holy Spirit, manifestations of the Holy Spirit often appeared along with it.

<!-- p80 -->
There are several lists of gifts in the New Testament:

<!-- p81 -->
- Motivational gifts given by God: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:6-8</span>

<!-- p82 -->
- Discussed in the chapter on the Church.

<!-- p83 -->
- The fivefold ministry gifts given by Jesus: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-13</span>

<!-- p84 -->
- Discussed in the chapter on the Church.

<!-- p85 -->
- The manifestation gifts given by the Holy Spirit: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span>

<!-- p86 -->
- Discussed here.

<!-- p87 -->
Each of these groups of gifts is given for different purposes and functions in different ways, but together they point to the **diversity** and **unity** of the gifts within the **body of Christ**. Each gift plays a unique role in fulfilling God's purposes.

<!-- p88 -->
### Purpose & Operation of the Gifts

<!-- p89 -->
The primary purpose of the gifts of the Holy Spirit is to build up the body of Christ and to glorify God:

<!-- p90 -->
- **Edification** - strengthening and encouraging believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
- **Unity** - promoting harmony and cooperation within the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:12-13</span>).

<!-- p92 -->
- **Evangelism** - demonstrating God's power as a witness to unbelievers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="43" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:43</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:6-7</span>).

<!-- p93 -->
- **Service** - meeting others' needs through works of mercy and generosity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:6-8</span>).

<!-- p94 -->
These gifts are not for personal glory, but for the common good (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:7</span>). When they are used in love and humility, reflecting the character of Christ, they work best (1 Corinthians 13).

<!-- p95 -->
Paul emphasized that spiritual gifts must be used decently and in order (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:40</span>). This ensures that the use of gifts promotes unity, peace, and understanding rather than confusion or division.

<!-- p96 -->
- Prophecies must be tested (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:29</span>).

<!-- p97 -->
- When delivering a message in tongues to the church, one should pray that it may be interpreted (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:27-28</span>).

<!-- p98 -->
- Gifts should always bring **edification** to the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:12</span>).

<!-- p99 -->
This order does not quench the Spirit, but ensures that the gifts operate in a way that **honors God** and **benefits others**.

<!-- p100 -->
The exercise of spiritual gifts must always be based on love. Paul devotes an entire chapter (1 Corinthians 13) in which he explains that without love, even the greatest spiritual gifts are meaningless. Love provides the motivation and framework to use the gifts in a way that lifts up, not tears down, others.

<!-- p101 -->
Furthermore, the gifts operate **as the Spirit wills** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:11</span>). They cannot be produced or controlled by human effort-they are sovereignly given and empowered by the Holy Spirit, often in moments of need or service. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 13:8</span> says that ultimately prophecy and tongues will cease. Theologically, those who believe the gifts of the Holy Spirit have ceased are known as ‘Cessationists’. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 13:10</span> says “when the prefect [Jesus] comes, that wish is in part will be cause to cease”. Jesus has not yet returned for His church, therefore the gifts are still in operation on the earth.

<!-- p102 -->
It is extremely important to recognize that the **gifts of the Spirit** should not be separated from the **fruit of the Spirit** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-23</span>). Gifts display power, while fruit reveals character—love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control.

<!-- p103 -->
- **Gifts without fruit**: A believer may operate in gifts but lack maturity or character, leading to pride, abuse, or harm.

<!-- p104 -->
- **Fruit without gifts**: A believer may have a godly character but lack empowerment to serve effectively.

<!-- p105 -->
Both are necessary. Gifts equip believers for service. Fruit is a sign of their spiritual health (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 7:16</span>) and provides a ‘sweet taste’ to those who partake of it.

<!-- p106 -->
### Explanation of the 9 revelation-related gifts of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p107 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span> lists the 9 revelation-related gifts of the Holy Spirit:

<!-- p108 -->
1.  **Word of wisdom** - Supernatural insight into God’s purposes or the direction of a situation. It applies divine wisdom to complex problems or decisions (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:10</span>).

<!-- p109 -->
2.  **Word of knowledge** - Supernatural disclosure of information about a person, event, or circumstance that could not be known naturally (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 4:17-18</span>).

<!-- p110 -->
3.  **Faith** - A supernatural ability to trust God miraculously even in seemingly impossible situations. This gift inspires courage and confidence (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 3:6-7</span>).

<!-- p111 -->
4.  **Gifts of healings** - Supernatural power to cure physical, emotional, or spiritual ailments and restore wholeness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 3:6-8</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 9:17-18</span>).

<!-- p112 -->
5.  **Working of miracles** - The ability to perform supernatural acts that defy natural laws, such as multiplying food or raising the dead (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40-41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 9:40-41</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 2:9-11</span>).

<!-- p113 -->
6.  **Prophecy** - Declaring God’s truth or will under the inspiration of the Holy Spirit. It should encourage, edify, and comfort (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>).

<!-- p114 -->
7.  **Discerning of spirits (discernment)** - The ability to distinguish whether a spiritual influence is from God, from the enemy, or from a human source (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 16:16-18</span>).

<!-- p115 -->
8.  **Diverse kinds of tongues** - Speaking languages unknown to the speaker, often for worship, prayer, or as a sign to unbelievers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:4</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:22</span>).

<!-- p116 -->
9.  **Interpretation of tongues** - The supernatural ability to interpret a message spoken in tongues for the building up of the assembly (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:27</span>).

<!-- p117 -->
Paul makes it clear that all the gifts are valuable and should work together for the common good of the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:7</span>). However, he also encourages believers to desire the greater gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:31</span>). The greater gifts are often considered those that directly edify the church, such as prophecy (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:1-5</span>).

<!-- p118 -->
- Prophecy is emphasized in particular because it strengthens, encourages, and comforts others (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>). Paul instructs believers to earnestly desire this gift because it increases clarity and understanding in the church.

<!-- p119 -->
- Gifts like tongues are extremely valuable for personal edification and prayer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:4</span>), but Paul teaches to pray for interpretation when giving a message to the church so that they may edify the congregation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:27-28</span>).

<!-- p120 -->
The Holy Spirit gives gifts, but to use them we must submit and surrender to the Holy Spirit. Like muscles and talents, as we practice yielding to the Holy Spirit to use His gifts, we become more confident and reliable in their use.
