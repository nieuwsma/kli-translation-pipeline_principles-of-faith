# Hearing God's Voice

<!-- p1 -->
The guest appendix below by Rev. Larry Low ([<u>www.LarryLow.com</u>](http://www.larrylow.com)) complements our exploration of the role of the Holy Spirit, presenting practical insights about hearing God’s voice. The guest contribution below is used with permission.

<!-- p2 -->
One morning during prayer, I asked **God** this question: “For the times that are coming to us, what is the most important thing I can teach Your people?” And He answered me very quickly, and when He spoke I felt it was very urgent: “**Teach them to hear My voice.**”

<!-- p3 -->
When the Lord gives this kind of direction, I always pick up pad and pencil and begin to write down what I know, or think I know, and the Scripture verses that support them. I like to study the Word on the subjects the Lord has led me to teach. I enjoy the anointing to preach, but to be honest I enjoy teaching the Word even more. It is an exciting journey to find things that have always been there yet have been overlooked.

<!-- p4 -->
David was certainly eager to discover the truths of God, for he prayed this way in Psalm 119 verse 18: “Open my eyes so that I may see wondrous things from Your law.”

<!-- p5 -->
This is what I want to see—wondrous things from His Word.

<!-- p6 -->
While I was studying and preparing to teach the second message on hearing and discerning the voice of God, He spoke to my heart like this:

<!-- p7 -->
“*Teach them to hear My voice,  
teach them to reverence and obey.*"

<!-- p8 -->
"*Yes, teach them to hear My voice and I will always lead them away:  
away from the troubles that could destroy them,*"

<!-- p9 -->
"*away from the trials they are now in,  
toward a place of peace, love, and joy where only a few have ever gone.*"

<!-- p10 -->
"*toward a place of refreshing,  
toward a place of divine ecstasy.*"

<!-- p11 -->
"*Oh yes, son, teach them to hear My voice  
and these people will be free forever*.”

<!-- p12 -->
05/27/00 9:10 A.M.

<!-- p13 -->
The eleven points below will help you understand how the Lord will speak to you:

<!-- p14 -->
1.  <u>Hearing God’s voice is not an audible sound, but an understanding in your heart.</u>

<!-- p15 -->
Once His word came so clearly and firmly that it might even have been audible. I answered Him out loud, because it felt so real. But there was no one there besides the Lord and me, so I cannot say for sure whether He actually spoke to me audibly, as we would speak to one another.

<!-- p16 -->
When Jesus was being baptized in the Jordan River, the Father said, “This is My Son, in whom I am well pleased,” but some who were there heard it as thunder; so He may have spoken to me audibly as well. But for now, please understand that when I say God spoke to me, it means more an understanding in my heart than a voice that came to my ears.

<!-- p17 -->
2.  <u>This understanding is not formed by the reasoning of your mind; yet it is a complete thought or perception.</u>

<!-- p18 -->
The Lord has never said anything to me that would confuse me. He has never said anything that would keep me thinking ‘why’. It is always an understanding with full comprehension. God is not the author of confusion (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:33</span>).

<!-- p19 -->
3.  <u>When He speaks, He speaks in complete thoughts.</u>

<!-- p20 -->
He never leaves us to fill in the blanks ourselves. In the blink of an eye, God can fill your heart with enough information to go on preaching for a lifetime. One touch of God will fill you with such a fire that it will help you keep blazing for eternity. He is a perfect communicator.

<!-- p21 -->
4.  <u>When God addresses us, He never drives. He leads.</u>

<!-- p22 -->
He is not a harsh taskmaster; He is a shepherd. Herdsmen drive, but a shepherd leads. Jesus is the great Shepherd of the sheep, and we are the sheep of His pasture.

<!-- p23 -->
5.  <u>When He speaks to His children, He never condemns; He assures.</u>

<!-- p24 -->
His children are those who have been born again by the confession of their faith in the shed blood of **Jesus** and in the work He accomplished at Calvary (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:3</span>, 5). Who wants to listen to someone who always points out our faults and condemns us? I believe most of **God’s** children are afraid that **God** will speak to them, because they have been told that He will condemn them for this or that. Let’s look at <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 3:19-20</span>. Who is said there to condemn or punish us? It is our soul or heart that condemns the children of **God**, but there is **God the Holy Spirit**, who knows all things and is greater than our hearts. He is there to be our Comforter. Read the Scriptures below, and this will help you understand that the Holy Spirit convicts only sinners. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33-34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:33-34</span> teaches us that nothing can come between us and **God’s** love. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:16</span>, 26 teaches us that the Holy Spirit is our Comforter, not a ‘condemner’; our Counselor, not a scolder; our Intercessor and Helper, not a hinderer. Also read <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 9:31</span> and 15:28 as examples of how the Holy Spirit (the voice of **God**) gives us help, comfort, and strength.

<!-- p25 -->
6.  <u>The voice of God can be tested so that you can be sure it is God, not your thoughts or the voice of familiar spirits.</u>

<!-- p26 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 4:1-3</span> directs us to “test every spirit,” so that we can be assured that it is from God. When you think you are hearing from God, you have both the right and the responsibility to question that voice (that is, to test or verify that spirit). The question to ask is: “Has Jesus Christ come in the flesh?” This is the very thing familiar spirits will never acknowledge—that Jesus came to earth in the flesh. He was fully man and fully God. No false spirit or religion believes that Jesus came to this earth in the flesh and delivered humankind forever from all their sins. Test the voice you have heard. You must also know that God will never lead you outside His written Word, the Bible. If you are hearing something that the Bible cannot support, you are not hearing from God. Cast it aside! Test the voice you heard to see whether it is from God or not.

<!-- p27 -->
7.  <u>The voice of God always glorifies Jesus Christ.</u>

<!-- p28 -->
The Holy Spirit is the voice of heaven. He is here on this earth to work with us, and Jesus said that He will always speak only what He hears, and He will always glorify Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13-14</span>). He has not come here to glorify your ministry, your works, or church programs. All those things are good, and many of them are necessary for the work we do, but the Holy Spirit speaks and glorifies the person of Jesus. If the voice you hear is glorifying you, you are not hearing from God. There is a difference between receiving glory and being encouraged and strengthened. Every time the Holy Spirit speaks to you—even when He is correcting (guiding) you through the Word—He will always encourage and strengthen you. But glory is when you believe that you yourself are someone special. Without Jesus you are nobody—He is the One who is special, not us.

<!-- p29 -->
8.  <u>The voice of God always reveals something to us.</u>

<!-- p30 -->
Let’s look at <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:14</span>. He always takes what belongs to **Jesus** and reveals it to us. Considering the word “reveal,” the Amplified adds these words: declare, disclose, and transmit. After **God** has declared something to us, He will always disclose it to us, and then, through your faith, He will transmit it to us. Study <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 2:10</span> and 12 as well.

<!-- p31 -->
9.  <u>The voice of God always brings peace.</u>

<!-- p32 -->
Read <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:26</span> and 27, and <span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 3:14</span> and 15. His voice never unsettles us. It is a voice that brings perfect peace. Inner peace comes because there is a “knowing” that makes you confident before God, which turns into perfect peace. If the voice you are hearing is unsettling you, you are not hearing from God—turn it off. Jesus promised us peace, not as the world gives, but the kind of peace He gives through the comforting words of the Holy Spirit and His continual indwelling (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:27</span>).

<!-- p33 -->
10. <u>The voice of God always brings truth.</u>

<!-- p34 -->
Read <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:17</span>, 26; 16:13. In fact, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span> calls the Holy Spirit the “Spirit of Truth.” God cannot lie! He is truth, and therefore He speaks only truth. People sometimes say: “I can’t believe what I heard is from God. It’s so good it seems too good to be true.” And possibly it is so good that it may not seem true; therefore you must test that spirit against the Word of God to make sure whether it is true or not. Some people simply cannot believe, because what they hear is always positive, and they have been taught that God will condemn them for faults. God speaks only truth, and it is always good news. The sad truth is that most people always hear only bad news from the voice they hear and do not expect to hear good news, so when good news comes, they suspect it as possibly untrue. **The truth is always good news!** It is the **truth** that makes us free, so how could the **truth** be anything but good news?

<!-- p35 -->
11. <u>The voice of **God** always leads us forward and upward.</u>

<!-- p36 -->
Deut. 28:1-2, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="30" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 30:21</span>, and <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span> read

<!-- p37 -->
God will never take you down! He will never belittle you; He only builds you up/enlightens you. When God speaks to you, He speaks from the standpoint that everything the Word has said about you has already been accomplished and you have already received it. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="33" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 33:3</span> says: “Call to Me, and I will answer you, and show you great and mighty things which you do not know.” Does that sound like He is belittling you or like destruction and darkness? There is no destruction and darkness in God, therefore He cannot say such destructive or depressing things. For destruction and darkness you must tune in to the world’s news agencies.

<!-- p38 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="29" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p39 -->
> **<u>Jeremiah 29:11</u>** (NKJV)
>
> For I know the thoughts that I think toward you, says the Lord, thoughts of peace and not of evil, to give you a future and a hope.

<!-- p40 -->
</span>

<!-- p41 -->
Now, if God has good thoughts of peace, a future, and hope toward us, why would He speak differently? The answer is, He does not speak like that, and He will not! He only leads us upward and forward with great hope, so that He can provide us the expected future.

<!-- p42 -->
I hope this encourages you to “Call upon Him” and to learn **how to hear His voice**. He has great things to tell you, but before all His good things can be useful to you, He needs your listening ears.
