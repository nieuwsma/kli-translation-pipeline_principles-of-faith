# Chapter 10 - Sanctified - Ready for God's Purposes

<!-- p1 -->
In the previous two chapters, we discussed some important topics. We explored the role and ministry of the Holy Spirit, as well as the role and ministry of the Church.

<!-- p2 -->
Just as we know we have a role in the Church and that we need training, purification is also necessary. To be truly ready for God's purposes, we must be sanctified.

<!-- p3 -->
The definition of **sanctification** is to purify something for a special purpose. It is closely related to the word **consecration**, which means to set something apart for a special purpose.

<!-- p4 -->
Imagine a master sculptor preparing to work on a large block of marble. Through his delicate craftsmanship, the master sculptor envisions what this raw, unshaped marble can be transformed into. With a very specific design in mind, he instructs his assistants to set this block of marble apart for a particular design (**Consecration**). As he refines the marble, adding texture, depth, and form, he continues purifying it for that specific design (**sanctifying it**). The act of dedicating the marble for his special project was quick, but the process of shaping the marble into the final design requires much patience.

<!-- p5 -->
At the time of salvation, we were set apart, consecrated to God. Through the work of purifying our souls with the help of the Spirit, we are transformed. This process of purification is what sanctifies us.

<!-- p6 -->
When we received Jesus through salvation, we were born again. We were justified, our sins were forgiven. Our spirit was revived; we became alive in Christ. We were adopted into God's family and became the very temple of the Holy Spirit. Such an amazing transformation that the Scriptures say, “*There is joy in the presence of the angels of God over one sinner who repents.*” (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 15:10</span>).

<!-- p7 -->
Receiving Jesus through salvation (**justification**) saves our spirit, purifies us, and makes us presentable before God, able to receive His Holy Spirit. However, even with so great a salvation, our souls (our mind, will, and emotions) still need work!

<!-- p8 -->
Was the work of salvation incomplete? No! The work of salvation is effective, but the renewal brought by the Holy Spirit must continually be carried on in the life of the believer. This process of renewal and transformation is **sanctification.**

<!-- p9 -->
We know the work continues even after conversion, because our painful memories did not disappear, many of our beliefs remained the same, and many of our habits or rituals did not vanish. Not only that, conversion did not separate our mind from its fleshly (carnal) nature.

<!-- p10 -->
The word ‘carnal’ comes from a Latin root meaning ‘body’ or ‘flesh’. The carnal nature is in each of us from birth; like an animal instinct, it exists to help us survive. The way the carnal nature operates is to be self-focused, self-centered, self-oriented. Children are natural examples of the nature of the flesh. Despite their innocence, their nature is often centered on their own wants, desires, and needs; and if you don't meet its every expectation, ‘watch out!’.

<!-- p11 -->
As we grow up, moving past childish ways into maturity is part of it, but under pressure we easily revert to self-centered patterns of thinking and living. As James says:

<!-- p12 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p13 -->
> **<u>James 4:1</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Where do wars and fights come from among you? Do they not come from your desires for pleasure that war in your members?
>

<!-- p14 -->
</span>

<!-- p15 -->
In his letter to the Romans (chapters 6–8), Paul explains this carnal nature in more detail. He says this carnal nature compels one to do the very things one hates and makes one do evil deeds. He describes this inner struggle as a war between his human nature and the spirit fashioned by God.

<!-- p16 -->
This conflict between the flesh and the spirit happens within each of us, and it is our responsibility to do something about it. If we do not confront our carnal nature, our souls will continue to be trapped in patterns of spiritual decline, even though our spirits have already been saved.

<!-- p17 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p20 -->
These words of Paul are both a warning to us and a promise. Even though we have been born again, we still have a fleshly mind that must be brought under the knowledge of Christ. In doing so, we walk in God’s life and peace.

<!-- p21 -->
Take courage, there is hope for us! We must walk in the Spirit and be led by the Spirit of God. In doing so, we do not walk according to the flesh.

<!-- p22 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="1,13,14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p23 -->

>
> Therefore there is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus, who do not walk according to the flesh, but according to the Spirit.
>
> For if you live according to the flesh you will die; but if by the Spirit you put to death the deeds of the body, you will live.
>
> For as many as are led by the Spirit of God, these are the sons of God.
>

<!-- p24 -->
</span>

<!-- p25 -->
How do we learn to walk in the Spirit? Is it *just a conscious decision*? We learn to walk in the Spirit the same way we learn to walk naturally. We are motivated to move and to grow, so we wrestle, struggle, crawl, and wobble until we can stand on our own feet. Our parents cannot walk in our place, but they can support, encourage, help, and enable our walking.

<!-- p26 -->
Likewise, God cannot live our faith in our place. He teaches us, but we must make the effort. As Paul said to the Philippians:

<!-- p27 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="12-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p28 -->
> **<u>Philippians 2:12-13</u>** (NKJV)
>

>
> for it is God who works in you both to will and to do for His good pleasure.
>

<!-- p29 -->
</span>

<!-- p30 -->
We must work out our own salvation. That does not mean we climb onto a wooden cross and are physically crucified, but we must crucify our own fleshly nature (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:24</span>). God has promised to work in us and to help us, but we must decide to live this, and persevere in it.

<!-- p31 -->
It is true that ‘we’ are dealing with our fleshly nature, but it is also true that ‘God’ is dealing with it alongside us. The work of the Cross (justification) was hard for Jesus—He had to sacrifice His life for us—but it is easy for us; it is a free gift. The work of the Spirit (sanctification) is easy for God—He walks in perfect truth and is willing and ready to dwell with us—but it is hard for us, because we must crucify our old nature, and our nature is not particularly willing to be crucified.

<!-- p32 -->
This process is collaborative. God helps, assists, guides, directs, and corrects. We listen, obey, follow, and respond. The result is growth and transformation. This is the pruning that Jesus said God would do within us. Jesus is the vine, His Father is the gardener, we are the branches:

<!-- p33 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

> Abide in Me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself, unless it abides in the vine, neither can you, unless you abide in Me.

<!-- p36 -->
Pruning is extremely beneficial for a plant. Remembering our chapter on repentance, we are called to turn from dead works (works that are no longer bearing fruit) and turn to God. Turning away from the dead places in our lives, and turning to God, empowers the great Gardener of our lives to prune branches, improve growth, and produce even greater spiritual fruit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:2</span>).

<!-- p37 -->
Pruning can feel painful, but it reflects God’s loving care because He is making us into vessels for His purposes (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 12:11</span>). Some may experience a sense of loss, even fear, as they go through the process of pruning. Yet God is gentle, and He will not break a bruised reed (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="42" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 42:3</span>). Grapevines are pruned in their dormant season so that the stress on the plant is reduced. The attentive gardener cuts away the old and woody growth that no longer produces as before; this allows the vine to direct energy toward the branches with the greatest potential for good fruit.

<!-- p38 -->
Like a gardener who prunes branches to direct growth, God may allow seasons of correction and discipline to refine our faith and character, yet His work is patient and kind. God prunes those who abide in Him. Our focus should be more on abiding in Christ and less on the work of pruning.

### **Memory systems and how to renew them**

<!-- p62 -->
#### **1. Explicit memory (declarative memory)**

<!-- p63 -->
**Purpose:** Stores facts, events, and experiences that we can consciously recall. It is divided into two types-**semantic** and **episodic** memory.

<!-- p64 -->
**Semantic memory - facts and knowledge.** Semantic memory is the place where we store facts, definitions, and general knowledge—such as historical dates, Bible verses, and practical information. It helps us predict outcomes, make decisions, and avoid danger (for example, recognizing poisonous plants). It gives us stability and order, but it can also lead to rigidity—over-reliance on rules rather than adapting to unexpected situations.

<!-- p65 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p66 -->
- **Study Scripture** to shape our worldview (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="119" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="105" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 119:105</span>).

<!-- p67 -->
- **Memorize truths** and doctrines to stand firm in faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 3:16</span>).

<!-- p68 -->
- **Read Christian books** such as *"Renewing the Mind" by Casey Treat* or *"Battlefield of the Mind" by Joyce Meyer* to develop godly patterns of thinking.

<!-- p69 -->
**Episodic memory - personal experiences.** Episodic memory stores our personal experiences, linking them to specific times and places—such as a wedding day or a traumatic event. These memories provide context, helping us learn from the past and build relationships. However, episodic memory can be influenced by emotional bias and distortion. Past trauma can create fearful associations, making it difficult to move forward.

<!-- p70 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p71 -->
- **Reflect and confess** to bring hidden pain into God’s light (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 1:9</span>).

<!-- p72 -->
- **Reinterpret past experiences** from the perspective of God’s grace (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:28</span>).

<!-- p73 -->
- **Seek inner healing** through prayer and counseling (*“Your Pain is Showing” by Larry F. Low, "The Bondage Breaker" by Neil T. Anderson*, *"Emotionally Healthy Spirituality" by Peter Scazzero*).

<!-- p74 -->
#### **2. Implicit memory (non-declarative memory)**

<!-- p75 -->
**Purpose:** Subconscious memory used for habits, skills, and emotional associations. It operates automatically, helping us respond quickly to situations. It is divided into **procedural memory** and **emotional conditioning**.

<!-- p76 -->
**Procedural memory - skills and habits.** Procedural memory allows us to perform tasks without having to think—such as driving, typing, or playing an instrument. It is efficient, saving our mental energy and time. But bad habits and negative patterns can also become deeply ingrained and be hard to break.

<!-- p77 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p78 -->
- **Practice spiritual disciplines** such as prayer and fasting to build godly habits (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 5:14</span>).

<!-- p79 -->
- **Train yourself in godliness** through repetition and obedience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 4:7</span>).

<!-- p80 -->
- **Read books such as** *"Atomic Habits" by James Clear* (practical) and *"Spiritual Disciplines for the Christian Life" by Donald Whitney* (biblical) so that habits that honor God can be established।

<!-- p81 -->
**Emotional conditioning - emotional associations.** Emotional conditioning links sensory cues (like smells or sounds) with emotions, triggering a response even before we consciously process it। This system protects us, but when past experiences are negative, it can also generate irrational fears or emotional reactions।

<!-- p82 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p83 -->
- **Pray for healing** from past wounds (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="41" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 41:10</span>)।

<!-- p84 -->
- **Practice forgiveness** and let go of bitterness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-32" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:31-32</span>)।

<!-- p85 -->
- **Replace fear with God's promises** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 1:7</span>)।

<!-- p86 -->
- **Seek guidance from such books** *“Your Pain is Showing” by Larry F. Low, "The Bondage Breaker" by Neil T. Anderson*, “*Soul Care" by Rob Reimer* to overcome emotional triggers।

<!-- p87 -->
#### **3. Working memory (short-term memory)**

<!-- p88 -->
**Purpose:** Provides temporary storage for problem-solving and decision-making। It helps us focus, hold information for a short time, and process tasks—such as remembering instructions or making quick decisions। Although powerful, it is also limited, easily distracted, and quickly overloaded, especially under stress।

<!-- p89 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p90 -->
- **Focus and meditate** on God's Word daily (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 1:2</span>)।

<!-- p91 -->
- **Renew your thoughts** through purposeful thinking (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:2</span>)।

<!-- p92 -->
- **Practice mindfulness** and simplify mental clutter (*"Habits of Grace" by David Mathis*)।

<!-- p93 -->
- **Read** *"The Celebration of Discipline" by Richard Foster* to sharpen focus through spiritual disciplines।

<!-- p94 -->
## Conclusion

<!-- p95 -->
Renewing the mind is not a quick fix—it is a lifelong process of gradual transformation। Each memory system plays a role in shaping how we think, feel, and behave। By working intentionally with the Holy Spirit, applying Scripture, and developing devotional habits, we can train our minds to align with God's truth। Whether addressing past wounds, learning new habits, or staying focused in the present, God has provided us with both **spiritual tools** and **practical wisdom** to help us walk in renewal and freedom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 10:5</span>)।

<!-- p96 -->
Our world is filled with suffering, tribulation, and trials। Through the disciplined renewal of the mind, our soul is transformed, and we can live a life full of freedom for God, filled with the fruit of His Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-25</span>)।
