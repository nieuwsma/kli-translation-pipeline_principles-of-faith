# Chapter 8 - The Holy Spirit- Comforter, Guide, and Power

<!-- p1 -->
The Holy Spirit is not a new spirit, but is God’s and was with God from the beginning. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 1:2</span> at the time of creation, the Spirit of God was hovering over the waters. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="NEH" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Nehemiah 9:20</span>, Scripture says that in the wilderness the Spirit did the work of teaching the people. By this same Holy Spirit God anointed kings, judges, and prophets in the Old Covenant. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JOL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Joel 2:28-29</span> the Spirit is promised to be poured out on all humankind. By this same Holy Spirit God raised Jesus from the dead, and now He dwells in us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:11</span>). The Holy Spirit is only one, and He is always the same. The way He has worked with people has appeared different at different times, but His heart has never changed.

<!-- p2 -->
Jesus gave His disciples and all of us the powerful promise of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p3 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p4 -->
> **<u>John 14:16-18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And I will pray the Father, and He will give you another Helper, that He may abide with you forever—
>
> the Spirit of truth, whom the world cannot receive, because it neither sees Him

<!-- p20 -->
The disciples had to wait for the promise of the outpoured Spirit, and this would empower them to be witnesses to the ends of the earth. The Holy Spirit is an integral part of God’s plan for humanity, and for us as believers, indispensable for fulfilling our mission, and for the world.

<!-- p21 -->
## Knowing the Holy Spirit

<!-- p22 -->
The Holy Spirit is first and foremost from God and belongs to God. The Scriptures refer to the Spirit as the Spirit of God, or the Spirit of the Lord, or the Spirit of Christ; although these highlight different aspects, they are all the same. The Holy Spirit, being fully God, is also eternal and holy (blameless, spotless, pure, not impure). In some translations the Holy Spirit is also referred to as the Holy Ghost.

<!-- p23 -->
In Greek the word for spirit is **Pneuma** (**NYOO-mah** - **G4151**), which means breath, or wind. The Hebrew word for spirit has the same meaning. Breath is important because we need its constant supply to live. It was the breath that God breathed into Adam to give him life, and likewise, before dying on the cross, what Jesus exhaled for the last time was also breath. Without breath there is no life. Like air, breath can be felt and experienced, but it is not as easily seen as the other “elements” water, fire, earth.

<!-- p24 -->
There are many other fundamental descriptors used to describe the Holy Spirit. Each of these is used together with ‘the Spirit of’, meaning these qualities are extremely important for defining the Spirit. For example, *the Spirit of truth* or *the Spirit of life*.

<!-- p25 -->
Of the Spirit:

<!-- p26 -->
- **Truth** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:17</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span> This indicates the Spirit’s role in leading believers into all truth, revealing God’s will and the reality of Christ.

<!-- p27 -->
- **Life** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:2</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:8</span> This highlights the Spirit’s role in granting believers spiritual and eternal life, and freeing them from the law of sin and death.

<!-- p28 -->
- **Grace** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:29</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ZEC" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Zechariah 12:10</span> This reflects the Spirit’s work of dispensing God’s grace, forgiveness, and compassionate favor toward the undeserving.

<!-- p29 -->
- **Glory** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 4:14</span> This indicates the Spirit’s role in revealing and manifesting God’s glory, especially in times of suffering and persecution.

<!-- p30 -->
- **Adoption** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:15</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 4:5</span> This denotes the Spirit’s work of making believers children of God, granting them the status of sons and daughters.

<!-- p31 -->
- **Holiness** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 1:4</span> This points to the sanctifying work of the Spirit, who sets believers apart for God’s purposes.

<!-- p32 -->
- **Power** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 1:7</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span> This describes the empowerment by the Spirit for courage, service, and the ability to live the faith effectively.

<!-- p33 -->
In all these attributes the Holy Spirit is also rightly identified as the Helper or Comforter. Jesus refers to the Holy Spirit as the coming Helper; He uses the Greek word ***Paraklētos** (*pah-RAH-klay-tos - **G3875**), which means advocate, helper, one called alongside.

<!-- p34 -->
An advocate speaks on behalf of another, like a defense attorney. They intercede and assist the one entrusted to their care.

<!-- p35 -->
A helper helps. They help to empower the work, but their primary task is to assist. The helper is not wholly responsible for completing the task, but comes alongside to help get it done.

<!-- p36 -->
In the ministry of the Holy Spirit there are many distinct purposes and roles. His work is inward (within the hearts and lives of believers), upward (in service and honor to God), and outward (our mission field, the world).

<!-- p37 -->
## Roles of the Holy Spirit-Inward, Upward, Outward

<!-- p38 -->
### Inward-The Holy Spirit unites us with Jesus

<!-- p39 -->
Before Jesus performed any miracles or began His own public ministry, He was baptized by John the Baptist and the Spirit of God descended upon Him, and a voice from heaven declared, ‘This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:17</span>).

<!-- p40 -->
When we accept Jesus by faith, we receive salvation and the seal of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p41 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p42 -->
> **<u>Ephesians 1:13-14</u>** (NKJV)
>
> After you heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation, you also believed in Him; and after believing, you were sealed with the Holy Spirit of promise,
>
> who is the guarantee of our inheritance until the redemption of the purchased possession, to the praise of His glory.
>

<!-- p43 -->
</span>

<!-- p44 -->
This seal is not merely metaphorical; it is real. It serves as the sign of our redemption and is the down payment of the full inheritance we will receive as God’s children. Romans 8 describes that through new birth we receive the same Spirit of God who raised Christ from the dead, and that Spirit dwells in our mortal bodies and we become a real temple of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 6:16</span>).

<!-- p45 -->
Receiving the Spirit of God transforms us, and it causes rivers of life-giving water to begin to flow from us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 7:38</span>). This renewal is instantaneous and complete, but continues throughout our whole life. The river of the Holy Spirit should never stop flowing in our lives!

<!-- p46 -->
Through salvation and the indwelling of the Holy Spirit we can abide in Christ and He in us. The Spirit of God is no longer merely on the outside, but can dwell with us deeply. From now into eternal life we can walk in fellowship and harmony with God.

<!-- p47 -->
There is much more to say about the inner work of the Holy Spirit and His role in our sanctification, but that will be discussed in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p48 -->
### The Holy Spirit from above bears witness about Jesus

<!-- p49 -->
The Holy Spirit is from God and is God Himself. Just as Jesus said that He did nothing unless the Father showed Him (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 5:19</span>), likewise the Holy Spirit does nothing unless directed by the Father. Because the Holy Spirit is the Helper, He does not work in His own way, but works in perfect harmony with the Father.

<!-- p50 -->
The Spirit bears witness about Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:26</span>), glorifies Jesus, and declares and reveals to us what Jesus has laid up for us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:14</span>).

<!-- p51 -->
The Holy Spirit will bear witness about Jesus, will glorify Him, will teach, and will remind us of Jesus’ teachings (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:26</span>), and will lead us into all truth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span>). Jesus told His disciples that He had many more things to teach them, but they could not bear them at that time; but the Holy Spirit would guide them into all truth. This is another subtle point of Scripture. These teachings, which Jesus wanted His disciples to know, were communicated in various ways. Jesus taught them personally before His crucifixion and also after His resurrection and before His ascension.

<!-- p52 -->
Jesus also promised that the Spirit of truth, who was poured out on them on the day of Pentecost, would lead them into all truth through divine revelation. Is there still divine revelation today? Absolutely! The gifts of the Holy Spirit, including prophecy and words of knowledge and wisdom, have not yet ceased! However, any prophecy or any word that comes from any spirit is not of private interpretation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2PE" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Peter 1:20</span>). This is extremely important. Some people mistakenly claim that they are listening to the Holy Spirit, but their actions and words do not bear witness to Jesus. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 4:3</span> warns against any spirit that does not bear witness to Jesus, saying that such a spirit is of the antichrist. This bears repeating: if any spirit deviates from the teachings of Christ, that is not the Holy Spirit, but an unholy spirit!

<!-- p53 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p54 -->
> **<u>1 John 4:1</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Beloved, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits, whether they are of God; because many false prophets have gone out into the world.
>

<!-- p55 -->
</span>

<!-- p56 -->
Throughout all of Scripture, we see examples of claims made in error, often by false prophets or by well-meaning people who spoke contrary to God’s will. For example, Jeremiah warned against prophets who spoke visions of their own hearts, about those who claimed there was peace as destruction was drawing near (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="23" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 23:16-17</span>). Likewise, in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 16:16-18</span>, the words of a slave girl about Paul and Silas were accurate but were inspired not by the Holy Spirit, but by an evil spirit. These examples remind us that words that look like truth can also arise from an ungodly source.

<!-- p57 -->
Every claim of “God told me!” or of a “new” teaching must be measured by the biblical tests of truth. To rightly discern, we must ask:

<!-- p58 -->
- Does it agree with Scripture?

<!-- p59 -->
- Does it glorify Jesus Christ?

<!-- p60 -->
- Does it bear godly fruit?

<!-- p61 -->
By applying these tests, we can distinguish the true guidance of the Holy Spirit from error or deception।

<!-- p62 -->
God is in the habit of giving us ample &amp; abundant wisdom and direction (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:5</span> &amp; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="33" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 33:3</span>)। God’s divine wisdom does not violate Scripture, but it also goes beyond the scope of Scripture। His *entolē* *(*commands*)* are instructions to be obeyed immediately, those are *rhema* words (as discussed in the chapter on faith)।

<!-- p63 -->
This wisdom, understanding, and guidance may not be given word-for-word in Scripture। For example, Scripture provides excellent general guidance on what to look for in a spouse, but it does not give specific instructions about exactly which person। God has promised to direct our path and to guide us into all truth, and we can be confident knowing that this wisdom and guidance that comes from the Holy Spirit will never violate any teaching of Scripture।

(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:22-23</span>)

The instrument Jesus has chosen for transformation on the earth is His church, empowered, emboldened, and guided by the Holy Spirit.

## Building a Relationship with the Holy Spirit

<!-- p101 -->
Every relationship takes time and experience to develop deeply. The Holy Spirit is our advocate, our comforter, our helper, and our guide. He empowers us and sanctifies us.

<!-- p102 -->
Begin to think of the Holy Spirit as a person. In our relationship we both can build it together, or we can do things that weaken the relationship. He can be ignored, He can be quenched, He can be blasphemed, or He can be listened to, honored, and obeyed.

<!-- p103 -->
Do not ignore the voice of the Holy Spirit. He is always speaking to us and to the Church. If you do not yet know how to discern the voice of the Holy Spirit, it takes time, willingness, and effort (as with all healthy and beneficial relationships). But do not worry, all the components necessary to begin developing a personal relationship with the Holy Spirit are already embedded within you.

<!-- p104 -->
The quickest and most reliable ways to build an effective relationship with the Holy Spirit are

<!-- p105 -->
1.  Call on Him in prayer, **personally**, pause, and wait until He speaks.

<!-- p106 -->
2.  Call on Him, together with a **group** of people who know Him (the Church), pause, and wait until He speaks.

<!-- p107 -->
I use the phrase ‘call on’ literally: with your mouth, or in whatever way you speak (with your hands, if you cannot speak aloud with your mouth). Why speak? A few reasons:

<!-- p108 -->
1.  We are speaking spirits made in the image of God. God speaks, and we speak as well.

<!-- p109 -->
2.  We use speech as the primary means of communication. You can also write what you want to say (journaling) and speak it out loud.

<!-- p110 -->
3.  Speaking is an intentional act, and it signals to the one you are talking to that you want to include them in the conversation.

<!-- p111 -->
#### What should you say?

<!-- p112 -->
If you have never talked with God, try introducing yourself. Sure, it feels a bit awkward, but that awkwardness is okay and temporary. Along with your introduction, tell God that you want to talk with Him and that you want Him to answer you.

<!-- p113 -->
Consider asking Him a question. It doesn’t have to be a deep theological question; you can start simple. Could you show me your love for me? Why am I feeling a certain way? What do you want to tell me?

<!-- p114 -->
#### Then what?

<!-- p115 -->
As in all effective relationships, you speak for a while, then pause and listen. God will speak.

<!-- p116 -->
God speaks in many ways, but He desires to speak personally and internally. It likely will not be an audible voice (as if your best friend shouted your name), but an inner voice that speaks to your heart and mind. Elijah refers to it as a ‘still small voice’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1KI" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Kings 19:11-13</span>).

<!-- p117 -->
The most common ways God speaks with people that I have personally seen—and I have had the blessing of helping dozens of people learn to speak with God directly—are some combination of the following:

<!-- p118 -->
- An ‘understanding’ - the person talking with God immediately receives a kind of insight that helps them.

<!-- p119 -->
- An ‘image’ - in Scripture also known as a vision or a dream (a vision is when you are awake, a dream is when you are asleep), God will give you an image in your mind. Perhaps it is a memory, or a place you have been or know? Explore the image; what do you see? As a result of it, what do you feel? What is happening?

<!-- p120 -->
- An inner voice - like an audible voice, but one that only you can hear, speaking to your heart and mind.

<!-- p121 -->
#### What does God say?

<!-- p122 -->
Not sure! But that’s the fun of it. I can say this: He is not a spirit of fear, but of perfect love, and He will never violate the teachings of Scripture. For example, God will never tell you to have an extramarital relationship.

<!-- p123 -->
What now?

<!-- p124 -->
Keep the conversation going! It really is that simple.

### Why build a relationship?

<!-- p129 -->
The Holy Spirit is a Helper. A Helper helps us! If we have a shared language and relationship with Him, things move forward much more easily. Imagine, at your work, if you had no relationship with someone, couldn’t speak the same language, and couldn’t communicate at all—how would you work together?!

<!-- p130 -->
We serve a God who wants to communicate with us. Jesus said that His sheep recognize His voice (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:27</span>), the natural abilities we need are already built into us, but we may lack experiential proficiency.

<!-- p131 -->
Speaking from personal experience, a personal and active relationship with the Holy Spirit is one of the finest parts of being a Christian. His presence gives us peace, comfort, and direction, but only when you know how to seek Him, listen, and obey.

<!-- p132 -->
Develop sensitivity to the Holy Spirit. Like many married people, over time you can communicate with your spouse in unique ways and sometimes even with just a glance. That glance alone tells you everything you need to know about what just happened and what is about to happen. Build that same kind of sensitivity and intimacy with the Holy Spirit. Involve Him in your whole life; this is a wonderful aspect of your relationship with Him that will provide great depth and richness to your journey of faith.

<!-- p133 -->
The Holy Spirit has come to lead and guide us, to empower us to become God’s people, and to see the people of the earth saved from the kingdom of darkness into the kingdom of light, fundamentally transformed. Knowing Him and building trust with Him are essential for daily life and for accomplishing the work Jesus has given us to do.

<!-- p134 -->
Our discussion of the work of the Holy Spirit does not end in this chapter. His work is discussed in detail in the remaining chapters of this book, especially in the chapters on sanctification and baptism.

<!-- p137 -->

<!-- p139 -->
