# Chapter 13 - Spiritual Authority

<!-- p1 -->
Jesus is the supreme and complete source of spiritual authority. As the victorious Christ, He has ultimate authority over everything in heaven and on earth.

<!-- p2 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p3 -->
> **<u>Matthew 28:18–20</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And Jesus came and spoke to them, saying, "All authority has been given to Me in heaven and on earth.
>
> Go therefore and make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit,
>
> teaching them to observe all things that I have commanded you; and lo, I am with you always, even to the end of the age." Amen.
>

<!-- p4 -->
</span>

<!-- p5 -->
In this chapter we will study Christ’s authority and power, what authority and powers He has entrusted to us (His church), and learn how to walk in the authority of Jesus’ name. We will also study angelic and demonic beings and examine their authority, power, and natures.

<!-- p6 -->
Authority means having the right to act. Power is the ability to implement it. Both are essential in spiritual life. Authority is different from power. The following table compares authority & power; let’s also consider a few examples.

<!-- p7 -->
[See Table 7](../tables/table7.md)

<!-- p8 -->
Here are a few examples to help understand the difference between power and authority.

<!-- p9 -->
Example 1: At work, an employee may have the talent and skills (power) needed to sell the company’s assets. This power becomes legitimate only if they also have the relevant authorization (authority) from the company’s owners. Without this authority, selling the company’s assets is theft.

<!-- p10 -->
Example 2: A police officer is given specialized training (power) in weapons and hand-to-hand combat on how to arrest a criminal and bring them under physical control. The job of enforcing criminal law (authority) is granted to them with the backing of the law.

## The Source of Jesus' Authority

<!-- p74 -->
Now that we have clearly looked at what authority is, we need to know who its source is. Jesus’ authority is both divine and delegated. It originates from God the Father, is affirmed by His death and resurrection, and operates in perfect harmony with the Father’s will. Though complete, His authority respects human free will, revealing God’s love rather than coercive control.

<!-- p75 -->
Jesus’ authority is rooted in His divine identity as the Son of God and is bestowed by the Father. The Father has entrusted all authority to Christ, including rule and judgment over heaven and earth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 11:27</span>). Jesus’ authority reflects the unity and love within the Triune God, wherein the Son perfectly fulfills the Father’s plan (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="35" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:35</span>).

<!-- p76 -->
Although Jesus, as the Son of God, possesses inherent authority, His victory over sin, death, and hell was fully established through His death and resurrection. Christ’s obedience in His sacrifice resulted in His exaltation (seated at the right hand of the Father), which secured His authority over all creation. By conquering death, Christ obtained supreme dominion over life and eternity.

## Laying on of hands

<!-- p116 -->
The act of laying on of hands is an important biblical practice used in both the Old and New Testaments, symbolizing blessing, appointment, healing, and the transfer of spiritual authority or gifts. It is not merely a physical act—it represents a spiritual connection and impartation under the direction of God's authority.

<!-- p117 -->
In the Old Testament, the laying on of hands was often associated with blessing and dedication. For example, Jacob blessed his grandsons Ephraim and Manasseh by laying hands on them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="48" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 48:14</span>). Likewise, Moses laid hands on Joshua to appoint him as his successor, imparting leadership authority and wisdom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="NUM" data-chapter="27" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Numbers 27:18-23</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="DEU" data-chapter="34" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Deuteronomy 34:9</span>). This act publicly signified God's choice and empowerment for the work ahead.

<!-- p118 -->
In the New Testament, the practice of laying on of hands is closely connected with the ministry of Jesus and the early church. Jesus regularly laid hands on the sick and healed them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 6:5</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 4:40</span>), demonstrating both compassion and authority over sickness and disease. The apostles continued this practice, to impart the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:17</span>), spiritual gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 4:14</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 1:6</span>), and to bless believers. It also played a role in appointing leaders and missionaries for ministry (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 13:3</span>).

<!-- p119 -->
The laying on of hands is a work of faith and a symbol of obedience to God's word. It is not a mechanical method, but a spiritual practice rooted in dependence on the Holy Spirit. For example, in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:6</span>, the apostles laid hands on those chosen to serve as deacons, dedicating them to God's service and publicly affirming their calling. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 19:6</span>, Paul laid hands on the believers, and they received the Holy Spirit, showing its role in spiritual empowerment.

<!-- p120 -->
However, the Bible also gives warnings about this practice:

<!-- p121 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p122 -->
> **<u>1 Timothy 5:22</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Do not lay hands on anyone hastily, nor share in other people's sins; keep yourself pure.
>

<!-- p123 -->
</span>

<!-- p124 -->
This highlights the importance of discernment and spiritual maturity when appointing a person to a leadership role. As we studied in the chapter on the church, leaders must be tested and proven so that the body of Christ is not harmed by promoting unqualified or immature believers.

<!-- p125 -->
Some additional guidelines for the practice of laying on of hands:

<!-- p126 -->
1.  Context-appropriate - the person on whom hands are being laid should be a willing participant.

<!-- p127 -->
2.  Physically appropriate - the practice of laying on of hands does not include sexual or sensual touch. In practice, placing a hand on the shoulder, arm, or forehead (with permission) is generally considered acceptable in the Western world.

<!-- p128 -->
3.  Culturally appropriate - in some cultures, touch between men and women outside of blood relation or marriage is not considered appropriate.

<!-- p129 -->
Laying on of hands remains a powerful expression of faith and obedience in the church today. It serves as a means of blessing, healing, appointment, and empowerment, always pointing to the authority of Christ and the work of the Holy Spirit. Whether praying for the sick, sending out missionaries, or appointing leaders, it reminds believers that they work not in their own strength but under the authority of Jesus Christ and in alignment with His purposes.

<!-- p130 -->
Now that we have studied the authority of Jesus and the authority of the believer, we will discuss other beings in the spiritual realm: angels and demons.

<!-- p131 -->
## Angels and Demons

<!-- p132 -->
### Angels

<!-- p133 -->
Angels are an important part of God's creation, serving as His messengers and representatives to carry out His divine purposes. The Bible reveals a spiritual realm filled with these beings who are powerful, wise, and obedient to God's commands. Though angels are often invisible, their presence and activities affect human history and spiritual warfare. Understanding their roles, classifications, and activities gives us deeper insight into God's plan and the unseen realities that surround us.

<!-- p134 -->
The very meaning of the word "angel" is "messenger," highlighting their primary function. Angels operate in obedience to God's will, whether delivering messages, providing protection, executing judgment, or engaging in spiritual warfare. They are not objects of worship; rather, they are examples of obedience and reverence, always pointing to the glory of God. This chapter explores the nature, classifications, and roles of angels, shedding light on their significance in Scripture and in our lives today.

<!-- p135 -->
In the Scriptures, angels are seen as **messengers**, **servants**, and **warriors**. Angels often function as messengers, delivering important announcements, warnings, and encouragement. They reveal God's plans and provide guidance in times of need (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26-38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 1:26-38</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 2:13</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 1:1</span>). Angels are actively involved in guarding, strengthening, and delivering believers. They minister to God's people in times of crisis and provide supernatural assistance (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="91" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 91:11</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 12:7-11</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 4:11</span>). Angels engage in spiritual warfare, battling evil forces to advance God's kingdom and protect believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="DAN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Daniel 10:13</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:7-9</span>).

<!-- p136 -->
#### The Nature of Angels

<!-- p137 -->
Angels are created beings, brought into existence by God before the foundation of the world. They are distinct from humans, bearing unique characteristics necessary for their heavenly roles. The Bible teaches that angels are spiritual beings; they are not bound by physical limitations, yet they can assume visible form when interacting with humans.

<!-- p138 -->
Angels are described as immortal, meaning they do not die or grow old. They are innumerable, with the heavenly realm filled with vast hosts. Although they are most often referred to with masculine pronouns, Scripture makes it clear that angels are sexless and do not marry or reproduce. These qualities highlight their purpose—as servants of God—who are wholly devoted to His will and command. Angels possess remarkable attributes that reflect their divine purpose. They are obedient, reverent, wise, and powerful. These qualities enable them to carry out God's will effectively and faithfully.

[See Table 8](../tables/table8.md)

<!-- p140 -->
Although the Bible highlights various roles and titles of angels, it does not provide an overall classification system. Nevertheless, some angels are identified by specific roles or functions:

<!-- p141 -->
- **Angel of the Lord** - Often regarded as a pre-incarnate appearance of Christ, appearing with divine authority. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="23" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 23:20-23</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JDG" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Judges 6:11-24</span>*)

<!-- p142 -->
- **Archangels** - High-ranking angels like Michael, who leads armies, and Gabriel, who delivers divine messages. (*Jude 9; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:7</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 1:19-26</span>*)

<!-- p143 -->
- **Cherubim** - Guardians of God's glory and presence, such as in Eden and on the Ark of the Covenant. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 3:24</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 25:22</span>*)

<!-- p144 -->
- **Seraphim** - Worshipers of God, continually proclaiming His holiness. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 6:1-6</span>*)

<!-- p145 -->
- **Elect angels** - Faithful angels who resisted Satan's rebellion and remained loyal to God. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:21</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 25:41</span>*)

<!-- p146 -->
These classifications emphasize the diversity and organization within the angelic realm, however, their primary focus is always to fulfill God's purposes.

<!-- p147 -->
#### Human Interaction with Angels

<!-- p148 -->
In Scripture, people often encounter angels, but we are never taught to command them or pray to them. Rather, angels act according to God's direction. They serve believers as **God's servants** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 1:14</span>). Their interactions include protection, guidance, and deliverance, always pointing us back to God's power and sovereignty.

<!-- p149 -->
- **Warnings against angel-worship**: Scripture forbids worshiping angels or seeking encounters with them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:18</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 22:8-9</span>).

<!-- p150 -->
- **Prayer to God, not to angels**: Believers should pray to God, who sends angels according to His will (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="91" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 91:11</span>).

<!-- p151 -->
- **Encouragement and trust**: Angels remind us of God's care, but our faith must rest fully in God, not in angels.

<!-- p152 -->
Angels are awe-inspiring beings created to serve God and to minister to His people. Though their work is often unseen, Scripture gives glimpses of their activities, revealing their loyalty, strength, and reverence. Angels remind us of God's provision and protection, yet they always point to His glory. As believers, we are called to trust in God's care, for angels, as His servants, work to accomplish His divine purposes.

<!-- p153 -->
### Demons

<!-- p154 -->
Just as the study of angels is necessary to understand the spiritual realm, so too is the study of demons. Scripture clearly describes their activity, influence, and defeat through Jesus Christ. Recognizing their role in spiritual warfare helps believers stand firm in faith without fear.

<!-- p155 -->
#### Origin and Nature of Demons

<!-- p156 -->
Demons are fallen angels who followed Satan's rebellion against God. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:7-9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 25:41</span>).* Satan, previously known as Lucifer (“light-bearer”), a high-ranking angel, sought to exalt himself above God and was cast out of heaven along with other rebellious angels. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 14:12-15</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EZK" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ezekiel 28:12-17</span>).* 

<!-- p157 -->
Demons are spirits without physical bodies, yet they can oppress and torment humans and animals. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 5:11-13</span>).* They seek to deceive, tempt, oppress, and destroy. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 11:14</span>). Unlike God,* they have limitations, and they are not omnipresent (everywhere), omniscient (all-knowing), or omnipotent (all-powerful)*.* Their ultimate defeat is certain, and they will suffer eternal punishment. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 20:10</span>)*
