# Chapter 4 - Jesus the Messiah

<!-- p1 -->
Based on our study on sin, we have concluded that sin exacts a serious price—those who have been sinned against need to be restored, and those who sin must make restitution। The cost of sin must be paid। In the Old Testament, God established various systems of sacrifice carried out by priests, individuals, and the community to deal with, resolve, and judge sin।

<!-- p2 -->
Despite so many sacrifices for sin, they were not sufficient—not because God had failed, but because people kept choosing sin। Animal sacrifices could provide limited atonement, but no matter how many animal sacrifices there were, they could not change the sinful nature of people।

<!-- p3 -->
God had a marvelous plan, namely the <u>Messiah</u> (in Hebrew, ***Mashiach,*** pronounced *Mah-shee-ahkh -* **H4899**) who is also known as the <u>Christ</u> (in Greek, ***Christos,*** pronounced *Khris-tos* - **G5547**) meaning “the Anointed One”; someone set apart for a special role (e.g., a king, priest, or redeemer)।

<!-- p4 -->
To understand who Jesus is, it helps to see what people in His time were expecting about the Messiah। These expectations shaped how they viewed Jesus and, in many cases, why they struggled to recognize Him as the fulfillment of God’s plan।

<!-- p5 -->
## Historical context

<!-- p6 -->
The Jewish people had long been waiting for the coming Messiah। Before Jesus, Jewish beliefs about the Messiah were shaped by Old Testament prophecies, whose interpretations were varied and not universally accepted। Most Jews expected the Messiah to be a political and military leader who would free Israel from foreign oppression (primarily Roman rule) and restore the Davidic kingdom। Below is a summary of common messianic expectations and how they did (or did not) align with biblical prophecy:

<!-- p7 -->
### Common Jewish expectations about the Messiah before Jesus

<!-- p8 -->
### **1. A political and military deliverer**

<!-- p9 -->
- **Belief:** The Messiah would be a king like David who would lead Israel in a victorious revolt against foreign powers and restore Israel’s independence।

<!-- p10 -->
- **Cited Scriptures:** <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 9:6-7</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="23" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 23:5-6</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ZEC" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Zechariah 9:9-10</span>।

<!-- p11 -->
- **Historical context:** Under Roman rule, the Jews were oppressed and bore heavy taxation; as a result they hoped for a Messiah who would overthrow Rome and establish a powerful earthly kingdom।

<!-- p12 -->
- **Truth:** Jesus is a king, but His kingdom is not of this world (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="18" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="36" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 18:36</span>)। At His first coming He established a spiritual kingdom, but He will return as the conquering King—to judge and to rule in righteousness—as described in the book of Revelation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 19:11-16</span>)।

<!-- p13 -->
### **2. Restorer of the Davidic kingdom**

<!-- p14 -->
- **Belief:** The Messiah would rebuild the reign of the Davidic line and bring a golden age of prosperity, justice, and peace।

<!-- p15 -->
- **Cited Scriptures:** <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2SA" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Samuel 7:12-16</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="AMO" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Amos 9:11</span>।

<!-- p16 -->
- **Interpretation:** The focus was often on national restoration rather than spiritual renewal।

<!-- p17 -->
- **Truth:** As a descendant of David, Jesus fulfills this promise by establishing an eternal kingdom। At present this kingdom is spiritual, but at His second coming it will become a physical reign (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="32-33" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 1:32-33</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 20:4-6</span>)।

<!-- p18 -->
### **3. A prophet like Moses**

<!-- p19 -->
- **Belief:** Some Jews believed the Messiah would be a prophet like Moses, who would speak with God directly and speak on His behalf।

<!-- p20 -->
- **Cited Scriptures:** <span class="x-scripture" data-book="DEU" data-chapter="18" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Deuteronomy 18:15-19</span>.

<!-- p21 -->
- **Expectation:** Many expected a prophetic figure, but they often viewed this role not as a central characteristic of the Messiah, but rather as an additional aspect.

<!-- p22 -->
- **Truth:** Jesus, as the ultimate Prophet, spoke God’s word and directly revealed the Father (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:18</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:10</span>). His prophetic role surpasses that of Moses, for He mediates a new and better covenant (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:6-7</span>).

<!-- p23 -->
### **4. A leader who will bring justice**

<!-- p24 -->
- **Belief:** The Messiah will judge the wicked, uphold the righteous, and give justice to the oppressed.

<!-- p25 -->
- **Cited Scriptures:** <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 11:3-5</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="72" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 72:1-4</span>.

<!-- p26 -->
- **Interpretation:** Justice was expected primarily as political and social rather than spiritual.

<!-- p27 -->
- **Truth:** Jesus brings ultimate justice. At His first coming, He provided spiritual redemption and justice through the cross (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:13-15</span>). At His second coming, He will execute judgment upon the wicked and establish perfect justice for all eternity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 20:11-15</span>).

<!-- p28 -->
### Various beliefs about the Messiah

<!-- p29 -->
Just as today the Christian world is divided into various distinct denominations, organizations, or frameworks of belief, so also the Jews of Jesus’ era were divided. In Jesus’ time, four major Jewish groups shaped Israel’s religious and political landscape, each with its own distinct beliefs and expectations regarding the Messiah. Understanding some background about these different groups can help provide meaningful context for the study of the New Testament Scriptures.

<!-- p30 -->
The Pharisees were the most influential among the common people, known for strict adherence to the Law (Torah) and the oral tradition. They believed in the resurrection, the afterlife, and the existence of angels, and they emphasized personal righteousness and obedience to God’s commands. Their messianic expectation centered on a figure who would uphold the Law, bring justice, and restore Israel both politically and religiously.

<!-- p31 -->
On the other hand, the Sadducees were a wealthy and politically influential group who controlled the temple and the priesthood. They rejected the oral tradition and accepted only the Torah as authoritative Scripture. The Sadducees denied the resurrection, the afterlife, and the existence of angels; instead, they focused on maintaining their own power and ensuring political stability through cooperation with Rome. As a result, they placed very little emphasis on the Messiah, since their primary concern was preserving the status quo.

<!-- p32 -->
The Essenes were a separatist group who withdrew from mainstream Jewish society and lived in secluded communities such as Qumran. They were deeply focused on ritual purity, apocalyptic expectations, and a disciplined way of life. The Essenes believed in two messianic figures: a priestly Messiah, who would restore the purity of the temple, and a royal Messiah, who would lead Israel in righteousness. This dual-Messiah concept reflected their hope for both spiritual and political renewal. There is no direct mention of the Essenes in Scripture.

<!-- p33 -->
Finally, the Zealots were armed nationalists who sought to overthrow Roman rule through violent rebellion. They rejected Rome’s authority and were deeply committed to restoring Israel’s sovereignty. Their messianic expectation was for a warrior-king Messiah, like David, who would lead them in battle against their oppressors and establish a powerful earthly kingdom.

<!-- p34 -->
While these groups focused on earthly expectations, Jesus’ mission was a far greater plan—one that addressed not only political oppression, but also humanity’s spiritual bondage to sin.

## The truth about the Messiah

<!-- p36 -->
### Jesus’ anointing

<!-- p37 -->
The Pharisees’ and the Zealots’ vision of the Messiah reflects a recurring pattern in human history—seeking deliverance by earthly means while rejecting God’s greater plan. Throughout Scripture we have seen that people often turn away from God—even though He is ever faithful. Even when God remains faithful and receives us, He is often forgotten and rejected, resulting in cycles of disobedience and decay that plagued earthly kingdoms.

<!-- p38 -->
Although many expected a political Messiah, Jesus revealed a far greater purpose for His anointing—one that met spiritual and eternal needs. If Jesus had come as a triumphant military leader according to the hopes and expectations of the Jews, it would only have been a matter of time before the kingdom collapsed because of human disobedience.

<!-- p39 -->
Rather, Jesus established a kingdom that cannot be shaken. His mission was rooted not in military might or political dominance, but in divine purpose. As the Anointed One, Jesus explained what He was anointed to do and how He would accomplish it:

<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="16-21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

Jesus was anointed-set apart by God-and sent with a divine mission, full of purpose and power।

<!-- p44 -->
Jesus proclaimed:

<!-- p46 -->
- deliverance - forgiveness, forgiveness of sins, freedom।

<!-- p47 -->
- the acceptable year (season) of the Lord।

<!-- p48 -->
Those to whom He came were:

<!-- p49 -->
- the brokenhearted - mentally and spiritually shattered।

<!-- p50 -->

<!-- p51 -->

<!-- p54 -->
- by restoring sight

<!-- p55 -->
- by placing them in a state of freedom

<!-- p56 -->
- binding up, healing, restoring

<!-- p57 -->
The purpose of Jesus’s ministry and service was personal and global—immediate and long-lasting। His purpose was not a single focal point, but part of God’s divine plan to unite humankind with God। When sin entered the world, it created a division between God and man। God had to drive man out of the garden and from His presence। Throughout the Old Testament people could be with God, but only partially। The Spirit of God being given to people was very rare, and for the most part people lived in a state separated from God। He chose a nation among the Jews, but to restore His relationship with humanity He wanted a permanent, sure, and complete way।

<!-- p58 -->
In Jesus’s time, the presence of God on earth was primarily limited to the innermost part of the temple, the Holy of Holies। Here, behind the thick veil that separated the innermost part from the sanctuary, the presence of God was with the Ark of the Covenant। Man was separated from God, and God was separate from His creation।

<!-- p59 -->
God sent Jesus to repair, rebuild, restore, and reconcile His relationship with man। (2 Corinthians 5:17-21)

<!-- p60 -->
### The Essential Work of Jesus

<!-- p61 -->
To fully reconcile humanity to God, the Messiah’s mission required not only proclamation and healing, but also the supreme act of atonement. Jesus had to become the perfect and complete sacrifice through His death on the cross.

<!-- p62 -->
The Epistle to the Hebrews, across its 13 chapters, explains in detail how Jesus became the perfect sacrifice, and that He is both the offering for sin and the High Priest who offers it (Hebrews 3 & 4). Jesus’s priesthood is not of the Levitical lineage (the tribe of Levi), but in the order of Melchizedek, of whom it is said that he has no beginning or end, meaning it is not limited by human lineage and custom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 7:3</span>). The limitation of the Levitical priesthood is that it depends on the blood of bulls and goats (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:12-13</span>), whose sacrifices are not sufficient for permanent and lasting remission of sins. From our study of sin and sacrifice, we know that the consequences of the people’s sins could be held back for one year (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:3</span>) on the Day of Atonement, but this sacrifice had to be renewed repeatedly.

<!-- p63 -->
Jesus, by dying on the cross (as recorded in Matthew 27, Mark 15, John 19, and Luke 23), shed His blood and paid for the sins of all humanity—past, present, and future. Isaiah 53 reveals Jesus as the Suffering Servant, who fully took upon Himself punishment and affliction. His once-for-all death is the ultimate, supreme sacrifice. Jesus is the Lamb of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:29</span>), without blemish or wrinkle (sinless); because He was perfect, complete, and holy, His sinless shedding of blood made atonement possible. **No other sacrifice is needed or acceptable. The former ones were only temporary—but Jesus’s was complete and final.**

<!-- p64 -->
Through His death and resurrection we have received a new and better covenant (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:6</span>), established in the blood of God’s own Son; Jesus is the mediator of this covenant, the go-between. Through Christ’s sacrifice we receive salvation, and He is the author of *‘eternal salvation to all who obey Him*’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 5:9</span>).

<!-- p65 -->
In Greek the word for salvation is **Sōtēria** (*so-tay-REE-ah - **G4991)**.* Its primary meaning is **salvation, deliverance, or preservation**. It denotes the act of saving, both in a spiritual sense (eternal salvation through Christ) and in a physical context (rescue from danger or harm). The term conveys the ideas of safety, wholeness, and restoration.

<!-- p66 -->
**Salvation or Deliverance:**

<!-- p67 -->
- Refers to God delivering humanity from sin and its consequences through Jesus Christ.

<!-- p68 -->
- Used in the context of eternal salvation (justification, sanctification, and glorification).

<!-- p69 -->
**Preservation:**

<!-- p70 -->
- Indicates being kept safe, protected, or preserved from danger.

<!-- p71 -->
**Wholeness or Healing:**

<!-- p72 -->
- Involves restoration to a state of wholeness or well-being.

<!-- p73 -->
Jesus’s sacrifice paid the price for our forgiveness and made a way to establish relationship with God, and it established a way for us to be with/abide in Him that depends on our obedience, not on our perfection or our ability to refrain from sin.

<!-- p74 -->
When we receive Jesus by faith (to be discussed later), we receive His salvation. This salvation joins us to God and records our names in ***the Lamb’s Book of Life*** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 20:11-15</span>). The Gospel of John says:

<!-- p75 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p76 -->
> **<u>John 3:16</u>** (NKJV)
>
> For God so loved the world that He gave His only begotten Son, that whoever believes in Him should not perish, but have eternal life.
>

<!-- p77 -->
</span>

<!-- p78 -->
To perish means to be ruined—like fruit that spoils before it is eaten. It is a life that loses its purpose. Jesus came to give us unending or eternal life and to save us from perishing. Eternal life does not begin after death; it begins when we receive Jesus as our salvation, when we are born anew.

### New Birth

<!-- p80 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

When we receive Jesus, we are ‘born again.’ Jesus used this phrase to describe a moment of transformation, in which we are born of the Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:1-15</span>). This happens through faith—choosing to place complete trust in Him, as if entering into a marriage covenant with heart and words.

<!-- p84 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="9-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

When we were born into the world (birth of the body and of water), many things happened: we took our first breath and were declared alive, our parents received us, we were given a name, became heirs, received food and protection, and continued to grow.

<!-- p88 -->
When we put our trust in Jesus and receive Him, we are born again, not of the body but of the Spirit. We become spiritually alive! Just as many things accompany physical birth, so too many things accompany spiritual birth:

<!-- p89 -->
1.  When we received Jesus, God wiped away our sins and declared us forgiven (**justified**). He gave us a fresh start. The wall of separation between us and Him has been removed. (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>)

3.  We were **adopted** into God’s family and became heirs. (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:5</span>)

<!-- p92 -->
4.  We receive sources of spiritual nourishment and eternal protection. (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:2</span>)
