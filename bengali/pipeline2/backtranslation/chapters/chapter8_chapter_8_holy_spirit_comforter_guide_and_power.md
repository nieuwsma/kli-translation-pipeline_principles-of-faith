# Chapter 8 - The Holy Spirit—Comforter, Guide, and Power

<!-- p1 -->
The Holy Spirit is not some new spirit; rather, He is of God and has been with God from the beginning. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 1:2</span>, at creation, the Spirit of God was hovering over the waters. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="NEH" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Nehemiah 9:20</span>, Scripture says that it was the Spirit who instructed the people in the wilderness. In the Old Testament, God anointed kings, judges, and prophets through this same Holy Spirit. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JOL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Joel 2:28-29</span>, the Spirit promised that He would be poured out on all humanity. By this same Holy Spirit God raised Jesus from the dead, and He now dwells in us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:11</span>). The Holy Spirit is one, and He is always the same. The ways He has worked with people have appeared different from time to time, but His heart has never changed.

<!-- p2 -->
Jesus gave His disciples, and all of us, a powerful promise of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p3 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p4 -->
> **<u>John 14:16-18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And I will pray the Father, and He will give you another Helper, that He may abide with you forever—
>
> the Spirit of truth—whom the world cannot receive, because it neither sees Him nor knows

<!-- p20 -->
The disciples were told to wait for the promise of the Spirit who would be poured out, and through this they would be able to be witnesses to the ends of the earth. The Holy Spirit is an integral part of God’s plan for humanity, and is essential for us as believers, for completing our mission, and for the world.

<!-- p21 -->
## Knowing the Holy Spirit

<!-- p22 -->
The Holy Spirit is first and foremost from God and of God. The Scriptures refer to the Spirit as the Spirit of God, or the Spirit of the Lord, or the Spirit of Christ; although these highlight different aspects, they are all the same. Since the Holy Spirit is fully God, He is eternal and holy (spotless, blameless, pure, not impure). In some translations the Holy Spirit is called the “Holy Ghost.”

<!-- p23 -->
In Greek the word for ‘spirit’ is **Pneuma** (**NYOO-mah** - **G4151**), which means breath or wind. In Hebrew the word for spirit has the same meaning. Breath is important, because we need it continuously to live. It was God’s breath that He breathed into Adam to make him alive; likewise, the last breath Jesus took before dying on the cross was also breath. Without breath there is no life. Breath, like wind, can be felt and experienced, but it is not as easily observable as the other ‘elements’—water, fire, earth.

<!-- p24 -->
There are several other key descriptors used to describe the Holy Spirit. These descriptors are used with the phrase ‘Spirit of ...’, meaning these qualities are essential to the definition of the Spirit. For example, *Spirit of truth* or *Spirit of life*.

<!-- p25 -->
‘Spirit of ...’:

<!-- p26 -->
- **Truth** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:17</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span> points to the Spirit’s role in guiding believers into all truth and revealing God’s will and the reality of Christ.

<!-- p27 -->
- **Life** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:2</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:8</span> In giving believers spiritual and eternal life and freeing them from the law of sin and death, the Spirit’s role is prominently displayed.

<!-- p28 -->
- **Grace** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:29</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ZEC" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Zechariah 12:10</span> reflects the Spirit’s work in dispensing God’s grace, forgiveness, and unmerited favor to humanity.

<!-- p29 -->
- **Glory** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 4:14</span> indicates the Spirit’s role in manifesting and reflecting God’s glory, especially in times of suffering and persecution.

<!-- p30 -->
- **Adoption** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:15</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 4:5</span> points to the Spirit’s work in making believers God’s children and granting them the status of sons and daughters.

<!-- p31 -->
- **Holiness** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 1:4</span> points to the sanctifying work of the Spirit, which sets believers apart for God’s purposes.

<!-- p32 -->
- **Power** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 1:7</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span> describes the Spirit’s work in empowering believers with courage, service, and the ability to live out the faith effectively.

<!-- p33 -->
In all these qualities the Holy Spirit is also rightly recognized as the Helper or Comforter. Jesus refers to the Holy Spirit as the coming Helper; He uses the Greek word ***Paraklētos** (*pah-RAH-klay-tos - **G3875**), which means advocate, helper, one called alongside.

<!-- p34 -->
An advocate speaks on behalf of another, like a defense attorney. They intercede and assist on behalf of the one for whom they are responsible.

<!-- p35 -->
A helper helps. They assist in strengthening and enabling the work, but their main task is to assist the work. The helper is not solely responsible for accomplishing the task; rather, they come alongside to help bring it to completion.

<!-- p36 -->
In His ministry the Holy Spirit has several distinct purposes and roles. His work is inward (in the hearts and lives of believers), upward (in service and acknowledgment of God), and outward (in our mission field, the world).

<!-- p37 -->
## The roles of the Holy Spirit—Inward, Upward, Outward

<!-- p38 -->
### Inward-The Holy Spirit unites us with Jesus

<!-- p39 -->
Before Jesus performed any miracles or began His public ministry, He was baptized by John the Baptist and the Spirit of God descended upon Him, and a voice from heaven declared, ‘This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:17</span>).

<!-- p40 -->
When we receive Jesus by faith, we obtain salvation and receive the seal of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p41 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p42 -->
> **<u>Ephesians 1:13-14</u>** (NKJV)
>
> In Him you also, having heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation, and having believed, were sealed with the Holy Spirit of promise,
>
> who is the guarantee of our inheritance until the redemption of the purchased possession, to the praise of His glory.
>

<!-- p43 -->
</span>

<!-- p44 -->
This seal is not metaphorical, but literal. It serves as a mark of our redemption and as the down payment of the full inheritance we receive as children of God. Romans 8 describes that through the new birth we receive in our mortal bodies the very same Spirit of God who raised Christ from the dead, and we become the true temple of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 6:16</span>).

<!-- p45 -->
Receiving the Spirit of God transforms us, and as a result rivers of living water flow from within us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 7:38</span>). This renewal is immediate and complete, yet it continues throughout our whole life. The river of the Holy Spirit should never stop in our lives!

<!-- p46 -->
Through salvation and the indwelling of the Holy Spirit we can abide in Christ and He in us. The Spirit of God is no longer kept outside, but He can dwell with us intimately. From now into eternity we can walk in fellowship and harmony with God.

<!-- p47 -->
There is much more to say about the inward work of the Holy Spirit and His role in our sanctification, but that will be discussed in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p48 -->
### Upward-The Holy Spirit testifies about Jesus

<!-- p49 -->
The Holy Spirit is from God and He is God. Just as Jesus said He did nothing except what the Father showed Him (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 5:19</span>), so also the Holy Spirit does nothing unless directed by the Father. Because the Holy Spirit is the Helper, He does not act by His own will, but works in perfect unity with the Father.

<!-- p50 -->
The Spirit bears witness about Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:26</span>), glorifies Jesus, and declares and reveals what Jesus has for us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:14</span>).

<!-- p51 -->
The Holy Spirit will testify of Jesus, glorify Him, teach, and remind us of Jesus’ teachings (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:26</span>), and will guide us into all truth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span>). Jesus told His disciples that He had much more to teach them, but at that time they could not bear it; however, the Holy Spirit would lead them into all truth. This is another nuanced point in theology. The teachings Jesus wanted His disciples to know were communicated in various ways. Jesus taught them personally, both before His crucifixion and after His resurrection and before His ascension.

<!-- p52 -->
Jesus also promised that the Spirit of truth, who was poured out upon them on the Day of Pentecost, would guide them into all truth through divine revelation. Is there divine revelation even today? **Certainly there is!** The gifts of the Holy Spirit—including prophecy and words of knowledge and wisdom—have not ceased! However, no prophecy or word that comes from any spirit is a matter of private interpretation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2PE" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Peter 1:20</span>). This is extremely important. Some will falsely claim that they are hearing the Holy Spirit, but their deeds and words do not bear witness to Jesus. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 4:3</span> warns about any spirit that does not bear witness to Jesus, saying that such a spirit is antichrist. This bears repeating: if any spirit goes beyond the teachings of Christ, it is not the Holy Spirit but unholy!

<!-- p53 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p54 -->
> **<u>1 John 4:1</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Beloved, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits, whether they are of God; because many false prophets have gone out into the world.
>

<!-- p55 -->
</span>

<!-- p56 -->
Throughout Scripture we see examples of claims made wrongly—often by false prophets, or by well-meaning people who spoke against God’s will. For example, Jeremiah warned against the prophets who spoke visions from their own minds, claiming peace even when destruction was imminent (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="23" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 23:16-17</span>). Likewise, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 16:16-18</span> records that the words of a slave girl about Paul and Silas were accurate, but they were not from the Holy Spirit; rather, they were inspired by an evil spirit. These examples remind us that words that seem true can also come from ungodly sources.

<!-- p57 -->
Every claim of ‘God told me!’ or any ‘new’ teaching should be measured by the standard of the test of biblical truth. To judge rightly, we must ask:

<!-- p58 -->
- Is it consistent with Scripture?

<!-- p59 -->
- Does it glorify Jesus Christ?

<!-- p60 -->
- Does it produce godly fruit?

<!-- p61 -->
By applying these tests, we can distinguish the true guidance of the Holy Spirit from error or deception.

<!-- p62 -->
God is accustomed to giving us abundant &amp; plentiful wisdom and direction (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:5</span> &amp; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="33" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 33:3</span>). God’s divine wisdom will not contradict Scripture, but it will extend beyond the scope of Scripture. His *entolē* *(*commands*)* are directives to be obeyed immediately; these are *rhema* words (discussed in the chapter on faith).

<!-- p63 -->
This wisdom, understanding, and guidance may not be in Scripture word-for-word. For example, Scripture gives excellent general direction on what qualities to look for in a life partner, but it does not give specific instructions regarding a particular person. God promises that He will direct our paths, lead us into all truth, and we can take comfort in knowing that this wisdom and leadership of the Holy Spirit will never contradict any teaching of Scripture.

### Outward-The Holy Spirit empowers the mission

<!-- p65 -->
Jesus has given us His mission, the Great Commission:

<!-- p66 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p67 -->

>
> And Jesus came and spoke to them, saying, "All authority has been given to Me in heaven and on earth.
>
> Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit,
>
> teaching them to observe all things that I have commanded you; and lo, I am with you always, even to the end of the age." Amen.
>

<!-- p68 -->
</span>

<!-- p69 -->
The Holy Spirit plays an integral part in the mission; He convicts the world concerning sin, righteousness, and judgment:

<!-- p70 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="8-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p71 -->
> **<u>John 16:8-11</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And when He has come, He will convict the world of sin, and of righteousness, and of judgment:
>
> of sin, because they do not believe in Me;
>
> of righteousness, because I go to My Father and you see Me no more;
>
> of judgment, because the ruler of this world is judged.
>

<!-- p72 -->
</span>

<!-- p73 -->
Going deeper, the Spirit brings conviction regarding these three truths:

<!-- p74 -->
1.  **Conviction concerning sin**: Reveals the reality of humanity’s rebellion against God, drawing people’s attention to the depravity in their own lives.

<!-- p75 -->
2.  **Conviction concerning righteousness**: Demonstrates that Jesus perfectly fulfilled the Father’s will, and affirms that Jesus’ sacrifice has provided the once-for-all atonement necessary for the forgiveness of sins.

<!-- p76 -->
3.  **Conviction concerning judgment**: Confirms Satan’s defeat, guarantees God’s justice, and also that everyone will face judgment for the deeds and actions of their lives.

<!-- p77 -->
The Holy Spirit also empowers the mission. The Holy Spirit gives believers the power to be bold witnesses who share the gospel even in the face of persecution. Being bold does not mean speaking loudly. It means having courage in the midst of fear. Power means strength, steadfastness, and ability. Despite threats and fear of punishment, the Holy Spirit gives the strength and ability to be witnesses to the gospel.

<!-- p78 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p79 -->
> **<u>Acts 1:8</u>** (NKJV)
>
> "But you shall receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you; and you shall be witnesses to Me in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the end of the earth."

<!-- p80 -->
</span>

<!-- p81 -->
The whole book of Acts is filled with events and accounts in which this common cycle can be seen:

<!-- p82 -->
1.  **Prayer**: Believers ask for courage and God's intervention.

<!-- p83 -->
2.  **Empowerment**: The Holy Spirit fills them with courage, strength, and guidance.

<!-- p84 -->
3.  **Proclamation**: They boldly proclaim the gospel, often with signs, wonders, and miracles.

<!-- p85 -->
4.  **Opposition**: Resistance arises, leading to persecution or challenges. Sometimes believers are martyred; at other times they are imprisoned and/or beaten.

<!-- p86 -->
5.  **Renewal**: The church returns to prayer, asks anew for empowerment, and the cycle continues.

<!-- p87 -->
The New Testament church grew primarily while facing determined opposition. Around the world, the church suffers violence at the hands of oppressive and evil rulers—who are determined to suppress the knowledge of the truth of Jesus.

<!-- p88 -->
God knew that this opposition would occur; Jesus prophesied about this:

<!-- p89 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p90 -->

>
> "And when they bring you to the synagogues and magistrates and authorities, do not worry about how or what you should answer, or what you should say.
>
> For the Holy Spirit will teach you in that very hour what you ought to say."
>

<!-- p91 -->
</span>

<!-- p92 -->
Do not be afraid! Jesus has given us this promise:

<!-- p93 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

> **<u>Matthew 16:18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And I also say to you that you are Peter, and on this rock I will build My church, and the gates of Hades shall not prevail against it.
>

<!-- p95 -->
</span>

<!-- p96 -->
This is a marvelous and comforting promise. Doors are stationary defensive measures. When Jesus says that *the gates of hell will not be able to stand*, He is painting the picture of a conquering army that breaks down the gates and takes the city!

<!-- p97 -->
The Holy Spirit grants supernatural courage to bear witness to the gospel even in the face of persecution. The results of this empowerment can be described, but they are best in experience. It is a supernatural (greater-than-human) boldness that compels believers to share. This boldness in proclamation is often accompanied by miraculous works of the demonstration of the Spirit and of power (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 2:1-5</span>). People are healed physically and mentally, the dead are raised, those who were tormented by evil spirits are set free, and people with hard hearts turn and repent.

Jesus said, we will do these works and even greater ones! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:12</span>). These collective works are supernatural, divine, and desperately needed in a world that is tearing itself apart, groaning in sorrow, filled with pain, and waiting for the clear revealing of the mature sons of God! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:22-23</span>)

<!-- p99 -->
Jesus’ chosen means for change in the world is His church, empowered, emboldened, and guided by the Holy Spirit.

## Building a Relationship with the Holy Spirit

<!-- p101 -->
Every relationship takes time and experience to develop deeply. The Holy Spirit is our advocate, our comforter, our helper, and our guide. He empowers us and sanctifies us.

<!-- p102 -->
Begin to think of the Holy Spirit as a person. In our relationship, both of us can work to build it up, or do things that weaken our relationship. He can be ignored, He can be quenched, He can be blasphemed, or He can be listened to, honored, and obeyed.

<!-- p103 -->
Do not ignore the voice of the Holy Spirit. He is always speaking to us and to the Church. If you still cannot distinguish and recognize the voice of the Holy Spirit, it requires time, willingness, and effort (like every healthy and wholesome relationship). But don’t worry, everything you need to begin building a personal relationship with the Holy Spirit already exists within you.

<!-- p104 -->
The quickest and most reliable way to build an effective relationship with the Holy Spirit is

<!-- p105 -->
1.  Call on Him in prayer **personally**, pause, and wait for Him to speak.

<!-- p106 -->
2.  As part of a **group** (the Church) of people who know Him, call on Him, pause, and wait for Him to speak.

<!-- p107 -->
By “call on,” I mean it literally, with your mouth, or however you speak (with your hands if you cannot speak out loud). Why speak? A few reasons:

<!-- p108 -->
1.  We are speaking spirits created in the image of God. God speaks, and so do we.

<!-- p109 -->
2.  We use speech as the primary means of communication. You can also write down what you want to say (journaling) and speak it out loud.

<!-- p110 -->
3.  Speaking is an intentional act and signals to the One you are speaking with that you want to engage in conversation with Him.

<!-- p111 -->
#### What will you say?

<!-- p112 -->
If you have never spoken with God, try introducing yourself. Of course, it will feel a bit awkward, but that awkwardness is okay and temporary. Along with introducing yourself, tell God that you want to speak with Him and ask Him to respond and speak to you.

<!-- p113 -->
Consider asking Him a question. It doesn’t need to be a deep theological question; you can start with something simple. Can You show me Your love for me? Why am I feeling a certain way? What do You want to say to me?

<!-- p114 -->
#### Then what?

<!-- p115 -->
Well, as in every effective relationship, you will speak for a bit, then pause and listen. God will speak.

<!-- p116 -->
God speaks in many ways, but He wants to do so personally and internally. It will likely not be an audible voice (like when your closest friend calls your name out loud), but rather an inner voice that speaks to your heart and mind. Elijah refers to it as a ‘still small voice’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1KI" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Kings 19:11-13</span>).

<!-- p117 -->
The ways I have personally seen God speak with people, and I have been blessed to teach dozens of people to speak directly with God, are usually some combination of the following:

<!-- p118 -->
- A knowing - the person speaking with God immediately receives an understanding that helps them.

<!-- p119 -->
- A picture - also called a vision or a dream in Scripture (a vision occurs when you are awake, a dream when you are asleep), God will give a picture in your mind. Perhaps it is a memory, or a place you have been or that you know? Explore that picture: What are you seeing? As a result, what are you feeling? What is happening?

<!-- p120 -->
- An inner voice - like an audible voice, but one that only you can hear, and which is spoken in your heart and mind.

<!-- p121 -->
#### What would God say?

<!-- p122 -->
I'm not sure! But that's the fun of it. I can say that He is not a spirit of fear, but of perfect love, and He will never violate the teachings of Scripture. For example, God will never tell you to have an extramarital affair.

<!-- p123 -->
What now?

<!-- p124 -->
Keep the conversation going! It really is that simple.

### Why build a relationship?

<!-- p129 -->
The Holy Spirit is a Helper. A Helper helps us! If we have a shared language and relationship with Him, things move along much more smoothly. Can you imagine trying to work with someone at your workplace with whom you have no relationship, you don't even speak the same language, and you can't communicate in any way?!

<!-- p130 -->
We serve a God who wants to communicate with us. Jesus said His sheep recognize His voice (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:27</span>). The necessary natural capacities are already built into us, but we may be lacking in practiced skill.

<!-- p131 -->
Speaking from personal experience, a personal and active relationship with the Holy Spirit is one of the best things about being a Christian. His presence brings us peace, comfort, and guidance, but only when you know how to seek Him, listen to Him, and obey Him.

<!-- p132 -->
Develop sensitivity to the Holy Spirit. As many married people, over time, can communicate with their spouse in a special way, sometimes with just a single glance. That one glance tells what has just happened and what is about to happen. Build exactly that kind of sensitivity and familiarity with the Holy Spirit. Include Him in your entire life, this is a wonderful part of your relationship with Him that will give limitless depth and richness to your journey of faith.

<!-- p133 -->
The Holy Spirit has come to lead and guide us, to empower us so that we become God’s people, and to see the people of the world radically transformed—so that they are delivered from the kingdom of darkness into the kingdom of light. Knowing Him and building trust in Him are essential for everyday life and for accomplishing the work Jesus has given us.

<!-- p134 -->
Our discussion of the work of the Holy Spirit does not end in this chapter. In the remaining chapters of this book, especially the chapters on sanctification and baptism, His work is discussed extensively.

## Personal self-reflection questions

<!-- p136 -->
1.  How have you experienced the Holy Spirit as your Comforter, Guide, or source of strength? Which role resonates the most in your life right now?

<!-- p137 -->
2.  Reflect on the difference between the Holy Spirit being “within” (internal work) and being “upon” (external work). How does this distinction affect your understanding of His work in your life?

<!-- p138 -->
3.  What steps can you take to build a deeper relationship with the Holy Spirit and to recognize His voice more clearly?

<!-- p139 -->
4.  To move forward with courage and to fulfill the mission God has given us, we must rely on the power of the Holy Spirit. What specific mission and goals has God given you?

<!-- p140 -->
5.  The Holy Spirit guides us on the path of truth and discernment. Is there any area of your life where you need clarity or wisdom? How can you invite Him to guide your way?
