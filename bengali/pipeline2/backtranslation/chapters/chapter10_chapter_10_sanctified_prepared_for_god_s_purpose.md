# Chapter 10 - Sanctified—Prepared for God's Purpose

<!-- p1 -->
In the past two chapters, we discussed several important topics. We explored the role and ministry of the Holy Spirit, and the role and ministry of the Church.

<!-- p2 -->
We also have a role in the Church, and just as we know we need training, we also need purification. To truly be prepared for God's purpose, we must be holy.

<!-- p3 -->
The definition of **Sanctification** (sanctification) is to purify something for a special purpose. It is closely related to the word **Consecration** (consecration), which means to set something apart for a special purpose.

<!-- p4 -->
Imagine a skilled sculptor preparing to work with a large piece of marble. The sculptor envisions how this raw and unrefined marble can be transformed through his careful craftsmanship. He has a very specific design in mind; he instructs his assistants to set this piece of marble apart for a special design (**consecration**). As he refines and shapes the marble, adding texture, depth, and form, he purifies it for that special design (**sanctifies**). The act of dedicating the marble for his special project was quick, but the process of shaping the marble into the final design requires considerable patience.

<!-- p5 -->
At salvation we were set apart, consecrated to God. Through the work of purifying our soul with the help of the Spirit, we are transformed. This process of purification is what makes us holy.

<!-- p6 -->
When we received Jesus through salvation, we were born anew. We were declared righteous, our sins were forgiven. Our spirit was regenerated; we became alive in Christ. We gained membership in God's family and became the literal temple of the Holy Spirit. Such a wondrous transformation that Scripture says, “*There is joy in the presence of the angels of God when a sinner repents.*” (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 15:10</span>).

<!-- p7 -->
Receiving Jesus through salvation (**justification**) saves our spirit, purifies us, and makes us acceptable to God so that we can receive His Holy Spirit. However, even after such great salvation, **our soul** (our mind, will, and emotions) still **needs work**!

<!-- p8 -->
Was the work of salvation incomplete? No! The work of salvation is effective, but in the believer’s life it must continue through the renewal brought by the Holy Spirit. This process of renewal and transformation is the work of **sanctification**.

<!-- p9 -->
We know that even after conversion there is work left to be done, because our painful memories have not been resolved, many of our beliefs remain the same, and many of our habits or rituals have not disappeared. Moreover, conversion has not separated our mind from its carnal (worldly) nature.

<!-- p10 -->
The word ‘Carnal’ comes from a Latin root meaning ‘flesh’ or ‘fleshly/corporeal.’ The bodily (carnal) nature is innate in each of us; like animal instincts, it helps keep us alive. The way this carnal nature operates is to be self-centered, self-focused, self-directed. Children are a natural example of the carnal nature. Despite their innocence, their bent is primarily toward their own wants, desires, and needs; and ‘Watch out!’—if you do not meet every one of their expectations.

<!-- p11 -->
Part of growing up is maturing beyond our childish behavior; but under pressure we very easily revert to self-centered thinking and ways of living. As James says:

<!-- p12 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p13 -->
> **<u>James 4:1</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Where do wars and fights come from among you? Do they not come from your desires for pleasure that war in your members?
>

<!-- p14 -->
</span>

<!-- p15 -->
Paul, in his letter to the Romans (chapters 6–8), explains this carnal nature in greater detail. He says that this carnal nature compels him to do what he dislikes and prompts him to do evil. He describes this inner conflict as a war between his human-derived nature and his God-derived spirit.

<!-- p16 -->
This conflict between flesh and spirit exists within each of us, and it is our task to do something about it. If we do not confront our carnal nature, then even though our spirit is redeemed, our soul will remain entangled in patterns of spiritual decay.

<!-- p17 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p18 -->
> **<u>Romans 8:6-8</u>** (NKJV)
>
> For to be carnally minded is death, but to be spiritually minded is life and peace.
>
> Because the carnal mind is enmity against God; for it is not subject to the law of God, nor indeed can be.
>
> So then, those who are in the flesh cannot please God.
>

<!-- p19 -->
</span>

<!-- p20 -->
Paul’s words here are both a warning and a promise to us. Though we have been born again, we still have a carnal mind that must be brought under the knowledge of Christ. When we do that, we walk in God’s life and peace.

<!-- p21 -->
Take courage, there is hope for us! We must walk according to the Spirit and be led by the Spirit of God. If we walk this way, we will not walk according to the flesh.

<!-- p22 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="1,13,14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p23 -->
> **<u>Romans 8:1,13,14</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Therefore, those who are in Christ Jesus, who do not walk according to the flesh but according to the Spirit—there is now no condemnation for them.
>
> For if you live according to the flesh, you will die; but if by the Spirit you put to death the deeds of the body, you will live.
>
> For those who are led by the Spirit of God, these are the children of God.
>

<!-- p24 -->
</span>

<!-- p25 -->
How do we learn to walk in the Spirit? Is it just a conscious decision? We learn to walk in the Spirit the way we learn to walk in natural life. We are driven to move and to grow, so we fight, struggle, crawl, and wobble until we can stand on our own feet. Our biological parents cannot walk for us, but on our journey they can give support, encouragement, help, and enablement.

<!-- p26 -->
In the same way, God cannot live out our faith for us. He will teach us, but the effort must be ours. As Paul said to the Philippians:

<!-- p27 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="12-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p28 -->
> **<u>Philippians 2:12-13</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Therefore, beloved, just as you have always obeyed—not only in my presence, but now much more in my absence—work out your own salvation with fear and trembling;
>
> for it is God who works in you—both to will and to work according to His good pleasure.
>

<!-- p29 -->
</span>

<!-- p30 -->
We must keep on working out our own salvation. That does not mean that we climb onto a wooden cross and are crucified physically; but we must indeed crucify our fleshly nature (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:24</span>). God has promised that He will work in us and help us, but we must choose to walk this path and remain steadfast in it.

<!-- p31 -->
It is true to say that ‘we’ are wrestling with our fleshly nature, but it is also true that ‘God’ is at work with it as well. The work of the cross (justification) was hard for Jesus—He had to lay down His life for us—but for us it is easy, because it is a free gift. The work of the Spirit (sanctification) is easy for God—He walks in perfect truth and is willing and eager to dwell with us—but for us it is hard, because we must crucify our old nature, and our nature is not very eager to be crucified.

<!-- p32 -->
This process is collaborative. God helps, assists, guides, leads, and corrects. We listen, obey, follow, and respond. The result is growth and transformation. This is the pruning that Jesus said God would do in us. Jesus is the vine, His Father is the gardener; and we are the branches:

<!-- p33 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p34 -->
> **<u>John 15:1-4</u>** (NKJV)
>
> "I am the true vine, and My Father is the gardener.
>
> Every branch in Me that does not bear fruit He cuts off; and every branch that bears fruit He prunes, so that it may bear more fruit.
>
> You are already clean because of the word I have spoken to you.
>
> Abide in Me, and I also will abide in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit by itself unless it abides in the vine, neither can you, unless you abide in Me.
>

<!-- p35 -->
</span>

<!-- p36 -->
Pruning is extremely beneficial for a plant. If we recall our chapter on repentance, we have been called to turn away from dead works (the works that no longer bear fruit) and to turn back to God. As a result of turning away from the dead places in our lives and turning back to God, this enables the great Gardener of our lives to prune the branches, enhance growth, and produce even more spiritual fruit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:2</span>).

<!-- p37 -->
Although pruning may seem painful, it nevertheless reveals God’s loving care as He shapes us into vessels of His purpose (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 12:11</span>). While going through the pruning process, some may experience a sense of loss, even fear. Yet God is gentle; He will not break a bruised reed (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="42" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 42:3</span>). Grapevines are commonly pruned during the dormant season to reduce stress on the plant. The gardener who tends the growth will cut away that old, woody growth which no longer bears as it once did; this allows the vine to channel its strength into those branches that have the greatest potential for good fruit.

<!-- p38 -->
Just as a gardener prunes branches to direct growth properly, so God may also allow seasons of correction and discipline to purify our faith and character; but His work is patient and kind. God prunes those who abide in Him. Our focus should be on abiding in Christ—not on the pruning work.

<!-- p39 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 12:11</span> reminds us that pruning “*yields the peaceful fruit of righteousness*” for those who have been trained by it.

<!-- p40 -->
How does righteousness train us? By being transformed through the renewal of our mind.

<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

In the next section, I will describe each memory system, their purpose and functions, a few practical steps to renew this aspect of your mind, along with some resources for continued practice and study।

<!-- p61 -->
### **Memory systems and how to renew them**

<!-- p62 -->
#### **1. Explicit Memory (Declarative Memory)**

<!-- p63 -->
**Purpose:** Stores information, events, and experiences that we can consciously recall। It is divided into two types-**Semantic** and **Episodic** memory।

<!-- p64 -->
**Semantic Memory - Facts and Knowledge.** In semantic memory we store facts, definitions, and general knowledge-such as historical dates, Bible verses, and practical information। It helps us predict outcomes, make decisions, and avoid danger (for example, recognizing poisonous plants)। While it gives us stability and order, it can also lead to rigidity-overreliance on rules instead of adapting to unexpected situations।

<!-- p65 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p66 -->
- **Study Scripture** to shape our worldview (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="119" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="105" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 119:105</span>)।

<!-- p67 -->
- **Memorize truth and doctrine** to remain firm in the faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 3:16</span>)।

<!-- p68 -->
- **Read Christian books** such as *"Renewing the Mind" by Casey Treat* or *"Battlefield of the Mind" by Joyce Meyer* to cultivate godly patterns of thinking।

<!-- p69 -->
**Episodic Memory - Personal Experiences.** In episodic memory we store our personal experiences, linking them to specific times and places-such as a wedding day or a traumatic event। These memories provide context, helping us learn from the past and build relationships। However, episodic memory is prone to emotional bias and distortion। Past trauma can create fearful associations, making it difficult to move forward।

<!-- p70 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p71 -->
- **Practice reflection and confession** so that hidden pain comes into God's light (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 1:9</span>)।

<!-- p72 -->
- **Reframe past experiences** through the lens of God's grace (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:28</span>)।

<!-- p73 -->
- **Seek inner healing** through prayer and counseling (*“Your Pain is Showing” by Larry F. Low, "The Bondage Breaker" by Neil T. Anderson*, *"Emotionally Healthy Spirituality" by Peter Scazzero*)।

<!-- p74 -->
#### **2. Implicit Memory (Non-declarative Memory)**

<!-- p75 -->
**Purpose:** The subconscious memory used for habits, skills, and emotional associations। It operates automatically, helping us respond quickly to situations। It is divided into **Procedural Memory** and **Emotional Conditioning**।

<!-- p76 -->
**Procedural Memory - Skills and Habits.** Procedural memory allows us to perform tasks without thinking-such as driving, typing, or playing a musical instrument। It is efficient; it saves our mental energy and time। However, bad habits and negative patterns can also become deeply ingrained and hard to break।

<!-- p77 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p78 -->
- **Practice spiritual disciplines** such as prayer and fasting to build godly habits (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 5:14</span>)।

<!-- p79 -->
- **Train in godliness** through repetition and obedience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 4:7</span>)।

<!-- p80 -->
- **Read such books** *"Atomic Habits" by James Clear* (practical) and *"Spiritual Disciplines for the Christian Life" by Donald Whitney* (Bible-based) to build habits that honor God.

<!-- p81 -->
**Emotional conditioning - emotional connections।** Emotional conditioning links sensory cues (such as smells or sounds) with feelings, so that responses are activated before we consciously process them. This system protects us, but if past experiences are negative it can also create irrational fears or emotional reactions.

<!-- p82 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p83 -->
- **Pray for healing from past wounds** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="41" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 41:10</span>).

<!-- p84 -->
- **Practice forgiveness** and be free from bitterness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-32" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:31-32</span>).

<!-- p85 -->
- **Replace fear with God's promises** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 1:7</span>).

<!-- p86 -->
- **Take guidance from books like these** *“Your Pain is Showing” by Larry F. Low, "The Bondage Breaker" by Neil T. Anderson*, “*Soul Care" by Rob Reimer* to overcome emotional triggers.

<!-- p87 -->
#### **3. Working memory (short-term memory)**

<!-- p88 -->
**Purpose:** Provides temporary storage for problem-solving and decision-making. It helps us focus attention, hold information for a short time, and process tasks—such as remembering instructions or making quick decisions. While it is powerful, it is limited, easily disrupted, and especially under stress it can easily become overloaded.

<!-- p89 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p90 -->
- **Focus on and meditate on God's Word daily** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 1:2</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
- **Renew your thoughts through intentional thinking** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:2</span>).

<!-- p92 -->
- **Practice mindfulness** and simplify mental clutter (*"Habits of Grace" by David Mathis*).

<!-- p93 -->
- **Read** *"The Celebration of Discipline" by Richard Foster* to sharpen focus through spiritual disciplines.

<!-- p94 -->
## Conclusion

<!-- p95 -->
Renewing the mind is not a quick fix—it is a process of gradual transformation that lasts a lifetime. Each memory system plays a role in shaping how we think, feel, and behave. By working intentionally with the Holy Spirit, applying Scripture, and cultivating godly habits, we can train our minds to align with God's truth. Whether it is addressing past wounds, learning new habits, or staying attentive to the present moment—whatever it may be—God has provided us with both spiritual tools and practical wisdom to walk in renewal and freedom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 10:5</span>).

<!-- p96 -->
Our world is filled with sorrow, disaster, and trials. Through the disciplined renewal of the mind, our inner being is transformed, and we can live a life of complete freedom, filled with the fruit of His Spirit, unto God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-25</span>).
