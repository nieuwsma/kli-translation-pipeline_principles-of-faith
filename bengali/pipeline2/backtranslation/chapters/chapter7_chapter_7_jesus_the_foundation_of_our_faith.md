# Chapter 7 - Jesus—the Foundation of Our Faith

<!-- p1 -->
Building on our study of Jesus as the Messiah, in this chapter we will continue discussing the life, person, and work of Jesus. We will see that Jesus is the mediator of a new and better covenant—and that He perfectly fulfills the roles of prophet, priest, and king. In this new covenant, as believers we are brought into a right relationship with God and move from a works-based covenant to a trust-based (faith-based) covenant. Finally, to complete our study about Jesus, we will read and study the *Apostles’ Creed*.

<!-- p2 -->
## Mediator of the New Covenant

<!-- p3 -->
The concept of a mediator refers to a go-between who bridges the gap between two parties to help establish relationship or agreement. In the Exodus narrative, Moses was the first mediator of the covenant God established with His people. Moses received the Law, offered sacrifices (priest), called God’s people to obedience (prophet), and judged them (judge/king). After Moses, throughout the rest of the Old Testament, prophets, the Levitical priesthood, and judges and later kings carried out this mediatorial role.

<!-- p4 -->
The role of mediators in the Old Testament:

<!-- p5 -->
1.  **Prophet:** Prophets were God’s messengers, sent to declare His heart to the people and to bring them back when they strayed. For example, Moses, as a prophet, was the mediator of the old covenant—he received the Law from God and taught it to Israel. The prophets also foretold the coming of the Messiah and the ultimate redemption of humanity. Some notable examples of prophets are: Isaiah, Jeremiah, Elijah, Elisha, Samuel.

<!-- p6 -->
2.  **Levitical priesthood:** Priests served as mediators by offering sacrifices for the atonement of the people’s sins and interceding before God on their behalf. In particular, the high priest would enter the Most Holy Place on the Day of Atonement and sprinkle blood on the mercy seat, symbolizing reconciliation with God. The priest’s ministry is to intercede for the people.

<!-- p7 -->
3.  **Judge/king:** Judges, and later kings, were appointed to rule over God’s people, enforce His Law, judge among the people, deliver them from their enemies, and lead them in righteousness. The book of Judges tells the stories of these leaders—Deborah, Samson, and Gideon—whom God raised up to deliver and guide Israel. Israel later had a succession of kings, many evil, some righteous (see 1st and 2nd Kings, 1st and 2nd Samuel, and 1<sup>st</sup> and 2<sup>nd</sup> Chronicles). Some notable examples are Saul, David, and Solomon.

<!-- p8 -->
It is noteworthy that all the mediators of the old covenant were fully human, not divine. As humans they were capable of sin, and many times did sin. Some mediators were themselves wicked and led people away from God into defiled worship: false prophets, lying and corrupt priests, and evil kings.

<!-- p9 -->
### Christ as Mediator of the New Covenant

<!-- p10 -->
Jesus’ death, resurrection, and ascension bring the old covenant to its fulfillment and establish the new covenant.

<!-- p11 -->
The new covenant is a transformative relationship between God and His people, established through the life, death, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. Whereas the old covenant focused on external laws and regulations, the new covenant emphasizes inner transformation and grace. At the Last Supper, Jesus inaugurated this covenant, declaring that His blood would establish it, and that in place of the repeated animal sacrifices of the old covenant, His once-for-all atonement would suffice (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 22:20</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:10</span>). Through this sacrifice, the new covenant provides complete forgiveness and cleansing from sin, fulfilling the demand for full and lasting reconciliation with God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 31:34</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:12-18</span>).

<!-- p12 -->
One of the central features of the new covenant is its internal nature, in which God writes His Law on His people’s hearts and, through the Holy Spirit, forms a heart-level relationship with them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 31:33</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 3:3</span>). This transformation enables believers to fulfill God’s will not merely by adhering to external commands, but by being empowered by the Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:1-4</span>). The new covenant also grants direct access to God, removing the need for any earthly priesthood. Jesus is the eternal High Priest, and believers are called a royal priesthood; through Christ’s sacrifice they may approach God with confidence (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 7:24-25</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:19-20</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:9</span>).

<!-- p13 -->
Whereas the old covenant was limited primarily to Israel, the new covenant is universal—anyone, of any nation or background, can partake in it by believing in Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 3:28</span>). This covenant is entirely based on grace—granting free salvation and forgiveness through faith in Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>). It does not rest on human effort, but on the empowering work of the Holy Spirit, who transforms believers and enables them to reflect God’s character and walk in obedience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EZK" data-chapter="36" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ezekiel 36:26-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:9-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-23</span>). Moreover, the new covenant is everlasting, surpassing the temporary and conditional nature of the old covenant (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="32" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 32:40</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:13</span>). It signifies the ultimate fulfillment of God’s plan of redemption, uniting humanity to Himself and enabling believers to live forever as His people under His reign.

<!-- p14 -->
Jesus is explicitly identified as the mediator of the new covenant

<!-- p15 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p17 -->
</span>

<!-- p18 -->
Through His atoning death and resurrection, Christ inaugurates this new covenant, which replaces the old sacrificial system and external laws with a covenant written on the hearts of believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 31:31-34</span>). Standing between God and humanity, He not only intercedes on behalf of His people but also provides the way of reconciliation through His own blood (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:12</span>). Unlike the mediators of the old covenant, Jesus is sinless, eternal, and fully able to fulfill the divine demands of justice and mercy—ensuring a lasting relationship between God and believers.

<!-- p19 -->
Jesus fulfills and perfects all these roles.

<!-- p20 -->
1.  **Prophet:** Jesus is the supreme Prophet; he is not merely the messenger of God’s word, but the Word made flesh (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:14</span>). He reveals God’s character, will, and plan of salvation with fullness and perfection (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 1:1-2</span>). His teaching, miracles, and prophetic declarations fulfill the Old Testament prophecies and lay the foundation of the new covenant.

<!-- p21 -->
2.  **Priest:** Jesus is the perfect High Priest; he offered himself once and for all as a sacrifice for sin (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 7:27</span>). Unlike the priests of the old covenant, who had to offer sacrifices repeatedly for their own sins and for the people, the sinless Jesus offered his own blood to secure eternal redemption (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:14</span>).

<!-- p22 -->
3.  **King &amp; Judge:** Jesus is the eternal King, who reigns in righteousness and justice. As the son of David, he fulfills the promise of an everlasting kingdom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2SA" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Samuel 7:16</span>). His reign is not merely earthly but cosmic; he rules over all creation and establishes peace through his victory over sin and death (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 2:9-11</span>). Jesus will come again as King and Judge, and will judge ‘both the living and the dead’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 10:42</span>).

<!-- p23 -->
In Christ the roles of Prophet, Priest, and King are united and perfectly fulfilled. As the Mediator of the new covenant, he closes the gap between a holy God and sinful humanity, so that believers may come boldly to God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 4:16</span>) and the promised eternal inheritance is secured in him.

## The Apostles’ Creed

<!-- p25 -->
The Apostles’ Creed is not found in Scripture; it was composed and refined on the basis of the church’s most ancient teaching. A creed is a formal confession of beliefs—you can think of it as a combination of an oath with a declaration of faith. Creeds are important because they are used to unite those who believe in a particular way and to distinguish them from those who do not. In the United States, it is common for attendees at civic ceremonies such as city council meetings or legislative-type assemblies to recite the Pledge of Allegiance. Although this pledge is widely used in the United States, it is not used in other countries, because the citizens of those countries are not obligated to pledge allegiance to the United States. This pledge does not make someone “American,” but it represents an important set of core beliefs for citizens.

<!-- p26 -->
The first recognized versions of the Creed were written in the fourth century AD. Over the centuries the Creed has undergone minor changes, and there is some variation based on different denominations. Not every Christian denomination uses this Creed, but in the broader scope of Christianity some form of it is widely accepted.

<!-- p27 -->
The Apostles’ Creed has been used for many centuries as a means of instruction and catechism, and it communicates the most fundamental truths of the Christian faith—the foundational beliefs one must confess and hold to be a Christian. Some denominations have a formal catechism process through which a new believer joins that particular denomination and is taught or initiated into its beliefs.

<!-- p28 -->
To come to God and receive salvation, a person does not need to fully understand these beliefs; salvation is by faith, not by any doctrinal exam! However, remaining steadfast in that faith, we should all learn to understand these foundational truths.

## A Brief Explanation of the Apostles’ Creed

<!-- p31 -->
The basic truths inherent in the Apostles’ Creed are many and exceedingly profound; a full explanation would certainly require many volumes. Although this chapter is about Jesus, many matters relate to the other persons of the Godhead as well. Here, focusing only on the most central points, a brief explanation of the Creed will be given. Several of these topics are discussed more deeply in various chapters of this book.

<!-- p32 -->
#### *I believe in God, the Father Almighty, Creator of heaven and earth.*

<!-- p33 -->
This affirms belief in God as Almighty, Creator of all, and Father. The title “Father” especially emphasizes God’s loving relationship with his children through faith, as well as with his creation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:12</span>). As Creator, he spoke the universe into existence, bringing order out of chaos (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 1:1-3</span>). His omnipotence is revealed in the scope of creation, and his fatherly care is reflected in his continual sustenance and provision. This foundational declaration establishes God as the source and ruler of all life, who is worthy of worship and trust.

<!-- p34 -->
#### *I believe in Jesus Christ, his only Son, our Lord.*

<!-- p35 -->
Jesus Christ is God’s only unique Son—eternally begotten, not made—and shares the divine nature (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:1-2</span>, 14). Jesus has been the “Word” from the beginning, and he became flesh and dwelt among us. As the “Christ” he is the anointed Messiah, fulfilling the Old Testament promises of a deliverer. The title “Lord” recognizes his authority over all creation and his rightful place as Ruler and King (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 2:10-11</span>). He is not only Lord; he is the Lord who saves (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 16:31</span>).

<!-- p36 -->
Jesus is not merely sent by God; he is of God himself. Jesus abides in the Father and the Father in Jesus. The Gospel of John repeatedly affirms that Jesus and the Father are one (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="30" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:30</span>), that Jesus did nothing of his own accord (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 5:19</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="49-50" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 12:49-50</span>), and that all he did was to glorify his Father (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="17" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 17:4</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:13</span>), and that it was done in his Father’s authority and power (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 8:28-29</span>).

<!-- p37 -->
#### *He was conceived by the power of the Holy Spirit and born of the Virgin Mary.*

<!-- p38 -->
This affirms Jesus’ miraculous conception by the Holy Spirit, which underscores his divine origin. Being born of the Virgin Mary, Jesus fulfills the prophecy of <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 7:14</span>, which foretold the Messiah’s virgin birth. This event signifies the Incarnation, in which the eternal Word of God took on human flesh (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:14</span>). Being fully God and fully man—both—Jesus bridges the gap between God and humanity, enabling him to be the perfect Mediator and Savior.

<!-- p39 -->
Jesus was conceived by the seed of the Holy Spirit, and he did not inherit the sinful nature that we, born of the seed of man, bear. Jesus was tempted and tested in every way, yet he was perfect and sinless. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 4:15</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 5:21</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:22</span>.

<!-- p40 -->
#### *He suffered under Pontius Pilate, was crucified, died, and was buried.*

<!-- p41 -->
Jesus’ suffering and death were real, historical events that occurred under the Roman governor Pontius Pilate, and which ground the Christian faith in verifiable history. His crucifixion was the fulfillment of Old Testament prophecies, such as <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="53" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 53:5</span>, which speaks of the suffering servant who will bear the sins of many. Jesus’ death was sacrificial; He took upon Himself the penalty of sin on behalf of humanity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:24</span>). His burial confirms the reality of His death and prepares for the significance of the resurrection.

<!-- p42 -->
#### *He descended to the realm of the dead.*

<!-- p43 -->
After His death, Jesus descended to the people/realm of the dead, which is often referred to in Scripture as *Hades* or *Sheol*. This act shows that even in death He fully identified Himself with humanity, and it signifies His victory over sin, death, and Satan (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 3:18-20</span>). By descending He proclaimed His victory and the completion of His redemptive work.

<!-- p44 -->
#### *On the third day He rose again.*

<!-- p45 -->
The resurrection of Jesus is the cornerstone of our Christian faith, confirming His divinity and the truth of His teaching. Rising from the dead He defeated sin and death, granting the assurance of eternal life to believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 15:17-22</span>). The resurrection also fulfills Scripture, including Jesus’ own predictions (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 16:21</span>). This miraculous event transformed the disciples’ despair into bold faith, forming the basis of the early Church’s witness.

<!-- p46 -->
#### *He ascended into heaven, and is seated at the right hand of the Father.*

<!-- p47 -->
Jesus’ ascension signifies His return in glory to the Father’s presence. Seated at the right hand of the Father (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:20</span>), He has assumed His role as our eternal High Priest, interceding on behalf of believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 7:25</span>). His position of authority further indicates that He has been exalted as King, reigning over all creation and guiding the mission of the Church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:22</span>) so that we may do greater works (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:12</span>) and fulfill the Great Commission He has given us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:18-20</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 16:15-18</span>). The ascension assures believers of His ongoing presence and power through the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p48 -->
#### *He will come again to judge the living and the dead.*

<!-- p49 -->
This points to Christ’s future return, when He will bring final judgment and restoration. Jesus will judge all people, reward the faithful, and bring justice to the unrepentant (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 4:1</span>). His return will fulfill God’s ultimate plan for creation, bringing in His eternal kingdom of righteousness and peace. This hope encourages believers to live faithfully and expectantly, knowing that their labor in the Lord is not in vain (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="58" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 15:58</span>).

<!-- p50 -->
#### *I believe in the Holy Spirit.*

<!-- p51 -->
The Holy Spirit, the third person of the Trinity, is God’s active presence and power in the world today. He convicts people of sin, regenerates hearts, and empowers believers to live holy lives (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:8-13</span>). The Spirit unites Christians into one body, that is, the Church, and equips them with spiritual gifts for service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:4-11</span>). He is the Comforter, Advocate, and Guide; He dwells within believers as the seal of their eternal inheritance (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>).

<!-- p52 -->
#### *The holy universal Church, the communion of saints.*

<!-- p53 -->
"The holy universal Church" refers to the universal body of Christ that includes all believers across time and space. The Church is set apart for God’s purposes, called to reflect His holiness and to share the gospel. "The communion of saints" emphasizes the unity of all Christians, living and dead, as members of Christ’s body (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:4-6</span>). This fellowship transcends earthly divisions, displaying the shared hope and mission of believers.

<!-- p54 -->
Jesus is the head of the Church. The Church is referred to as the Bride of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="25-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:25-27</span>) and as the Body of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 1:18</span>)—both ways are mentioned.

<!-- p55 -->
#### *The forgiveness of sins.*

<!-- p56 -->
This highlights the heart of the gospel: through Jesus’ sacrificial death God offers full forgiveness of sins to all who repent and believe (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:7</span>). This forgiveness restores the broken relationship between humanity and God, removing guilt and shame. It calls believers to extend forgiveness to others as a reflection of the grace they have received (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 6:14-15</span>).

<!-- p57 -->
#### *The resurrection of the body.*

<!-- p58 -->
Like Jesus, at the end of time we too will experience bodily resurrection. This resurrection will transform the mortal, perishable body into a glorious, imperishable body fit for eternal life (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42-44" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 15:42-44</span>). This emphasizes the Christian hope not only for individuals but also for the restoration of all creation, as God makes all things new (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="21" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 21:4-5</span>).

<!-- p59 -->
#### *And the life everlasting. Amen.*

<!-- p60 -->
Eternal life with God is the ultimate promise of the Christian faith. Through a relationship with Christ, this life begins even now and will be fully realized in the new heavens and the new earth, where believers will dwell with God forever (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 22:1-5</span>). This concluding declaration reflects the joy and confidence of salvation, echoing the trust and hope expressed throughout the entire confession of faith.

<!-- p61 -->
## Conclusion

<!-- p62 -->
In the foundational truths expressed in the Apostles’ Creed, we see that the life and work of Jesus are central to God’s plan for humanity. Each statement of the confession of faith holds the core aspects of our belief in God and who Jesus is—His divinity, humanity, mission, and ultimate victory. These truths point to His unique role as the Mediator of the New Covenant. As Mediator, He invites us to come to God with boldness, trusting in His perfect sacrifice and continual intercession. This calls us to worship Him as the true King and to follow His teaching as the supreme Prophet. Ultimately, the study of Jesus leads us into a deeper relationship with Him, transforming us into a people who live in the light of His grace and truth.

<!-- p63 -->
Understanding who Jesus is and what He has accomplished is the foundation of the Christian faith. Jesus’ many names, titles, and roles illuminate various aspects of His character and mission, providing a fuller picture of His love and authority.

<!-- p64 -->
Just as in our study about God, the Scriptures are filled with various names that point to Jesus. Each name presents a specific aspect of His comprehensive character, ministry, and role. These names are given to unveil an aspect of Jesus to us. For your personal study, an index table is included in the appendix: *Table 12 - Names of Jesus.* Read through these names and study the Scripture references.
