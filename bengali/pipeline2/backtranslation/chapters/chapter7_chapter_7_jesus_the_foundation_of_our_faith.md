# Chapter 7 - Jesus—the Foundation of Our Faith

<!-- p1 -->
Building on our study of Jesus as the Messiah, in this chapter we will continue discussing the life, person, and work of Jesus. We will see that Jesus is the mediator of a new and better covenant—and that He perfectly fulfills the roles of prophet, priest, and king. In this new covenant, as believers we are brought into a right relationship with God and move from a works-based covenant to a trust-based (faith-based) covenant. Finally, to complete our study about Jesus, we will read and study the *Apostles’ Creed*.

<!-- p2 -->
## Mediator of the New Covenant

<!-- p3 -->
The concept of a mediator refers to a go-between who bridges the gap between two parties to help establish relationship or agreement. In the Exodus narrative, Moses was the first mediator of the covenant God established with His people. Moses received the Law, offered sacrifices (priest), called God’s people to obedience (prophet), and judged them (judge/king). After Moses, throughout the rest of the Old Testament, prophets, the Levitical priesthood, and judges and later kings carried out this mediatorial role.

<!-- p4 -->
The role of mediators in the Old Testament:

<!-- p5 -->
1.  **Prophet:** Prophets were God’s messengers, sent to declare His heart to the people and to bring them back when they strayed. For example, Moses, as a prophet, was the mediator of the old covenant—he received the Law from God and taught it to Israel. The prophets also foretold the coming of the Messiah and the ultimate redemption of humanity. Some notable examples of prophets are: Isaiah, Jeremiah, Elijah, Elisha, Samuel.

<!-- p6 -->
2.  **Levitical priesthood:** Priests served as mediators by offering sacrifices for the atonement of the people’s sins and interceding before God on their behalf. In particular, the high priest would enter the Most Holy Place on the Day of Atonement and sprinkle blood on the mercy seat, symbolizing reconciliation with God. The priest’s ministry is to intercede for the people.

<!-- p7 -->
3.  **Judge/king:** Judges, and later kings, were appointed to rule over God’s people, enforce His Law, judge among the people, deliver them from their enemies, and lead them in righteousness. The book of Judges tells the stories of these leaders—Deborah, Samson, and Gideon—whom God raised up to deliver and guide Israel. Israel later had a succession of kings, many evil, some righteous (see 1st and 2nd Kings, 1st and 2nd Samuel, and 1<sup>st</sup> and 2<sup>nd</sup> Chronicles). Some notable examples are Saul, David, and Solomon.

<!-- p8 -->
It is noteworthy that all the mediators of the old covenant were fully human, not divine. As humans they were capable of sin, and many times did sin. Some mediators were themselves wicked and led people away from God into defiled worship: false prophets, lying and corrupt priests, and evil kings.

### Christ as Mediator of the New Covenant

<!-- p10 -->
Jesus’ death, resurrection, and ascension bring the old covenant to its fulfillment and establish the new covenant.

<!-- p11 -->
The new covenant is a transformative relationship between God and His people, established through the life, death, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. Whereas the old covenant focused on external laws and regulations, the new covenant emphasizes inner transformation and grace. At the Last Supper, Jesus inaugurated this covenant, declaring that His blood would establish it, and that in place of the repeated animal sacrifices of the old covenant, His once-for-all atonement would suffice (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 22:20</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:10</span>). Through this sacrifice, the new covenant provides complete forgiveness and cleansing from sin, fulfilling the demand for full and lasting reconciliation with God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 31:34</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:12-18</span>).

<!-- p12 -->
One of the central features of the new covenant is its internal nature, in which God writes His Law on His people’s hearts and, through the Holy Spirit, forms a heart-level relationship with them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 31:33</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 3:3</span>). This transformation enables believers to fulfill God’s will not merely by adhering to external commands, but by being empowered by the Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:1-4</span>). The new covenant also grants direct access to God, removing the need for any earthly priesthood. Jesus is the eternal High Priest, and believers are called a royal priesthood; through Christ’s sacrifice they may approach God with confidence (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 7:24-25</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:19-20</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:9</span>).

<!-- p13 -->
Whereas the old covenant was limited primarily to Israel, the new covenant is universal—anyone, of any nation or background, can partake in it by believing in Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 3:28</span>). This covenant is entirely based on grace—granting free salvation and forgiveness through faith in Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>). It does not rest on human effort, but on the empowering work of the Holy Spirit, who transforms believers and enables them to reflect God’s character and walk in obedience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EZK" data-chapter="36" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ezekiel 36:26-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:9-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-23</span>). Moreover, the new covenant is everlasting, surpassing the temporary and conditional nature of the old covenant (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="32" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 32:40</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:13</span>). It signifies the ultimate fulfillment of God’s plan of redemption, uniting humanity to Himself and enabling believers to live forever as His people under His reign.

<!-- p18 -->
Through His atoning death and resurrection, Christ inaugurates this new covenant, which replaces the old sacrificial system and external laws with a covenant written on the hearts of believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 31:31-34</span>). Standing between God and humanity, He not only intercedes on behalf of His people but also provides the way of reconciliation through His own blood (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:12</span>). Unlike the mediators of the old covenant, Jesus is sinless, eternal, and fully able to fulfill the divine demands of justice and mercy—ensuring a lasting relationship between God and believers.

<!-- p19 -->
Jesus fulfills and perfects all these roles.
