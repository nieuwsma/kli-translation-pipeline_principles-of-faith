# Chapter 6 - Faith Toward God

<!-- p1 -->
Faith is a foundational concept in Christianity. The word ‘faith’ is used so commonly in Christianity that sometimes its meaning gets replaced by subtle misconceptions and misunderstandings. But it is not something you can hold in your hand—it is formed in the heart as you learn to trust in God. Conviction is faith.

<!-- p2 -->
The author of Hebrews instructs us that we should have ‘faith toward God.’

### Definition of Faith

<!-- p10 -->
Faith is a commonly misunderstood and misrepresented aspect of Christianity. As the saying goes, “There are ditches on both sides of the road”! In modern Christianity the word ‘faith’ is used so frequently without proper explanation that it can acquire a distorted definition through misunderstandings. Often faith is presented either as a kind of abstract, general statement of belief, or almost as a mystical power or “magic,” something you have in a certain measurable and limited amount.

<!-- p11 -->
#### What Faith Is Not

<!-- p12 -->
When faith is misrepresented <u>abstractly</u>, it lacks any definite, firm idea or conviction. A little probing makes this clear, because such faith cannot be explained specifically. If you ask, ‘What do you believe?’ or ‘Why do you believe?’—a person with this understanding often cannot clarify beyond ‘I believe.’ <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 3:15</span> says that we should always be ready to give an answer for the hope that is in us.

<!-- p13 -->
Sometimes people think of faith as a magical power—but Scripture does not teach that. Faith is a real relationship built on trust. In Scripture faith is much more dynamic and relational—it is rooted in conviction, trust, and assurance in God’s character and promises. It is not an impersonal force, but a responsive engagement toward God that shapes thoughts, feelings, and actions.

<!-- p14 -->
Why is faith related to conviction?

<!-- p15 -->
Our faith shapes how we see and interact with the world around us. Faith molds perception and prompts our actions. Our minds are wired this way—we live according to what we believe is possible.

<!-- p16 -->
We act based on what we perceive, understand, and believe to be possible. When we walk down a corridor, our brain identifies the boundaries of the floor and walls and automatically tells our feet where we can step and what to avoid. This truth extends beyond just physical movement in the world around us.

<!-- p17 -->
Suppose you believe that it is impossible for you to become financially self-sufficient or to live a healthy and active life. In that case your actions will follow that belief, and, true to your belief, you will not do what you believe to be impossible—or perhaps not even attempt it.

<!-- p18 -->
If you believe that God’s character is cruel and punitive, that perception of Him will keep you from walking with Him as His child.

<!-- p19 -->
#### Faith Is Conviction

<!-- p20 -->
So, what is faith? Conviction! The Greek word ***pistis*** means conviction, or the result of being persuaded. We ought to have conviction toward God. In English we might say faith (conviction) ‘**in** God’. If you are married, it is hoped that you have ‘**in’** faith toward your spouse. You believe that they will do what they said they would do, and you believe that they are sincere and truthful. This confidence should not be confined only to their words, but should extend to their overall person.

<!-- p21 -->
It takes time and experience to build faith, conviction, and trust in a relationship. In life, hopefully you have learned how to build a trust-based relationship with someone. The principles you have learned that help you increase trust in someone you know will likewise help you grow in faith in God. In a relationship, faith grows over time, through experience, through consistency, and through small and large actions—things that build your trust in the other person’s character and nature.

<!-- p22 -->
‘Faith in God’ is relational. A relationship is built by two parties, not by one. The good news about our faith in God is that God Himself takes responsibility for His side of the relationship (made possible through Jesus’ atoning death, and the fullness of His Spirit in the new birth), and He also helps with our side (through the work of the Comforter - the Holy Spirit). God is doing almost all the work; our part (which we will discuss later in this chapter) is to walk in obedience *with* Him.

<!-- p23 -->
### Jesus matures our faith

<!-- p24 -->
Jesus is the author and finisher of our faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 12:2</span>). Our faith is designed to continually grow and expand. We do not arrive at some single stage where our ‘faith’ becomes sufficient; rather, our faith keeps increasing. Think about a deep and personal relationship in your life. Just as in any relationship, we do not reach some specific milestone where now we ‘know’ that person. We keep getting to know them, increasing our knowledge of them, and in this our relationship is refined, formed, and grows.

<!-- p25 -->
How does our faith in God grow? By continually bringing Him into experience across the range of His many manifestations, modes of expression, and attributes. Again, think of a deep personal relationship in your life. You deepen the relationship by engaging with them in different ways, different formats, through different media, in different settings. You talk with them, send messages, maybe even write. You see them one-on-one and also in group settings. You talk about what you value; you may even challenge one another. Countless ways—with no end. Through these multifaceted expressions you come to know the person more deeply.

<!-- p26 -->
Each of us should know God through Jesus as our Savior. Think about the names of God (*see appendix),* just because we know God as Savior does not mean we have experienced Him as Redeemer, Healer, Counselor, Lord.

<!-- p27 -->
If you are in a place where you have been persuaded (believing) to know God as your Savior, then this relationship-building will give you such confidence in His character that you will be able to know Him in other ways as well.

<!-- p28 -->
I have been blessed to have several very deep relationships in my life. One such relationship is with my wife. Because of the level of trust (faith) we have toward one another in our relationship, my wife can tell me some things she will do—things I have not yet seen in reality—and I have the confidence to believe them because of her proven character.

<!-- p29 -->
This example shows how our deep relationship with God can come into experience with trust and assurance! We may not yet have fully experienced Him (in fact, in this life we never will fully), but as we mature in relationship, we can trust Him beyond the boundaries of what we personally know and have already seen happen.

<!-- p30 -->
Jesus grows our faith through experiences with Him. One important way we can and should bring Him into experience is His Word.

### Faith grows through the Word of God

<!-- p32 -->
Consider this passage from Romans:

> But they have not all obeyed the gospel. For Isaiah says, “Lord, who has believed our report?”

<!-- p35 -->

<!-- p36 -->
When we replace the word *faith* with *conviction*, it sounds like this:

<!-- p37 -->
"*Then conviction comes by hearing and hearing by the word of God*." This truth is inherently self-evident. A primary way to make someone persuaded is to persuade them (to make them believe), and we become persuaded when someone presents an ‘argument’—or a rational and clear explanation.

<!-- p38 -->
Two Greek words are used in the Bible to mean ‘word’-**logos** and **rhema**. Understanding the difference between them shows us how faith grows.

<!-- p39 -->
**Logos**: Generally refers to the concept, thought, reason, or broad expression of God’s word. It encompasses a universal, foundational truth or message and is often used to describe God’s eternal wisdom and Christ Himself as the living Word.

<!-- p40 -->
**Rhema (G4487)**: emphasizes a **specific, spoken word or utterance**. It generally refers to a particular word or message given at a specific moment, especially a spoken or expressed statement that applies directly to someone’s situation.

<!-- p41 -->
*Logos* is like the overall message of a book, whereas *Rhema* is like a specific paragraph or sentence that speaks directly to someone at a particular moment. Where *Logos* is God’s overarching truth, *Rhema* is a portion of that truth when it is spoken to a person in a personal, applicable way. Together, they highlight the fullness of God’s communication: *Logos* as the broad, foundational truths of God’s character and will, and *Rhema* as the specific, applicable statement that brings that truth to life in an individual’s life and circumstances.

<!-- p42 -->
### An example of a Rhema word in practice

<!-- p43 -->
God promises revelation and knowledge to all who desire it. When He speaks with us, that utterance is a *Rhema* word. Rhema words come from God and work to confirm, lead, and guide us. These are not a substitute for *Logos* words, nor do they carry the authority of Scripture. *Rhema* words must be tested by the same standards as prophecy or the interpretation of tongues (see the chapter on the Holy Spirit for a detailed study on this topic).

<!-- p44 -->
At various times in my career I have prayed for divine direction. A few years ago, I was considering beginning to look for new opportunities and, during prayer, I heard God say to me, ‘*Just wait for Me; I have kept an opportunity for you.*’ I was beginning to look for a new job because I was dissatisfied in my current role and felt discouraged. God’s word of direction, ‘*Wait for Me*,’ helped me find peace and confidence in an otherwise restless season.

<!-- p45 -->
Three to four months passed, I kept working hard and faithfully, keeping my attitude positive. God would remind me, *‘Wait for Me’*, and then His promise was fulfilled. I received a promotion opportunity and a generous pay increase.

<!-- p46 -->
I could have continued my job search; I could have left. I could have started somewhere new. No doubt, someone else would have gotten that promotion! But because I had sought the Lord’s counsel and been assured by His **Rhema word**, I obeyed and benefited. God’s word is **living and personal**, and He promises that He will give us manifold and generous wisdom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:5</span>).

<!-- p47 -->
Dozens of times, if not hundreds, I have prayed and asked God what to do and He has given me a Rhema word. Not each one produced the same kind of spectacular result, but all of them brought a sense of peace and confidence that I was obeying God and that He would set things right. These were the words I needed at that moment! Many times, when God gives a Rhema word, it comes from the Logos. He brings some portion of Scripture to my remembrance, “*Do not fear, for I am with you!*” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="41" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 41:10</span>), “*By His stripes we are healed*” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="53" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 53:5</span>), “*Do not grow weary in doing good!*” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:9</span>), the countless examples go on!

<!-- p48 -->
Receiving a Rhema word not only strengthens our faith, but also deepens our relationship with God, because we trust Him more fully in daily decisions. In the chapter on the Holy Spirit we will continue our study of learning to hear from God.

<!-- p49 -->
Our faith is also strengthened by what we see God do, but sometimes what we see in the natural world challenges what we hear from God. Reconciling the discrepancy between what we see and what we hear is an important ministry of the Holy Spirit, who promises that He will lead us into all truth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span>).

Thomas believed in Jesus and trusted his fellow disciples, but he did not believe that Jesus had been raised bodily; he insisted that until he experienced it personally, he could not actively believe it.

When Jesus appeared to His disciples and calmed them (I imagine it would be quite terrifying for someone to appear in the middle after passing through locked doors), He spoke to Thomas. He did not criticize or blame Thomas, nor did He accuse him of being part of a wicked and adulterous generation that seeks a sign (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 16:4</span>); rather, He revealed Himself to Thomas.

<!-- p60 -->
Jesus also says, ‘*Blessed are those who have not seen and have believed*’d. This is not Jesus encouraging ‘blind or ignorant faith’ or rejecting the use of empirical evidence. But He is saying that when we can believe without empirical proof, we are more blessed. Why? If our faith, our trust, were limited only to what has been demonstrably seen, then we would never have confidence for future works. We would have strong confidence in what has been accomplished, but we would not walk in God’s peace for our tomorrow and our future.

<!-- p61 -->
Using a relational example, what if my attitude were always, ‘<u>I will only believe it when I see it!</u>?’ Instead, a trust-based relationship says, ‘I know you are a trustworthy person’.

In the workplace, when a boss or supervisor gives instructions, they are not “talking just to talk,” but are giving directions that they want their employees to follow.

Returning to my personal example in this chapter, if I had not obeyed God's word to me—wait on Him—I would not have experienced peace in that season, nor would I have received the blessing of promotion!

### Practical steps to grow your faith

<!-- p153 -->
Walking with God is relational, but if you want a good relationship, some skills need to be learned. In earthly relationships, the skills that build strong and secure relationships are grounded in the following:

<!-- p154 -->
- Open and free dialogue and communication

<!-- p155 -->
- Healthy conflict resolution

<!-- p156 -->
- Commitment

<!-- p157 -->
- Consistency

<!-- p158 -->
If you want a strong, vibrant relationship, you must show up consistently, express yourself (communication) (our thoughts and emotions), receive the other person (listening), and walk with them! All these skills apply to our relationship with God as well.

<!-- p159 -->
Beyond basic relationship skills, there are specific practices and disciplines that will strengthen your relationship with God:

<!-- p160 -->
- **Cultivate and maintain a life of prayer, listen to and communicate with God.** Regular, heartfelt prayer is essential for an intimate relationship with God. Through prayer you can share your worries, joys, and gratitude, and seek His guidance and strength. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 4:6-7</span>*) Becoming sensitive to the voice of the Holy Spirit helps you discern God's will, find wisdom in decisions, and grow in spiritual maturity. Take quiet, meditative time to listen for His guidance. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:14</span>*)

<!-- p161 -->
- **Make time for worship.** Worship is a powerful way to draw near to God, expressing love and devotion. Worship through praise, song, or silent meditation deepens your awareness of His presence. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="100" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 100:2-4</span>*)

<!-- p162 -->
- **Read, study, and meditate on Scripture.** God's Word reveals His character, promises, and guidance for life. Investing time in studying Scripture increases your faith, understanding, and spiritual resilience. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>*)

<!-- p163 -->
- **Keep His commandments.** Obeying God's instructions demonstrates your love, reverence, and trust in Him. Walking in obedience aligns your life with His purposes and brings blessing. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:15</span>*)

<!-- p164 -->
- **Actively join and serve in a local church.** Engaging in a church community provides spiritual support, encouragement, and opportunities to serve others. Being involved builds a sense of belonging and strengthens your commitment to God's mission. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:24-25</span>*)

<!-- p165 -->
- **Build relationships with other believers.** Fellowship with other Christians provides accountability, encouragement, and shared wisdom. These relationships enrich your faith journey and help you grow through mutual support. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="27" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 27:17</span>*)

<!-- p166 -->
Our ‘faith’ in God is not just a one-time event of salvation, but an ongoing practice and relationship that we are called to nurture throughout our lives. The benefits of a trust-based and beneficial relationship are countless, but to see it flourish we must do the good work of nurturing that trust and growth.
