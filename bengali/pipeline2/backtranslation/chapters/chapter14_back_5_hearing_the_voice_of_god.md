# Hearing the Voice of God

<!-- p1 -->
The following guest addendum by Rev. Larry Low ([<u>www.LarryLow.com</u>](http://www.larrylow.com)) complements our exploration of the role of the Holy Spirit, providing practical insights about hearing the voice of God. The following guest contribution is used with permission.

<!-- p2 -->
One morning during prayer, I asked **God** this question: “For the days that are coming toward us, what is the most important thing I can teach Your people?” And He answered me very quickly, and when He said: “**Teach them to hear My voice.**” I sensed the matter was very urgent.

<!-- p3 -->
When the Lord gives such direction, I always pick up a pad and pencil and begin to write down what little I know, or what I think I know, and what the Scriptures support. I love to do word studies on the subjects the Lord leads me to teach. I love the anointing for preaching, but I truly love teaching the Word of God. It is a thrill to discover things that have always been there but have been overlooked.

<!-- p4 -->
David surely longed to uncover God’s truths, for in Psalm 119:18 he prayed: “Open my eyes, that I may behold wondrous things out of Your law.”

<!-- p5 -->
This is what I want to see—wondrous things from His Word.

<!-- p6 -->
As I was studying and preparing to teach the second message about hearing and discerning the voice of God, He spoke the following to my heart:

<!-- p7 -->
“*Teach them to hear My voice,  
teach them to honor and to obey.*

<!-- p8 -->
*Yes, teach them to hear My voice and I will always lead them away:  
away from the dangers and troubles that would have destroyed them,*

<!-- p9 -->
*away from the trials they are in,  
to a place of peace, love, and joy where very few have ever been.*

<!-- p10 -->
*away to a place of revival,  
to a place of divine ecstasy.*

<!-- p11 -->
*Oh yes, teach them to hear My voice son  
and these people will be free forever*.”

<!-- p12 -->
05/27/00 9:10 A.M.

<!-- p13 -->
The following eleven points will help you understand how the Lord will communicate with you:

<!-- p14 -->
1.  <u>Hearing the voice of God is not an audible sound, but an inner knowing in your heart.</u>

<!-- p15 -->
There was a time when His word came so clearly and vividly that it might as well have been audible. I answered Him out loud because it was that real. However, there was no one there but the Lord and me, so I cannot say with certainty whether He spoke to me as in a conversational exchange between us.

<!-- p16 -->
When Jesus was being baptized in the Jordan River there was a time the Father said: “This is My Son, in whom I am well pleased,” but some of the people there also said it sounded like thunder, so I can assume He could speak to me audibly as well, but for now, I want you to understand that when I say God spoke to me, it is more an inner knowing in my heart than words heard in my ears.

<!-- p17 -->
2.  <u>This inner knowing is not formed by the reasoning of your mind; rather, it is a complete thought or perception.</u>

<!-- p18 -->
The Lord has never told me anything that confused me. He has never told me anything that left me thinking, ‘Why.’ It is always an inner knowing with full understanding. God is not the author of confusion (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:33</span>).

<!-- p19 -->
3.  <u>When He speaks, He speaks in complete thoughts.</u>

<!-- p20 -->
He never leaves us to fill in the blanks. In the blink of an eye, God can fill your heart with enough information to keep you preaching for a lifetime. One touch from God can fill you with a fire that will keep you burning forever. He is a perfect Communicator.

<!-- p21 -->
4.  <u>When God speaks with us, He never drives. He leads.</u>

<!-- p22 -->
He is not a harsh taskmaster; He is a Shepherd. Herdsmen drive, but shepherds lead. Jesus is the Great Shepherd of the sheep, and we are the sheep of His pasture.

<!-- p23 -->
5.  <u>When He speaks to His children, He never condemns; He assures.</u>

<!-- p24 -->
His children are those who have been born again (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:3</span>, 5) by the confession of their faith in the shed blood of **Jesus** and the work of the cross. Who wants to listen to someone who is always holding our faults against us? I believe most of **God’s** children are afraid to hear **God’s** voice, because they have been told that **God** will convict them about this and that. See <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 3:19-20</span>. Who does it say there condemns or judges us? It is our own soul or heart that condemns **God’s** child, but **God the Holy Spirit** knows all things and is greater than our hearts. He has come to be our Comforter. Read the following scriptures, and in them you will see that the Holy Spirit is the one who convicts sinners. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33-34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:33-34</span> teaches us that nothing can separate us from **God’s** love. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:16</span>, 26 teaches us that the Holy Spirit is our Comforter, not our condemner; our Counselor, not our punisher; our Intercessor and Helper, not our hinderer. Read also <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 9:31</span> and 15:28—showing how the Holy Spirit (**God’s** voice) helps us, comforts us, and strengthens us.

<!-- p25 -->
6.  <u>God’s voice can be tested so that you can know for sure it is God, not your own thoughts or the voices of familiar spirits.</u>

<!-- p26 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 4:1-3</span> instructs us to “test every spirit,” so that we can be sure it is from God. When you think you are hearing from God, you have the right and the responsibility to ask that voice a question (that is testing or proving the spirit). The question to ask is: “Did Jesus Christ come in the flesh?” Familiar spirits will never confess this one thing—that Jesus came to earth in the flesh. He was fully man and fully God. No false spirit or religion will confess that Jesus came to this earth in the flesh and has delivered humankind once and for all from all their sins. Test the voice you are hearing. Also know that God will never lead you outside His written Word, the Bible. If you hear something that is not supported by the Bible, then you are not hearing from God. Get rid of it! Test the voice you are hearing—see whether it is from God.

<!-- p27 -->
7.  <u>The voice of God always glorifies Jesus Christ.</u>

<!-- p28 -->
The Holy Spirit is the voice of heaven. He has come here on earth to work with us, and Jesus said He will only say what He hears, and He will always glorify Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13-14</span>). He has not come to glorify your ministry or your work or your church programs. These things are good and most are necessary for us to do, but the Holy Spirit speaks and glorifies the person of Jesus. If in the voice you are hearing you yourself are being glorified, then you are not hearing the voice of God. There is a difference between being glorified and being encouraged and strengthened. Whenever the Holy Spirit speaks with you, He always encourages and strengthens you—even when He is correcting (guiding) you with the Word. But “glory” is when you think you are somebody. Apart from Jesus you are nobody—He is the “Somebody,” not us.

<!-- p29 -->
8.  <u>God’s voice will always reveal something to us.</u>

<!-- p30 -->
See <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:14</span>. He always takes what belongs to **Jesus** and reveals it to us. Considering the word “reveal,” the Amplified translation adds the words: declare, unveil, and transmit. **God** first declares to us, then He unveils something to us, and then by your faith He transmits it to us. Study also <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 2:10</span> and 12.

<!-- p31 -->
9.  <u>The voice of God always brings peace.</u>

<!-- p32 -->
Read <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:26</span> and 27, and <span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 3:14</span> and 15. His voice never unsettles us. It is a voice that brings perfect peace. Inner peace comes because there is a “knowing” that gives us confidence before God, and that turns into perfect peace. If the voice you are hearing is agitating you, then you are not hearing from God—shut it off. Jesus promised to give us peace—not as the world gives, but as He gives through the comforting words and continual presence of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:27</span>).

<!-- p33 -->
10. <u>The voice of God always brings truth.</u>

<!-- p34 -->
Read <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:17</span>, 26; 16:13. In fact, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span> calls the Holy Spirit: “the Spirit of Truth.” God cannot lie! He is truth, therefore He will speak only truth. Many have even said to me: “I can’t believe what I’m hearing is from God—it’s too good to be true.” And perhaps it does sound too good to be true; that’s why you must test the spirit—compare it with God’s Word and make sure it is truth. Some simply cannot believe it because what they hear is always positive, whereas they have been taught that God picks at our faults. God will speak only truth, and it will always be good news. The sad truth is that most people hear only bad news from the voice they hear and never expect to hear good news; so when good news comes, they suspect it as possibly untrue. **Truth is always Good News!** It is **that Truth** that sets us free, so how can **Truth** be anything other than Good News?

<!-- p35 -->
11. <u>The voice of **God** always takes us forward and upward.</u>

<!-- p36 -->
Read Deuteronomy 28:1-2, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="30" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 30:21</span>, and <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span>

<!-- p37 -->
God will never put you down! He will never belittle you; He only builds you up. When God speaks with you, He speaks from a perspective that what the Word says about you is already accomplished and you have received it. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="33" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 33:3</span> says: “Call to Me, and I will answer you, and show you great and mighty things, which you do not know.” Does that sound like putting you down or like evil and despair? There is no evil or despair in God; therefore He cannot speak to you any message of pessimism or gloom. For evil and despair you will have to pay attention to the world’s news agencies.

<!-- p38 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="29" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p39 -->
> **<u>Jeremiah 29:11</u>** (NKJV)
>
> For I know the plans that I have for you, says the Lord—plans of peace, not of evil—to give you a future and a hope.

<!-- p40 -->
</span>

<!-- p41 -->
Now, if God thinks good thoughts toward us, of peace, a future, and hope, then why would He speak differently? The answer is, He would not, and He does not either! He simply leads us on an upward and forward path in great hope, so that He may grant us the future we expect.

<!-- p42 -->
I hope this will encourage you to “call upon Him” and to learn how to **hear His voice**. He has great things to tell you, but before all His good things can benefit you, He wants your attentive ear.
