# Chapter 11 - Baptism-Water and Spirit

<!-- p1 -->
In our modern age, detached from Jewish roots, baptism is generally thought of only as water baptism, and the baptism of the Holy Spirit (Pentecost).

<!-- p2 -->
The Epistle to the Hebrews was written to a Jewish audience. The vast majority of Christians in our modern age have no Jewish roots or heritage. Lacking Jewish roots and culture, many of us do not have the cultural context that 1st-century Messianic Jews (Jewish believers who acknowledge Jesus) shared. Because we did not grow up in Jewish tradition, we generally do not know very much about the things Jewish believers knew—festivals, fasts, prayers, washings, statutes, and rituals.

<!-- p3 -->
In Greek, the word used for baptisms, or washings, is: **Baptismos** (bap-tis-mos’ - **G909**) - the act of dipping or washing. It denotes ceremonial washing or ritual purification. It can refer to immersions, purification rites, or washings common in Jewish and Christian contexts.

<!-- p4 -->
Baptism is one of the foundational teachings mentioned in Hebrews:

<!-- p5 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p6 -->
> **<u>Hebrews 6:1-2</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Therefore, leaving the discussion of the elementary principles of Christ, let us go on to perfection, not laying again the foundation of repentance from dead works and of faith toward God,
>
> of the doctrine of baptisms, of laying on of hands, of resurrection of the dead, and of eternal judgment.
>

<!-- p8 -->
## The Washings of the Old Covenant - Historical Context

<!-- p9 -->
In the Old Covenant, ceremonial washings played a central role in maintaining ritual purity before God and symbolizing holiness. These washings were prescribed in the Law of Moses and applied both to the priests who served in the tabernacle and to the people of Israel. For example, before entering the tabernacle or approaching the altar, the priests were required to wash their hands and feet in the bronze laver, symbolizing preparation to serve in the presence of a holy God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="30" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 30:18-21</span>). Similarly, rites to cleanse a person from impurity—such as contact with a dead body or skin disease—included washing with water as a sign of restoration to holiness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LEV" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Leviticus 14:8</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="NUM" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Numbers 19:17-19</span>). These acts served as outward expressions of an inner desire for holiness, emphasizing the importance of being set apart for God.

<!-- p10 -->
The ceremonial washings of the Old Testament required regular repetition to maintain ritual purity. Unlike a permanent solution, these rites provided only temporary external cleansing, leaving the deeper problem of sin unresolved. The author of Hebrews highlights this limitation, explaining that these practices were “symbolic for the present time,” unable to cleanse the worshiper’s conscience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:9-10</span>). These washings served as constant reminders of human imperfection and pointed to the need for something greater—a final cleansing that could purify not just the body but also the heart and soul.

<!-- p11 -->
The ceremonial washings of the Old Covenant find their fulfillment in Christ, who through His sacrifice provides complete and everlasting cleansing. Unlike the temporary rites of the Law, the blood of Christ thoroughly purifies, cleansing not only the body but also the conscience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:13-14</span>). Through His death and resurrection, Jesus accomplishes what the Old Testament rites could only foreshadow—true reconciliation with God. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:22</span> calls believers to “draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, having our hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience and our bodies washed with pure water.” This language connects Christ’s work to the imagery of Old Testament washings while demonstrating its far greater effectiveness. His sacrifice not only covers sin but removes it completely (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 1:7</span>). Thus, the ceremonial washings served as a kind of shadow, pointing to the profound cleansing that only Christ can provide, fulfilling humanity’s need for holiness through divine intervention.

<!-- p12 -->
To reiterate, Christians are not obligated to perform any ceremonial washings. Under the Old Covenant, washings did not save, nor do they in the New Covenant. Ceremonial washings will not improve our standing with God or earn us favor. Paul strongly condemns the ‘Judaizers’ (read the Epistle to the Galatians), who tried to conflate demands from the Jewish context (e.g., circumcision) as necessary for salvation. Salvation is by faith in Jesus alone!

<!-- p13 -->
## John’s Baptism

<!-- p14 -->
In the New Testament canon, multiple baptisms are mentioned. We have already discussed the baptisms and washings of Jewish tradition.

<!-- p15 -->
The ministry of John the Baptist centered on preparing the way for the coming of Jesus Christ. His baptism, described as a baptism of repentance for the forgiveness of sins (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 1:4</span>), called people to confess their sins, turn their hearts back to God, and get ready for the Messiah’s arrival. It served as an outward sign of inward repentance, symbolizing the desire for spiritual cleansing and readiness for the Kingdom of God. However, John’s baptism was temporary and preparatory—it pointed to the greater work that Christ would accomplish. Though John’s baptism emphasized repentance, it did not provide full forgiveness or spiritual rebirth, as it lacked the indwelling and empowerment of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:11-12</span>). Rather, John openly declared that One greater than he was coming, who would baptize not only with water but with the Holy Spirit and fire (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 3:16</span>).

<!-- p16 -->
John’s baptism finds its fulfillment and is surpassed through the death, burial, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. Christian baptism, instituted by Jesus and practiced by the apostles, signifies a deeper reality—union with Christ, death to sin, and resurrection to new life (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 6:3-4</span>). Unlike John’s baptism, Christian baptism is directly tied to faith in Jesus’ atoning sacrifice and the indwelling of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:38</span>). The coming of the Holy Spirit at Pentecost marks the New Covenant and the beginning of Spirit-empowered living, which John’s baptism could not impart. For this reason, John’s baptism is no longer practiced, as it has been replaced by the baptism ordained by Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19</span>), which includes repentance, faith, and believers being sealed by the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>).

<!-- p17 -->
## Christ’s Baptism

<!-- p18 -->
Christ’s baptism set a powerful example for His followers and established the foundation for Christian water baptism. Though Jesus had no sin, He was baptized by John “to fulfill all righteousness” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:15</span>). In doing so, He identified Himself with humanity’s need for repentance and foreshadowed His own death, burial, and resurrection. At His baptism, the Holy Spirit descended like a dove, and the Father declared, “This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:16-17</span>). This moment marked the beginning of Jesus’ public ministry and revealed the unity of the Father, Son, and Spirit, hinting at the spiritual transformation believers would experience through faith in Christ.

<!-- p19 -->
Jesus later commanded His followers to be baptized, commissioning them, “Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19</span>). The water baptism instituted by Christ becomes for believers **an act of obedience** and **a public declaration of faith**. It signifies their identification with Jesus’ death, burial, and resurrection (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 6:3-4</span>). Going under the water symbolizes death to sin, and coming up out of the water illustrates rising to new life in Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:12</span>). However, it is important to clarify that this outward act is not the source of salvation; rather, it is **a visible sign** of the inward work of grace already accomplished by the blood of Jesus.

<!-- p20 -->
Water baptism is a **symbol** of the cleansing power of Christ’s blood and the renewal of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:26</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>). It signifies repentance, faith, and a commitment to follow Jesus. It is an external testimony of an inner change—a way through which believers publicly declare their allegiance to Christ and their participation in the body of Christ, that is, the Church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:41</span>). Although it was commanded by Jesus and seen as an example in the early church, it does not **save** anyone. Salvation is by grace through faith only (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:8–9</span>), and true cleansing occurs spiritually through Christ’s blood (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 1:5</span>). Water baptism is a response to that salvation, an act of obedience, not a condition for it.

<!-- p21 -->
In the New Testament, water baptism is regularly associated with **repentance and faith** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:38</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 16:16</span>), both of which require a personal decision to follow Christ—something infants are not able to make. Baptism symbolizes the believer’s conscious identification with Christ’s death, burial, and resurrection (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 6:3–4</span>), and signifies their commitment to live in obedience to Him. Some traditions (e.g., Catholic, Lutheran) practice infant baptism as a covenantal sign (similar to circumcision), but since infants cannot express personal faith or repentance, the practice of **infant baptism** is not consistent with the biblical pattern of **believer’s baptism**. Instead, many churches hold **dedication ceremonies** for children, as Hannah dedicated Samuel to the Lord (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1SA" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Samuel 1:27–28</span>) and as Mary and Joseph presented Jesus in the temple (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 2:22</span>). Dedication is not a substitute for baptism, but a commitment on the part of parents to raise their child in the faith, teach God’s Word, and model a godly life—until the child is old enough to decide for themselves to follow Christ and be baptized.

<!-- p22 -->
Unlike the **ceremonial washings** of the old covenant, which required repeated rituals for purity, Christian baptism is a one-time event that reflects the finished work of grace. The washings in the Old Testament were symbols of external purity and foreshadowed the deeper spiritual cleansing that comes through Christ. By contrast, New Testament baptism symbolizes the inner renewal and regeneration accomplished by the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>). It points to the believer’s participation in the new covenant, where cleansing from sin is permanent and based on Christ’s finished work.

<!-- p23 -->
Water baptism is closely connected with the **promise of the Holy Spirit**. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:38</span>, Peter calls the people to repent, be baptized, and receive the gift of the Holy Spirit. While water baptism publicly signifies the believer’s repentance and faith, the true baptism occurs inwardly when the Holy Spirit washes, regenerates, and seals the believer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:13</span>). This spiritual washing makes us members of Christ’s body and empowers us to live holy lives. The **role of the Holy Spirit** in baptism reminds us that salvation is accomplished not through rituals, but through God’s transforming power.

<!-- p24 -->
It is important to distinguish between the **symbolic act** of water baptism and the spiritual reality of salvation through Christ’s blood. The Bible clearly teaches that salvation is based on faith in Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 10:9–10</span>) and not on works, including baptism (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:8–9</span>). However, water baptism is an **act of obedience** that publicly identifies the believer with Christ and the Church. It displays an outward commitment to live as Jesus’ disciple, but it neither adds to nor completes the work of salvation.

<!-- p25 -->
Christ’s water baptism set an example for believers to follow, demonstrating humility, obedience, and readiness for service. For Christians today, water baptism serves as a public declaration of faith, symbolizing death to sin and resurrection to new life. Although it is commanded by Jesus and an important step of obedience, it is not required for salvation. True cleansing occurs through faith in Jesus’ blood, and empowerment for Christian living comes through the work of the Holy Spirit within the believer. Baptism unites us with Christ and His Church, reminding us of the inner transformation and renewal that God has already accomplished.

<!-- p26 -->
Having established water baptism as an outward sign of faith, we now turn to the baptism of the Holy Spirit, which empowers believers for ministry and service.

<!-- p27 -->
## Baptism of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p28 -->
Baptism in the Holy Spirit is a deep and transformative experience given by God that empowers believers for ministry, service, and holy living. Every believer receives the Holy Spirit at new birth—this is how we become spiritually alive. But afterward, we should also receive baptism in the Holy Spirit, which empowers us with boldness, spiritual gifts, and effective ministry.

<!-- p29 -->
While water baptism is a public declaration of faith and symbolizes the cleansing of sin through Christ’s blood, baptism in the Spirit represents God’s empowering presence in the believer’s life. Together, these two forms of baptism comprise complementary aspects of the Christian life—one as an outward sign of obedience and the other as an inner current of power and renewal. The baptism of the Holy Spirit is the Spirit upon us, not within. At salvation all believers receive the Spirit within, but when God’s Spirit comes upon them, they receive power and boldness for ministry.

<!-- p30 -->
John the Baptist prophesied the baptism in the Holy Spirit, declaring,

<!-- p31 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p32 -->
> **<u>Matthew 3:11</u>** (NKJV)
>
> I indeed baptize you with water unto repentance, but He who is coming after me is mightier than I, whose sandals I am not worthy to carry. He will baptize you with the Holy Spirit and fire.
>

<!-- p33 -->
</span>

<!-- p34 -->
This promise pointed to a greater work that Jesus would accomplish after His resurrection—to send the Holy Spirit to indwell and empower His followers. Jesus confirmed this when He instructed His disciples to wait for the gift of the Spirit, explaining that they would receive power to be His witnesses (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>). The fulfillment of this promise occurred on the Day of Pentecost, when the disciples were filled with the Holy Spirit, evidenced by speaking in tongues, prophesying, and boldly proclaiming the gospel (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:1–4</span>).

<!-- p35 -->
This outpouring marked the beginning of the **Church age** and demonstrated that the Spirit’s power is now available to all believers. This promise was not limited to the apostles, but extended to all believers, as Peter declared, *“For the promise is for you and your children and for all who are far off—as many as the Lord our God will call”* (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="39" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:39</span>).

<!-- p36 -->
### The Spirit within vs. the Spirit upon

<!-- p37 -->
When someone places their faith in Jesus, the Holy Spirit gives them new life within. This is what it means to be **born again** (regenerated). At that moment of faith, the Holy Spirit begins to dwell **within** the believer, making them spiritually alive and adopting them into God’s family (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:5–6</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>). This is the new birth, or being born anew, as Jesus described in John 3.

<!-- p38 -->
Scriptural basis:

<!-- p39 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>* - “He saved us through the washing of regeneration and renewing of the Holy Spirit.”

<!-- p40 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>* - The Holy Spirit seals believers as the **guarantee** of their inheritance.

<!-- p41 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:9</span>* - "If anyone does not have the Spirit of Christ, he does not belong to Him."

<!-- p42 -->
This work of regeneration is universal for all believers at the time of salvation and establishes the Spirit’s permanent indwelling. It does not depend on any external work, such as water baptism, and it guarantees salvation by grace through faith.

<!-- p43 -->
In contrast, baptism in the Holy Spirit is a distinct and subsequent experience that equips believers with power for service and witness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>). Whereas regeneration deals with new life and identity, the aim of Spirit baptism is empowerment **upon** and the release of spiritual gifts. This baptism is often accompanied by signs and manifestations of the gifts of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p44 -->
Scriptural basis:

<!-- p45 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p46 -->
> **<u>Acts 1:8</u>** (NKJV)
>
> But you will receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you; and you will be my witnesses in Jerusalem, in all Judea and Samaria, and to the ends of the earth."
>

<!-- p47 -->
</span>

<!-- p48 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:15-17</span>* - The Samaritans believed and were baptized, but later they received the Holy Spirit through prayer and the laying on of hands.

<!-- p49 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 19:6</span>* - The disciples in Ephesus, who were already believers, received the Holy Spirit when Paul laid his hands on them.

<!-- p50 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit does not replace regeneration; rather, it builds on it to equip the believer with boldness for spiritual service and ministry. Being baptized in the Holy Spirit is a free gift for the saved, not a condition for being saved. No <u>particular</u> manifestation of a gift of the Holy Spirit is necessary to prove someone’s ‘baptism’ in the Holy Spirit, but we should expect, as seen in Acts, that the baptism of the Holy Spirit will be manifested today as well with signs and boldness! Some people mistakenly think that if someone does not speak in tongues, then he is not saved, or that he does not have the baptism of the Holy Spirit. Not everyone will receive every gift or manifestation of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29-31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:29-31</span>).

<!-- p51 -->
## How to Receive the Baptism of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p52 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit is described in Scripture as a gift promised to believers and received by faith. It is distinct from salvation and is primarily for empowerment and witness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>). The baptism of the Holy Spirit is a gift of empowerment for believers, which Jesus promised and which was fulfilled at Pentecost. It is received through repentance, faith, and asking God. Although the laying on of hands is often used as a means, ultimately it is God who gives the baptism in the Spirit.

<!-- p53 -->
Scripture presents speaking in tongues and prophecy as common signs, but the purpose of this baptism is much greater—to impart courage, power, and spiritual gifts for ministry. Every believer is encouraged to seek this experience as part of his spiritual journey, trusting that God will equip him for His work.

<!-- p54 -->
Regarding how this baptism is received, the Bible outlines several key principles:

<!-- p55 -->
### **1. The baptism of the Holy Spirit is a free gift from God**

<!-- p56 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit is not something believers can earn—it is a free gift given by God. God desires to give good gifts to His children and is eager to give the Holy Spirit to those who ask with faith and a desire for empowerment.

<!-- p57 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p58 -->
> **<u>Luke 11:13</u>** (NKJV)
>
> So if you, being evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to those who ask Him!"
>

<!-- p59 -->
</span>

<!-- p60 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="38-39" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p61 -->
> **<u>Acts 2:38-39</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Then Peter said to them, "Repent, and let every one of you be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of sins; and you will receive the gift of the Holy Spirit.
>

<!-- p63 -->
###  **2. Pray, seek, and receive in faith**

<!-- p64 -->
Scripture emphasizes that the baptism in the Holy Spirit is asked for in prayer and received through faith-filled expectation. Faith, combined with a willingness to ask and to receive, is the key to experiencing the baptism of the Spirit. If you're not sure whether God wants to give you the baptism of the Holy Spirit, ask Him about it—He is a good Father! If He prompts you, you may feel a tangible presence of God in your body that you've never felt before. Even if not, that's okay! God is a great God, and He doesn't have to be confined to any perfect formula. If you sense that God is drawing your heart, yield yourself to Him!

## Gifts of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p79 -->
When the Holy Spirit is poured out, and people are baptized in the Holy Spirit, it is often accompanied by manifestations of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p80 -->
There are several lists of gifts in the New Testament:

<!-- p81 -->
- God-given motivational gifts: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:6-8</span>

<!-- p82 -->
- Discussed in the chapter on the church.

<!-- p83 -->
- Jesus-given fivefold ministry gifts: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-13</span>

<!-- p84 -->
- Discussed in the chapter on the church.

<!-- p85 -->
- Manifestation gifts given by the Holy Spirit: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span>

<!-- p86 -->
- Discussed here.

<!-- p87 -->
Each of these sets of gifts is given for different purposes and functions differently, but collectively they point to the **diversity** and **unity** reflected by the gifts within the **body of Christ**. Each gift plays a unique role in fulfilling God’s purpose.

<!-- p88 -->
### Purpose &amp; Operation of the Gifts

<!-- p89 -->
The primary purpose of the gifts of the Holy Spirit is to build up the body of Christ and glorify God:

<!-- p90 -->
- **Edification** - strengthening and encouraging believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
- **Unity** - increasing harmony and cooperation within the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:12-13</span>).

<!-- p92 -->
- **Evangelism** - demonstrating God’s power as a testimony to unbelievers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="43" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:43</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:6-7</span>).

<!-- p93 -->
- **Service** - meeting others’ needs through acts of mercy and generosity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:6-8</span>).

<!-- p94 -->
These gifts are not for personal glory, but for the common good (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:7</span>). They are most effective when exercised with love and humility, reflecting the character of Christ (1 Corinthians 13).

<!-- p95 -->
Paul emphasized that spiritual gifts should be exercised decently and in order (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:40</span>). This ensures that, rather than confusion or division, unity, peace, and understanding are increased in the use of the gifts.

<!-- p96 -->
- Prophecies should be evaluated (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:29</span>).

<!-- p97 -->
- When a message in tongues is given to the congregation, pray that there may be an interpretation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:27-28</span>).

<!-- p98 -->
- The gifts should always build up the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:12</span>).

<!-- p99 -->
This order does not quench the Spirit; rather, it ensures that the gifts operate in a way that honors God and benefits others.

<!-- p100 -->
The operation of spiritual gifts should always be grounded in love. Without love, even the greatest spiritual gifts are meaningless—Paul devoted an entire chapter (1 Corinthians 13) to explain this. Love provides the motivation and framework that helps use the gifts in ways that build others up, not tear them down.

<!-- p101 -->
Additionally, the gifts operate as the Spirit wills (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:11</span>). They cannot be manufactured or controlled by human effort—they are sovereignly given and empowered by the Holy Spirit, often at the moment of need or service. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 13:8</span> says that ultimately prophecy and tongues will cease. Theologically, those who believe that the gifts of the Holy Spirit have ceased are called ‘cessationists.’ <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 13:10</span> says “when the prefect [Jesus] comes, that wish is in part will be cause to cease”。 Since Jesus has not yet returned for His church, the gifts are still active on earth.

<!-- p102 -->
It is critically important to recognize that the gifts of the Spirit should not be separated from the fruit of the Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-23</span>). Gifts display power, but fruit reveals character—love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control.

<!-- p103 -->
- Gift without fruit: A believer may operate in the gifts yet lack maturity or character, which can lead to pride, misuse, or harm.

<!-- p104 -->
- Fruit without gifts: A believer may have godly character but lack empowerment to minister effectively.

<!-- p105 -->
Both are necessary. The gifts equip believers for service. The fruit is a sign of their spiritual health (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 7:16</span>) and provides a ‘pleasant taste’ to those who receive it.

<!-- p106 -->
### Explanation of the 9 manifestation gifts of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p107 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span> lists the 9 manifestation gifts of the Holy Spirit:

<!-- p108 -->
1.  **Word of Wisdom (Word of Wisdom)** - A supernatural insight into God’s purpose or direction in a situation. It applies divine wisdom to complex problems or decisions (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:10</span>).

<!-- p109 -->
2.  **Word of Knowledge (Word of Knowledge)** - A supernatural revelation of information about a person, event, or situation that could not be known naturally (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 4:17-18</span>).

<!-- p110 -->
3.  **Faith** - The supernatural ability to trust God even in impossible circumstances. This gift inspires courage and confidence (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 3:6-7</span>).

<!-- p111 -->
4.  **Gifts of Healings** - The supernatural power to heal physical, emotional, or spiritual ailments and restore wholeness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 3:6-8</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 9:17-18</span>).

<!-- p112 -->
5.  **Working of Miracles (Working of Miracles)** - The ability to perform miraculous works that defy natural laws, such as multiplying food or raising the dead (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40-41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 9:40-41</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 2:9-11</span>).

<!-- p113 -->
6.  **Prophecy (Prophecy)** - Declaring God’s truth or will under the inspiration of the Holy Spirit. It should be encouraging, edifying, and consoling (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>).

<!-- p114 -->
7.  **Distinguishing between spirits (discernment)** - The ability to judge whether a spiritual influence is from God, from the enemy, or from a human source (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 16:16-18</span>).

<!-- p115 -->
8.  **Various kinds of tongues** - Speaking in a language unknown to the speaker, often for worship, prayer, or as a sign to unbelievers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:4</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:22</span>).

<!-- p116 -->
9.  **Interpretation of tongues** - The ability to interpret a message spoken in tongues so that the church may be built up (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:27</span>).

<!-- p117 -->
Paul makes it clear that all the gifts are valuable and should work together for the common good of the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:7</span>). However, he encourages believers to desire the greater gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:31</span>). The greater gifts are often understood to be those that most directly edify the church, such as prophecy (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:1-5</span>).

<!-- p118 -->
- Prophecy receives particular emphasis because it strengthens, encourages, and comforts others (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>). Paul instructs believers to earnestly desire this gift, because it promotes clarity and understanding in the church.

<!-- p119 -->
- Gifts like tongues are extremely valuable for personal edification and prayer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:4</span>), but Paul teaches that when a message in tongues is given to the church, one should pray for interpretation so that it edifies the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:27-28</span>).

<!-- p120 -->
Although the Holy Spirit gives the gifts, we must surrender to and obey the Holy Spirit in order to use them. Like muscles and talents, as we practice surrendering to the Holy Spirit in using His gifts, we become more confident and dependable in using them.
