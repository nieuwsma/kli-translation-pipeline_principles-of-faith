# Chapter 10 - Sanctified-Prepared for the Purpose of God

<!-- p1 -->
In the previous two chapters, we addressed several important topics. We explored the role and ministry of the Holy Spirit, as well as the role and ministry of the Church.

<!-- p2 -->
We have a role to play in the Church, and if we know that we need training, we also need purification. To be truly prepared for God’s purposes, we must be sanctified.

<!-- p3 -->
The definition of **sanctification** is to purify something for a particular purpose. This is closely related to the term **consecration** which means to set something apart for a particular purpose.

<!-- p4 -->
Imagine a master sculptor preparing to work a large block of marble. The master envisions how this raw, unshaped marble can be transformed by his careful work. He has a very precise design in mind; he orders his assistants to set this block of marble apart for a special design (**consecration**). As he refines and works the marble, adding texture, depth, and form, he purifies it (**sanctifies it**) for this special design. The act of consecrating the marble to his special project was quick, whereas the process of shaping the marble into its final design requires considerable patience.

<!-- p5 -->
We were set apart, consecrated to God at the moment of salvation. By the act of purifying our souls with the help of the Spirit, we are transformed. This process of purification is what sanctifies us.

<!-- p6 -->
When we received Jesus through salvation, we were born again. We were justified, our sins forgiven. Our spirit was regenerated; we became alive in Christ. We were adopted into the family of God and became the very temple of the Holy Spirit. Such a wonderful transformation that the Scriptures say, “*there is joy in the presence of the angels of God over one sinner who repents.*” (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 15:10</span>).

<!-- p7 -->
Receiving Jesus through salvation (**justification**) saves our spirit, purifies us, and makes us presentable before God, able to receive His Holy Spirit. However, despite such a great salvation, **our souls** (our mind, our will, and our emotions) still **need work**!

<!-- p8 -->
Was the work of salvation incomplete? No! The work of salvation is effective but must continue in the life of the believer through the renewal brought by the Holy Spirit. This process of renewal and transformation is the work of **sanctification.**

<!-- p9 -->
We know there is work left to do after conversion because our painful memories have not been resolved, many of our beliefs have remained the same, and many of our habits or rituals have not disappeared. Moreover, conversion has not separated our thoughts from their fleshly (carnal) nature.

<!-- p10 -->
The word 'carnal' comes from the Latin root meaning 'flesh' or 'meat'. The carnal nature is in each of us from birth; like an animal’s instincts, it exists to help us survive. The way the carnal nature operates is to be self-centered, self-directed, ego-centered. Children are natural examples of a carnal nature. Despite their innocence, their nature is mainly focused on their wants, their desires, their needs; and 'watch out!' if you do not meet all their expectations.

<!-- p11 -->
Growing up consists in part of outgrowing our childish ways, but under pressure we very easily return to a self-centered pattern of thinking and living. As James says:

<!-- p12 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p13 -->
> **<u>James 4:1</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Where do wars and quarrels come from among you? Do they not come from your passions that wage war in your members?
>

<!-- p14 -->
</span>

<!-- p15 -->
Paul explains this carnal nature in more detail in his epistle to the Romans (chapters 6 - 8). He says that this carnal nature keeps him doing what he hates and drives him to do evil. He describes this inner conflict as a war between his nature shaped by man and his spirit shaped by God.

<!-- p16 -->
This conflict between the flesh and the spirit exists in each of us, and it is up to us to act. If we do not confront our carnal nature, our souls will remain entangled in patterns of spiritual decay, even if our spirits have been redeemed.

<!-- p17 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p18 -->
> **<u>Romans 8:6-8</u>** (NKJV)
>
> For the mind of the flesh is death; but the mind of the Spirit is life and peace.
>
> Because the mind of the flesh is enmity against God; for it does not submit to the law of God, nor indeed can it.
>
> So then, those who are in the flesh cannot please God.
>

<!-- p19 -->
</span>

<!-- p20 -->
These words of Paul are both a warning and a promise for us. Even if we are born again, we still have a carnal mind that must be brought to submit to the knowledge of Christ. In doing so, we walk in the life and peace of God.

<!-- p21 -->
Take courage, there is hope for us! We must walk according to the Spirit and be led by the Spirit of God. In doing so, we will not walk according to the flesh.

<!-- p22 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="1,13,14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p23 -->
> **<u>Romans 8:1,13,14</u>** (NKJV)
>
> There is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus, who do not walk according to the flesh, but according to the Spirit.
>
> For if you live according to the flesh you will die; but if by the Spirit you put to death the deeds of the body, you will live.
>
> For as many as are led by the Spirit of God, these are sons of God.
>

<!-- p24 -->
</span>

<!-- p25 -->
How do we learn to walk by the Spirit? Is it *just a conscious choice*? We learn to walk by the Spirit in the same way we learn to walk in the natural. We are motivated to move and grow, so we struggle, we flail, we crawl, we wobble, until we can stand on our own two feet. Our natural parents cannot walk in our place, but they can support us, encourage us, help us, and enable us in our walking.

<!-- p26 -->
Likewise, God cannot put our faith into practice for us. He will teach us, but we must exert effort. As Paul said to the Philippians:

<!-- p27 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="12-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p28 -->
> **<u>Philippians 2:12-13</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Therefore, my beloved, as you have always obeyed, not as in my presence only, but now much more in my absence, work out your own salvation with fear and trembling;
>
> for it is God who works in you both to will and to do for His good pleasure.
>

<!-- p29 -->
</span>

<!-- p30 -->
We must work out our own salvation. This does not mean that we climb onto a wooden cross to be physically crucified, but we must crucify our carnal nature (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:24</span>). God promises that He will work in us and help us, but we must choose to put this into practice, and stick with it.

<!-- p31 -->
It is true to say that “we” deal with our carnal nature, but it is also true to say that “God” deals with it. The work of the cross (justification) was hard for Jesus, He had to sacrifice His life for ours, but it was easy for us, it is a free gift. The work of the Spirit (sanctification) is easy for God, He walks in perfect truth and is willing and desirous to abide with us, but it is hard for us, because we must crucify our old nature, and our nature is not very interested in being crucified.

<!-- p32 -->
This process is collaborative. God helps, assists, guides, directs, and corrects. We listen, obey, follow, and respond. The result is growth and transformation. This is the pruning that Jesus said God would do in us. Jesus is the vine, His Father the vinedresser, we are the branches:

<!-- p33 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p34 -->
> **<u>John 15:1-4</u>** (NKJV)
>
> "I am the true vine, and My Father is the vinedresser.
>
> Every branch in Me that does not bear fruit He takes away; and every branch that bears fruit He prunes, that it may bear more fruit.
>
> You are already clean because of the word which I have spoken to you.
>
> Abide in Me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself, unless it abides in the vine, neither can you, unless you abide in Me.
>

<!-- p35 -->
</span>

<!-- p36 -->
Pruning is incredibly beneficial for a plant. Thinking back to our chapter on repentance, we are called to turn away from dead works (those works that no longer bear fruit) and to turn to God. The result of this turning away from the dead places of our lives, and toward God, allows the great gardener of our lives to prune branches, improve growth, and produce more abundant spiritual fruit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:2</span>).

<!-- p37 -->
Although pruning may seem painful, it reflects God’s loving care as He shapes us into vessels for His purpose (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 12:11</span>). Some may feel a sense of loss, even fear, as they go through the pruning process. However, God is gentle, and even a bruised reed He will not break (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="42" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 42:3</span>). Vines are pruned during their dormant season in order to minimize stress on the plant. The gardener who watches over growth will cut the old woody shoots that no longer produce fruit as before; this allows the vine to direct energy toward the branches with the most potential for good fruit.

<!-- p38 -->
Like a gardener who prunes branches to direct growth, God may allow seasons of correction and discipline to refine our faith and character, but His work is patient and kind. God prunes those who abide in Him. Our focus should be more on abiding in Christ, and less on the work of pruning.

<!-- p39 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 12:11</span> reminds us that pruning “*produces the peaceable fruit of righteousness*” for those who are trained by it.

<!-- p40 -->
How are we shaped by righteousness? By being transformed by the renewal of our mind.

<!-- p41 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p42 -->
> **<u>Romans 12:1-2</u>** (NKJV)
>
> I therefore exhort you, brothers, by the mercies of God, to present your bodies as a living sacrifice, holy, pleasing to God, which is your reasonable service.
>
> Do not be conformed to this present age, but be transformed by the renewal of your mind, so that you may discern what is the will of God—what is good, pleasing, and perfect.
>

<!-- p43 -->
</span>

<!-- p44 -->
Paul sets forth two simple requirements for transformation:

<!-- p45 -->
<u>1. Present your bodies to God as a living sacrifice</u>. A living sacrifice does not physically die during the sacrifice, but since a living sacrifice cannot die, you must continually bring it onto the altar!

<!-- p46 -->
<u>2. Renew your mind!</u> Renewal, based on the Greek word: **Anakainōsis** (*An-ak-ah'-ee-no-sis -* **G342**) means a renewal or a renovation, emphasizing an ongoing process of inner change and growth.

<!-- p47 -->
This renewal produces transformation. Transformation is based on the Greek word: **Metamorphoō** (*met-am-or-fo'-o -* **G3339**) which means to change into another form; to transform or transfigure, emphasizing a complete change. Metamorphoō is the origin of the English word metamorphosis, which is the process by which caterpillars become butterflies.

<!-- p48 -->
God wants to fundamentally transform our thoughts and our way of thinking. His desire is that we not be conformed to the way of the world, but that we know what is good (beneficial), pleasing (acceptable), and the perfect will (complete and lacking nothing) of God.

<!-- p49 -->
The requirements for transformation are indeed that simple, although it does take discipline!

<!-- p50 -->
Our fight against the flesh will not be won by our flesh, but by God’s equipment for the battle of the spirit. His words (the sword of truth) are powerful to correct every imagination, every lofty thought, and to place under arrest (captivity) every thought that opposes obedience to God.

<!-- p51 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="3-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p52 -->
> **<u>2 Corinthians 10:3-6</u>** (NKJV)
>
> For though we walk in the flesh, we do not fight according to the flesh.
>
> For the weapons of our warfare are not carnal, but mighty in God for pulling down strongholds,
>
> casting down arguments and every high thing that exalts itself against the knowledge of God, and bringing every thought into captivity to the obedience of Christ,
>
> being ready also to punish all disobedience, when your obedience is complete.
>

<!-- p53 -->
</span>

<!-- p54 -->
When the authorities arrest someone, they put handcuffs on them and place them in a cell. If that person escapes their custody, they do not turn a blind eye; they will mobilize considerable resources and will not rest until that person has been brought back under control. We too should act with such zeal in the fight for our thoughts!

<!-- p55 -->
We are renewed by obedience and the experience of the truth. Jesus said that if we abide in his words, we would be his disciples, we would experience (<u>know</u>) the truth, and it would make us free (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-32" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 8:31-32</span>)!

<!-- p56 -->
Experiencing the truth is different from knowing the truth. Experience is the Greek word **Ginōskō** (Ghin-oce'-ko **- G1097**); it means to know by personal experience or an intimate understanding; it implies progressive learning.

<!-- p57 -->
It is not our academic knowledge of the truth that transforms us and renews our mind; it is our personal experience of the truth. We are sanctified by his truth! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="17" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 17:17</span>).

## How to renew your mind

<!-- p59 -->
Renewing our mind is not a one-size-fits-all process - it depends on which part of the mind we are talking about! God designed our mind with incredible complexity, comprising several different memory systems, each fulfilling a specific purpose. Some operate automatically in the background, while others require focused and intentional thought. These systems are gifts from God to help us survive, grow, and thrive, but they can also lead us astray when misused or left unrenewed. Understanding how these systems function gives us insight into how to cooperate with God’s process of transformation.

<!-- p60 -->
In the following section, I will describe each of the memory systems, their purpose and function, some practical steps to renew this aspect of your mind, as well as resources to continue practice and study.

<!-- p61 -->
### **Memory systems and how to renew them**

<!-- p62 -->
#### **1. Explicit memory (declarative memory)**

<!-- p63 -->
**Purpose :** Stores facts, events, and experiences that we can consciously remember. It is divided into two types-**semantic** and **episodic**.

<!-- p64 -->
**Semantic memory - Facts and knowledge.** Semantic memory is where we store facts, definitions, and general knowledge-things like historical dates, Bible verses, and practical information. It helps us predict outcomes, make decisions, and avoid danger (e.g., recognizing toxic plants). While it brings us stability and order, it can also lead to rigidity-an excessive reliance on rules rather than adapting to unexpected situations.

<!-- p65 -->
**How to renew it :**

<!-- p66 -->
- **Study the Scriptures** to shape our worldview (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="119" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="105" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 119:105</span>).

<!-- p67 -->
- **Memorize truths** and doctrines to stand firm in the faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 3:16</span>).

<!-- p68 -->
- **Read Christian books** such as *"Renewing the Mind" by Casey Treat* or *"Battlefield of the Mind" by Joyce Meyer* to develop godly thought patterns.

<!-- p69 -->
**Episodic memory - Personal experiences.** Episodic memory stores our personal experiences, linking them to specific moments and places-like a wedding day or a traumatic event. These memories provide context, helping us learn from the past and build relationships. However, episodic memory is subject to emotional biases and distortions. Past traumas can create fearful associations, making it difficult to move forward.

<!-- p70 -->
**How to renew it :**

<!-- p71 -->
- **Reflect and confess** in order to bring hidden pain into God’s light (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 1:9</span>).

<!-- p72 -->
- **Recontextualize past experiences** in the light of God’s grace (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:28</span>).

<!-- p73 -->
- **Seek inner healing** through prayer and counseling (*“Your Pain is Showing” by Larry F. Low, "The Bondage Breaker" by Neil T. Anderson*, *"Emotionally Healthy Spirituality" by Peter Scazzero*).

<!-- p74 -->
#### **2. Implicit memory (non-declarative memory)**

<!-- p75 -->
**Purpose :** Unconscious memory used for habits, skills, and emotional associations. It operates automatically, helping us respond quickly to situations. It is divided into **procedural memory** and **emotional conditioning**.

<!-- p76 -->
**Procedural memory - Skills and habits.** Procedural memory allows us to perform tasks without thinking-like driving, typing, or playing an instrument. It is efficient, saving us mental energy and time. However, bad habits and negative patterns can also take deep root and be difficult to break.

<!-- p77 -->
**How to renew it :**

<!-- p78 -->
- **Practice spiritual disciplines** like prayer and fasting to build godly habits (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 5:14</span>).

<!-- p79 -->
- **Train yourself for godliness** through repetition and obedience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 4:7</span>).

<!-- p80 -->
- **Read books like** *"Atomic Habits" by James Clear* (practical) and *"Spiritual Disciplines for the Christian Life" by Donald Whitney* (biblical) to establish habits that honor God.

<!-- p81 -->
**Emotional conditioning - Emotional associations.** Emotional conditioning links sensory cues (such as smells or sounds) to feelings, triggering responses before we consciously process them. This system protects us, but it can also cause irrational fears or emotional reactions when past experiences are negative.

<!-- p82 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p83 -->
- **Pray for healing** from past wounds (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="41" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 41:10</span>).

<!-- p84 -->
- **Practice forgiveness** and free yourself from bitterness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-32" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:31-32</span>).

<!-- p85 -->
- **Replace fear with God's promises** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 1:7</span>).

<!-- p86 -->
- **Seek guidance in books like** *“Your Pain is Showing” by Larry F. Low, "The Bondage Breaker" by Neil T. Anderson*, “*Soul Care" by Rob Reimer* to overcome emotional triggers.

<!-- p87 -->
#### **3. Working memory (short-term memory)**

<!-- p88 -->
**Purpose:** Provides temporary storage for problem-solving and decision-making. It helps us focus, briefly retain information, and process tasks, such as remembering instructions or making quick decisions. Although powerful, it is also limited, prone to distraction, and easily overloaded, especially under stress.

<!-- p89 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p90 -->
- **Focus and meditate** daily on the Word of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalms 1:2</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
- **Renew your thoughts** through intentional reflection (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:2</span>).

<!-- p92 -->
- **Practice mindfulness** and simplify mental clutter (*"Habits of Grace" by David Mathis*).

<!-- p93 -->
- **Read** *"The Celebration of Discipline" by Richard Foster* to sharpen your focus through spiritual disciplines.

<!-- p94 -->
## Conclusion

<!-- p95 -->
The renewal of the mind is not a quick fix—it is a lifelong process of progressive transformation. Each memory system plays a role in how we think, feel, and act. By intentionally partnering with the Holy Spirit, applying Scripture, and developing godly habits, we can train our minds to align with God's truth. Whether processing past wounds, learning new habits, or staying focused in the moment, God has provided both **spiritual tools** and **practical wisdom** to help us walk in renewal and freedom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 10:5</span>).

<!-- p96 -->
Our world is filled with hardships, tribulations, and trials. Through the disciplined renewal of the mind, our souls are transformed, and we can live a life of freedom for God, filled with the fruit of His Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-25</span>).
