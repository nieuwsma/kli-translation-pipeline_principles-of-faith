# Chapter 9 - The Church &amp; our role within it

<!-- p1 -->
The Church is the way God manifests Himself on the earth - through His people, accomplishing His work. We are called to be His body; we are His hands and His feet to accomplish His work and His purpose. Without our efforts, the harvest will remain in the field and will not be gathered.

<!-- p2 -->
## Who is the Church?

<!-- p3 -->
The Church, ***Ekklesia** (eh-klee-SEE-ah - **G1577**),* is derived from two Greek words:

<!-- p4 -->
- **"Ek"**: "Out of."

<!-- p5 -->
- **"Kaleo"**: "To call."

<!-- p6 -->
Together, **Ekklesia** literally means "called out" or "assembly called out". In the ancient Greek city-states, it referred to a gathering of citizens summoned for a specific purpose, such as legislative or judicial meetings. It was a secular term designating an assembly of people.

<!-- p7 -->
In the New Testament, it is primarily used to refer to the community of believers in Jesus Christ. It can refer to:

<!-- p8 -->
- **The local Church**: A specific gathering of believers in a particular place (e.g., the church at Corinth, Philippi, etc.).

<!-- p9 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p10 -->
> **<u>1 Corinthians 1:2</u>** (NKJV)
>
> To the church of God which is at Corinth, to those who are sanctified in Christ Jesus, called to be saints, with all who in every place call on the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, both theirs and ours:
>

<!-- p11 -->
</span>

<!-- p12 -->
- **The universal Church**: The collective body of all believers across time and space.

<!-- p13 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p14 -->
> **<u>Matthew 16:18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And I also say to you that you are Peter, and on this rock I will build My church, and the gates of Hades shall not prevail against it.
>

<!-- p15 -->
</span>

<!-- p16 -->
The Church is the people, not the building or the place where they meet. **The called out of the world**: reflects the idea that Christians are called out of the world and set apart for God's purposes (e.g., <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:9</span>) for a specific mission and purpose (the Great Commission). The Church is technically any gathering of two or more believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="18" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 18:20</span>). In general, the Church organizes into regular meetings, with possibly a fixed place and time, but wherever two or more people gather in the name of Jesus, that is where the Church is!

<!-- p17 -->
The Church was established by Jesus and grew in the 1st century A.D. through the work of the disciples who followed Him. The Church that we have and experience today in all its local expressions across the earth is the direct result of the original work of Christ and His apostles. As believers, we must commit to a local church for fellowship, provision, equipping for ministry, and to accomplish good works!

<!-- p18 -->
To help us understand what the Church is, the Scriptures give us powerful images that show what it is meant to be.

<!-- p19 -->
## Understanding the Church

<!-- p20 -->
There are several fundamental metaphorical images used to depict the attributes of the Church:

<!-- p21 -->
1.  The Body

<!-- p22 -->
2.  The Bride

<!-- p23 -->
3.  The Temple

<!-- p24 -->
4.  The Family

<!-- p25 -->
5.  The Flock

<!-- p26 -->
6.  The Vine and the Branches

<!-- p27 -->
Each analogy highlights a unique aspect of the Church, but together, they reveal the Church’s identity as a unified and diverse community under the leadership of Christ.

<!-- p28 -->
### The Body

<!-- p29 -->
The Church is often described as the **Body of Christ**, emphasizing both its unity and its diversity. With Christ as the head, the Church functions as a living organism where every believer plays an essential and potentially varied role. Each member is endowed with unique gifts and contributes to the health and mission of the body according to its own function. This image highlights the interdependence of believers, specifically that the body must be connected to function, and that our function is collaborative, not competitive. When each part works together, the body grows and flourishes in unity and love (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:12-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:15-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 1:18</span>).

<!-- p30 -->
### The Bride

<!-- p31 -->
The Church is described as **the Bride of Christ**, reflecting an intimate and loving relationship with Jesus. Just as a bride is cherished and prepared for her husband, the Church is sanctified and cleansed by Christ’s sacrificial love. This metaphor captures the devotion and faithfulness expected of the Church, as well as the deep affection Christ has for His people. The imagery also points to the ultimate union between Christ and the Church, celebrated at the marriage supper of the Lamb, where the Church is presented in glory without spot or blemish (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="25-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:25-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 19:7-9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="21" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 21:2</span>).

<!-- p32 -->
### The Temple

<!-- p33 -->
The Church is described as **the Temple of God**, the dwelling place of His Spirit. Believers are described as living stones being built together on the foundation of the apostles and prophets, with Christ Himself as the cornerstone. This holy structure signifies the presence of God among His people and their role as a community of worship and prayer. The temple imagery underscores the importance of holiness and the Church’s collective purpose to glorify God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 3:16-17</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:19-22</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:4-5</span>).

<!-- p34 -->
### The Family

<!-- p35 -->
The Church is described as **the Family of God**, emphasizing relational unity and care among its members. By the Spirit of adoption, believers are made children of God and members of His household. This family imagery highlights the love, belonging, and shared inheritance believers have in Christ. It also reflects the call to treat one another with compassion and humility as brothers and sisters in the family of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:19</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:15-17</span>). Furthermore, through the image of the family, this depicts how the members of the Church are to grow into maturity, being taught and nurtured by spiritual mothers and fathers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:1-2</span>), so that we may move from infants drinking the pure milk of the Word to fully formed adults able to eat the solid food of the Word (Hebrews 5).

<!-- p36 -->
### The Flock

<!-- p37 -->
The Church is described as **the Flock**, sheep under the care of Jesus, the Good Shepherd. This image expresses the Church’s dependence on Christ for guidance, nourishment, and protection. It also highlights the responsibility of the leaders of the Church to faithfully shepherd God’s flock as undershepherds, reflecting the example of Christ’s love and care. The flock imagery speaks of trust, obedience, and the close relationship between the great Shepherd and His sheep (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:14-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:2</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>).

<!-- p38 -->
### The Vine and the Branches

<!-- p39 -->
The Church is described as **the Vine and the Branches**, illustrating the essential connection between believers and Christ. Jesus is the true vine, and believers are the branches who must abide in Him to bear fruit. This metaphor highlights dependence on Christ for spiritual vitality, growth, and fruitfulness. Without Him, believers can do nothing, but by abiding in Him, they glorify God and fulfill their purpose. Jesus will prune so that more fruit may be borne in our lives (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:1-5</span>).

<!-- p40 -->
## Roles of the Church

<!-- p41 -->
The mission of the Church, much like the role of the Holy Spirit, can be understood through three dimensions : upward, inward, and outward, each reflecting a unique aspect of its calling in the plan of God. These dimensions guide the Church in fulfilling its role as a worshiping, edifying, and missional community.

<!-- p42 -->
### Upward : worship and glorification of God

<!-- p43 -->
The first objective of the Church is to worship and glorify God. Corporate worship, where believers gather to exalt God together, is a visible reflection of his glory and a response to his greatness. As the psalmist declares: “*Worship the LORD in the splendor of holiness; tremble before him, all the earth*” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="96" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 96:9</span>). Beyond corporate worship, the Church is called to lead lives of obedience and sacrifice, offering themselves as “living sacrifices” in an act of spiritual worship (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:1-2</span>). This upward orientation keeps the Church centered on the majesty of God and aligns its priorities with his will.

<!-- p44 -->
### Inward : building up the body

<!-- p45 -->
The Great Commission commands us ‘*teach them to observe’*, this command goes far beyond formal teaching methods like this book. Discipleship (addressed in more detail in the chapter on discipleship) consists in shaping the disciple into the likeness of the discipline, as a parent raises their child. Good parents do not hand an encyclopedia to a five-year-old, challenging them to figure it out on their own! They model, demonstrate, correct, guide, nourish, love, care, and lead like shepherds.

<!-- p46 -->
Parents sacrifice themselves for their children because they love them, because they are part of the same body, because they share the same blood. As members of the body and family of Christ, we share the same body, we share the same blood. Communion reminds us, through its symbolism, that we have been united to one another by the sacrifice of Christ. The act of communion should be a point of unity, reminding us that we all need redemption, and that salvation comes only from God. In this unity, we see that no part of the body is greater than another, except for our head, Christ.

<!-- p47 -->
### Outward : reaching the world

<!-- p48 -->
The best-known part of the Church’s mission extends outward to reach the world with the Gospel. Jesus’ Great Commission calls his disciples to evangelize and make disciples of all nations (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19-20</span>). Strengthened by the Holy Spirit, the Church is to be his witnesses to the ends of the earth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>).

<!-- p49 -->
Jesus is the light of the world (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 8:12</span>). Light radiates in all directions from the source. His light shines through its manifestations : the Word, his Spirit, and us, his body. We are the light of the world ; through our lives we must radiate his light into our darkened worlds and manifest good works that glorify God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 5:14-16</span>). Practically, this means that the Church is called to defend justice and mercy, both spiritual and physical. As James says, our pure worship consists in caring for the vulnerable, the orphan and the widow (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:27</span>).

<!-- p50 -->
## The leadership of the Church

<!-- p51 -->
Across Christendom, there is a great variety of denominations, meetings, gatherings, and assemblies. There are very few universal rules regarding Church leadership, but the ones we have are very important :

<!-- p52 -->
1.  Jesus is the sole head of the Church. All serve him (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 1:18</span>).

<!-- p53 -->
2.  There are high standards for those who lead ; they are worthy of double honor, for they will receive a double judgment (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:17</span>).

<!-- p54 -->
2.1  They should be mature, not puffed up with pride, accountable (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:1-13</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 1:5-9</span>).

<!-- p55 -->
2.2.  They must be servants of all (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42-45" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 10:42-45</span>).

<!-- p56 -->
2.3.  They must devote themselves to the Word and to prayer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:4</span>).

<!-- p57 -->
2.4.  They are responsible for equipping the body for the work of ministry.

<!-- p58 -->
3.  There are no superior or inferior members in the body. The Scriptures that support this are :

<!-- p59 -->
3.1.  There must be no partiality between rich and poor (James 2).

<!-- p60 -->
3.2.  There is no division between Jew and Gentile, slave or free, male or female (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 3:27-28</span>).

<!-- p61 -->
The structure of the Church, beyond leadership rules, is important, but much of it is situational and a matter of tradition rather than being universally dictated. “<u>That's how we've always done it !</u>” is a common assertion; if you go back far enough, there was usually a good reason why it was done that way. This is generally neither good nor bad, but a matter of taste, needs, and availability.

<!-- p62 -->
A useful example of tradition as the foundation of structure is the family evening meal. The most important part of the family meal is that everyone is fed and that the meal is nourishing. The variations are endless : what they eat, how they eat, how they season their food, etc. The different combinations are generally acceptable and largely a matter of taste and preferences.

<!-- p63 -->
A Church of 10,000 members has different needs and capacities than a Church of 50. The pattern established in the book of Acts and throughout the canon of the New Testament was the model of **elders** and **deacons**. Although leadership structures may differ from one Church and denomination to another, this model ultimately represents the minimum viable leadership structure for the Church. Let us examine these leadership positions and how they are selected.

<!-- p64 -->
### Elders (Overseers)

<!-- p65 -->
Role and responsibilities:

<!-- p66 -->
- **Spiritual leadership** - Ensuring the oversight, guidance, and pastoral care of the Church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:2</span>).

<!-- p67 -->
- **Teaching and preaching** - Tasked with teaching sound doctrine and protecting against false teachings (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:17</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 1:9</span>).

<!-- p68 -->
- **Shepherd the flock** - Caring for the spiritual needs of the congregation, modeling humility and service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:2-3</span>).

<!-- p69 -->
- **Guard the Church** - Serving as watchmen, defending the Church against false doctrines and division (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29-31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:29-31</span>).

<!-- p70 -->
- **Equip the saints** - Training and equipping believers for works of ministry and discipleship (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-12</span>).

<!-- p71 -->
Selection process:

<!-- p72 -->
- **Appointed in every Church** - The elders were appointed by the apostles or their representatives, often through prayer and fasting (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 14:23</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 1:5</span>).

<!-- p73 -->
- **Confirmed by the Holy Spirit** - Their appointment was regarded as guided by the Spirit, recognizing God's call and gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>).

<!-- p74 -->
- **Tested and approved** - Candidates had to demonstrate maturity and faithfulness before being entrusted with leadership (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:10</span>). They cannot be new converts ! ((<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:6</span>)

<!-- p75 -->
### Deacons (Servants)

<!-- p76 -->
Role and responsibilities:

<!-- p77 -->
- **Practical service** - Focused on meeting material needs in the Church, freeing the elders to concentrate on prayer and teaching (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-4</span>).

<!-- p78 -->
- **Support ministry** - Helping with hospitality, care for the poor, and administrative tasks (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:3</span>).

<!-- p79 -->
- **Promote unity** - Addressing issues of fairness and care in order to prevent division (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-7</span>).

<!-- p80 -->
- **Examples of faith** - Serve as models of humility, faith, and service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:13</span>).

<!-- p81 -->
Selection process (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-6</span>) :

<!-- p82 -->
- **Recognized by the congregation** - Chosen on the basis of their reputation and spiritual character.

<!-- p83 -->
- **Approved by the leaders** - The apostles examined and appointed the chosen deacons by prayer and the laying on of hands.

<!-- p84 -->
- **Confirmed in service** - The deacons were first tested to prove their faithfulness before being officially appointed (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:10</span>).

<!-- p85 -->
### Qualifications for leadership

<!-- p86 -->
#### Qualifications for elders and deacons :

<!-- p87 -->
- Moral character - Both must be blameless, dignified, and demonstrate self-control (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:2</span>, 8).

<!-- p88 -->
- Family life - Both must be faithful in marriage (“husband of one wife”) and manage their household well (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:4-5</span>, 12).

<!-- p89 -->
- Spiritual maturity - Both must be tested and approved before their appointment (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:6</span>, 10).

<!-- p90 -->
- Reputation - Both must have a good reputation inside and outside the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:7</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
- Faith and integrity - Both must hold the faith with a pure conscience and put their convictions into practice (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:9</span>).

<!-- p92 -->
### Examples of structures

<!-- p93 -->
Building on what we have learned so far about the rules and structures of leadership, let’s examine two examples of church structure found in the New Testament, Jerusalem and Antioch.

<!-- p94 -->
### Church of Jerusalem

<!-- p95 -->
The church in Jerusalem was primarily led by the **apostles**, who focused on teaching, prayer, and spiritual oversight (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:42</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:4</span>). As the community grew, the need for an administrative structure became evident, particularly to address practical matters such as the care of widows and the distribution of resources. This led to the appointment of **deacons** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-7</span>). These individuals, chosen for their spiritual maturity and good reputation, took care of practical ministries in order to allow the apostles to devote themselves to their spiritual responsibilities. Over time, **elders** also emerged as leaders in the church in Jerusalem. For example, at the Jerusalem council (Acts 15), apostles and elders participated together in decision-making, showing a collaborative leadership structure.

<!-- p96 -->
### Church of Antioch

<!-- p97 -->
The church in Antioch had a more decentralized and more diverse leadership structure than that of Jerusalem. It was led by a group of **prophets and teachers**, including Barnabas, Saul (Paul), Simeon, Lucius, and Manaen (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 13:1</span>). The church in Antioch was also much more oriented toward reaching non-Jews, while the church in Jerusalem reflected its Jewish roots more. Although the New Testament does not explicitly mention deacons in Antioch, it is likely that practical roles existed given the church’s growth and its participation in sending relief to Jerusalem (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29-30" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 11:29-30</span>). The church in Antioch became a model for sending missionaries, with the leaders praying, fasting, and laying hands on Paul and Barnabas to send them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 13:2-3</span>). This demonstrates a structure that balances spiritual oversight (prophets and teachers) with missionary action and practical support.

<!-- p98 -->
Unlike Paul’s corrections addressed to the Corinthians and the Galatians, Scripture does not criticize the leadership structures of Jerusalem or Antioch. This suggests that these churches, under the guidance of the Holy Spirit, were free to adapt their gatherings to meet their own needs and circumstances within the framework of sound doctrine and apostolic teaching (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:42</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>).

<!-- p99 -->
Throughout these passages, certain terms are used that we have not yet defined or studied: apostle, prophet, evangelist. These gifts are part of the five ministries that Jesus gave to the church.

<!-- p100 -->
### The fivefold ministry gifts

<!-- p101 -->
Jesus established five distinct ministry gifts, often called the fivefold ministry gifts, to equip believers for service and build up the Body of Christ.

<!-- p102 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p103 -->
> **<u>Ephesians 4:11-13</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And He Himself gave some to be apostles, some prophets, some evangelists, and some pastors and teachers,
>
> for the equipping of the saints for the work of ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ,
>
> till we all come to the unity of the faith and of the knowledge of the Son of God, to a mature man, to the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ;
>

<!-- p104 -->
</span>

<!-- p105 -->
The five gifts given to equip the saints for the work of ministry are:

<!-- p106 -->
1.  **Apostles** - Pioneers and visionaries who lay foundations, plant churches, and advance the Kingdom of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:20</span>). Apostles embody boldness and faith, showing how to venture into new territory and take spiritual initiative.

<!-- p107 -->
2.  **Prophets** - Messengers and guides who proclaim God’s truth, bringing encouragement, edification, and direction (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>). Prophets inspire faith, call others to align with the Word of God, and demonstrate dependence on hearing the voice of God.

<!-- p108 -->
3.  **Evangelists** - Proclaimers of the Gospel who focus on seeking the lost and spur others to action in sharing Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:5-8</span>). Evangelists teach disciples to share their faith effectively, model boldness in witness, and inspire compassion for the lost.

<!-- p109 -->
4.  **Pastors (Shepherds)** - Guardians and nurturers who protect, guide, and provide spiritual oversight of the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>). Pastors teach disciples the importance of community, caring for others, and accountability within the body of Christ.

<!-- p110 -->
5.  **Teachers** - Instructors and trainers who clarify doctrine and help believers grow in understanding and applying the Word of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 2:15</span>). Teachers equip disciples to study Scripture deeply, apply it in their daily lives, and teach others effectively.

<!-- p111 -->
## Leadership and gifts

<!-- p112 -->
An often misunderstood aspect of leadership in the church is the distinction between the authority and responsibility of leadership and specific gifts.

<!-- p113 -->
This confusion centers on our use of words like teacher, pastor, prophet, apostle, evangelist. These words denote gifts. There is no **office** of « apostle » in the New Testament Church. There are people who do the work of an apostle, who have the gift or call of an apostle. Having the gift of an apostle does not confer authority or responsibility.

<!-- p114 -->
I am an experienced musician; I have played the saxophone for over 20 years. I played in high school and college and played semi-professionally for several summers while working with an evangelism team. Despite my skill, my gift, and my ability as a saxophonist, I do not have the right to walk into your church and stand on the stage with the worship team.

<!-- p115 -->
Likewise, some people are gifted to be a pastor/shepherd, but that does not make them « THE PASTOR » of the local church nor does it make them « your » pastor. The fivefold ministry gifts do not lead the church, it is the leaders (elders and deacons) who lead it. It is very common for leaders to possess specific fivefold gifts, but that gift strengthens their leadership, it does not create it. Likewise, it is common for people still in training, endowed with gifts from the fivefold ministry, to serve and aspire to leadership, but God is concerned first with character, then with gifts. We must all come to maturity, able to discern the Word of God, filled with the Spirit, and doing good works.

<!-- p116 -->
Every leader and every gift must first submit to being a disciple, a member of the body.

<!-- p117 -->
Jesus gave specific ministry gifts to equip the saints for the work of ministry. These are different from motivational gifts (addressed later in this chapter) or manifestation gifts (addressed in the chapter on baptisms). As disciples grow, the fivefold ministry gifts equip them for ministry, while elders and deacons provide oversight to ensure sound doctrine and solid practice.

<!-- p118 -->
## Your role in the church

<!-- p119 -->
So far in this chapter, we have addressed church leadership and the primary analogies for the church. Let us now build on the specific analogy of the *body* and consider a marathon runner.

<!-- p120 -->
### You are part of the whole

<!-- p121 -->
The Church, as the Body of Christ, depends on the collaboration of each of its members to accomplish its mission. Just as the body cannot function properly if a part is missing, the Church needs every believer to participate actively, grow, and serve. Every member of the body has a very important role. Being part of the body means staying connected. A body can survive with diminished abilities without a member, but the member itself cannot live when it is separated from the body. We must be an active part of the Church and not allow ourselves to be dismembered. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:24</span> says that we should gather together and not forsake the assembly.

<!-- p122 -->
To really work and function together you need fellowship ! You need to know others, love others and, ideally, appreciate others ! Build strong relationships and friendships and repair them when they are strained or hurt. When a joint is injured or inflamed, you have to work to rest it and/or set it back in place ; so that the dislocated joint does not break ! A limb that is broken and does not heal will eventually be separated, and you cannot be part of the body, alone.

<!-- p123 -->
We do not all have the same function. Some of us may share the Gospel from a stage, others around a dinner table. But we are ALL called to be ministers of reconciliation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 5:18-19</span>). We have all been brought into the priesthood of believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:9</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 1:6</span>). This means that we are all expected to be able to do the work of the ministry by the power of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p124 -->
Toes work with the feet, the feet with the ankles, and so on-each part contributing to the overall mission. Marathoners must train if they want to be able to finish the race. You need to be ready - not just by learning, but by actually putting into practice what you have learned. The Church, like a marathoner, thrives when each member plays its role in unison.

<!-- p125 -->
There are a great number of people in your life that only you can reach. The Pastor cannot be the only one trying to shepherd them, and the Evangelist cannot be the only one trying to reach them. God wants to use you to reach unbelievers by the power of His Holy Spirit. We all do the work of the ministry ; the Holy Spirit helps us.

<!-- p126 -->
### You need training

<!-- p127 -->
Believers must train and equip themselves for their unique roles in the Church, just as a runner strengthens specific muscles for the race. You will not succeed if you set out on a marathon without having trained, and not all training is equal, you must be adequately prepared for the race. Jesus gave specific ministry gifts to equip believers, not to do all the work themselves, but to empower each member to serve effectively in their unique role.

<!-- p128 -->
Training for a marathon will result in transformation. You need strength and you need to be transformed. It is our responsibility to submit to God and let Him transform us in renewal (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:1-2</span>). Transformation should lead you to maturity, enabling you to carry your own loads and support others. You should be able to bear A LOT of fruit. This will be addressed in detail in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p129 -->
Training is hard, sometimes it is uncomfortable. Sometimes, the transformation you have to go through, you would much rather skip it. But if you want to reach the goal, you must stay on the narrow path. At times, you may need warning, comfort, or support - all offered with patience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Thessalonians 5:14</span>).

<!-- p130 -->
Every great marathoner has a coach to train them, you will need a coach to help you. Of course, you have the Holy Spirit, but He often relies on His coaches, the mature men and women of God, in the Body of Christ with whom you are called to work alongside.

<!-- p131 -->
As individuals we must first and foremost submit to God and then, in that context, to a body of believers. Submission in the Church means honoring godly leadership (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 13:17</span>) and submitting to one another in humility (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:21</span>).

<!-- p132 -->
Submission can be difficult, especially when you have already experienced broken trust or failed leadership. It does not imply blind obedience or control, but reflects a spirit of reverence and cooperation. Worldly submission is demanded by those in positions of authority and can be demeaning and lead to resentment. Biblical submission, rooted in humility and discernment, strengthens the Church and fosters accountability. Be willing to listen to guidance and constructive feedback.

<!-- p133 -->
Now that we have laid solid foundations - you are a significant part of the body but need training - let us move on to finding where you fit.

<!-- p134 -->
### Finding your place

<!-- p135 -->
Finding your role in the Church involves seeking God's direction, understanding your spiritual gifts, and actively engaging within your church community. It is a journey of prayer, exploration, and growth, where you discover how God has uniquely equipped you to contribute to His kingdom. It is very important to note that your role in the Church will likely evolve over time and will probably not be the same throughout your life.

<!-- p136 -->
Here is a practical approach to help you find your place, they are presented as a series of steps for clarity, but you will likely repeat the same step several times during your walk with God.

<!-- p137 -->
#### Step 1 - Seek God's direction through prayer and the Word

<!-- p138 -->
Start by **seeking God's direction through prayer**. God is the ultimate source of your purpose and calling (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="29" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 29:11</span>). Pray regularly for clarity about how you can serve in the Church, asking Him to reveal your spiritual gifts and passions. Trust that He will direct your steps as you submit your plans to His will (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 3:5-6</span>).

<!-- p139 -->
**Study the Word of God** to deepen your understanding of the Church's mission and your role within it. Key passages such as 1 Corinthians 12 and <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-12</span> shed light on spiritual gifts and the diversity of roles in the Body of Christ. Reflect on examples of people in Scripture who discovered and fulfilled their role, such as Paul, Timothy, and Lydia.

<!-- p140 -->
#### Step 2 - Identify Your Gifts, Passions, and Skills

<!-- p141 -->
Identifying your **spiritual gifts** is an essential step. You may find it helpful to take a spiritual gifts assessment. These gifts are given by God to build up the Church. There are several categories of gifts listed in the New Testament:

<!-- p142 -->
- **Motivational gifts** given by God : <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:6-8</span>

<!-- p143 -->
- Addressed after this list.

<!-- p144 -->
- Fivefold ministry gifts given by Jesus : <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-13</span>

<!-- p145 -->
- Addressed earlier in this chapter.

<!-- p146 -->
- Manifestation gifts given by the Holy Spirit : <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span>

<!-- p147 -->
- Addressed in the chapter on baptism.

<!-- p148 -->
The **motivational gifts** reflect the way a believer is shaped to think, feel, and act. They motivate believers to serve in specific ways that build up the Church. Each gift complements the others, ensuring that the Church functions as a united body with diverse roles (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:4-5</span>). They function as grace-given abilities (Greek : ***charis** - khar'-ece - **G5463 -** an unmerited gift) which means they are given by God, not deserved or learned. Although the gifts come from God, a person who possesses a gift can become more skillful in its use.

<!-- p149 -->
The motivational gifts are:

<!-- p150 -->
1.  **Prophecy** - A God-given inclination to declare truth boldly, often with an emphasis on conviction and righteousness, guiding others to align with the Word of God.\*

<!-- p151 -->
2.  **Service (Helps)** - Responds to practical needs through acts of service and assistance to support others.

<!-- p152 -->
3.  **Teaching** - Explains and clarifies truth, helping others understand and apply the Word of God.

<!-- p153 -->
4.  **Exhortation (Encouragement)** - Motivates, encourages, and comforts others to inspire growth and perseverance.

<!-- p154 -->
5.  **Generosity** - Provides resources generously to meet needs and advance the work of God without seeking recognition.

<!-- p155 -->
6.  **Leadership (Administration)** - Organizes and leads others to achieve goals with vision and effectiveness.

<!-- p156 -->
7.  **Mercy** - Shows compassion and care to comfort and support those who are suffering or in need.

<!-- p157 -->
\* The prophecy used in the context of Romans is understood as different from prophecy as a manifestation gift of the Holy Spirit (1 Corinthians). The Greek word used is the same in both Scriptures, but the context is different. For further study of the difference, I recommend reading:

<!-- p158 -->
- *The Gift of Prophecy* by Kenneth E. Hagin ([www.rhema.org](http://www.rhema.org))

<!-- p159 -->
<!-- -->

<!-- p160 -->
- *The Gifts and Ministries of the Holy Spirit* by Lester Sumrall

<!-- p161 -->
As you identify your gifts, consider your **passions and skills**. God often calls us to serve in areas that we are passionate about or in which we are skilled (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 31:2-6</span>). Reflect on what excites you about serving God and others, and think about how your professional skills, hobbies, and life experiences could contribute to ministry.

<!-- p162 -->
Seek the counsel of **leaders and mentors** who can provide wisdom and discernment (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 15:22</span>). Meet with a pastor or ministry leader to discuss your interests and ask for honest feedback on where you might best fit. Pray about the feedback you receive. Consider joining a discipleship group to receive ongoing training and support.

<!-- p163 -->
#### Step 3 - Be faithful now

<!-- p164 -->
The Parable of the Talents (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-30" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 25:14-30</span>) reminds us that we must be faithful in small things in order to be trustworthy with greater ones (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 16:10</span>).

<!-- p165 -->
**Get involved and serve** in various ministries to explore where God is calling you. Start small by volunteering in areas of need within your church and in the ministries it supports. For example:

<!-- p166 -->
- Join the welcome team, the children’s ministry, or evangelism programs. Consider facilitating a discipleship-oriented group for new believers around this book *Principles of Faith,* using the group discussion questions and end-of-chapter activities to facilitate dialogue.

<!-- p167 -->
- Be willing to step out of your comfort zone to discover new opportunities.

<!-- p168 -->
- Observe where God is working through you and where you feel the most effective.

<!-- p169 -->
Practice giving. We can give to the church in many important ways, and we are asked to do so in order to accomplish the mission.

<!-- p170 -->
- Time - we can give of our time and volunteer. When we give our time, we put our efforts and talents into action. This can also be an excellent way to learn new skills and to increase those we already have.

<!-- p171 -->
- Resources - we can give of our resources. Most often today, this probably consists of giving financially to the church and ministries (not only the “institution”, but those in need in the church). Giving money is helpful, because the church can purchase the necessary resources with it, but people have given their material resources beyond money as well, for example extra homes, clothing, cars, food, building materials, tools, etc. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="32-35" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 4:32-35</span> we see an example of the early believers giving freely, sharing the proceeds from the sale of extra land and homes, and giving so that ‘no one was in need’.

<!-- p172 -->
Working alongside others in ministry can reveal your strengths and deepen your understanding of your role. Get involved in small groups or church activities to foster connections. Listen to the burdens and needs of those around you and consider how you can support them. Building relationships within the church helps you connect with others and understand the needs of the community.

<!-- p173 -->
#### Step 4 - Get help!

<!-- p174 -->
Jesus said that in life we would have tribulations! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:33</span>) We are encouraged not to carry the weight of our burdens alone but to cast them on God who cares for us! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:7</span>)

<!-- p175 -->
Sometimes on our journey we can be hurt. Marathoners, even when they do “everything right”, can stress their bodies to the point of injury, or they can stumble and fall.

<!-- p176 -->
Not every ‘fall’ is related to sin, but some are, not every weakness is ‘our fault’, sometimes others sin against us. Whatever the cause of our weakness or our injury, we need to be restored and healed.

<!-- p177 -->
There is great freedom in seeking inner healing and restoration. We are called to cover the vulnerable parts of the body (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:23</span>) and to restore those who are caught in a fault (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:1</span>).

<!-- p178 -->
There is much to be said on this subject, and we explore themes of healing more broadly in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p179 -->
#### Step 5 - Be patient

<!-- p180 -->
Be attentive to confirmations from God and from others. Often, God confirms your calling through fruit, joy, and the affirmation of those around you. Reflect on the areas where your efforts have borne fruit and listen to feedback regarding the impact of the ministry. This can help you recognize where you are most effective in serving the Body of Christ.

<!-- p181 -->
Be patient and trust God’s timing. Discovering your role can take time and involve trial and error (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 1:6</span>). Trust that God will guide you as you remain faithful and open to his direction. During this period, commit to growth and training by participating in workshops, Bible studies, and other resources that equip you for service. Do it with the Church, engage with the Body of Christ to discover your gifts.

<!-- p182 -->
Remember, the Church is not a building or an institution; it is a living, dynamic body made up of people like you. As you step into your role, you contribute to its mission and glorify Christ as its head.
