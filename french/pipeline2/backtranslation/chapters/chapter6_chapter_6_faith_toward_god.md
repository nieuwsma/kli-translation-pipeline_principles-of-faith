# Chapter 6 - Faith Toward God

<!-- p1 -->
Faith is a foundational concept of Christianity. The word faith is used so commonly in Christianity that, sometimes, its meaning is supplanted by subtle misconceptions and misunderstanding. But this is not something you can hold in your hands-this is something that is formed in your heart as you come to trust God. Persuasion is belief.

<!-- p2 -->
The author of the Epistle to the Hebrews teaches us that we must have « faith toward God ».

### Defining Faith

<!-- p10 -->
Faith is a commonly misunderstood and misrepresented aspect of Christianity. As the saying goes, “there is a ditch on both sides of the road”! Because the word faith is so often used without adequate explanation in our modern Christianity, it can come to gather erroneous definitions amplified by misunderstanding. Faith is often presented either as a blanket statement of abstract belief, or as an almost mystical power or “magic,” something you possess in a specific, measured, and limited quantity.

<!-- p11 -->
#### What Faith Is Not

<!-- p12 -->
Faith represented <u>abstractly</u> has no specific conviction or persuasion. This often becomes evident with a little exploration, as this representation of faith cannot be made specific. If you ask, ‘what do you believe’ or ‘why do you believe,’ the person with this understanding of faith often cannot clarify beyond ‘I believe.’ <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 3:15</span> says that we should be ready to give an answer concerning the hope that is in us.

<!-- p13 -->
Sometimes people regard faith as a magical power-but that is not what Scripture teaches. Faith is about a real relationship founded on trust. In Scripture, faith is much more dynamic and relational-it is a reality rooted in persuasion, trust, and confidence in God’s character and promises. It is not an impersonal force, but a committed response to God that shapes thoughts, feelings, and actions.

<!-- p14 -->
Why is belief relevant to persuasion?

<!-- p15 -->
Our beliefs shape the way we see and interact with the world around us. Belief shapes perception and precipitates our actions. This is how our minds are wired-we live according to what we believe is possible.

<!-- p16 -->
We act according to what we perceive, understand, and believe to be possible. When we walk down a hallway, our brain detects the boundaries of the floor and wall and tells our feet automatically where we can step and what to avoid. This truth extends beyond our physical navigation of the world around us.

<!-- p17 -->
Suppose you believe that it is impossible for you to become financially independent or to live a healthy, active life. In that case, your actions will follow that belief and, true to that belief, you will not do, nor likely even try, what you believe is impossible.

<!-- p18 -->
If you believe that God has a loveless and punitive character, that perception of Him will keep you from walking with Him as His child.

<!-- p19 -->
#### Faith Is a Persuasion

<!-- p20 -->
So, what is faith ? Persuasion ! The Greek word ***pistis*** means persuasion, or the result of being persuaded. We must have persuasion toward God. In English, one could say faith (persuasion) ‘**in** God’. If you are married, you hopefully have faith ‘**in’** your spouse. You believe that he/she will do what he/she said he/she would do, and you believe that he/she is genuine and sincere. This trust should go beyond what he/she has said, to extend to his/her person in general.

<!-- p21 -->
Developing faith, persuasion, and trust in a relationship takes time and experience. Hopefully, over the course of your life, you have learned to develop a relationship of trust with another person. The principles you have learned, which allow you to increase your trust in someone you know, will also enable you to increase your faith in God. Faith in a relationship grows over time, through experience, with consistency, and through small and large actions that reinforce your trust in the other person's character and nature.

<!-- p22 -->
‘Faith toward God’ is relational. Relationships are built by two, not one. The good news concerning our faith in God is that God is responsible for His part of the relationship (made possible by the atoning death of Jesus, and the filling of His Spirit at the new birth), and He also helps us on our side (through the work of the Comforter - the Holy Spirit). God does almost all the work, our part (as we will discuss further in this chapter) is to walk *WITH* Him in obedience.

<!-- p23 -->
### Jesus matures our faith

<!-- p24 -->
Jesus is the author and the perfecter of our faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 12:2</span>). Our faith is designed to grow and continually expand. We do not reach a single point where we have enough ‘faith’, but rather our faith increases. Consider a deep and personal relationship you have in your life today. As in any relationship, we do not arrive at a specific milestone where, from then on, we ‘know’ the person. We continue to know them, and to increase our knowledge of them, and that refines, shapes, and grows our relationship.

<!-- p25 -->
How do we make our faith in God grow ? By continuing to experience Him through His many revelations, manifestations, and attributes. Again, consider a deep personal relationship in your life. You increase the depth of the relationship by engaging it in different ways, in different formats, through different media, in different contexts. You talk to them, send them messages, perhaps write to them. You see them individually, and in a group. You talk about what you value, and perhaps you challenge each other. The many ways extend endlessly. Through these multiple expressions, you come to know the person more deeply.

<!-- p26 -->
We should all learn to know God, through Jesus, as our savior. Think back to the names of God (*see the appendix),* just because we know God as savior does not mean we have already experienced Him as deliverer, healer, counselor, Lord.

<!-- p27 -->
If you are in a situation where you have been persuaded (faith) to know God as your savior, building this relationship will give you confidence in His character to know Him in other ways.

<!-- p28 -->
I am blessed to have several very deep relationships in my life. One of these relationships is with my wife. In our relationship, because of the level of trust (faith) we have in each other, my wife can tell me things she will do that I have not yet seen manifest, and I am confident to believe her because of her proven character.

<!-- p29 -->
This example shows how our deep relationship with God can be lived, with confidence and assurance ! We may not yet have fully experienced Him (indeed, we will never fully know Him during this life), but as we mature in our relationship, we can trust Him beyond the limit of what I personally know, and of what I have already seen happen.

<!-- p30 -->
Jesus increases our faith through experience with Him. An important way by which we can and should experience Him is through His word.

<!-- p36 -->
When we replace the word *faith* with *persuasion*, it reads :

<!-- p37 -->
*‘So persuasion comes by hearing and hearing comes by the Word of God’*. This truth is naturally self-evident. A major way by which someone comes to have persuasion is by having been persuaded (convinced), and we become convinced when someone presents an ‘argument’-or provides a reasoned and articulated explanation.

<!-- p38 -->
There are two Greek words for ‘Word’ in the Bible-**logos** and **rhema**. Understanding the difference helps us see how faith grows.

<!-- p39 -->
**Logos** : generally refers to the **concept, idea, reason, or the broader expression of the word of God**. It encompasses a universal and foundational truth or message and is often used to describe the eternal wisdom of God and Christ Himself as the living Word.

<!-- p40 -->
**Rhema (G4487)** : emphasizes a **specific, spoken word**. It generally refers to a particular word or message given at a specific moment, notably a spoken or revealed word that applies directly to a person’s situation.

<!-- p41 -->
*Logos* is like the entire message of a book, whereas *rhema* is like a particular passage or sentence that speaks directly to someone at a given moment. While the *logos* is the overarching truth of God, the *rhema* is the moment when a part of that truth is addressed to an individual in a personal and applicable way. Together, they illustrate the fullness of God’s communication: the *logos* as the broad and foundational truths of God’s character and will, and the *rhema* as the specific, applied word that makes that truth come alive in a person’s life and situation.

<!-- p42 -->
### An example of a rhema word in action

<!-- p43 -->
God promises revelation and wisdom to all who ask. When He speaks to us, that word is a *rhema* word. Rhema words come from God and serve to confirm, direct, and guide us. They do not replace the *logos* word, and they do not carry the authority of Scripture. *Rhema* words must be judged by the same standard as prophecy or the interpretation of tongues (see the chapter on the Holy Spirit for an in-depth study of this subject).

<!-- p44 -->
At several points in my career, I prayed for divine direction. Several years ago, I was considering beginning to look for a new opportunity and, during my prayer time, I heard God say to me, ‘*Wait for Me, I have an opportunity for you.*’ I was beginning to look for a new job because I was dissatisfied with my current role and felt frustrated. God’s word of direction, ‘*wait for Me*’, helped me to have peace and confidence in an otherwise turbulent season.

<!-- p45 -->
Three of four months passed, I continued to work hard and faithfully, keeping a positive attitude. God kept reminding me, *‘wait for Me’*, then His promise was fulfilled. I had the opportunity to be promoted and I received a generous pay raise.

<!-- p46 -->
I could have continued my job search ; I could have left. I could have started somewhere else. Without a doubt, someone else would have gotten that promotion! But because I sought the Lord’s counsel and was convinced by His **rhema**, I obeyed and received the benefit of it. God’s words are **living and personal**, and He promises to give us manifold and generous wisdom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:5</span>).

<!-- p47 -->
I have dozens, even hundreds of times, when I prayed and asked God what to do and He gave me a rhema word. Not all of them had the same spectacular result, but they all brought a sense of peace and confidence that I was obeying God and that He would work things out. These were the words I needed for the moment! Many times, when God brings a rhema word, it comes from the logos. He will bring to my remembrance a passage of Scripture, “Do not fear, for I am with you!” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="41" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 41:10</span>), “By His stripes we are healed” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="53" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 53:5</span>), “Let us not grow weary of doing good!” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:9</span>), examples abound!

<!-- p48 -->
Receiving a rhema word not only strengthens our faith, but also deepens our relationship with God, as we trust Him more in our daily decisions. We will continue our study of learning to hear God in the chapter on the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p49 -->
Our faith is also strengthened by what we see God do, but sometimes what we see in the natural world challenges what we hear from God. Reconciling the inconsistency between what we see and what we hear is an important ministry of the Holy Spirit, who promises to guide us into all truth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span>).

### A faith greater than what we can see

<!-- p51 -->
After His resurrection, Jesus appeared several times to many of His disciples. Thomas (one of the twelve) had not been with the other disciples, so he had missed the appearance of the risen Savior. Thomas believed in Jesus and trusted his fellow disciples, but he did not believe that Jesus had been bodily raised; he insisted that he could not actively believe it until he had experienced it personally.

When Jesus appeared to His disciples and calmed them (I imagine it is quite alarming when someone appears in the midst of you, having come through locked doors), He spoke to Thomas. He did not criticize or blame Thomas nor accuse him of being part of the evil and adulterous generation that seeks a sign (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 16:4</span>) but He revealed Himself to Thomas.

<!-- p60 -->
Jesus goes on to say, ‘*blessed are those who have not seen and have believed*d’. This is not Jesus encouraging a ‘blind or ignorant faith’ nor rejecting the use of empirical evidence. But He is saying that we are more blessed when we can believe without needing empirical proof. Why? If our faith, our trust, were limited only to what we had seen demonstrated empirically, we could never have any confidence in future work. We would have great confidence in what would have been accomplished, but we would not walk in the peace of God for our tomorrows and for our future.

<!-- p61 -->
Taking a relational example, how difficult would my relationship with my wife be if my attitude were constantly ‘I’ll believe it when I see it!’? Instead, a relationship founded on trust says ‘I know that you are a trustworthy person’.

### Faith produces obedience and good works

<!-- p80 -->
Our trust relationship with God is strengthened by obedience, whose result brings good works! These works do not save us and do not earn us God’s love - they are the natural result of a life rooted in Him.

<!-- p81 -->
Scripture presents a natural progression of faith. As we come to know God and experience His presence, we grow in our belief in Him. This belief spurs us to act in a way consistent with our faith, leading us to obey His commands. Our obedience produces good works, which yield a harvest of positive and beneficial outcomes.

<!-- p82 -->
Consider again this passage from Romans:

<!-- p83 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

Breaking this down in reverse order:

<!-- p87 -->
1.  Hearing the Word (**rhema**) of God produces faith (**pistis**)

<!-- p88 -->
2.  Faith, which is a state of belief or persuasion, will produce the action or verb of believing. Again, consider the person who believes they can be set free from their debts and acts accordingly!

<!-- p90 -->
3.  Our act of believing is demonstrated by obedience.

<!-- p91 -->
The word for ‘obeyed’ is **hupakouō** (**hoop-ak-oo'-o**- G5219): **hupakouō** means to hear under (as a subordinate), that is, to listen attentively; by implication to heed or conform to an order or authority: - give ear, be obedient to, obey.

<!-- p92 -->
In a workplace setting, when the boss or supervisor gives instructions, they are not “talking just to talk,” but giving directives they expect their employees to follow.

<!-- p93 -->
When we truly listen to God, it should lead us to action—to trust Him and obey. As the Scriptures say in:

<!-- p94 -->

<!-- p96 -->

The counselor will teach them the principles of financial freedom and help them set up a budget. This counsel will give rise to (trust / faith) in what they must do to be debt-free. But trust is only the first step; the person must obey the principles they have learned through disciplined practice in order to receive the good outcome and the blessing of being debt-free.

<!-- p98 -->
The book of James says that our belief (faith) must produce works! We already know what works are (for example, the ‘dead’ works from <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 6:1</span>).

We do not receive salvation by works! When we receive salvation (by faith) we do works! Let me say it again, you cannot work hard enough, nor make enough sacrifices to earn your salvation, it is the free gift of God that we receive by faith!

The result of these works (obedience) is that they produce a good, beneficial harvest. The time required to receive the harvest, or the measure of that harvest, varies, but it will produce a good harvest! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 8:22</span>).

A constant truth since the Old Testament is that obedience is better than sacrifice. It is preferable to rightly follow what we ought to do, rather than need to make a sacrifice to make up for what we did not do correctly. Thankfully, there is the grace of God when we stray from the path, miss the mark (sin), and need to turn back to God.

<!-- p108 -->
As we build our faith in God, it will naturally produce obedience in us. This obedience is for our benefit and produces a good harvest. To return to my personal example in this chapter, I would not have known peace in this season, nor the blessing of a promotion if I had not obeyed God’s word to me, to *wait for Him*!

### Practical steps to grow your faith

<!-- p153 -->
Walking with God is relational, but there are skills to learn if you want good relationships. In human relationships, the skills that produce strong and secure relationships are based on:

<!-- p154 -->
- Open and free dialogue and communication

<!-- p155 -->
- Healthy conflict resolution

<!-- p156 -->
- Commitment

<!-- p157 -->
- Consistency

<!-- p158 -->
You must be consistently present, reveal (communicate) yourself (our thoughts and emotions), welcome the other person (listen), and walk with them, if you want a strong and dynamic relationship! All these skills apply to our relationship with God.

<!-- p159 -->
Beyond basic relational skills, there are specific practices and disciplines that will strengthen your relationship with God:

<!-- p160 -->
- **Build and maintain a life of prayer, listen to and communicate with God.** Regular and sincere prayer is essential to a close relationship with God. Through prayer, you can share your worries, your joys, and your gratitude, seeking His direction and His strength. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 4:6-7</span>*) Being sensitive to the voice of the Holy Spirit helps you discern the will of God, find wisdom in decisions, and grow in spiritual maturity. Take time for quiet reflection in order to hear His direction. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:14</span>*)

<!-- p161 -->
- **Spend time in worship.** Worship is a powerful way to draw near to God, expressing love and reverence. Through praise, singing, or quiet reflection, worship deepens your awareness of His presence. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="100" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalms 100:2-4</span>*)

<!-- p162 -->
- **Read, study, and meditate on the Scriptures.** The Word of God reveals His character, His promises, and His instructions for life. Devoting time to the study of the Scriptures helps you grow in faith, understanding, and spiritual resilience. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>*)

<!-- p163 -->
- **Obey His commandments.** Obedience to God’s instructions demonstrates your love, your respect, and your trust in Him. Walking in obedience aligns your life with His purpose and brings blessings. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:15</span>*)

<!-- p164 -->
- **Participate actively in a local church and serve.** Engaging in a church community offers spiritual support, encouragement, and opportunities to serve others. Being involved helps develop a sense of belonging and strengthens your commitment to God’s mission. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:24-25</span>*)

<!-- p165 -->
- **Build relationships with other believers.** Fellowship with other Christians provides accountability, encouragement, and shared wisdom. These relationships enrich your faith journey and help you grow through mutual support. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="27" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 27:17</span>*)

<!-- p166 -->
Our « faith toward God » is not a one-time event for salvation, but an ongoing practice and relationship that we are meant to cultivate throughout our lives. The benefits of a trusting and beneficial relationship are incredibly numerous, but we must do the necessary work to foster that trust and that growth in order to see it flourish.
