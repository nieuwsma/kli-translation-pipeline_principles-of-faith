# Chapter 13 - Spiritual Authority

<!-- p1 -->
Jesus is the ultimate and complete source of spiritual authority. As the triumphant Christ, he has final authority over all things in the heavens and on the earth.

<!-- p2 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p3 -->
> **<u>Matthew 28:18-20</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And Jesus came and spoke to them, saying, "All authority has been given to Me in heaven and on earth.
>
> Go therefore and make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit,
>
> teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you; and behold, I am with you always, until the end of the world." Amen.
>

<!-- p4 -->
</span>

<!-- p5 -->
In this chapter, we will study the authority and power of Christ, which authorities and powers he has delegated to us (his Church), and how to operate in the authority of the name of Jesus. We will also study angelic and demonic beings and examine their authority, power, and natures.

<!-- p6 -->
Authority means having the right to act. Power is the ability to exercise it. Both are essential in spiritual life. Authority is different from power. Let us look at the following table that compares authority &amp; power and consider some examples.

<!-- p7 -->
[See Table 7](../tables/table7.md)

<!-- p8 -->
Here are some examples to help understand the difference between power and authority.

<!-- p9 -->
Example 1: At work, an employee may have the talents and skills to carry out a sale of the company's assets (power). This power is lawful if he also has the corresponding authorization (authority) from the owners of the company. Without this authority, selling the company's assets is theft.

<!-- p10 -->
Example 2: A police officer receives special training in weapons and close-quarters combat to learn to apprehend and physically restrain a criminal (power). Police officers have been given the professional responsibility, backed by laws (authority), to enforce criminal laws.

## The source of Jesus' authority

<!-- p74 -->
Now that we have examined precisely what authority is, we must know who its source is. Jesus’ authority is both divine and delegated. It comes from God the Father, is attested by his death and resurrection, and operates in perfect harmony with the Father’s will. Though absolute, his authority respects human free will, manifesting God’s love rather than coercive control.

<!-- p75 -->
Jesus’ authority is rooted in his divine identity as the Son of God and is conferred on him by the Father. The Father has entrusted all authority to Christ, including judgment and sovereignty over heaven and earth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 11:27</span>). Jesus’ authority reflects the unity and love within the Trinity, where the Son perfectly fulfills the Father’s plan (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="35" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:35</span>).

<!-- p76 -->
Although Jesus had inherent authority as the Son of God, his victory over sin, death, and hell was fully established by his death and resurrection. Christ’s obedience in his sacrifice led to his exaltation (seated at the right hand of the Father), securing his authority over all creation. By conquering death, Christ acquired absolute dominion over life and eternity.

## Angels and demons

<!-- p132 -->
### Angels

<!-- p133 -->
Angels are an important part of God's creation, serving as His messengers and His agents to accomplish His divine purposes. The Bible reveals a spiritual realm inhabited by these beings, who are powerful, intelligent, and obedient to God's commands. Although angels are often invisible, their presence and actions influence human history and spiritual warfare. Understanding their roles, classifications, and activities gives us deeper insight into God's plan and the unseen realities that surround us.

<!-- p134 -->
The term "angel" itself means "messenger," which highlights their primary function. Angels act in obedience to God's will, whether delivering messages, providing protection, executing judgments, or engaging in spiritual battles. They are not objects of worship but examples of obedience and reverence, always pointing back to the glory of God. This chapter explores the nature, classifications, and roles of angels, shedding light on their importance in Scripture and in our lives today.

<!-- p135 -->
In Scripture angels are seen as **messengers**, **servants**, and **warriors**. Angels frequently act as messengers, bringing important announcements, warnings, and encouragement. They reveal God's plans and provide direction in times of need (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26-38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 1:26-38</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 2:13</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 1:1</span>). Angels are actively involved in protecting, strengthening, and delivering believers. They serve God's people in times of distress and bring supernatural help (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="91" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 91:11</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 12:7-11</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 4:11</span>). Angels engage in spiritual warfare, fighting demonic forces to advance the kingdom of God and defend believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="DAN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Daniel 10:13</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:7-9</span>).

<!-- p136 -->
#### The nature of angels

<!-- p137 -->
Angels are created beings, brought into existence by God before the foundation of the world. They are distinct from humans and possess unique characteristics that equip them for their heavenly roles. The Bible teaches that angels are spiritual beings, not subject to physical limitations, but able to take visible forms when they interact with humans.

<!-- p138 -->
Angels are described as immortal, that is, they do not die or age. They are innumerable, vast armies filling the heavenly realms. Although they are often referred to in the masculine, Scripture clearly indicates that angels are sexless and neither marry nor reproduce. These qualities highlight their calling as servants of God, wholly devoted to His will and His commands. Angels possess remarkable traits that reflect their divine purpose. They are obedient, reverent, wise, and powerful. These qualities equip them to carry out God's will effectively and faithfully.

- **The Angel of the Lord** - Often considered a pre-incarnate appearance of Christ, manifesting with divine authority. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="23" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 23:20-23</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JDG" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Judges 6:11-24</span>*)

<!-- p142 -->
- **Archangels** - High-ranking angels such as Michael, who leads armies, and Gabriel, who delivers divine messages. (*Jude 9; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:7</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 1:19-26</span>*)

<!-- p143 -->
- **Cherubim** - Guardians of the glory and the presence of God, as in the Garden of Eden and the Ark of the Covenant. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 3:24</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 25:22</span>*)

<!-- p144 -->
- **Seraphim** - Worshipers of God, continually declaring His holiness. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 6:1-6</span>*)

<!-- p145 -->
- **Elect angels** - Faithful angels who resisted Satan’s rebellion and remain loyal to God. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:21</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 25:41</span>*)

<!-- p146 -->
These classifications highlight the diversity and organization within the angelic realm, but their primary purpose is always to accomplish God’s purposes.

<!-- p147 -->
#### Human interaction with angels

<!-- p148 -->
Humans often encounter angels in Scripture, but we are never instructed to command them or to address prayers to them. On the contrary, angels act according to God’s direction. They serve believers as **servants of God** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 1:14</span>). Their interactions include protection, guidance, and deliverance, always pointing us back to God’s power and sovereignty.

<!-- p149 -->
- **Warnings against the worship of angels**: Scripture forbids worshiping angels or seeking encounters with them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:18</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 22:8-9</span>).

<!-- p150 -->
- **Prayer to God, not to angels**: Believers should pray to God, who sends angels according to His will (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="91" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 91:11</span>).

<!-- p151 -->
- **Encouragement and trust**: Angels remind us of God’s provision, but our faith must rest entirely in God, not in them.

<!-- p152 -->
Angels are awe-inspiring beings created to serve God and to minister to His people. Although their work is often invisible, Scripture offers glimpses of their activity, revealing their faithfulness, strength, and reverence. Angels remind us of God’s provision and protection, but they always point back to His glory. As believers, we are called to trust in God’s care, knowing that angels act as His servants to accomplish His divine purposes.

<!-- p153 -->
### Demons

<!-- p154 -->
Just as the study of angels is necessary to understand the spiritual world, so is the study of demons. Scripture clearly describes their activity, their influence, and their defeat by Jesus Christ. Recognizing their role in spiritual warfare helps believers to stand firm in the faith without fear.

<!-- p155 -->
#### Origin and nature of demons

<!-- p156 -->
Demons are fallen angels who followed Satan’s rebellion against God. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:7-9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 25:41</span>).* Satan, formerly known as Lucifer (“bearer of light”), an exalted angel, sought to exalt himself above God and was cast out of heaven with other rebellious angels. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 14:12-15</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EZK" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ezekiel 28:12-17</span>).*

<!-- p157 -->
Demons are spirits without physical bodies, but can oppress and torment humans and animals. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 5:11-13</span>).* They seek to deceive, tempt, oppress, and destroy. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 11:14</span>). Unlike God,* they have limits and are not omnipresent (everywhere), omniscient (all-knowing), omnipotent (all-powerful)*.* Their final defeat is certain, and they will face eternal punishment. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 20:10</span>)*

<!-- p158 -->
The Scriptures describe the devil’s work and activity as:

<!-- p159 -->
- Opposes the work of God *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Thessalonians 2:18</span>)*

<!-- p160 -->
- Deceives the world *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 4:4</span>)*

<!-- p161 -->
- Tempts and accuses believers *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 22:31</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:10</span>)*

<!-- p162 -->
- Afflicts and harms *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JOB" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Job 1:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 13:16</span>)*

<!-- p163 -->
There are many names and descriptions of the devil, each describing a different aspect of his work.

<!-- p164 -->
[See Table 9](../tables/table9.md)

<!-- p165 -->
The Gospels and the Epistles further clarify the work of the enemy. Evil spirits, being disembodied, must for the most part carry out their work through willing and open instruments. God works through people; so does the devil. This oppression often manifests as torment, self-harm, and destructive behaviors *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 8:27-29</span>).* Their attacks cause fear, temptation, and discouragement *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 12:7</span>).* The most common source of their power (not their authority) in someone’s life is the influence of lies and emotional strongholds *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 10:3-5</span>).* 

<!-- p166 -->
It is important to note that every lie a person believes, every infirmity in their body, or every stronghold in their life is not necessarily the direct result of demonic influence, but these places of wounding are places where the enemy can exploit us and keep us in a state of frustration and oppression.

<!-- p167 -->
#### The Authority of Jesus over the Works of the Enemy

<!-- p168 -->
Do not be afraid, Jesus has complete and total authority over the works of the enemy. Jesus demonstrated his power over demons during his ministry *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 1:34</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 5:1-20</span>).* The works of the enemy, and Satan himself, are defeated by the death and resurrection of Jesus *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:15</span>).* 

<!-- p169 -->
As believers, we have received authority delegated by Jesus to cast out demons in his name:

<!-- p170 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p171 -->
> **<u>Mark 16:17</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Here are the signs that will accompany those who have believed: in my name they will cast out demons ; they will speak with new tongues ;
>

<!-- p172 -->
</span>

<!-- p173 -->
Moreover, as heirs of salvation, we have the authority to submit to God, to resist the devil, and he will flee from us! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 4:7</span>).

<!-- p174 -->
To deal with an evil or unclean spirit, the pattern expressed in Scripture is to take authority over it in the name of Jesus and command it to leave. The Holy Spirit promises to give us wisdom and discernment if we ask! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:5</span>). We do not need to make a spectacle of it, nor a drama like in the movies, nor even to talk to them, it's a scheme of the devil to distract us. The power of Satan is established and maintained only by the lies he manages to make us believe.

<!-- p175 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="18" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p176 -->
> **<u>Matthew 18:18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> "Truly, I tell you, whatever you bind on earth will be bound in heaven, and whatever you loose on earth will be loosed in heaven.
>

<!-- p177 -->
</span>

<!-- p178 -->
We speak to them in the name of Jesus and command them to leave.

<!-- p179 -->
There are two major cautions in Scripture when it comes to the demonic:

<!-- p180 -->
1.  **Only those who have a relationship with Jesus have authority over the demonic**. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 19:11-20</span> speaks of the sons of a Jewish chief priest named Sceva. They tried to intervene in the life of someone who was oppressed and tormented by the devil, and the evil spirit refused to leave because they had no relationship with Jesus. The man who was oppressed leaped on them, stripped them of their clothes, and beat them. Just as you should not pretend to be a police officer, **do not pretend to be someone who has a relationship with Jesus; rather, have a relationship with Jesus**!

<!-- p181 -->
2.  **When an evil spirit leaves, it goes away for a time and will look to return**. If it finds the place it inhabited empty, it will return worse. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="43-45" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 12:43-45</span>. When we minister to a person oppressed by the devil, that person must fill the void that this evil spirit occupied in their life. This means, at a minimum, establishing a personal relationship with Jesus and strengthening it through obedience to the Holy Spirit. Often, an evil or unclean spirit is able to operate in someone’s life through the choices they have made, based on their beliefs. Anyone delivered from the devil must replace that old belief system and be transformed by the renewing of their mind (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:1-2</span>).

<!-- p182 -->
We serve an extraordinary and powerful God, and we are ‘more than conquerors’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="37" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:37</span>) over the defeated enemy. We do not need to fear our defeated enemy, for Jesus has given us his authority to crush his head! The devil’s time is short, and he will ultimately be judged along with every principality and power on the earth that rejects the lordship of Christ.
