# Chapter 11 - Baptisms-Water and Spirit

<!-- p1 -->
In our modern era, separated from Jewish roots, baptism is generally considered only in terms of water baptism, and baptism of the Holy Spirit (Pentecost).

<!-- p2 -->
The Epistle to the Hebrews was written for a Jewish audience. The overwhelming majority of Christians in our time have neither Jewish roots nor heritage. Without Jewish roots and culture, many of us lack the cultural context that first-century Messianic Jews shared (those Jewish believers professing Jesus). Not having been raised in the Jewish tradition, we generally know little about the feasts, fasts, prayers, washings, rites, and rituals that Jewish believers would know.

<!-- p3 -->
The word for baptisms, or washings, in Greek is : **Baptismos** (bap-tis-mos’ - **G909**) - The act of immersing or washing. Refers to ceremonial washings or ritual cleansing. It can indicate immersion, purification rites, or washings practiced in Jewish and Christian contexts.

<!-- p4 -->
Baptisms are part of the foundational doctrines mentioned in Hebrews:

<!-- p5 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p6 -->
> **<u>Hebrews 6:1-2</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Therefore, leaving the discussion of the elementary principles of Christ, let us go on to perfection, not laying again the foundation of repentance from dead works and of faith toward God,

## Washings of the Old Covenant - Historical Context

<!-- p9 -->
In the Old Covenant, ceremonial washings played a central role in maintaining ritual purity and symbolizing holiness before God. These washings were prescribed in the Law of Moses and applied both to the priests who served in the Tabernacle and to the people of Israel. For example, the priests had to wash their hands and feet at the bronze laver before entering the Tabernacle or approaching the altar, symbolizing their preparation to serve in the presence of a holy God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="30" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 30:18-21</span>). Likewise, the purification rites for those who were defiled, such as contact with a corpse or skin diseases, involved washing with water as a sign of restoration to purity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LEV" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Leviticus 14:8</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="NUM" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Numbers 19:17-19</span>). These acts served as outward demonstrations of an inner desire for holiness, underscoring the importance of being set apart for God.

<!-- p10 -->
Ceremonial washings in the Old Testament required regular repetition to maintain ritual purity. Unlike permanent solutions, these rites offered only a temporary external cleansing, leaving unresolved the deeper issue of sin. The author of Hebrews highlights this limitation, explaining that these practices were “symbolic for the present time,” unable to purify the worshiper’s conscience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:9-10</span>). These washings acted as constant reminders of human imperfection and the need for something greater-a final cleansing that could wash not only the body, but also the heart and soul.

<!-- p11 -->
The ceremonial washings of the Old Covenant find their fulfillment in Christ, who provides a complete and eternal cleansing through His sacrifice. In contrast to the temporary rites of the law, the blood of Christ fully purifies, cleansing not only the flesh but also the conscience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:13-14</span>). Through His death and resurrection, Jesus accomplished what the Old Testament rites could only foreshadow-a true reconciliation with God. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:22</span> calls believers to “draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, having our hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience and our bodies washed with pure water.” This language connects Christ’s work to the imagery of Old Testament washings while showing its far superior effectiveness. His sacrifice does not merely cover sin, but removes it completely (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 1:7</span>). Thus, the ceremonial washings served as a shadow, pointing to the deeper cleansing that only Christ could offer, satisfying the need to attain holiness through divine intervention.

<!-- p12 -->
To reiterate this point, Christians have no obligation to perform any ceremonial washing. The washings did not provide salvation under the old covenant and do not provide salvation in the new covenant. A ceremonial washing will not increase our standing before God, nor obtain His favor for us. Paul vigorously condemns the Judaizers of Galatia (read Galatians) who attempted to introduce requirements from the Jewish context as necessary for salvation (e.g., circumcision). Salvation is by faith in Jesus alone!

<!-- p13 -->
## The Baptism of John

<!-- p14 -->
In the New Testament canon, several baptisms are mentioned. We have already addressed the baptisms and washings of the Jewish tradition.

<!-- p15 -->
The ministry of John the Baptist was centered on preparing the way for the coming of Jesus Christ. His baptism, described as a baptism of repentance for the forgiveness of sins (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 1:4</span>), called people to confess their sins, turn their hearts to God, and prepare for the arrival of the Messiah. It served as an outward sign of an inward repentance, symbolizing a desire for spiritual cleansing and readiness for the kingdom of God. However, John’s baptism was temporary and preparatory-it pointed to the greater work that Christ would accomplish. While John’s baptism focused on repentance, it did not bring complete forgiveness or spiritual regeneration, for it lacked the indwelling and power of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:11-12</span>). Instead, John openly declared that someone greater than he was coming, who would baptize not only with water but with the Holy Spirit and fire (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 3:16</span>).

<!-- p16 -->
John’s baptism was fulfilled and surpassed by the death, burial, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. Christian baptism, instituted by Jesus and practiced by the apostles, signifies a deeper reality-union with Christ, death to sin, and resurrection to new life (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 6:3-4</span>). Unlike John’s baptism, Christian baptism is directly connected to faith in the atoning sacrifice of Jesus and the indwelling of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:38</span>). The coming of the Holy Spirit at Pentecost marked the new covenant and the beginning of a Spirit-led life, which John’s baptism could not offer. For this reason, John’s baptism is no longer practiced, having been replaced by the baptism commanded by Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19</span}), which integrates repentance, faith, and the sealing of believers by the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>).

<!-- p17 -->
## The Baptism of Christ

<!-- p18 -->
The baptism of Christ gave a powerful example to His disciples and established the foundation of the Christian practice of water baptism. Though Jesus was without sin, He submitted to baptism by John to “fulfill all righteousness” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:15</span>). In doing so, He identified with humanity’s need for repentance and foreshadowed His own death, burial, and resurrection. At His baptism, the Holy Spirit descended like a dove, and the Father declared: “This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:16-17</span>). This moment marked the beginning of Jesus’ public ministry and revealed the unity of the Father, the Son, and the Spirit, anticipating the spiritual transformation that believers would experience through faith in Christ.

<!-- p19 -->
Later, Jesus commanded His disciples to be baptized, charging them to “go and make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19</span>). Water baptism, as instituted by Christ, became an **act of obedience** and a **public declaration of faith** for believers. It represents their identification with the death, burial, and resurrection of Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 6:3-4</span>). Going down into the water symbolizes dying to sin, and coming up out of the water illustrates resurrection to a new life in Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:12</span}). Yet it is crucial to clarify that this outward act is not the source of salvation but a **visible sign** of the **inner work of grace** already accomplished by the blood of Jesus.

<!-- p20 -->
Water baptism is a **symbol** of the purifying power of the blood of Christ and the renewal of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:26</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>). It signifies repentance, faith, and a commitment to follow Jesus. It is an outward testimony of an inward transformation-a way for believers to publicly declare their allegiance to Christ and their participation in the body of Christ, the Church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:41</span>). Although it is commanded by Jesus and observed in the early Church, it does not **save** a person. Salvation is by grace through faith alone (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>), and true cleansing takes place spiritually through the blood of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 1:5</span>). Water baptism is a response to this salvation, an act of obedience rather than a condition for obtaining it.

<!-- p21 -->
In the New Testament, water baptism is consistently connected to **repentance and faith** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:38</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 16:16</span>), both of which require a personal decision to follow Christ—something infants are incapable of. Baptism symbolizes the believer’s deliberate identification with the death, burial, and resurrection of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 6:3-4</span>), as well as their commitment to live in obedience to Him. Some denominations (e.g., Catholics, Lutherans) practice infant baptism as a covenant sign similar to circumcision; however, since infants cannot express personal faith or repentance, the practice of **infant baptism** does not align with the biblical pattern of **believer’s baptism**. Instead, many churches practice **dedication ceremonies** for children, following the example of Hannah dedicating Samuel to the Lord (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1SA" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Samuel 1:27-28</span>) and of Mary and Joseph presenting Jesus at the temple (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 2:22</span>). Dedication is not a substitute for baptism but rather a commitment by parents to raise their child in the faith, teaching them the Word of God and modeling a godly life until the child is old enough to make their own decision to follow Christ and be baptized.

<!-- p22 -->
Unlike the **ceremonial washings** of the Old Covenant, which required repeated rites for purification, Christian baptism is a one-time event that reflects a finished work of grace. The washings of the Old Testament symbolized external cleansing and foreshadowed the deeper spiritual cleansing that comes through Christ. In contrast, New Testament baptism symbolizes the inner renewal and new birth accomplished by the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>). It indicates the believer’s participation in the new covenant, where cleansing from sin is permanent and grounded in the finished work of Christ.

<!-- p23 -->
Water baptism is closely connected to the **promise of the Holy Spirit**. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:38</span>, Peter calls people to repent, be baptized, and receive the gift of the Holy Spirit. While water baptism publicly signifies the believer’s repentance and faith, the true baptism takes place inwardly when the Holy Spirit washes, regenerates, and seals the believer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:13</span>). This spiritual washing makes us members of the body of Christ and empowers us to live a holy life. The **role of the Holy Spirit** in baptism reminds us that salvation is not accomplished by rites but by the transforming power of God.

<!-- p24 -->
It is important to distinguish between **the symbolic act of water baptism** and the spiritual reality of salvation through the blood of Christ. The Bible clearly teaches that salvation is based on faith in Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 10:9-10</span>) and not on works, including baptism (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>). However, water baptism is an **act of obedience** that publicly identifies the believer with Christ and the Church. It displays an outward commitment to live as a disciple of Jesus, but it adds nothing to the work of salvation nor completes it.

<!-- p25 -->
The water baptism of Christ set an example for believers to follow, demonstrating humility, obedience, and readiness for ministry. For Christians today, water baptism serves as a public declaration of faith, symbolizing death to sin and resurrection to a new life. Though it is a command of Jesus and an important step of obedience, it is not required for salvation. True cleansing takes place through faith in the blood of Jesus, and the ability to live the Christian life comes from the work of the Holy Spirit in the believer. Baptism unites us to Christ and His Church, reminding us of the inner transformation and renewal that God has already accomplished.

<!-- p26 -->
Having established that water baptism is an outward sign of faith, we now turn to the baptism of the Holy Spirit, which empowers believers for ministry and service.

<!-- p27 -->
## Baptism in the Holy Spirit

<!-- p28 -->
Baptism in the Holy Spirit is a profound and transformative experience promised by God to empower believers for ministry, service, and a holy life. Every believer receives the Holy Spirit when they are born again—that is how we are made spiritually alive. But then, we should also receive baptism in the Holy Spirit, which gives us boldness, spiritual gifts, and effective ministry.

<!-- p29 -->
Water baptism is a public declaration of faith and symbolizes the cleansing of sin by the blood of Christ; the baptism of the Spirit represents God’s empowering presence in the believer’s life. Together, these two modes of baptism are complementary aspects of the Christian walk—one as an outward sign of obedience and the other as an inner source of power and renewal. The baptism of the Holy Spirit is the Spirit on us rather than in us. At salvation, all believers receive the Spirit within them, but when the Spirit of God comes upon them, they receive power and boldness for ministry.

<!-- p30 -->
Baptism in the Holy Spirit was announced by John the Baptist, who declared,

<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p32 -->
> **<u>Matthew 3:11</u>** (NKJV)
>
> I baptize you with water for repentance; but He who comes after me is mightier than I, and I am not worthy to carry His sandals. He will baptize you with the Holy Spirit and fire.
>

<!-- p33 -->
</span>

<!-- p34 -->
This promise pointed to a greater work that Jesus would accomplish after His resurrection—sending the Holy Spirit to indwell and empower His disciples. Jesus confirmed it when He commanded His disciples to wait for the gift of the Spirit, explaining that they would receive power to be His witnesses (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>). The fulfillment of this promise took place on the Day of Pentecost, when the disciples were filled with the Holy Spirit, evidenced by speaking in tongues, prophecy, and boldness to proclaim the Gospel (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:1-4</span>).

<!-- p35 -->
This outpouring marked the beginning of the **Church Age** and demonstrated that the Spirit’s power was now available to all believers. This promise was not limited to the apostles, but extends to all who believe, as Peter declared, *“The promise is for you and for your children and for all who are far off-for all whom the Lord our God will call”* (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="39" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:39</span>).

<!-- p36 -->
### Spirit in us vs Spirit on us

<!-- p37 -->
When someone puts their faith in Jesus, the Holy Spirit gives them new life inside. This is what it means to be born again (**regenerated**). At that moment of faith, the Holy Spirit dwells **WITHIN** the believer, making them spiritually alive and adopting them into the family of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:5-6</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>). This is the new birth, or being born again, as Jesus described in John 3.

<!-- p38 -->
Biblical foundation:

<!-- p39 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>* - “He saved us through the washing of regeneration and renewal by the Holy Spirit.”

<!-- p40 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>* - The Holy Spirit seals believers as a **guarantee** of their inheritance.

<!-- p41 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:9</span>* - “Whoever does not have the Spirit of Christ does not belong to Him.”

<!-- p42 -->
This work of regeneration is universal for all believers at the moment of salvation and establishes a permanent indwelling of the Spirit. It does not depend on any external action, such as water baptism, and guarantees salvation by grace through faith.

<!-- p43 -->
By contrast, the baptism in the Holy Spirit is a distinct and subsequent experience that equips believers with power for service and witness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>). Whereas regeneration concerns new life and identity, the baptism of the Spirit emphasizes power **UPON** and the deployment of spiritual gifts. This baptism is often accompanied by signs and manifestations of the gifts of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p44 -->
Biblical foundation:

<!-- p45 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p46 -->
> **<u>Acts 1:8</u>** (NKJV)
>
> But you will receive power when the Holy Spirit comes upon you; and you will be my witnesses in Jerusalem, in all Judea and Samaria, and to the ends of the earth."
>

<!-- p47 -->
</span>

<!-- p48 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:15-17</span>* - The Samaritans had believed and had been baptized, but they later received the Holy Spirit through prayer and the laying on of hands.

<!-- p49 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 19:6</span>* - Disciples in Ephesus, already believers, received the Holy Spirit when Paul laid hands on them.

<!-- p50 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit does not replace regeneration but is added to it, equipping the believer for spiritual service and boldness in ministry. Being baptized in the Holy Spirit is a free gift for those who are saved, not a requirement to be saved. It is not required that someone manifest a <u>specific</u> gift of the Holy Spirit to prove their ‘baptism’ in the Holy Spirit, but we should expect the baptism of the Holy Spirit to be manifested today in the same way as in the book of Acts, with accompanying signs and boldness! Some people are mistaken in thinking that if a person does not speak in tongues, they are not saved, or that they do not have the baptism of the Holy Spirit. Not everyone will receive every gift or manifestation of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29-31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:29-31</span>).

<!-- p51 -->
## How to receive the baptism of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p52 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit is described in Scripture as a gift promised to believers and received by faith. It is distinct from salvation and serves primarily for power and witness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>). The baptism of the Holy Spirit is a gift of power for believers, promised by Jesus and fulfilled at Pentecost. It is received through repentance, faith, and asking God. Although the laying on of hands is often used as a method, it is ultimately God who baptizes with the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p53 -->
Scripture presents speaking in tongues and prophecy as a common sign, but the purpose of this baptism is much greater-bringing boldness, power, and spiritual gifts for ministry. Every believer is encouraged to seek this experience as part of their spiritual journey, trusting God to equip them for His work.

<!-- p54 -->
The Bible lays out several foundational principles regarding how this baptism is received:

<!-- p55 -->
### **1. The baptism of the Holy Spirit is a free gift from God**

<!-- p56 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit is not something believers can earn - it is a free gift given by God. God wants to give good gifts to His children and is eager to give the Holy Spirit to those who ask with faith and a desire to be clothed with power.

<!-- p57 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p58 -->
> **<u>Luke 11:13</u>** (NKJV)
>
> If you then, being evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to those who ask Him!"
>

<!-- p59 -->
</span>

<!-- p60 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="38-39" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p61 -->
> **<u>Acts 2:38-39</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Then Peter said to them, "Repent, and let every one of you be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins; and you shall receive the gift of the Holy Spirit.
>

###  **2. Ask, seek, and receive by faith**

<!-- p64 -->
Scripture emphasizes that the baptism in the Holy Spirit is received by asking in prayer and with faith-filled expectation. Faith, combined with the willingness to ask and receive, is the key to experiencing the baptism of the Spirit. If you are not sure that God wants to give you the baptism of the Holy Spirit, ask Him—He is a good Father! When He prompts you, you may feel a tangible presence of God in your body that you have never experienced before. It's okay if you don't! God is a great God, and He does not need to be boxed into a perfect formula. If you feel God tugging at your heart, yield to Him!

## The gifts of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p79 -->
When the Holy Spirit was poured out, and people were baptized in the Holy Spirit, manifestations of the Holy Spirit often accompanied it.

<!-- p80 -->
There are several lists of gifts in the New Testament:

<!-- p81 -->
- Motivational gifts given by God: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:6-8</span>

<!-- p82 -->
- Addressed in the chapter on the Church.

<!-- p83 -->
- Fivefold ministry gifts given by Jesus: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-13</span>

<!-- p84 -->
- Addressed in the chapter on the church.

<!-- p85 -->
- Manifestation gifts given by the Holy Spirit: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span>

<!-- p86 -->
- Addressed here.

<!-- p87 -->
Each set of these gifts is given for different purposes and functions differently, but collectively they highlight the diversity and unity of gifts within the Body of Christ. Each gift has a unique role in accomplishing God’s purposes.

<!-- p88 -->
### Objectives &amp; Functioning of the Gifts

<!-- p89 -->
The primary purpose of the gifts of the Holy Spirit is to edify the Body of Christ and to bring glory to God:

<!-- p90 -->
- **Edification** - Strengthen and encourage believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
- **Unity** - Promote harmony and cooperation within the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:12-13</span>).

<!-- p92 -->
- **Evangelism** - Manifest the power of God as a testimony to non-believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="43" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:43</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:6-7</span>).

<!-- p93 -->
- **Service** - Meet the needs of others through acts of mercy and generosity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:6-8</span>).

<!-- p94 -->
These gifts are not for personal glory but for the common good (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:7</span>). They function best when exercised in love and humility, reflecting the character of Christ (1 Corinthians 13).

<!-- p95 -->
Paul emphasized that spiritual gifts must be exercised decently and in order (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:40</span>). This ensures that the use of the gifts promotes unity, peace, and understanding, rather than confusion or division.

<!-- p96 -->
- Prophecies must be evaluated (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:29</span>).

<!-- p97 -->
- When a message in tongues is given to the assembly, pray that it may be interpreted (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:27-28</span>).

<!-- p98 -->
- The gifts must always bring edification to the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:12</span>).

<!-- p99 -->
This order does not quench the Spirit but ensures that the gifts operate in a way that honors God and benefits others.

<!-- p100 -->
The exercise of spiritual gifts must always be rooted in love. Paul devotes an entire chapter (1 Corinthians 13) to explaining that without love, even the greatest spiritual gifts are meaningless. Love provides the motivation and the framework to use the gifts in a way that builds others up rather than tearing them down.

<!-- p101 -->
Moreover, the gifts operate as the Spirit wills (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:11</span>). They cannot be manufactured or controlled by human effort-they are sovereignly given and made effective by the Holy Spirit, often in moments of need or ministry. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 13:8</span> says that in time prophecy and tongues will cease. Theologically, people who believe that the gifts of the Holy Spirit have ceased are known as ‘Cessationists’. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 13:10</span> says “when the prefect \[Jesus\] comes, that wish is in part will be caused to cease”. Since Jesus has not yet returned for His Church, the gifts are still at work on the earth.

<!-- p102 -->
It is crucial to recognize that the **gifts of the Spirit** must not be separated from the **fruit of the Spirit** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-23</span>). While the gifts demonstrate power, the fruit reveals character-love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control.

<!-- p103 -->
- **Gifts without Fruit**: A believer can exercise gifts while lacking maturity or character, which leads to pride, abuse, or harm.

<!-- p104 -->
- **Fruit without Gifts**: A believer may have godly character but lack the power to minister effectively.

<!-- p105 -->
Both are necessary. The gifts equip believers for service. The fruit is a sign of their spiritual health (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 7:16</span>) and provides a ‘pleasant taste’ to those who consume it.

<!-- p106 -->
### Explanation of the 9 manifestation gifts of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p107 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span> lists the 9 manifestation gifts of the Holy Spirit:

<!-- p108 -->
1.  **Word of wisdom** - Supernatural insight into God's purposes or direction for a situation. It applies divine wisdom to complex problems or decisions (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:10</span>).

<!-- p109 -->
2.  **Word of knowledge** - A supernatural revelation of information about a person, an event, or a circumstance that could not have been known naturally (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 4:17-18</span>).

<!-- p110 -->
3.  **Faith** - A supernatural ability to trust God for the miraculous, even in impossible situations. This gift inspires boldness and confidence (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 3:6-7</span>).

<!-- p111 -->
4.  **Gifts of healings** - The supernatural power to heal physical, emotional, or spiritual illnesses and restore wholeness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 3:6-8</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 9:17-18</span>).

<!-- p112 -->
5.  **Working of miracles** - The ability to accomplish supernatural acts that defy natural laws, such as multiplying food or raising the dead (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40-41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 9:40-41</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 2:9-11</span>).

<!-- p113 -->
6.  **Prophecy** - Declaring God's truth or will under the inspiration of the Holy Spirit. It should be encouraging, edifying, and comforting (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>).

<!-- p114 -->
7.  **Distinguishing (Discernment) of spirits** - The ability to discern whether a spiritual influence comes from God, the enemy, or of human origin (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 16:16-18</span>).

<!-- p115 -->
8.  **Various kinds of tongues** - Speaking in languages unknown to the speaker, often as an act of worship, prayer, or as a sign to unbelievers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:4</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:22</span>).

<!-- p116 -->
9.  **Interpretation of tongues** - The supernatural ability to interpret the message spoken in tongues so that the Church may be edified (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:27</span>).

<!-- p117 -->
Paul specifies that all the gifts have value and must work together for the common good of the Church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:7</span>). However, he also encourages believers to desire the greater gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:31</span>). The greater gifts are often interpreted as those that most directly edify the Church, such as prophecy (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:1-5</span>).

<!-- p118 -->
- Prophecy is particularly emphasized because it strengthens, encourages, and comforts others (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>). Paul instructs believers to earnestly desire this gift because it promotes clarity and understanding in the Church.

<!-- p119 -->
- Gifts like tongues are highly valued for personal edification and prayer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:4</span>), but Paul teaches that when a message in tongues is given to the Church to pray for interpretation in order to ensure that they edify the Church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:27-28</span>).

<!-- p120 -->
While the Holy Spirit gives the gifts, we must yield and submit to the Holy Spirit to use them. As with muscles and talents, when we practice yielding to the Holy Spirit to use His gifts, we become more confident and reliable in their use.
