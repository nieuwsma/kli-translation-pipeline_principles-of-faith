# Chapter 12 - Discipleship

<!-- p1 -->
Discipleship means following Jesus, learning from Him, and becoming more like Him in the way we live and love. It involves a lifelong journey of obedience, transformation, and service, guided by the Holy Spirit, and rooted in the Word of God.

<!-- p2 -->
Discipleship is the primary command of the Great Commission. Jesus defined this mission in :

<!-- p3 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p4 -->
> **<u>Matthew 28:18-20</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And Jesus came and spoke to them, saying : "All authority has been given to Me in heaven and on earth.
>
> Go therefore and make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit,
>
> teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you; and behold, I am with you always, to the end of the age." Amen.
>

<!-- p5 -->
</span>

<!-- p6 -->
We, His disciples, must *« go and make disciples of all nations. »* Discipleship is about personal growth and the multiplication of that growth by teaching others to follow Christ. Discipleship is the vehicle by which we build the Kingdom of God; the teachings of Jesus are the message.

<!-- p7 -->
The call to be a disciple is more than attending church or learning Bible verses-it’s an invitation to live like Jesus and to partner with Him to advance the Kingdom of God. True discipleship helps believers become spiritually mature and effective for ministry (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:13</span>). It shapes both the heart and the skills needed to serve others and share the Gospel. As Paul instructed Timothy in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 2:2</span>, discipleship follows a model of spiritual multiplication, where trained disciples continue to raise up new followers to bring others to Christ.

<!-- p8 -->
Discipleship is both an individual call and a global mission. On a personal level, it involves growing in faith, developing an active spiritual life, being in relationship with others &amp; God. At the same time, it fuels the Church’s mission to reach every nation with the Gospel, ensuring that the message of Christ continues to spread and transform lives across generations.

<!-- p9 -->
## Why Discipleship Matters

<!-- p10 -->
The impact of discipleship goes beyond personal transformation-it has an impact on the Kingdom. Paul reminded Timothy in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 2:2</span> to entrust what he had learned to faithful men who would then be able to teach others also. This generational model highlights how discipleship is the engine that drives the growth and sustainability of the Church. Discipleship matters because it was Jesus’ chosen method to spread the Gospel and build His Church.

<!-- p11 -->
Rather than focusing on large crowds, Jesus intentionally invested in a small group of disciples, teaching them, modeling faithfulness, and preparing them to continue His mission. This personal and relational approach ensured that His message would multiply, since each disciple was equipped to make other disciples.

<!-- p12 -->
At its core, discipleship is central to fulfilling the Great Commission (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19-20</span>). Jesus did not just call us to make converts but to make disciples-followers rooted in the Word of God, empowered by the Holy Spirit, and equipped for ministry. Through discipleship, believers grow in their knowledge of Scripture, develop spiritual disciplines, and learn to walk in obedience to Christ. It prepares the Church to impact the world, advance the Gospel, and reflect the glory of God to all nations.

<!-- p13 -->
### Nature vs Nurture

<!-- p14 -->
In the study of biology, psychology, and anthropology we often consider the impact of nature versus nurture. The question often asked is this: Is a person more the result of their innate, built-in genetic inheritance? Or are they more the result of their environment and upbringing? The truth is that each person is a mixture of nature and nurture. The two elements, in strong interaction, choreograph the development of a human soul.

<!-- p15 -->
When you accepted Jesus, the miracle of salvation caused you to be born again of an incorruptible seed. You received a godly nature! Your spiritual DNA was rewritten! This godly nature causes a drive in our hearts to be close to our Heavenly Father, to know Him, to experience Him.

<!-- p16 -->
Just like a newborn, whose wonderful, beautiful, and innocent nature must be shaped by loving parents to develop into a healthy and mature individual, we too must be shaped and nourished to be developed.

<!-- p17 -->
Discipleship is the process of spiritual development that builds on our godly nature and makes us spiritually mature. By investing in discipleship, we ensure that the faith is passed on, that leaders are raised up, and that the Church remains healthy and dynamic-both locally and globally.

<!-- p18 -->
### Didactic Model vs Apprenticeship Model

<!-- p19 -->
In modern Western education, the student’s primary relationship is with the material. The student’s learning is focused on content, materials, books, videos, assignments, etc. The transformation of education happens via the curriculum. This teaching method is known as the *didactic model*.

<!-- p20 -->
The paradigm of the didactic model has not always been the norm. The model and method used in the time of Jesus were of the *apprenticeship model.* This model of learning places the emphasis on knowledge that is more 'caught than taught' and focuses more on the relationship between the student and the teacher than on that between the student and the curriculum.

<!-- p21 -->
The didactic model gradually became the default method over about a millennium, from the early medieval universities of Europe in the 11<sup>th</sup> - 13<sup>th</sup> centuries, the invention of the printing press in the 15<sup>th</sup> century, the Enlightenment in the 17<sup>th</sup> &amp; 18<sup>th</sup> centuries, and the impact of the Industrial Revolution on information and education in the 19<sup>th</sup> &amp; 20<sup>th</sup> centuries.

<!-- p22 -->
The advantage of the didactic model is that it can scale more efficiently than the apprenticeship model. Massive Open Online Courses (MOOCs) can have thousands of students at the same time, whereas even a highly skilled master may be able to have, perhaps, only a few dozen apprentices simultaneously.

<!-- p23 -->
The outcome of the didactic model is that the student masters the content; however, the student does not become like the teacher. The teacher is a facilitator of the curriculum. The teacher imparts knowledge.

<!-- p24 -->
In the apprenticeship model, the master shares more than knowledge; he passes on wisdom. The outcome of the apprenticeship model is that the student has become so like the master that he can reproduce the master's work and in turn take on his own apprentices.

<!-- p25 -->
The didactic model has its usefulness and place in the Kingdom of God; a good example is this book or any other Christian educational material. Jesus, as a rabbi, skilled and trained in the rabbinic method, practiced the apprenticeship model as the primary model for his disciples.

<!-- p26 -->
Jesus describes the apprenticeship model in:

<!-- p27 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p28 -->
> **<u>Luke 6:40</u>** (NKJV)
>
> A disciple is not above his teacher, but everyone who is perfectly trained will be like his teacher.
>

<!-- p29 -->
</span>

<!-- p30 -->
In the didactic model, the student becomes a bearer of knowledge; in the apprenticeship model, the student becomes like the teacher. Let us study the key words of this passage in Luke.

<!-- p31 -->
[See Table 6](../tables/table6.md)

<!-- p32 -->
In summary, the aim and objective of the apprenticeship model used in discipleship are twofold:

<!-- p33 -->
**First, the disciple becomes fully trained**, like the teacher. This does not mean that the disciple imitates everything his teacher does. We are not commanded to create clones, but rather to mature men and women who can carry the message of Jesus to the ends of the earth.

<!-- p34 -->
**Second, the disciple now becomes a teacher for others**. The disciple must pass on to someone else what he has learned, otherwise the message of the kingdom stops with him. This does not mean that he will necessarily have exactly the same gifts as his teacher, nor that he will enter full-time ministry, but it does mean that he must reproduce himself for his own training to have been complete.

<!-- p35 -->
Any organism that does not reproduce is sterile, and its lineage is cut off. An animal that cannot reproduce is not healthy, a church that cannot reproduce is just as unhealthy.

## Jesus' model and process of discipleship

<!-- p37 -->
Jesus demonstrated in the Gospels the mature and complete example of discipleship with his chosen disciples. We should use these steps in a general way as the foundation of our own walk to become disciples and to make disciples.

<!-- p38 -->
The apprenticeship model has two roles: master and student. Every master has been a student and, hopefully, every student will become a master. I will use the terms *disciple* to refer to the student and *disciple-maker* to refer to the master/teacher.

<!-- p39 -->
Every discipleship relationship goes through a progression of eight phases. The phases, or stages, are not completely isolated from one another; quite often, the relationship flows between the phases. At each phase, there is an emphasis for the disciple, and for the disciple-maker. We will study in depth the attributes of disciple-makers later in this chapter.

<!-- p40 -->
The eight phases of discipleship are:

<!-- p41 -->
1.  Invitation/Call

<!-- p42 -->
2.  Teaching

<!-- p43 -->
3.  Demonstration

<!-- p44 -->
4.  Participation/Trial

<!-- p45 -->
5.  Correction

<!-- p46 -->
6.  Empowerment/Authorization

<!-- p47 -->
7.  Multiplication

<!-- p48 -->
8.  Stay connected

<!-- p49 -->
### Invitation/Call

<!-- p50 -->
Discipleship begins with a personal invitation from Jesus to follow him. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 4:19</span>, Jesus called his first disciples, saying, *“Follow me, and I will make you fishers of men.”* This invitation was both relational and transformational-it required abandoning old ways of living and entering into a new purpose centered on God's mission. Jesus’ call was not to spectator passivity, but to participation in his ministry and the work of the Kingdom.

<!-- p51 -->
Today's disciple-makers must invite others into authentic relationships that inspire spiritual growth and obedience. The invitation to discipleship is deeply personal. Jesus meets people where they are, addresses their unique needs and circumstances, but he also calls them to go further-to grow, serve, and become more like him. This model reflects the relational nature of discipleship, where the focus is not only on knowledge, but on a relationship with Christ that transforms life.

<!-- p52 -->
### Teaching

<!-- p53 -->
Teaching is at the heart of discipleship. Jesus devoted a large part of his ministry to teaching the principles of the Kingdom of God, notably in the Sermon on the Mount (Matthew 5-7). His teachings provided a foundation for living in righteousness, loving others, and walking in obedience to God. Instruction in the Word of God remains central to discipleship today, because Scripture is the primary tool for spiritual growth, correction, and training in righteousness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>).

<!-- p54 -->
Disciple-makers must commit to faithfully teaching the Bible, helping others understand it and apply it to their lives. Teaching involves more than the transmission of knowledge-it requires modeling a Christlike life so that disciples see faith in action. Jesus embodied the truths he taught, showing that effective teaching is both verbal and visible. In the same way, disciple-makers should strive to live what they teach, providing practical examples of obedience and faithfulness.

<!-- p55 -->
### Demonstration

<!-- p56 -->
Jesus not only taught his disciples- he demonstrated how to live the truths he proclaimed. Jesus showed what it means to love, serve, and carry out ministry in concrete ways. Some examples from the Gospels are Jesus washing the disciples' feet as an act of humility and service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 13:12-15</span>), healing the sick, and feeding the hungry. Through his actions, he provided the pattern of compassion, faith, and boldness, leaving a clear example for his disciples to imitate.

<!-- p57 -->
Discipleship requires showing, not just telling. Today's disciple-makers must be transparent about their faith journey, including their struggles and victories, so that others can see how faith works in real life. Whether it's praying with someone in need, sharing testimonies, or serving the community, discipleship thrives when it is rooted in practical demonstration. Actions often speak louder than words, making demonstration a powerful tool for forming others in the faith.

<!-- p58 -->
### Participation/Trial

<!-- p59 -->
Jesus did not leave his disciples on the sidelines- he actively involved them in ministry experiences. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 9:1-6</span> and <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 10:5-15</span>, Jesus sent the disciples to preach the Gospel, heal the sick, and cast out demons. These hands-on opportunities allowed the disciples to learn by doing, gaining confidence and skills under his guidance.

<!-- p60 -->
Modern discipleship should also include practical involvement. Disciple-makers must invite disciples to participate in ministry, whether by leading prayer groups, sharing testimonies, or serving at evangelism events. Providing safe spaces for practice allows disciples to grow in confidence while being supported and encouraged. We learn best by doing, and participation is the key to turning theory into action.

<!-- p61 -->
### Correction

<!-- p62 -->
Growth often requires correction, and Jesus lovingly corrected His disciples when they misunderstood His teachings or failed in their tasks. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33-37" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 9:33-37</span>, He addressed their pride by teaching them servant leadership. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-32" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 22:31-32</span>, He prayed for Peter, preparing him to rise from failure and strengthen others.

<!-- p63 -->
Correction in discipleship should be full of grace and constructive-rooted in Scripture and aimed at restoration (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 4:2</span>). It is not about condemnation, but about helping disciples grow and avoid pitfalls. Disciple-makers should provide accountability, using the Word of God to guide and encourage growth while modeling humility by accepting correction themselves.

<!-- p64 -->
### Empowerment/Authorization

<!-- p65 -->
Jesus not only taught and corrected- He empowered His disciples to accomplish His mission. He gave them authority to preach the Gospel, heal the sick, and cast out demons (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:18-20</span>). After His resurrection, He promised the Holy Spirit as the source of power for their ministry (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>).

<!-- p66 -->
Modern discipleship should include empowering others to exercise their spiritual gifts and serve effectively. This involves identifying gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-13</span>), encouraging their use, and releasing disciples to serve. Empowerment also emphasizes dependence on the Holy Spirit for strength, confidence, and wisdom, to ensure that disciples are spiritually equipped for the work to which God has called them.

<!-- p67 -->
### Multiplication

<!-- p68 -->
The goal of discipleship is not only personal growth- it is multiplication. Jesus illustrated this by training the disciples to make other disciples (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19-20</span>). After His resurrection, the apostles continued this mission of multiplication, spreading the Gospel and planting churches throughout the known world. Paul's instructions in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 2:2</span> reinforce this principle, urging Timothy to teach others who will teach others.

<!-- p69 -->
Discipleship is about equipping disciples to make disciples, creating a chain reaction of faith that expands the Kingdom of God. This process requires intentionality-teaching disciples how to mentor, teach, and train others, while sending them with confidence and with purpose (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 10:15</span>).

<!-- p70 -->
### Staying connected

<!-- p71 -->
Discipleship does not end when a person is sent. Jesus showed an ongoing connection with His disciples, continuing to encourage and instruct them even after His resurrection (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="21" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 21:15-19</span>). The early Church maintained this model, gathering regularly for teaching, fellowship, and prayer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42-47" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:42-47</span>).

<!-- p72 -->
Modern discipleship should also foster ongoing relationships, offering accountability, encouragement, and support as disciples continue to grow and lead. Maintaining unity in the Body of Christ ensures that disciples are never isolated, but remain strengthened and encouraged in their faith journey.

<!-- p73 -->
## Who does discipleship?

<!-- p74 -->
Everyone in the Body of Christ is responsible first to become a disciple, then to contribute to the process of making disciples. There is a diversity of gifts and functions, so our contributions may differ, but we are all responsible. The people ultimately most responsible for the state and health of discipleship in the local church are the leaders (**elders and deacons - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 14:23</span>**), but everyone plays a role.

<!-- p75 -->
Discipleship is a living and growing process- like raising a child to maturity. Healthy discipleship brings strength and life to people. Like a parent who strengthens and nurtures a child, and in so doing makes the child healthy and mature, disciple-makers help to nurture, strengthen, and mature disciples. The ultimate goal of discipleship is not the ‘transfer of knowledge’, but transformation.

<!-- p76 -->
We must all become perfect (mature) and make disciples. Although we have diverse gifts, there are common characteristics for all disciple-makers.

<!-- p77 -->
### Characteristics of a disciple-maker

<!-- p78 -->
A disciple-maker is someone who not only follows Christ, but is also committed to helping others grow in their faith. This role requires specific characteristics that reflect both spiritual maturity and practical dedication. Disciple-makers must continually rely on the strength, wisdom, and grace of God to overcome the challenges involved in leading other people in their spiritual journey.

<!-- p79 -->
#### Rooted in Christ

<!-- p80 -->
The foundation of a disciple maker’s life must be deeply rooted in Christ. Jesus said: *"I am the vine; you are the branches. If you abide in Me and I abide in you, you will bear much fruit; apart from Me you can do nothing"* (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:5</span>). Effective disciple makers know that the ability to grow and to help others comes from staying close to Jesus every day. However, resistance to personal growth and spiritual change can hinder this dependence. Disciple makers must continually cultivate their personal relationship with Jesus through prayer, the study of Scripture, and obedience, showing those they disciple what it means to abide in Christ.

<!-- p81 -->
#### Committed to the mission

<!-- p82 -->
A disciple maker must remain committed to the mission of making disciples, as commanded by Jesus in the Great Commission (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19-20</span>). This mission requires intentional focus and clear priorities, recognizing that making disciples is not an optional task but the central purpose of the Church. However, challenges such as time constraints, busyness, and personal distractions can make it difficult to stay focused. Disciple makers must intentionally schedule time for mentoring, teaching, and relationship-building, viewing disciple making as a lifestyle rather than an occasional activity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 19:10</span>).

<!-- p83 -->
#### Led by the Spirit

<!-- p84 -->
Disciple making cannot be accomplished by human strength alone; it requires the guidance and power of the Holy Spirit. Jesus promised His disciples that they would receive power when the Holy Spirit came upon them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>), equipping them to be witnesses and servants of the Gospel. Disciple makers must be sensitive to the leading of the Spirit, relying on His wisdom to navigate complex situations, cultural barriers, and difficult conversations. This dependence on the Holy Spirit enables disciple makers to act with love, boldness, and spiritual discernment, ensuring that their efforts are directed by the Spirit rather than by themselves.

<!-- p85 -->
#### Humble and teachable

<!-- p86 -->
Humility is a distinguishing mark of an effective disciple maker. As Paul taught: *"Do nothing out of rivalry or vain conceit. Instead, in humility consider others as better than yourselves"* (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 2:3</span>). Disciple makers recognize that they too are lifelong learners who must remain teachable and accountable. They welcome correction and feedback, modeling humility for those they walk with. Pride can be a significant obstacle to growth, both for disciple makers and for their disciples. Embracing accountability and maintaining a servant’s heart fosters trust and creates an environment conducive to growth and transparency.

<!-- p87 -->
#### Patient and persevering

<!-- p88 -->
Discipleship is rarely instantaneous—it is a long-term process that requires patience and perseverance. Paul reminds believers to *"not grow weary in doing good, for at the proper time we will reap if we do not give up"* (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:9</span>). Growth in the faith often involves setbacks, questions, and failures, but disciple makers are called to walk alongside others, offering encouragement and support every step of the way. It is easy to become discouraged when progress seems slow, but disciple makers trust God, believing He is at work even when results are not immediately visible.

<!-- p89 -->
Being a disciple maker requires a blend of spiritual dependence, commitment, and humility. It is a calling that demands patience and perseverance while remaining Spirit-led and teachable. Disciple makers are not expected to be perfect, but they must be faithful—abiding in Christ, investing in others, and trusting God to bring growth and transformation in His timing. These qualities reflect the love of Christ and help others become mature disciples who, in turn, can make disciples.

<!-- p90 -->
## Practical tools for discipleship

<!-- p91 -->
Effective discipleship requires intentional tools and practices that foster spiritual growth, accountability, and community. These tools help disciples develop a solid foundation in the faith, grow in intimacy with God, and be equipped to make disciples. Practical methods such as Bible study, prayer, small groups, and mentoring provide both the structure and the support needed for ongoing growth.

<!-- p92 -->
### Bible study

<!-- p93 -->
Engaging deeply with Scripture is the foundation of discipleship. Jesus emphasized the importance of God’s Word in spiritual growth, declaring that *"man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceeds from the mouth of God"* (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 4:4</span>). Regular Bible study and memorization enable disciples to internalize truth, combat lies, and apply God’s wisdom to everyday life.

<!-- p94 -->
Disciples should be encouraged to immerse themselves in Scripture, not merely as an academic exercise but to know God and see their hearts transformed (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="119" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="105" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalms 119:105</span>). Practical approaches such as reading plans, study guides, and group discussions provide opportunities to explore doctrine and discuss application in community. By developing regular study habits, disciples lay a solid foundation for spiritual maturity and fruitful ministry (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>).

<!-- p95 -->
### Prayer

<!-- p96 -->
Prayer is the lifeline of discipleship, connecting believers to God’s presence, power, and guidance. Jesus taught His disciples how to pray in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 11:1-4</span>, emphasizing both dependence on God and spiritual alignment with His will. Through prayer, disciples learn to seek God’s wisdom, intercede for others, and receive strength for ministry.

<!-- p97 -->
Teaching disciples to pray—both individually and corporately—equips them to cultivate intimacy with God and discern His leading (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 4:6-7</span>). Incorporating prayer journals, prayer groups, and fasting practices fosters spiritual discipline and steadfastness, ensuring that disciples remain Spirit-led and mission-centered.

<!-- p98 -->
### Small groups and community

<!-- p99 -->
Discipleship thrives in community. The early Church modeled relational discipleship through fellowship, prayer, and breaking bread together (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42-47" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:42-47</span>). Small groups and accountability partnerships provide a safe space for disciples to grow together, share their struggles, and celebrate their victories.

<!-- p100 -->
The local church serves as a central hub for discipleship relationships, offering opportunities for group Bible studies, prayer meetings, and fellowship events. These gatherings enable disciples to practice love, bear one another’s burdens (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:2</span>), and encourage one another toward spiritual maturity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:24-25</span>). Small groups also provide a framework for multiplying leaders, as mature disciples train and send others to lead groups and walk alongside new believers.

<!-- p101 -->
### Mentoring

<!-- p102 -->
Mentoring offers a personal and intentional approach to discipleship. Jesus modeled this by investing deeply in the twelve disciples, walking with them through teaching, correction, and encouragement. Similarly, one-on-one relationships allow disciple makers to tailor guidance to individual needs, helping disciples grow in faith, character, and leadership.

<!-- p103 -->
Mentors serve as accountability partners, offering support, prayer, and wisdom during seasons of growth and struggle. Practical tools such as spiritual checkups, goal setting, and regular check-ins ensure that disciples remain focused and motivated. Peer relationships within mentoring structures also foster mutual growth, as both mentor and disciple learn from shared experiences (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="27" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 27:17</span>).

## **Personal Reflection Questions**

<!-- p105 -->
1.  What does being a disciple of Jesus mean to you personally? How do you actively follow Him in your daily life?

<!-- p106 -->
2.  Reflect on Jesus’ model of discipleship. Which aspect of His approach—inviting, teaching, demonstrating, or empowering—stands out most to you, and why?

<!-- p107 -->
3.  How can you multiply your faith by making disciples? What challenges or fears might hold you back, and how can you overcome them? (Notice that I said multiply your faith, not multiply your disciples!)

<!-- p108 -->
4.  What characteristics of a disciple maker (e.g., patience, humility, relational focus) do you see in yourself? Which traits do you feel called to develop further?

<!-- p109 -->
5.  Think about the people in your life. Who might God be calling you to disciple? How can you take the first step to build that relationship?
