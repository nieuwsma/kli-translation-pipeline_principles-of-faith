# Chapter 8 - Holy Spirit-Comforter, Guide, and Power

<!-- p1 -->
The Holy Spirit is not a new spirit but He is of God and has been with God since the very beginning. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 1:2</span> the Spirit of God hovers over the waters during creation. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="NEH" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Nehemiah 9:20</span>, the Scriptures say that it was the Spirit who instructed the people in the wilderness. It is by this same Holy Spirit that God anointed the kings, the judges, and the prophets in the Old Testament. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JOL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Joel 2:28-29</span> it is promised that the Spirit will be poured out on all humanity. It is by this same Holy Spirit that God raised Jesus from the dead, and He now lives in us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:11</span>). There is only one Holy Spirit, and He has always been the same. The way He works with people has seemed different at different times, but His heart has never changed.

<!-- p2 -->
Jesus gave His disciples, and all of us, the powerful promise of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p3 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p4 -->
> **<u>John 14:16-18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And I will pray the Father, and He will give you another Helper, that He may abide with you forever—
>
> the Spirit of truth, whom the world cannot receive, because it neither sees Him nor knows Him

<!-- p20 -->
The disciples were to wait for the promise of the poured-out Spirit, and that this would enable them to be witnesses to the ends of the earth. The Holy Spirit is an integral part of God’s plan for humanity, and is essential for us as believers, for carrying out our mission, and for the world.

<!-- p21 -->
## Getting to Know the Holy Spirit

<!-- p22 -->
The Holy Spirit is, first and foremost, of God and from God. The Scriptures refer to the Spirit as the Spirit of God, or the Spirit of the Lord, or the Spirit of Christ; although these expressions highlight different aspects, they designate one and the same reality. The Holy Spirit, being fully God, is also eternal and is holy (blameless, innocent, pure, not profane). Some translations refer to the Holy Spirit as the Holy Ghost.

<!-- p23 -->
The word for spirit in Greek is **Pneuma** (**NYOO-mah** - **G4151**); it means breath, or wind. The Hebrew word for spirit means the same thing. Breath is significant because we need it constantly to live. It was the breath of God that He breathed into Adam to make him live; likewise, it was Jesus’ breath that He exhaled last before dying on the cross. Without breath there is no life. Breath, like the wind, can be felt and experienced but is not as easily observable as other 'elements' water, fire, earth.

<!-- p24 -->
There are several other foundational adjectives to describe the Holy Spirit. Each of these adjectives is used in conjunction with 'the Spirit of', which means these properties are vital to the definition of the Spirit. For example, the Spirit of truth or the Spirit of life.

<!-- p25 -->
The Spirit of:

<!-- p26 -->
- **Truth** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:17</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span> Refers to the Spirit’s role in guiding believers into all truth, revealing God’s will and the reality of Christ.

<!-- p27 -->
- **Life** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:2</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:8</span> Highlights the Spirit’s role in imparting spiritual and eternal life to believers, freeing them from the law of sin and death.

<!-- p28 -->
- **Grace** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:29</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ZEC" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Zechariah 12:10</span> Reflects the Spirit’s work in dispensing God’s unmerited favor, forgiveness, and kindness to humanity.

<!-- p29 -->
- **Glory** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 4:14</span> Indicates the Spirit’s role in revealing and manifesting the glory of God, particularly in times of suffering and persecution.

<!-- p30 -->
- **Adoption** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:15</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 4:5</span> Refers to the Spirit’s work in making believers children of God, conferring on them the status of sons and daughters.

<!-- p31 -->
- **Holiness** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 1:4</span> Emphasizes the sanctifying work of the Spirit, setting believers apart for God’s purposes.

<!-- p32 -->
- **Power** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 1:7</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span> Describes the empowering of believers by the Spirit for boldness, ministry, and the ability to live out their faith effectively.

<!-- p33 -->
In all these attributes the Holy Spirit is also rightly recognized as the Helper or the Comforter. Jesus refers to the Holy Spirit as the coming helper; he uses the Greek word ***Paraklētos** (*pah-RAH-klay-tos - **G3875**), which means advocate, helper, one called alongside.

<!-- p34 -->
An advocate speaks on behalf of another, like a defense attorney. He intercedes and helps the person in his care.

<!-- p35 -->
The helper helps. He contributes to strengthening the work, but his task is primarily to support the work. The helper is not solely responsible for carrying out the work, but he comes alongside to help accomplish it.

<!-- p36 -->
The Holy Spirit has several distinct purposes and roles in his ministry. His work is inward (in the hearts and lives of believers), upward (in serving and acknowledging God), and outward (toward our mission field, the world).

<!-- p37 -->
## Roles of the Holy Spirit-Inward, Upward, Outward

<!-- p38 -->
### Inward-The Holy Spirit unites us to Jesus

<!-- p39 -->
Before Jesus performed the slightest miracle or launched his public ministry, he was baptized by John the Baptist and the Spirit of God descended upon him, and a voice from heaven proclaimed, ‘This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:17</span>).

<!-- p40 -->
When we accept Jesus by faith, we receive salvation and the seal of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p41 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p42 -->
> **<u>Ephesians 1:13-14</u>** (NKJV)
>
> In Him you also trusted, after you heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation; in Him also, having believed, you were sealed with the Holy Spirit of promise,
>
> who is the guarantee of our inheritance until the redemption of the purchased possession, to the praise of His glory.
>

<!-- p43 -->
</span>

<!-- p44 -->
This seal is not metaphorical but literal. It serves as a sign of our redemption and constitutes a down payment on the full inheritance we receive as children of God. Romans 8 describes that, through the new birth, we receive in our mortal bodies the same Spirit of God who raised Christ from the dead, and we become the literal temple of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 6:16</span>).

<!-- p45 -->
This receiving of the Spirit of God transforms us and causes rivers of living water to flow from us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 7:38</span>). This renewal is immediate and complete, but it continues throughout our lives. The river of the Holy Spirit should never stop flowing in our lives !

<!-- p46 -->
Through salvation and the indwelling of the Holy Spirit in us, we can abide in Christ and He in us. The Spirit of God is no longer confined to the outside, but can dwell intimately with us. We can walk in fellowship and harmony with God now and for eternal life.

<!-- p47 -->
There would be much more to say about the inward work of the Holy Spirit and His role in our sanctification, but that will be addressed in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p48 -->
### Upward-The Holy Spirit bears witness of Jesus

<!-- p49 -->
The Holy Spirit comes from God and is of God. Just as Jesus said He did nothing unless the Father showed Him (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 5:19</span>), so the Holy Spirit also does nothing except under the Father’s direction. Because the Holy Spirit is the Comforter, He does not act on His own, but works in perfect harmony with the Father.

<!-- p50 -->
The Spirit bears witness of Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:26</span>), glorifies Jesus, and announces and declares what Jesus has for us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:14</span>).

<!-- p51 -->
The Holy Spirit will testify of Jesus, glorify Him, teach and bring to our remembrance the teaching of Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:26</span>), and guide us into all truth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span>). Jesus told His disciples that He had much more to teach them, but that they could not bear it yet, and that the Holy Spirit would guide them into all truth. This is another nuanced point in theology. These teachings that Jesus wanted His disciples to know were communicated in several ways. Jesus instructed them in person, both before His crucifixion, as well as after the resurrection and before His ascension.

<!-- p52 -->
Jesus also promised that the Spirit of truth, who was poured out on them on the day of Pentecost, would lead them into all truth by divine revelation. Is there still divine revelation today? Certainly yes! The gifts of the Holy Spirit, including prophecy and words of knowledge and wisdom, have not yet ceased! However, no prophecy nor any word coming from a spirit is of private interpretation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2PE" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Peter 1:20</span>). This is crucial. Some people will falsely claim to listen to the Holy Spirit, but their actions and their words do not bear witness of Jesus. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 4:3</span> warns against any spirit that does not bear witness of Jesus, saying that such a spirit is of the Antichrist. This bears repeating: if a spirit veers from the teaching of Christ, it is not the Holy Spirit, but a profane spirit!

<!-- p53 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p54 -->
> **<u>1 John 4:1</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Beloved, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits, whether they are of God; because many false prophets have gone out into the world.
>

<!-- p55 -->
</span>

<!-- p56 -->
Throughout Scripture, we see examples of erroneous declarations, often made by false prophets or by well-meaning people who spoke contrary to the will of God. For example, Jeremiah warned against prophets who set forth the visions of their own hearts, proclaiming peace when destruction was imminent (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="23" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 23:16-17</span>). Likewise, in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 16:16-18</span>, the words of a slave girl about Paul and Silas were accurate, but inspired by a demonic spirit, and not by the Holy Spirit. These examples remind us that even words that seem true can come from ungodly sources.

<!-- p57 -->
Any claim such as “God told me!” or a “new” teaching should be measured against the biblical criteria of truth. To discern properly, we must ask ourselves:

<!-- p58 -->
- Is this consistent with Scripture?

<!-- p59 -->
- Does this exalt Jesus Christ?

<!-- p60 -->
- Does it produce fruit according to God?

<!-- p61 -->
By applying these tests, we can distinguish the true guidance of the Holy Spirit from errors or deception.

<!-- p62 -->
God customarily grants us ample &amp; abundant wisdom &amp; direction (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:5</span> &amp; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="33" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 33:3</span>). God’s divine wisdom will not contradict the Scriptures, but it will go beyond the scope of the Scriptures. His *entolē* *(*commandments*)* are immediate prescriptions to be observed, these are *rhema* words (as discussed in the chapter on faith).

<!-- p63 -->
This wisdom, this understanding, and this guidance are unlikely to be found word for word in the Scriptures. For example, the Scriptures give excellent general guidance on what to look for in a spouse, but do not give precise prescriptions concerning the exact person. God does indeed promise to direct our path, to lead us into all truth, and we can take courage knowing that this wisdom and guidance from the Holy Spirit will never contradict any teaching of Scripture.

Jesus gave us His mission, the Great Commission:

<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p68 -->
</span>

<!-- p69 -->
The Holy Spirit plays a crucial role in the mission, convicting the world of sin, righteousness, and judgment:

<!-- p70 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="8-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

Going further, the Spirit brings conviction of these three truths:

<!-- p74 -->
1.  **Conviction of sin**: Revealing the reality of human rebellion against God, drawing people’s attention to the depravity of their own lives.

<!-- p75 -->
2.  **Conviction of righteousness**: Demonstrating Jesus’ perfect fulfillment of the Father’s will, and that Jesus’ sacrifice has provided the once-for-all atonement necessary for the forgiveness of sins.

<!-- p76 -->
3.  **Conviction of judgment**: Affirming Satan’s defeat, the certainty of God’s justice, and that all will appear at a judgment for the work and deeds of their lives.

<!-- p77 -->
The Holy Spirit also empowers the mission. The Holy Spirit gives believers the power to be bold witnesses in the face of persecution to share the Gospel. Boldness does not mean speaking loudly. It means having courage despite fear. Power is strength, vigor, and ability. The Holy Spirit gives the strength and ability to be a witness to the Gospel despite threats and the fear of punishment.

<!-- p78 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

</span>

<!-- p82 -->
1.  **Prayer** : Believers ask for boldness and God's intervention.

<!-- p83 -->
2.  **Empowerment** : The Holy Spirit fills them with courage, power, and direction.

<!-- p84 -->
3.  **Proclamation** : They proclaim the Gospel with boldness, often with signs, wonders, and miracles.

<!-- p85 -->
4.  **Opposition** : Resistance arises, leading to persecutions or challenges. Sometimes believers were martyred, other times imprisoned and/or beaten.

<!-- p86 -->
5.  **Renewal** : The Church returns to prayer, seeking a renewal of power, and the cycle continues.

<!-- p87 -->
The New Testament Church grew extensively in the face of determined opposition. Throughout the world, the Church endures violence at the hands of oppressors and wicked rulers determined to stifle the knowledge of the truth of Jesus.

God knew this opposition would arise; Jesus prophesied it:

<!-- p89 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

Do not be afraid! Jesus promised us that:

<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

</span>

The Holy Spirit brings a supernatural boldness to proclaim the testimony of the Gospel in the face of persecution. The result of this empowerment can be described, but it is best experienced. It is a supernatural boldness (beyond the human) that drives believers to share. This boldness in preaching is often accompanied by supernatural acts of demonstration of the Spirit and of power (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 2:1-5</span>). These collective works are supernatural, divine, and desperately needed in a world that is tearing itself apart, miserable, full of pain, waiting for the mature sons of God to be clearly manifested! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:22-23</span>)

<!-- p99 -->
The instrument chosen by Jesus to bring about change on the earth is His Church, clothed with power, emboldened, and led by the Holy Spirit.

## Building a Relationship with the Holy Spirit

<!-- p101 -->
Every relationship takes time and experience to develop deeply. The Holy Spirit is our advocate, our comforter, our help, and our guide. He strengthens us and sanctifies us.

<!-- p102 -->
Start to consider the Holy Spirit as a person. In our relationship, we can either work to strengthen it or do things that weaken it. He can be ignored, He can be quenched, He can be blasphemed, or He can be listened to, honored, and obeyed.

<!-- p103 -->
Do not ignore the voice of the Holy Spirit. He is always speaking, to us and to the Church. If you do not yet know how to recognize the voice of the Holy Spirit, it takes time, willingness, and effort (like all healthy and beneficial relationships). But do not worry, you already have within you all the necessary components to begin developing a personal relationship with the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p104 -->
The fastest and most reliable ways to build an effective relationship with the Holy Spirit are to

<!-- p105 -->
1.  Invoke Him **individually** in prayer, pause, and wait for Him to speak.

<!-- p106 -->
2.  Invoke Him within a **group** of people who know Him (the Church), pause, and wait for Him to speak.

<!-- p107 -->
I use the expression ‘invoke’ to mean, literally, with your mouth, or in the way you express yourself (with your hands if you cannot speak out loud with your mouth). Why speak? A few reasons:

<!-- p108 -->
1.  We are speaking spirits made in the image of God. God speaks, and so do we.

<!-- p109 -->
2.  We use speech as our primary means of communication. You can also write what you want to say (keep a journal) and speak it out loud.

<!-- p110 -->
3.  Speaking is an intentional act and signals to the one you are addressing that you wish to engage them in a conversation.

<!-- p111 -->
#### What should you say?

<!-- p112 -->
If you have never spoken to God, try introducing yourself. Of course, this will feel a bit odd, but that discomfort is normal and temporary. In addition to introducing yourself, tell God that you want to speak to Him and that you want Him to answer you.

<!-- p113 -->
Consider asking Him a question. It does not have to be a deep theological question; it can start simply. Can You show me Your love for me? Why do I have a certain feeling? What would You like to tell me?

<!-- p114 -->
#### And then?

<!-- p115 -->
Well, in any effective relationship, you talk a bit, then you pause and listen. God will speak.

<!-- p116 -->
God speaks in many ways, but He wants to do so in a personal and inward way. It probably will not be an audible voice (as if your best friend said your name out loud) but an inner voice that speaks to your heart and your mind. Elijah calls this the ‘still small voice’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1KI" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Kings 19:11-13</span>).

<!-- p117 -->
The most common ways I have personally seen God speak to people, and I have been fortunate to help dozens of people learn to speak directly to God, are a combination of the following:

<!-- p118 -->
- A certainty - the person who is speaking to God receives an immediate understanding that helps them.

<!-- p119 -->
- An image - also called in Scripture a vision or a dream (a vision is when you are awake, a dream when you are asleep), God will give you an image in your mind. Perhaps it is a memory, or a place where you have been or that you know? Explore the image, what do you see? What do you feel as a result? What is happening?

<!-- p120 -->
- An inner voice - like an audible voice, but one that only you can hear, and addressed to your heart and your mind.

<!-- p121 -->
#### What will God say?

<!-- p122 -->
Not sure! But that’s what makes it fun. I can say that He is NOT the spirit of fear, but of perfect love, and that He will never violate the teaching of Scripture. For example, God will never tell you to commit adultery.

<!-- p123 -->
And now?

<!-- p124 -->
Keep the dialogue going! It really is as simple as that.

### Why establish a relationship?

<!-- p129 -->
The Holy Spirit is the Comforter. The Comforter helps us! Work goes much more smoothly if we have a common language and a relationship with Him. Can you imagine trying to work with someone at your job if you had no relationship, did not speak the same language, and could not communicate?!

<!-- p130 -->
We serve a God who wants to communicate with us. Jesus said that His sheep know His voice (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:27</span>), we already have within us all the natural abilities we need, but we may lack the mastery that comes with experience.

<!-- p131 -->
From my personal experience, a personal and active relationship with the Holy Spirit is one of the best things in the Christian life. His presence brings us peace, comfort, and direction, but only if you know how to seek Him, listen to Him, and obey Him.

<!-- p132 -->
Develop a sensitivity with the Holy Spirit. Like many married people over time, you can communicate with your spouse in a unique way and sometimes with just a glance. The glance alone tells you everything you need to know about what just happened and what is going to happen. Develop this kind of sensitivity and familiarity with the Holy Spirit. Include Him in your whole life; it’s a wonderful part of your relationship with Him that will bring so much depth and richness to your walk of faith.

<!-- p133 -->
The Holy Spirit came to lead and guide us, to give us the power to be the people of God, and to see the peoples of the Earth radically transformed, delivered from the kingdom of darkness into the kingdom of light. Learning to know Him and building a relationship of trust with Him is essential to daily life and to fulfilling the work Jesus has entrusted to us.

<!-- p134 -->
Our discussion of the work of the Holy Spirit does not end in this chapter. His work is addressed in detail throughout the remaining chapters of this book, especially the chapters on sanctification and baptism.
