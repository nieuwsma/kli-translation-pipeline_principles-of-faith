# Chapter 14 - Keep Watch

<!-- p1 -->
Having received the spiritual authority of Christ on the Earth to establish His kingdom, we should work with diligence and haste, for Jesus will return!

<!-- p2 -->
The return of Christ is an important part of our faith. The study of the return of Christ and of the end times is called **eschatology**. Many books on eschatology have been written, and *most of them disagree with each other*! More specifically, they diverge on the interpretations (figurative, literal, symbolic), the order, and the timing of events in the Scriptures. I recall the saying of the great Pentecostal evangelist Reinhard Bonke - “I’ll split hairs in heaven when I’m done breaking chains on the Earth!”

<!-- p3 -->
As an author, I hesitate to dive very deeply into this hotly debated subject given that this book focuses mainly on the fundamentals of the faith. I will largely stick to the broadest view of the events described in Scripture and focus on what matters most - **the** **mission of the Gospel** !

<!-- p4 -->
## Universally accepted points:

<!-- p5 -->
1.  Jesus is returning

<!-- p6 -->
2.  Resurrection of the dead

<!-- p7 -->
3.  Judgment for all

<!-- p8 -->
4.  Eternal destiny

<!-- p9 -->
5.  A defeated enemy

<!-- p10 -->
6.  New heavens and new earth

<!-- p11 -->
For each point, we will review what the concept is, examine reference passages from Scripture, consider why it matters, and what we should do in light of that.

<!-- p12 -->

<!-- p33 -->

<!-- p34 -->

<!-- p35 -->

### **3. Judgment for all**

<!-- p37 -->
**What it is :** All people—believers and nonbelievers—will stand before God for judgment. Believers will appear before the **Judgment Seat of Christ** for rewards, while nonbelievers will face the **Great White Throne Judgment**.

<!-- p38 -->
#### Great White Throne Judgment

<!-- p39 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p40 -->
> **<u>Revelation 20:11-12</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Then I saw a great white throne and Him who sat on it; from whose face the earth and the heaven fled away. And there was found no place for them.
>
> And I saw the dead, small and great, standing before God, and books were opened. And another book was opened, which is the Book of Life. And the dead were judged according to their works, by the things which were written in the books.
>

<!-- p41 -->
</span>

<!-- p42 -->
As described in the chapter on sin, there will be a great and dreadful final judgment for all unbelievers. The punishment will be eternal separation from God! **This judgment is not for the believer.**

<!-- p43 -->
#### Judgment Seat of Christ

<!-- p44 -->
As believers, our names have been written in the **Lamb’s *Book of Life*** *(*See the book of *Revelation)**.** Thus, we will appear* *at the **Judgment Seat of Christ***. This judgment is not for condemnation, but rather for reward! Again**,** this is not a moment of **shame or fear** for believers, but a **celebration** of what God has accomplished through us. It reminds us that our lives count-not only for today but for **eternity**.

At this judgment, we will receive rewards for the good works we have done! All those who are at this judgment lived and died as believers, so their eternal salvation is guaranteed. These laurel crowns highlight God’s recognition of believers’ faithfulness and service in His Kingdom.

## Common points of disagreement

<!-- p89 -->
To be complete, if you are curious about the common points of division in eschatology, here is a **very** brief overview of the most frequently debated points.

<!-- p90 -->
- **Timing of the rapture:** Pre-, mid-, or post-tribulation? Opinions differ on when the rapture occurs in relation to the **tribulation**. Some denominations do not believe in the rapture.

<!-- p91 -->
- **Purpose of the tribulation:** Is it a literal judgment for Israel or a symbolic period of struggle?

<!-- p92 -->
- **Millennial reign:** Is it literal **(premillennial)**, symbolic **(amillennial)**, or spiritual **(postmillennial)**?

<!-- p93 -->
- **The final rebellion of Satan:** Takes place after the millennium (literal or figurative event?).

<!-- p94 -->
It is excellent to study eschatology, but let us remember, as D.L. Browning said, “May the main thing always remain the main thing!”

## **Final thoughts**

<!-- p96 -->
The return of Christ is the culmination of God's plan and the reason we live and labor with urgency and intention. The parables of the ten virgins (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 25:1-13</span>) and of the two men in the field (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="24" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40-42" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 24:40-42</span>) remind us to be vigilant and faithful, always ready for His return. Whether by the rewards of crowns or the hope of the resurrection, our motivation is clear-Christ is returning!

<!-- p97 -->
**May we be found ready, faithful, and expectant on that glorious day!**
