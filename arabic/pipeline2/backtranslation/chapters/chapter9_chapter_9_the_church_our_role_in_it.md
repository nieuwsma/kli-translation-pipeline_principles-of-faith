# Chapter 9 - The Church and Our Role in It

<!-- p1 -->
The church is the way God reveals Himself on earth—through His people, as He does His work. We have been called to be His body, His hands and feet to accomplish His work and purpose. And without our efforts, the harvest will remain in the field and will not be gathered.

<!-- p2 -->
## Who is the Church?

<!-- p3 -->
The church, ***Ekklesia** (eh-klee-SEE-ah - **G1577**),* is derived from two Greek words:

<!-- p4 -->
- **"Ek"**: "out of."

<!-- p5 -->
- **"Kaleo"**: "to call."

<!-- p6 -->
Together, **Ekklesia** literally means "called out" or "a called-out assembly." In the ancient Greek city-states, it referred to an assembly of citizens summoned for a specific purpose, such as legislative or judicial meetings. It was a secular term denoting a group of people.

<!-- p7 -->
In the New Testament, it is used primarily to refer to the community of believers in Jesus Christ. It can refer to:

<!-- p8 -->
- **The local church**: A particular gathering of believers in a specific place (for example, the church in Corinth, Philippi, etc.).

<!-- p9 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p10 -->
> **<u>1 Corinthians 1:2</u>** (NKJV)
>
> To the church of God which is at Corinth, to those sanctified in Christ Jesus, called to be saints, with all who in every place call on the name of Jesus Christ our Lord, both theirs and ours:
>

<!-- p11 -->
</span>

<!-- p12 -->
- **The universal church**: The collective body of all believers across time and space.

<!-- p13 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p14 -->
> **<u>Matthew 16:18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And I also say to you: You are Peter, and on this rock I will build My church, and the gates of Hades shall not prevail against it.
>

<!-- p15 -->
</span>

<!-- p16 -->
The church is the people, not the building or the place where they gather. **The called-out ones**: this reflects the idea that Christians are called out of the world and set apart for God’s purposes (for example, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:9</span>) for a specific mission and purpose (the Great Commission). Technically, the church is any two or more believers gathering together (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="18" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 18:20</span>). The church is usually organized in regular meetings, and perhaps at a specific place and time, but wherever two or more gather in the name of Jesus, there the church is!

<!-- p17 -->
The church was founded by Jesus and grew in the first century AD through the work of the disciples who followed Him. The church we have and experience today in all its local bodies across the earth is a direct result of the work of Christ and His original apostles. And as believers, we should commit to a local church for fellowship, support, equipping for ministry, and to do good works!

<!-- p18 -->
And to help us understand what the church is, the Scriptures give us powerful images that show what it is supposed to be.

<!-- p19 -->
## Understanding the Church

<!-- p20 -->
There are several key metaphors used to depict the characteristics of the church:

<!-- p21 -->
1.  The Body

<!-- p22 -->
2.  The Bride

<!-- p23 -->
3.  The Temple

<!-- p24 -->
4.  The Family

<!-- p25 -->
5.  The Flock

<!-- p26 -->
6.  The Vine and the Branches

<!-- p27 -->
Each metaphor highlights a unique aspect of the church, and together these images reveal the church’s identity as a unified and diverse community under the leadership of Christ.

<!-- p28 -->
### The Body

<!-- p29 -->
The church is often described as the **body of Christ**, which emphasizes both its unity and its diversity. With Christ as the head, the church functions as a living organism in which each believer plays an essential and sometimes varied role. Every member is uniquely gifted and contributes to the health and mission of the body according to their particular function. This image highlights the mutual dependence of believers, especially that the body must be connected in order to function, and that our roles are cooperative, not competitive. When every part works together, the body grows and flourishes in unity and love (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:12-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:15-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 1:18</span>).

<!-- p30 -->
### The Bride

<!-- p31 -->
The church is described as the **Bride of Christ**, reflecting an intimate, loving relationship with Jesus. Just as a bride is honored and prepared for her husband, the church is sanctified and cleansed by Christ’s self-giving love. This metaphor captures the devotion and faithfulness expected of the church, as well as the deep affection Christ has for his people. This image also points to the ultimate union between Christ and the church, celebrated at the marriage supper of the Lamb, where the church is presented in glory, without spot or blemish (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="25-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:25-27</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 19:7-9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="21" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 21:2</span>).

<!-- p32 -->
### The Temple

<!-- p33 -->
The church is described as the **temple of God**, the dwelling place of his Spirit. Believers are described as living stones being built together on the foundation of the apostles and prophets, with Christ himself as the cornerstone. This holy structure symbolizes God’s presence among his people and their role as a community of worship and prayer. The temple image underscores the importance of holiness and the church’s corporate purpose of glorifying God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 3:16-17</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:19-22</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:4-5</span>).

<!-- p34 -->
### The Family

<!-- p35 -->
The church is described as the **family of God**, highlighting the unity of relationships and care among its members. By the Spirit of adoption believers are made children of God and members of his household. This family image emphasizes the love, belonging, and shared inheritance that believers have in Christ. It also reflects the call to treat one another with compassion and humility as brothers and sisters in God’s family (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:19</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:15-17</span>). Moreover, through the image of the family, it portrays how church members ought to mature, being taught and nurtured by spiritual mothers and fathers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:1-2</span>), so that we move from infants who drink the pure milk of the word to adults capable of eating the solid food of the word (Hebrews 5).

<!-- p36 -->
### The Flock

<!-- p37 -->
The church is described as the **flock**, sheep under the care of Jesus the good Shepherd. This image conveys the church’s dependence on Christ for guidance, provision, and protection. It also affirms the responsibility of church leaders to shepherd God’s flock faithfully as under-shepherds, following Christ’s example in love and care. The image of the flock speaks of trust, obedience, and the close relationship between the great Shepherd and his sheep (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:14-16</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:2</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>).

<!-- p38 -->
### The Vine and the Branches

<!-- p39 -->
The church is described as the **vine and the branches**, showing the essential connection between believers and Christ. Jesus is the true vine, and believers are the branches who must abide in him to bear fruit. This metaphor highlights dependence on Christ for spiritual vitality, growth, and fruitfulness. Without him believers can do nothing, but by abiding in him they glorify God and fulfill their purpose. And Jesus will prune us so that we bear more fruit in our lives (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:1-5</span>).

<!-- p40 -->
## Roles of the Church

<!-- p41 -->
The church’s mission, like the role of the Holy Spirit, can be understood through three dimensions: upward, inward, and outward, each of which reflects a unique aspect of her calling in God’s plan. These dimensions guide the church in carrying out its role as a worshiping, edifying, and missional community.

<!-- p42 -->
### Upward: Worship and Glorifying God

<!-- p43 -->
The church’s primary purpose is to worship God and glorify Him. Corporate worship, where believers gather to glorify God together, is a visible reflection of His glory and a response to His greatness. As the psalmist declares: “*Worship the LORD in the beauty of holiness؛ tremble before Him, all the earth*” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="96" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 96:9</span>). In addition to corporate worship, the church is called to live lives of obedience and sacrifice, offering themselves as “living sacrifices” as an act of spiritual worship (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:1-2</span>). This upward focus keeps the church centered on the majesty of God and aligns her priorities with His will.

<!-- p44 -->
### Inward: Building up the Body

<!-- p45 -->
The Great Commission commands us to ‘teach them to observe’, and this goes far beyond formal methods of instruction such as this book. Disciple-making (which we discuss in more detail in the chapter on discipleship) is shaping the follower into the likeness of the one they learn from, as parents raise their child. Good parents do not hand a five-year-old an encyclopedia and challenge them to figure it out on their own! Rather, they model, demonstrate, correct, guide, nurture, love, care for, and shepherd.

<!-- p46 -->
Parents sacrifice for their children because they love them, because they are part of the same body, and because they share the same blood. And as members of the body of Christ and His family, we share the same body and share the same blood. The Lord’s Supper, through its symbolism, reminds us that we have been united to one another through the sacrifice of Christ. The act of the Lord’s Supper ought to be a point of unity, reminding us that we all need redemption, and that salvation is from God alone. And in this unity we see that no part of the body is greater than another except our head, Christ.

<!-- p47 -->
### Outward: Reaching the World

<!-- p48 -->
The most well-known part of the church’s mission extends outward to reach the world with the gospel. The Great Commission calls Jesus’ followers to evangelize and make disciples of all nations (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19-20</span>). And by the power of the Holy Spirit, the church is to be His witnesses to the ends of the earth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>).

<!-- p49 -->
Jesus is the light of the world (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 8:12</span>). Light radiates in all directions outward from the source. It illumines by its manifestations: the Word, His Spirit, and us, His body. We are the light of the world؛ our lives should radiate His light into our dark worlds and display good works that glorify God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 5:14-16</span>). Practically, this means that the church is called to advocate for justice and mercy, spiritually and physically. And as James says, our pure worship is to care for the vulnerable, the orphan, and the widow (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:27</span>).

<!-- p50 -->
## Church Leadership

<!-- p51 -->
Across Christianity there is a wide spectrum of denominations, assemblies, gatherings, and councils. There are very few general rules for church leadership, but the rules we do have are extremely important:

<!-- p52 -->
1.  Jesus is the sole head of the church. All serve Him (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 1:18</span>).

<!-- p53 -->
2.  There are high standards for those who lead; they are worthy of double honor, because they will receive a double judgment (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:17</span>).

<!-- p54 -->
2.1  They should be mature, not arrogant, and responsible (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:1-13</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 1:5-9</span>).

<!-- p55 -->
2.2.  They must be servants of all (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42-45" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 10:42-45</span>).

<!-- p56 -->
2.3.  They must devote themselves to the word and to prayer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:4</span>).

<!-- p57 -->
2.4.  They are responsible for equipping the body for the work of ministry.

<!-- p58 -->
3.  There are no greater or lesser members in the body. The verses that support this are:

<!-- p59 -->
3.1.  There should be no favoritism between rich and poor (James 2).

<!-- p60 -->
3.2. There is no distinction between Jew and Gentile، slave or free، male or female (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 3:27-28</span>).

<!-- p61 -->
Church structure، alongside rules of leadership، is important but much of it is situational and a matter of tradition rather than being universally mandated. "<u>This is how we always did it!</u>" is a common phrase؛ if you go back far enough، there was often a good reason for why they did it that way. And this is generally neither good nor bad، but a matter of taste، need، and availability.

<!-- p62 -->
A useful example of tradition as a basis for structure is the time of family dinner. The most important part of family dinner is that everyone gets food and that the meal is nourishing. The variations are endless: what they eat، how they eat، how they season their food، etc. Different configurations are generally acceptable and are to a large extent a matter of taste and preference.

<!-- p63 -->
A church that has 10,000 members has different needs and capacities than a church of 50 members. The pattern adopted in the book of Acts and across the New Testament canon was the model of elders and deacons. And while leadership structures may look different among churches and denominations، this model ultimately represents the minimum viable structure for church leadership. Let us take a look at these leadership offices and how they are chosen.

<!-- p64 -->
### Elders (Overseers)

<!-- p65 -->
Role and responsibilities:

<!-- p66 -->
- **Spiritual leadership** - Providing oversight، guidance، and pastoral care for the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:2</span>).

<!-- p67 -->
- **Teaching and preaching** - Responsible for teaching sound doctrine and protecting against false teaching (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:17</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 1:9</span>).

<!-- p68 -->
- **Shepherding the flock** - Caring for the spiritual needs of the congregation، providing a model of humility and service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:2-3</span>).

<!-- p69 -->
- **Guarding the church** - Serving as watchmen، defending the church from false doctrine and division (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29-31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:29-31</span>).

<!-- p70 -->
- **Equipping the saints** - Training believers and equipping them for works of service and discipleship (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-12</span>).

<!-- p71 -->
Selection process:

<!-- p72 -->
- **Appointed in every church** - Elders were appointed by the apostles or their delegates، often with prayer and fasting (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 14:23</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 1:5</span>).

<!-- p73 -->
- **Confirmed by the Holy Spirit** - Their appointment was viewed as being led by the Spirit، recognizing the call and gifts of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>).

<!-- p74 -->
- **Tested and approved** - Candidates had to demonstrate maturity and faithfulness before leadership was entrusted to them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:10</span>). They must not be recent converts! ((<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:6</span>)

<!-- p75 -->
### Deacons (Servants)

<!-- p76 -->
Role and responsibilities:

<!-- p77 -->
- **Practical service** - Focusing on meeting physical needs in the church، allowing the elders to devote themselves to prayer and teaching (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-4</span>).

<!-- p78 -->
- **Support of ministry** - Assisting with hospitality، care for the poor، and administrative tasks (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:3</span>).

<!-- p79 -->
- **Promoting unity** - Addressing issues of fairness and care to prevent division (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-7</span>).

<!-- p80 -->
- **Examples of faith** - They serve as models to be emulated in humility, faith, and service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:13</span>).

<!-- p81 -->
The selection process (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-6</span>):

<!-- p82 -->
- **Known to the congregation** - Chosen based on their reputation and spiritual character.

<!-- p83 -->
- **Approved by the leaders** - The apostles reviewed the chosen deacons and appointed them through prayer and the laying on of hands.

<!-- p84 -->
- **Proven in service** - The deacons were tested first to prove their faithfulness before being officially appointed (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:10</span>).

<!-- p85 -->
### Leadership qualifications

<!-- p86 -->
#### Qualifications for both elders and deacons:

<!-- p87 -->
- Moral conduct - They must be blameless, dignified, and show self-control (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:2</span>, 8).

<!-- p88 -->
- Family life - They must be faithful in marriage (“husband of one wife”) and manage their household well (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:4-5</span>, 12).

<!-- p89 -->
- Spiritual maturity - They must be tested and proven before appointment (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:6</span>, 10).

<!-- p90 -->
- Reputation - Both need a good reputation inside and outside the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:7</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
- Faith and integrity - They must hold the faith with a pure conscience and live according to their beliefs (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 3:9</span>).

<!-- p92 -->
### Examples of structures

<!-- p93 -->
Using what we have learned so far about the rules and structures of leadership, let us look at two examples of church structure found in the New Testament, Jerusalem and Antioch.

<!-- p94 -->
### The church in Jerusalem

<!-- p95 -->
The church in Jerusalem was led primarily by **the apostles**, who focused on teaching, prayer, and spiritual oversight (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:42</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:4</span>). As the congregation grew, the need for an administrative structure became apparent, especially to address practical matters such as caring for widows and distributing resources. This led to the appointment of **deacons** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:1-7</span>). These individuals, chosen for their spiritual maturity and good reputation, took on practical ministries so that the apostles could devote themselves to their spiritual responsibilities. Over time, **elders** also emerged as leaders in the church at Jerusalem. For example, during the Jerusalem Council (Acts 15), both the apostles and the elders were involved in decision-making, showing a collaborative leadership structure.

<!-- p96 -->
### The church in Antioch

<!-- p97 -->
The church in Antioch had a more decentralized and diverse leadership structure compared to Jerusalem. It was led by a team of **prophets and teachers**, including Barnabas, Saul (Paul), Simeon, Lucius, and Manaen (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 13:1</span>). The church at Antioch was also more oriented toward reaching the Gentiles, while the church at Jerusalem more clearly reflected its Jewish roots. Although the New Testament does not explicitly mention deacons in Antioch, it is likely that practical roles existed given the church’s growing size and its involvement in sending aid to Jerusalem (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29-30" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 11:29-30</span>). The church at Antioch became a model for sending missionaries, as the leaders prayed, fasted, and laid hands on Paul and Barnabas to send them out (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 13:2-3</span>). This shows a structure that balances spiritual oversight (the prophets and teachers) with missionary outreach and practical support.

<!-- p98 -->
Unlike Paul’s corrections to the Corinthians and Galatians, Scripture does not criticize the leadership structures in Jerusalem or Antioch. This suggests that these churches, under the guidance of the Holy Spirit, were free to adapt their gatherings to meet their unique needs and circumstances within the framework of sound teaching and apostolic doctrine (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:42</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>).

<!-- p99 -->
Throughout these sections certain terms are used that we have not yet defined and studied: *Apostle, Prophet, Evangelist*. These gifts are part of the fivefold ministry that Jesus gave to the church.

<!-- p100 -->
### The Fivefold Ministry Gifts

<!-- p101 -->
Jesus established five distinct ministry gifts, often referred to as the **fivefold ministry gifts**, to equip believers for service and to build up the body of Christ.

<!-- p102 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p103 -->
> **<u>Ephesians 4:11-13</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And He Himself gave some to be apostles, some prophets, some evangelists, and some pastors and teachers,
>
> for the equipping of the saints for the work of ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ,
>
> till we all come to the unity of the faith and of the knowledge of the Son of God, to a perfect man, to the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ;
>

<!-- p104 -->
</span>

<!-- p105 -->
The five gifts given to equip the saints for the work of ministry are:

<!-- p106 -->
1.  **Apostles** - Pioneers and visionaries who lay foundations, plant churches, and advance the kingdom of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:20</span>). Apostles model boldness and faith, showing how we step into new territories and take spiritual initiative.

<!-- p107 -->
2.  **Prophets** - Messengers and guides who proclaim God’s truth, bringing encouragement, edification, and direction (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>). Prophets inspire faith, call others to align with the Word of God, and demonstrate reliance on hearing God’s voice.

<!-- p108 -->
3.  **Evangelists** - Proclaimers of the gospel who focus on reaching the lost and mobilizing others to action in sharing Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:5-8</span>). Evangelists teach disciples how to share their faith effectively, model boldness in witness, and inspire compassion for the lost.

<!-- p109 -->
4.  **Pastors (shepherds)** - Caregivers and developers who protect, guide, and provide spiritual oversight for the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 20:28</span>). Pastors teach disciples the importance of community, caring for others, and accountability within the body of Christ.

<!-- p110 -->
5.  **Teachers** - Instructors and trainers who clarify doctrine and help believers grow in understanding and applying the Word of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 2:15</span>). Teachers equip disciples to study Scripture deeply, apply it in their daily lives, and teach others effectively.

<!-- p111 -->
## Leadership versus Gifts

<!-- p112 -->
A widely misunderstood aspect of leadership in the church is the distinction between leadership authority and responsibility, and specific gifts.

<!-- p113 -->
This confusion centers on our use of words like teacher, pastor, prophet, apostle, evangelist. These words are gifts. There is no office of 'apostle' in the New Testament church. There are people who do the work of an apostle, who have the gift or calling of an apostle. Possessing the gift of an apostle does not grant them authority or responsibility.

<!-- p114 -->
I am a skilled musician; I have played the saxophone for more than 20 years. I played through school and college and played semi-professionally for several summers while I was working with an evangelistic team. Although I have the skill, talent, and ability to be a saxophonist, I do not have the right to come to your church and stand on the platform with the worship team.

<!-- p115 -->
Similarly, there are people gifted in shepherding, but that does not make them ‘THE PASTOR’ of the local church nor does it make them ‘your’ pastor. The fivefold ministry gifts do not lead the church; the leaders (elders and deacons) do. It is very common for leaders to have specific fivefold ministry gifts, but that gift enhances their leadership; it does not create it. Likewise, it is common for people who are still growing and who have fivefold gifts to desire leadership, but God cares first about character, then about gifts. We all must become mature, able to discern the Word of God, filled with the Spirit, doing good works.

<!-- p116 -->
Every leader and every gift must first submit to discipleship and be part of the body.

<!-- p117 -->
Jesus gave specific ministry gifts to equip the saints for the work of ministry. These are different from the motivational gifts (discussed later in this chapter) or the manifestation gifts (discussed in the chapter on baptisms). As disciples grow, the fivefold ministry gifts equip them for service, while the elders and deacons provide oversight to ensure sound teaching and practice.

<!-- p118 -->
## Your Role in the Church

<!-- p119 -->
So far in this chapter we have covered church leadership and the core metaphors of the church. Let’s build on the metaphor of the *body* and think about a marathon runner.

<!-- p120 -->
### You are part of the whole

<!-- p121 -->
The church, as the body of Christ, depends on every member working together to accomplish its mission. Just as the body cannot function properly if it loses a part of itself, the church needs every believer to participate actively, to grow, and to serve. Every member in the body has a very important role. Being part of the body means staying connected. The body may be able to survive with diminished ability without a limb, but the limb itself cannot live if it is separated from the body. We must be an active part of the church and not become like severed members. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:24</span> says that we should meet together and not forsake meeting.

<!-- p122 -->
To truly work and function together you need fellowship! You need to know others, love them, and ideally also like them! Build strong relationships and friendships and repair them when they are under strain or have been damaged. When a joint is injured or inflamed, you must work to rest it or set it back into place; so that a dislocated joint does not become broken! A broken limb that does not heal will eventually be amputated, and you cannot be part of the body on your own.

<!-- p123 -->
We do not all have the same function. Some of us may share the gospel from a platform, and others around the dinner table. But we are all called to be ministers of reconciliation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 5:18–19</span>). And we have all been received into the priesthood of believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:9</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 1:6</span>). This means that we all ought to be able to do the work of ministry by the power of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p124 -->
Fingers work with the feet, and the feet with the ankles, and so on—each part contributes to the overall mission. Marathon runners must train if they want to be able to finish the race. You need to be prepared—not only by learning, but by actually practicing what you have learned. The church, like a marathon runner, thrives when each member plays its role in harmony.

<!-- p125 -->
There are a great many people in your life whom only you can reach. The pastor cannot be the only one trying to shepherd them, nor the evangelist the only one trying to reach them. God wants to use you to reach unbelievers by the power of His Holy Spirit. We all do the work of ministry; and the Holy Spirit helps us.

<!-- p126 -->
### You need training

<!-- p127 -->
Believers must train and be equipped for their unique roles in the church, just as a runner strengthens specific muscles for the race. You will not succeed if you jump into a marathon without training, and not all training is the same; you need to be adequately prepared for the race. Jesus gave specific ministry gifts to equip believers, not so that the holders of those gifts would do all the work themselves, but to enable every member to serve effectively in their unique role.

<!-- p128 -->
Training for a marathon will bring about transformation. You need strength and you need to change. It is our responsibility to submit to God and let Him change us by renewal (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:1–2</span>). Transformation should lead you to maturity, enabling you to carry your own burdens and support others. You should be able to bear much fruit. This will be discussed at length in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p129 -->
Training is hard, and sometimes uncomfortable. At times you would much prefer to skip the transformation you need to go through. But if you want to reach the goal, you must stay on the narrow path. At certain times you may need warning, or comfort, or support—all offered with patience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Thessalonians 5:14</span>).

<!-- p130 -->
Every great marathon runner has a coach who trains them, and you will also need a coach to help you. Of course you have the Holy Spirit, but He often relies on His coaches—namely, the men and women who are mature in God—in the body of Christ whom you are called to work alongside.

<!-- p131 -->
As individuals, we need to submit first and foremost to God, and secondly, in this context, to a body of believers. Submission in the church means honoring godly leadership (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 13:17</span>) and submitting to one another in humility (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:21</span>).

<!-- p132 -->
Submission can be difficult, especially if you have previously experienced betrayal of trust or failed leadership. This does not mean blind obedience or control, but reflects a spirit of respect and cooperation. Universal submission demanded by those in authority can be demeaning and lead to resentment. But biblical submission, rooted in humility and discernment, strengthens the church and enhances accountability. Be ready to listen to guidance and constructive feedback.

<!-- p133 -->
Now that we have laid a solid foundation—that you are a meaningful part of the body but you need training—let us move on to finding the place that suits you.

<!-- p134 -->
### Finding your place

<!-- p135 -->
Finding your role in the church involves seeking God’s guidance, understanding your spiritual gifts, and actively engaging with your church community. It is a journey of prayer, exploration, and growth, in which you discover how God has uniquely equipped you to contribute to His kingdom. It is also very important to note that your role in the church will likely change over time; it is unlikely to remain the same throughout your life.

<!-- p136 -->
Here is a practical approach to help you find your place, presented as sequential steps for clarity, but you will likely repeat the same step several times during your walk with God.

<!-- p137 -->
#### Step 1 - Seek God’s guidance through prayer and the Word

<!-- p138 -->
Begin by **seeking God’s guidance through prayer**. God is the ultimate source of your purpose and calling (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="29" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 29:11</span>). Pray regularly for clarity on how you can serve in the church, asking Him to reveal your spiritual gifts and inclinations. Trust that He will direct your steps when you submit your plans to His will (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 3:5–6</span>).

<!-- p139 -->
**Study God’s Word** to deepen your understanding of the church’s mission and your role in it. Key passages such as 1 Corinthians 12 and <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11–12</span> highlight spiritual gifts and the diversity of roles in the body of Christ. Reflect on examples of individuals in the Bible who discovered and fulfilled their roles, such as Paul, Timothy, and Lydia.

<!-- p140 -->
#### Step 2 - Discover your gifts, passions, and skills

<!-- p141 -->
Identifying your **spiritual gifts** is an essential step. You may find it helpful to take a spiritual gifts assessment. These gifts are given by God for the edification of the church. There are several categories of gifts mentioned in the New Testament:

<!-- p142 -->
- **Motivational gifts** given by God: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:6-8</span>

<!-- p143 -->
- Will be discussed after this list.

<!-- p144 -->
- The fivefold ministry gifts given by Jesus: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-13</span>

<!-- p145 -->
- Discussed earlier in this chapter.

<!-- p146 -->
- Manifestation gifts given by the Holy Spirit: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span>

<!-- p147 -->
- Discussed in the chapter on baptism.

<!-- p148 -->
The **motivational gifts** reflect the way a believer is shaped to think, feel, and act. They motivate believers to serve in specific ways that build up the church. Each gift complements the others, ensuring that the church functions as one unified body with diverse roles (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:4-5</span>). They function as grace-endowed abilities (Greek: ***charis** - khar'-ece - **G5463 -** unmerited gift*) that are given by God, not earned or learned. Although the gifts come from God, a person who has a gift can become more proficient in using it.

<!-- p149 -->
The motivational gifts are:

<!-- p150 -->
1.  **Prophecy** - A God-given inclination to boldly proclaim truth, often with an emphasis on conviction and righteousness, directing others to align with God’s Word.\*

<!-- p151 -->
2.  **Service (Helps)** - Meets practical needs through acts of service and assistance to support others.

<!-- p152 -->
3.  **Teaching** - Explains and clarifies truth, helping others understand and apply God’s Word.

<!-- p153 -->
4.  **Exhortation (Encouragement)** - Motivates, encourages, and comforts others to inspire growth and perseverance.

<!-- p154 -->
5.  **Giving** - Provides resources generously to meet needs and advance God’s work without seeking recognition.

<!-- p155 -->
6.  **Leadership (Administration)** - Organizes and leads others to achieve goals with vision and efficiency.

<!-- p156 -->
7.  **Mercy** - Shows compassion and care to comfort and support those who are suffering or in need.

<!-- p157 -->
\* Prophecy in the context of Romans is understood to be different from prophecy as a manifestation gift of the Holy Spirit (1 Corinthians). The Greek word used is the same in both passages, but the context is different. For more in-depth study on the difference, I recommend reading:

<!-- p158 -->
- *The Gift of Prophecy* by Kenneth E. Hagin ([www.rhema.org](http://www.rhema.org))

<!-- p159 -->
<!-- -->

<!-- p160 -->
- *Gifts and Ministries of the Holy Spirit* by Lester Sumrall

<!-- p161 -->
When identifying your gifts, think about your **passion and skills**. God often calls us to ministry in the areas we feel passionate about or in which we have skill (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 31:2-6</span>). Reflect on what excites you in serving God and others, and consider how your professional skills, hobbies, and life experiences can contribute to ministry.

<!-- p162 -->
Seek guidance from **leaders and mentors** who can offer wisdom and insight (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 15:22</span>). Meet with a church pastor or ministry leader to discuss your interests and ask for honest feedback about where you might fit best. Pray over the feedback you receive. Consider joining a discipleship group to receive ongoing training and support.

<!-- p163 -->
#### Step 3 - Be faithful now

<!-- p164 -->
The Parable of the Talents (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-30" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 25:14-30</span>) reminds us that we must be faithful in little things to be entrusted with greater ones (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 16:10</span>).

<!-- p165 -->
**Get involved and serve** in various ministries to explore where God is calling you. Start small by volunteering in areas of need within your church and in the ministries they support. For example:

<!-- p166 -->
- Join the hospitality team, children’s ministry, or outreach programs. Consider leading a guided discipleship group for new believers around this book *Principles of Faith,* using the group discussion questions and the activities at the end of the chapters to facilitate dialogue.

<!-- p167 -->
- Be open to stepping out of your comfort zone to discover new opportunities.

<!-- p168 -->
- Notice where God is working through you and where you feel most effective.

<!-- p169 -->
Practice giving. We can give to the church in many meaningful ways, and we are commanded to do so to accomplish the mission.

<!-- p170 -->
- Time - We can give our time and volunteer. When we give our time, we are using our efforts and our gifts. This can also be a great way to learn new skills and increase existing ones.

<!-- p171 -->
- Resources - We can give from our resources. Often today this is most likely through financial giving to the church and to ministries (not just “the institution”, but also to those in need in the church). Giving money is helpful, because the church can purchase needed resources with it, but people have given their material resources beyond money, such as extra homes, clothing, cars, food, building materials, tools, etc. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="32-35" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 4:32-35</span> we see an example of the early believers giving freely, sharing the proceeds from selling land and extra houses and giving so that ‘no one had need’.

<!-- p172 -->
Working side by side with others in ministry can reveal your strengths and deepen your understanding of your role. Get involved in small groups or church activities to strengthen connections. Listen to the burdens and needs of those around you and consider how you can support them. Building relationships within the church helps you connect with others and understand the needs of the community.

<!-- p173 -->
#### Step 4 - Ask for help!

<!-- p174 -->
Jesus said that in life we will face trouble! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:33</span>) And we are encouraged not to carry the weight of our burdens alone but to cast them on God who cares for us! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 5:7</span>)

<!-- p175 -->
Sometimes in our journey we may sustain an injury. Marathon runners, even when they do “everything right,” may strain their bodies to the point of injury, or they may stumble and fall.

<!-- p176 -->
Not every ‘fall’ is related to sin, but some is, and not every weakness is ‘our fault,’ for sometimes others sin against us. Regardless of the cause of our weakness or injury, we should be restored and healed.

<!-- p177 -->
There is great freedom in seeking inner healing and restoration. We are called to cover the fragile parts of the body (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:23</span>) and to restore those who have fallen into transgressions (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:1</span>).

<!-- p178 -->
There is much to be said on this topic, and we survey topics of healing in general in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p179 -->
#### Step 5 - Be patient

<!-- p180 -->
Pay attention to confirmation from God and from others. God often confirms your calling through fruit, joy, and the commendation of those around you. Reflect on the places where your efforts have borne fruit and listen to feedback regarding the ministry’s impact. This can help you identify where you are most effective in serving the body of Christ.

<!-- p181 -->
Be patient and trust God’s timing. Discovering your role may take time and involve trial and error (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 1:6</span>). Trust that God will guide you as long as you remain steadfast, faithful, and open to His guidance. During this time, commit to growth and training by attending workshops, Bible studies, and other resources that equip you for ministry. Do this with the church, and get involved with the body of Christ to discover your gifts.

<!-- p182 -->
Remember that the church is not a building or an institution; it is a living, vibrant body made up of people like you. By stepping into your role, you contribute to its mission and glorify Christ, its head.
