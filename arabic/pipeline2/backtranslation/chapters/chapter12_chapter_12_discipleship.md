# Chapter 12 - Discipleship

<!-- p1 -->
Discipleship means following Jesus، learning from Him، and becoming more like Him in the way we live and love. It involves a lifelong journey of obedience، transformation، and service، led by the Holy Spirit، and rooted in the Word of God.

<!-- p2 -->
Discipleship is central to the Great Commission. Jesus defined this mission in:

<!-- p3 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p4 -->
> **<u>Matthew 28:18-20</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And Jesus came and spoke to them, saying، "All authority has been given to Me in heaven and on earth.
>
> Go therefore and make disciples of all nations، baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit،
>
> teaching them to observe all things that I have commanded you؛ and lo, I am with you always, to the end of the age." Amen.
>

<!-- p5 -->
</span>

<!-- p6 -->
We، His followers، are to *“go and make disciples of all nations.”* Discipleship is about personal growth and multiplying that growth through teaching others to follow Christ. Discipleship is the means by which we build the Kingdom of God؛ and the teachings of Jesus are the message.

<!-- p7 -->
The call to be a disciple is more than attending church or memorizing verses from the Bible- it is a call to live like Jesus and to partner with Him in the advance of the Kingdom of God. True discipleship helps believers become spiritually mature and effective for service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:13</span>). It shapes the heart and the skills needed to serve others and share the gospel. As Paul instructed Timothy in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 2:2</span>، discipleship follows a pattern of spiritual multiplication، where trained disciples continue to raise up new followers to lead others to Christ.

<!-- p8 -->
Discipleship is both an individual calling and a global mission. On a personal level، it involves growing in faith، developing an active spiritual life، and being in relationship with others &amp; God. At the same time، it fuels the church’s mission to reach every nation with the gospel، ensuring that Christ’s message continues to expand and transform lives across generations.

<!-- p9 -->
## Why Discipleship Matters

<!-- p10 -->
The impact of discipleship goes beyond personal transformation- it has a Kingdom impact. Paul reminded Timothy in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 2:2</span> to entrust what he had learned to faithful people who would be able to teach others also. This pattern across generations highlights how discipleship is the engine that drives the church’s growth and sustainability. Discipleship matters because it is the way Jesus chose to spread the gospel and build His church.

<!-- p11 -->
Instead of focusing on large crowds، Jesus intentionally invested in a small group of followers، teaching them، setting an example in faithfulness for them، and preparing them to carry His message forward. This personal relational approach ensured the multiplication of His message، as each disciple is equipped to make more disciples.

<!-- p12 -->
At its core، discipleship is central to fulfilling the Great Commission (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19-20</span>). Jesus did not call us merely to make new believers، but to make disciples-followers rooted in the Word of God، empowered by the Holy Spirit، and equipped for service. Through discipleship، believers grow in their knowledge of Scripture، develop spiritual practices، and learn to walk in obedience to Christ. It equips the church to impact the world، proclaim the gospel، and reflect the glory of God to all nations.

<!-- p13 -->
### Nature versus Nurture

<!-- p14 -->
In the study of biology, psychology, and anthropology، we often consider the impact of nature versus nurture. The frequently asked question is: Is a person more the product of innate genetic inheritance embedded within? Or is he or she more the product of environment and upbringing? The truth is that every person is a mixture of nature and nurture. Both elements، in strong interaction، coordinate the growth of the human person.

<!-- p15 -->
When you accepted Jesus، the miracle of salvation made you born again from imperishable seed. You received a godly nature! And your spiritual DNA was rewritten! This godly nature stirs our hearts to draw near to our heavenly Father، to know Him، and to experience Him.

<!-- p16 -->
And just as the newborn، whose wonderful beautiful innocent nature should be shaped by loving parents to become a whole mature person، so we too must be shaped and nurtured in order to develop.

<!-- p17 -->
Discipleship is the process of spiritual growth that builds on our godly nature and makes us spiritually mature. And by investing in discipleship، we ensure the transmission of the faith، the raising up of new leaders، and that the church remains healthy and vibrant-locally and globally.

<!-- p18 -->
### The Didactic Model versus the Apprentice Model

<!-- p19 -->
In modern Western education، the primary relationship that connects the student is to the subject matter. The student’s learning is oriented toward content، material، books، videos، assignments، etc. Educational transformation happens through the curriculum. This method of teaching is known as *the didactic model*.

<!-- p20 -->
The didactic model of education has not always been the norm. The model and method used in the time of Jesus were the *apprentice model.* This learning model affirms that knowledge is caught more than it is taught, and it focuses more on the relationship between the student and the teacher than on the relationship between the student and the curriculum.

<!-- p21 -->
The didactic model gradually became the default approach over about a thousand years, beginning with the early European universities of the Middle Ages in the 11<sup>th</sup> - 13<sup>th</sup> centuries, the invention of printing in the 15<sup>th</sup> century, the Enlightenment in the 17<sup>th</sup> &amp; 18<sup>th</sup> centuries, and the impact of the Industrial Revolution on information and education in the 19<sup>th</sup> &amp; 20<sup>th</sup> centuries.

<!-- p22 -->
The advantage of the didactic model is that it scales more efficiently than the apprentice model. Massive Open Online Courses (MOOCs) can include thousands of students at the same time, whereas even a very skilled teacher can have only a few dozen apprentices at once.

<!-- p23 -->
The outcome of the didactic model is that the student masters the content, yet the student does not become like the teacher. The teacher is a facilitator of the curriculum. The teacher transfers knowledge.

<!-- p24 -->
In the apprentice model, the teacher shares more than mere knowledge; they bequeath wisdom. And the result of the apprentice model is that the student becomes like the teacher to such an extent that they can replicate the teacher’s work and take on apprentices of their own.

<!-- p25 -->
The didactic model has a benefit and a place in the kingdom of God; a good example is this book or any other Christian instructional material. Jesus, as a rabbi, skilled and trained in the rabbinic method, practiced the apprentice model as the primary model for his disciples.

<!-- p26 -->
Jesus describes the apprentice model in:

<!-- p27 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p28 -->
> **<u>Luke 6:40</u>** (NKJV)
>
> A disciple is not above his teacher, but everyone who is perfectly trained will be like his teacher.
>

<!-- p29 -->
</span>

<!-- p30 -->
In the didactic model the student becomes a bearer of knowledge, whereas in the apprentice model the student becomes like the teacher. Let us study the key words from that passage in Luke.

<!-- p31 -->
[See Table 6](../tables/table6.md)

<!-- p32 -->
In short, the purpose and aim of the apprentice model used in discipleship are twofold:

<!-- p33 -->
**First, the disciple becomes fully trained**, like the teacher. This does not mean the disciple imitates everything their teacher does. We were not commanded to make identical copies, but to build men and women to maturity who can carry the message of Jesus to the ends of the earth.

<!-- p34 -->
**Second, the disciple now becomes a teacher of others**. The disciple must pass on what they have learned to someone else, otherwise the message of the kingdom stops with them. This does not necessarily mean they will have exactly the same gifts as their teacher, or that they will enter full-time ministry, but it does mean they must make disciples for their training to be complete.

<!-- p35 -->
Any living being that does not reproduce is sterile, and its lineage ends. An animal that cannot reproduce is unhealthy, and likewise a church that cannot reproduce is unhealthy.

## Jesus’ Model and the Discipleship Process

<!-- p37 -->
Jesus provided the mature and complete example of discipleship in the Gospels with his chosen disciples. We should use these steps generally as a foundation for our journey to become disciples and make disciples.

<!-- p38 -->
The apprentice model has two roles: the teacher and the student. Every teacher was a student, and we hope every student becomes a teacher. I will use the terms *disciple* to refer to the student and *disciple-maker* to refer to the teacher/trainer.

<!-- p39 -->
Every discipleship relationship goes through a sequence of eight stages. These stages, or steps, are not entirely separate from one another; the relationship often moves among them. In each stage there is a particular focus on the disciple and on the disciple-maker. We will study the qualities of disciple-makers in depth later in this chapter.

<!-- p40 -->
The eight stages of discipleship are:

<!-- p41 -->
1.  Invitation/Call

<!-- p42 -->
2.  Teaching

<!-- p43 -->
3.  Demonstration

<!-- p44 -->
4.  Participation/Trying

<!-- p45 -->
5.  Correction

<!-- p46 -->
6.  Empowerment/Delegation

<!-- p47 -->
7.  Multiplication

<!-- p48 -->
8.  Staying Connected

<!-- p49 -->
### Invitation/Call

<!-- p50 -->
Discipleship begins with a personal invitation from Jesus to follow him. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 4:19</span>, Jesus called his first disciples, saying: *“Follow Me, and I will make you fishers of men.”* This invitation was both relational and transformative—it required leaving old ways of life and entering into a new purpose centered on God’s mission. Jesus’ call was not to spectatorship, but to participation in his ministry and the work of his kingdom.

<!-- p51 -->
Disciple-makers today must invite others into authentic relationships that inspire spiritual growth and obedience. The call to discipleship is highly personal. Jesus meets individuals where they are, addressing their specific needs and circumstances, but he also calls them deeper—to grow, to serve, and to imitate him more. This pattern reflects the relational nature of discipleship, where the focus is not merely on knowledge but on a life-changing relationship with Christ.

<!-- p52 -->
### Teaching

<!-- p53 -->
Teaching is at the heart of discipleship. Jesus spent a significant part of his ministry teaching the principles of the kingdom of God, especially in the Sermon on the Mount (Matthew 5-7). His teachings provided a foundation for living righteously, loving others, and walking in obedience to God. Teaching the word of God remains central to discipleship today, as the Scriptures are the primary tool for spiritual growth, correction, and training in righteousness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>).

<!-- p54 -->
Disciple-makers must be committed to faithfully teaching the Bible and helping others understand and apply it in their lives. Teaching goes beyond transferring knowledge—it also requires embodying a Christlike way of life so that disciples can see faith at work. Jesus embodied the truths he taught, showing that effective teaching is both verbal and visible. Similarly, disciple-makers should strive to live what they teach, offering practical examples of obedience and faithfulness.

<!-- p55 -->
### Demonstration

<!-- p56 -->
Jesus did not stop at teaching his disciples—he showed them how to live out the truths he proclaimed. Jesus demonstrated the meaning of love, service, and ministry in practical ways. Examples of this in the Gospels include his washing of the disciples’ feet as an act of humility and service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 13:12-15</span>), his healing of the sick, and his feeding of the hungry. Through his actions, he provided a model of compassion, faith, and boldness, leaving a clear example for his followers to emulate.

<!-- p57 -->
Discipleship requires demonstration, not just telling. Disciple-makers today should be transparent about their faith journey, including struggles and victories, so that others can see how faith works in everyday life. Whether by praying with those in need, sharing testimonies, or serving the community, discipleship flourishes when it is rooted in practical demonstration. Actions often speak louder than words, making demonstration a powerful tool for training others in the faith.

<!-- p58 -->
### Participation/Trying

<!-- p59 -->
Jesus did not keep his disciples on the sidelines—he actively involved them in ministry experiences. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 9:1-6</span> and <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 10:5-15</span>, Jesus sent the disciples to preach the gospel, heal the sick, and cast out demons. These practical opportunities allowed them to learn by doing, gaining confidence and skills under his guidance.

<!-- p60 -->
Contemporary discipleship should also include practical participation. Disciple-makers should invite disciples to participate in ministry, whether by leading prayer groups, sharing testimonies, or serving at outreach events. Providing safe spaces for practice enables disciples to grow in boldness while receiving support and encouragement. Learning happens best when it is applied, and participation is the key to turning theory into action.

<!-- p61 -->
### Correction

<!-- p62 -->
Growth often requires correction, and Jesus lovingly corrected his disciples when they misunderstood his teachings or failed in their tasks. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33-37" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 9:33-37</span>, he addressed their pride by teaching them about servant leadership. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-32" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 22:31-32</span>, he prayed for Peter, preparing him to recover from failure and strengthen others.

<!-- p63 -->
Correction in discipleship should be grace-saturated and constructive—rooted in Scripture and aimed at restoration (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 4:2</span>). It is not condemnation but helping disciples grow and avoid pitfalls. Disciple-makers should provide accountability, using the Word of God to guide and encourage growth while modeling humility in receiving correction themselves.

<!-- p64 -->
### Empowerment/Delegation

<!-- p65 -->
Jesus did not stop at teaching and correcting—he empowered his disciples to carry out his mission. He gave them authority to preach the gospel, heal the sick, and cast out demons (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:18-20</span>). After his resurrection, he promised the Holy Spirit as the source of power for their ministry (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>).

<!-- p66 -->
Contemporary discipleship should include empowering others to operate in their spiritual gifts and minister effectively. This includes identifying gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-13</span>), encouraging their use, and releasing disciples into ministry. Empowerment also emphasizes reliance on the Holy Spirit for power, boldness, and wisdom, ensuring disciples are spiritually equipped for the work God has called them to.

<!-- p67 -->
### Multiplication

<!-- p68 -->
The goal of discipleship is not merely personal growth—but multiplication. Jesus modeled this by training the disciples to make more disciples (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19-20</span>). After his resurrection, the apostles continued this multiplying mission, spreading the gospel and planting churches throughout the known world. Paul’s instruction in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 2:2</span> reinforces this principle, urging Timothy to teach others who will teach others.

<!-- p69 -->
Discipleship entails equipping disciples to disciple others, creating a chain reaction of faith that expands God’s kingdom. This pathway requires intentionality—teaching disciples how to shepherd, teach, and train others, while being sent with confidence and purpose (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 10:15</span>).

<!-- p70 -->
### Staying Connected

<!-- p71 -->
Discipleship does not end when someone is sent. Jesus modeled ongoing connection with his disciples, continuing to encourage and teach them even after his resurrection (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="21" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 21:15-19</span>). The early church maintained this pattern, meeting regularly for teaching, fellowship, and prayer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42-47" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:42-47</span>).

<!-- p72 -->
Contemporary discipleship should also strengthen ongoing relationships, providing accountability, encouragement, and support as disciples continue to grow and lead. Maintaining unity in the body of Christ ensures that disciples are never isolated but remain strengthened and encouraged in their faith journey.

<!-- p73 -->
## Who Does Discipleship?

<!-- p74 -->
Everyone in the body of Christ is first responsible to become disciples, then to contribute to the disciple-making process. There are variations in gifts and functions, so our contributions may look different, but we are all responsible. The people ultimately more responsible than others for the state and health of discipleship in the local church are the leaders (**elders and deacons - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 14:23</span>**), but everyone has a role.

<!-- p75 -->
Discipleship is a living, growing process - like nurturing a child to maturity. Healthy discipleship brings strength and life to people. And like a parent who strengthens and nourishes a child, thus making the child healthy and mature, disciple-makers help to nourish, strengthen, and mature disciples. The ultimate goal of discipleship is not 'the transfer of knowledge,' but transformation.

<!-- p76 -->
We must all become complete (mature) and make disciples. And although we will possess diverse gifts, there are common characteristics for all disciple-makers.

<!-- p77 -->
### Traits of a Disciple-Maker

<!-- p78 -->
A disciple-maker is someone who not only follows Christ, but is also committed to helping others grow in their faith. This role requires specific traits that reflect both spiritual maturity and practical devotion. Disciple-makers must continually rely on God’s strength, wisdom, and grace to overcome the challenges that accompany leading others in their spiritual journeys.

<!-- p79 -->
#### Rooted in Christ

<!-- p80 -->
The foundation of a disciple maker’s life must be deeply rooted in Christ. Jesus said: *"I am the vine; you are the branches. If you remain in Me and I in you, you will bear much fruit; for without Me you can do nothing"* (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:5</span>). Effective disciple makers know that the ability to grow and help others comes from staying close to Jesus every day. However, resistance to personal growth and spiritual change can hinder this dependence. Disciple makers must continually cultivate their personal relationship with Jesus through prayer, Bible study, and obedience, embodying what it means to abide in Christ for those they disciple.

<!-- p81 -->
#### Committed to the Mission

<!-- p82 -->
A disciple maker must remain committed to the mission of making disciples as Jesus commanded in the Great Commission (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19-20</span>). This mission requires intentional focus and prioritization, recognizing that disciple making is not an optional task but the primary purpose of the church. However, challenges such as limited time, busyness, and personal distractions can make it difficult to maintain focus. Disciple makers must intentionally set aside time for mentoring, teaching, and relationship-building, and view disciple making as a lifestyle, not a casual activity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 19:10</span>).

<!-- p83 -->
#### Led by the Spirit

<!-- p84 -->
Disciple making cannot be accomplished by human strength alone; it requires the guidance and empowerment of the Holy Spirit. Jesus promised His disciples that they would receive power when the Holy Spirit came upon them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>), equipping them to be witnesses and ministers of the gospel. Disciple makers must be sensitive to the Spirit’s leading, relying on His wisdom to navigate complex situations, cultural barriers, and difficult conversations. This dependence on the Holy Spirit enables disciple makers to act with love, boldness, and spiritual discernment, ensuring that their efforts are Spirit-led rather than self-driven.

<!-- p85 -->
#### Humble and Teachable

<!-- p86 -->
Humility is a distinguishing mark of an effective disciple maker. As Paul exhorted: *"Let nothing be done through selfish ambition or conceit, but in lowliness of mind let each esteem others better than himself"* (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 2:3</span>). Disciple makers recognize that they too are lifelong learners, and they must remain teachable and accountable. They welcome correction and feedback, embodying humility for those they mentor. Pride can be a major barrier to growth, both for disciple makers and their disciples. Embracing accountability and maintaining a servant’s heart build trust and create an environment for growth and transparency.

<!-- p87 -->
#### Patient and Persevering

<!-- p88 -->
Discipleship is rarely instantaneous—it is a long-term process that requires patience and perseverance. Paul reminds believers that *"let us not grow weary while doing good, for in due season we shall reap if we do not lose heart"* (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:9</span>). Growth in faith often involves stumbles, questions, and failures, yet disciple makers are called to walk alongside others, offering encouragement and support every step of the way. It is easy to feel discouraged when progress seems slow, but disciple makers trust that God is at work even when results are not immediately visible.

<!-- p89 -->
Being a disciple maker requires a combination of spiritual dependence, commitment, and humility. It is a calling that demands patience and perseverance while remaining Spirit-led and teachable. Disciple makers are not expected to be perfect, but they should be faithful—abiding in Christ, investing in others, and confident that God brings growth and transformation in His timing. These qualities reflect the love of Christ and help others become mature disciples who can, in turn, disciple others.

<!-- p90 -->
## Practical Tools for Discipleship

<!-- p91 -->
Effective discipleship requires intentional tools and practices that foster spiritual growth, accountability, and community. These tools help disciples build a strong foundation in the faith, grow in intimacy with God, and be equipped to disciple others. Practical methods such as Bible study, prayer, small groups, and mentoring provide both the framework and the support needed for ongoing growth.

<!-- p92 -->
### Bible Study

<!-- p93 -->
Deep engagement with Scripture is the foundation of discipleship. Jesus affirmed the importance of God’s Word in spiritual growth, declaring that *"Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceeds from the mouth of God"* (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 4:4</span>). Regular Bible study and memorization enable disciples to internalize truth, resist lies, and apply God’s wisdom in daily life.

<!-- p94 -->
Disciples should be encouraged to immerse themselves in Scripture, not merely as an academic exercise, but to know God and see their hearts transformed (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="119" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="105" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 119:105</span>). Practical curricula, such as reading plans, study guides, and group discussions, provide opportunities to explore doctrine and discuss application within community. By building regular study habits, disciples lay a firm foundation for spiritual maturity and fruitful ministry (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>).

<!-- p95 -->
### Prayer

<!-- p96 -->
Prayer is the lifeline of discipleship, connecting believers to God’s presence, power, and guidance. Jesus taught His disciples how to pray in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 11:1-4</span>, emphasizing both dependence on God and spiritual alignment with His will. Through prayer, disciples learn to seek God’s wisdom, intercede for others, and receive strength for service.

<!-- p97 -->
Teaching disciples how to pray—individually and corporately—equips them to cultivate intimacy with God and discern His leading (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 4:6-7</span>). Incorporating prayer journals, prayer groups, and fasting practices strengthens spiritual discipline and empowerment, ensuring that disciples remain Spirit-led and mission-focused.

<!-- p98 -->
### Small Groups and Community

<!-- p99 -->
Discipleship thrives within the context of community. The early church modeled relational discipleship through fellowship, prayer, and breaking bread together (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42-47" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:42-47</span>). Small groups and accountability partnerships provide a safe space for disciples to grow together, share their struggles, and celebrate victories.

<!-- p100 -->
The local church serves as the primary hub for discipleship relationships, providing opportunities for group Bible studies, prayer meetings, and fellowship events. These gatherings enable disciples to practice love and bear one another’s burdens (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:2</span>), and to encourage one another toward spiritual maturity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:24-25</span>). Small groups also provide a framework for multiplying leaders, as mature disciples train others and send them to lead groups and mentor new believers.

<!-- p101 -->
### Mentoring

<!-- p102 -->
Mentoring provides a personal and intentional approach to discipleship. Jesus embodied this by investing deeply in the twelve disciples, walking with them through teaching, correction, and encouragement. Likewise, one-on-one relationships enable disciple-makers to tailor guidance to individual needs, helping disciples grow in faith, character, and leadership.

<!-- p103 -->
Mentors serve as accountability partners, offering support, prayer, and wisdom in times of growth and struggle. Practical tools such as spiritual assessments, goal-setting, and regular check-ins help disciples stay focused and motivated. Peer relationships within mentoring structures also foster mutual growth, as both mentor and disciple learn from shared experiences (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="27" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 27:17</span>).

## **Questions for Personal Reflection**

<!-- p105 -->
1.  What does it personally mean to you to be a disciple of Jesus? How do you practically follow Him in your daily life?

<!-- p106 -->
2.  Reflect on Jesus’ model of discipleship. Which aspect of His approach—invitation, teaching, demonstration, or empowerment—stands out to you most, and why?

<!-- p107 -->
3.  How can you multiply your faith by discipling others? What challenges or fears might hold you back, and how can you overcome them? (Note that I said multiply your faith, not multiply your disciples!)

<!-- p108 -->
4.  What qualities that a disciple-maker possesses (such as patience, humility, and relational focus) do you see in yourself? Which qualities do you feel called to develop further?

<!-- p109 -->
5.  Think about the people in your life. Whom might God be calling you to disciple? How can you take the first step in building that relationship?

## Questions for Group Discussion (20-30 minutes)

<!-- p111 -->
1.  **Icebreaker question:** Who has had the greatest impact on your spiritual growth, and what did they do that influenced you?

<!-- p112 -->
2.  **Exploring Jesus’ model:** Read <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 4:19</span>. How does Jesus’ call ‘Follow Me’ embody the essence of discipleship? What does this look like today?

<!-- p113 -->
3.  **The call to make disciples:** Discuss <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19-20</span>. Why is making disciples central to the mission of the church? How can we, as individuals, participate in this mission?

<!-- p114 -->
4.  **Overcoming challenges:** What are some common obstacles to making disciples (such as time, fear, and lack of confidence)? How can we support one another in overcoming these obstacles?

<!-- p115 -->
5.  **Practical application:** What is one specific step you can take this week to invest in someone’s spiritual growth? How can we, as a group, encourage one another in this process?
