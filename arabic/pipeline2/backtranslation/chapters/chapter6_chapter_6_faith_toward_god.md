# Chapter 6 - Faith toward God

<!-- p1 -->
Faith is a foundational concept in Christianity. The word faith is used so often in Christianity that the meaning of the word is sometimes displaced by subtle ambiguity and misunderstanding. But it is not something you can hold in your hands-but rather, something that forms in your heart as you come to trust God. Conviction is faith.

<!-- p2 -->
The writer of Hebrews teaches us that we should have 'faith toward God'.

### Definition of Faith

<!-- p10 -->
Faith is an aspect that is often misunderstood and misrepresented in Christianity. As the saying goes, "There is a ditch on both sides of the road"! And because the word faith is used so much without adequate explanation in our contemporary Christianity, it can acquire false definitions that are amplified through misunderstanding. Faith is often portrayed either as a blanket statement of an abstract belief or as a quasi-mystical power or "magic," something you possess in a set and limited measure.

<!-- p11 -->
#### What Faith Is Not

<!-- p12 -->
Faith distorted in an <u>abstract</u> form carries no specific conviction or persuasion. This often shows up with a little probing, as this representation of faith cannot be made specific. If you ask, 'What do you believe?' or 'Why do you believe?', the person who understands faith this way often cannot explain beyond 'I believe.' <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 3:15</span> says that we should be ready to give an answer for the hope that is in us.

<!-- p13 -->
Sometimes people think of faith as a magical power-but that is not what the Bible teaches. Faith has to do with a real relationship built on trust. In the Bible, faith is much more dynamic and relational-it is rooted in conviction, trust, and assurance in the character of God and His promises. It is not an impersonal force but a responsive interaction with God that shapes thoughts, feelings, and actions.

<!-- p14 -->
Why is belief related to conviction?

<!-- p15 -->
Our beliefs shape the way we see the world around us and interact with it. Belief shapes perception and prompts our actions. Our minds are programmed this way-we live based on what we believe is possible.

<!-- p16 -->
We act according to what we perceive, understand, and believe is possible. When we walk down a hallway, our brain picks up the boundaries of the floor and the wall and tells our feet automatically where we can step and what should be avoided. This reality extends far beyond our physical navigation of the world around us.

<!-- p17 -->
Suppose you believe that it is impossible to become financially independent or to live a healthy and active lifestyle. Then your actions will follow that belief, and you will not do-or most likely will not even try-what you believe is impossible.

<!-- p18 -->
If you believe that God has an unloving, punitive character, that perception of Him will keep you from walking with Him as His child.

<!-- p19 -->
#### Faith Is Conviction

<!-- p20 -->
So, what is faith? Conviction! The Greek word ***pistis*** means conviction, or the result of being convinced. We should have conviction toward God. In English we might say faith (conviction) ‘**in** God’. If you are married, you may have faith ‘**in’** your spouse. You believe that they will do what they said they would do, and you believe that they are honest and genuine. This trust should extend beyond what they have said, to include them in general.

<!-- p21 -->
Developing faith, conviction, and trust in a relationship takes time and experience. We hope that in your life you have learned how to develop a relationship built on trust with another person. The principles you have learned that enable you to increase your trust in someone you know will also enable you to increase your faith in God. Faith within a relationship increases over time, through experience, with consistency, and through small and large actions that build your trust in the nature and character of the other party.

<!-- p22 -->
‘Faith toward God’, is relational. Relationships are built by two parties, not by one. The good news regarding our faith in God is that God is responsible for His side of the relationship (which was made available through the atoning death of Jesus, and the filling of His Spirit at the new birth), and He helps us on our side as well (through the work of the Comforter - the Holy Spirit). God does most of the work, and our role (as we will discuss later in this chapter) is to walk *with Him* in obedience.

<!-- p23 -->
### Jesus matures our faith

<!-- p24 -->
Jesus is the author and finisher of our faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 12:2</span>). Our faith is meant to continually grow and expand. We do not reach a single point at which we have enough ‘faith’; rather, our faith increases. Consider a deep and personal relationship you have in your life today. As in any relationship, we do not reach a specific milestone at which we now ‘know’ the person. Rather, we continue knowing them, and increase our knowledge of them, and this refines, shapes, and grows our relationship.

<!-- p25 -->
How do we grow our faith in God? By continuing to experience Him through His many manifestations, appearances, and attributes. Again, consider a deep personal relationship in your life. The depth of the relationship increases by interacting with them in different ways, different forms, different mediums, and different environments. You talk with them, message them, and perhaps write to them. You see them privately and in group contexts. You talk about what you value, and perhaps challenge one another. And the many ways extend without end. And through these many expressions you come to know the person more deeply.

<!-- p26 -->
We should all know God, through Jesus, as our Savior. Remember the names of God (*see the appendix),* for merely knowing God as Savior does not mean we have yet experienced Him as Deliverer, Healer, Counselor, and Lord.

<!-- p27 -->
If you are at a place where you have been convinced (faith) in knowing God as your Savior, then building this relationship will grant you confidence in His nature to know Him in other ways.

<!-- p28 -->
I am blessed to have several very deep relationships in my life. One of these relationships is with my wife. In our relationship, and because of the level of trust (faith) between us, my wife can tell me things she will do which I have not yet seen fulfilled, and I have confidence to believe her because of her proven track record.

<!-- p29 -->
And this example is how our deep relationship with God can be experienced, with trust and certainty! We may not have fully experienced Him yet (and in fact we will not fully experience Him in this life), but as we mature in our relationship, we can trust Him beyond the limits of what I personally know, and what I have already seen happen.

<!-- p30 -->
Jesus increases our faith through experience with Him. And an important way we can and should experience Him is through His Word.

<!-- p36 -->
When we replace the word faith with the word conviction, the sentence becomes:

<!-- p37 -->
*‘So conviction comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God’*. This truth is self-evident in nature. And one of the primary ways to have conviction is for a person to have been persuaded (convinced), and we are convinced when someone presents an ‘argument’-or provides a logical and detailed explanation.

<!-- p38 -->
There are two Greek words for ‘Word’ in the Bible-**logos** and **rhema**. Understanding the difference helps us see how faith grows.

<!-- p39 -->
**Logos**: Generally refers to the concept, the idea, the mind/reason, or the broader expression of the Word of God. It includes a basic or universal truth or message, and is often used to describe the eternal wisdom of God and Christ Himself as the living Word.

<!-- p40 -->
**Rhema (G4487)**: emphasizes a **specific spoken word or saying**. It usually refers to a particular word or message given at a certain moment, especially a spoken or declared word that applies directly to someone's situation.

<!-- p41 -->
*Logos* is like the full message of a book, whereas *Rhema* is like a specific passage or sentence that addresses a person directly at a particular moment. And while *Logos* is God's comprehensive truth, *Rhema* is when a portion of that truth is spoken to an individual in a personal, applicable way. Together they illustrate the fullness of God's communication: *Logos* as broad and foundational truths about God's character and will, and *Rhema* as the specific applied word that brings that truth to life in a person's life and circumstance.

<!-- p42 -->
### An example of a Rhema word in practical application

<!-- p43 -->
God promises revelation and wisdom to all who ask. When He speaks to us, that utterance is a *Rhema* word. Rhema words are from God and serve to confirm, lead, and guide us. They are not a substitute for the *Logos*, nor do they carry the authority of Scripture. *Rhema* words should be judged by the same standard as prophecy or interpretation of tongues (see the chapter on the Holy Spirit for an expanded study on this topic).

<!-- p44 -->
At several points in my career, I prayed asking for divine guidance. A few years ago, I was thinking of starting to look for a new opportunity, and during a time of prayer I heard God say to me: ‘*Just wait for Me; I have an opportunity for you.*’ I began to look for a new job because I was not satisfied with my current role and was feeling discouraged. God's directive word ‘*Wait for Me*’ helped me to have peace and confidence in an otherwise turbulent season.

<!-- p45 -->
Three out of four months passed, and I continued to work hard and faithfully, keeping a positive attitude. God kept reminding me, *‘Wait for Me’*, and then His promise was fulfilled. I received an opportunity for promotion and a generous increase in pay.

<!-- p46 -->
I could have kept looking for a job; I could have left. I could have started somewhere new. And no doubt someone else would have gotten that promotion! But because I sought the Lord’s counsel and was convinced by a **Rhema word** from Him, I obeyed and benefited. God's words are **living and personal**, and He promises to give us ample and generous wisdom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:5</span>).

<!-- p47 -->
I have dozens, if not hundreds, of times when I prayed and asked God what to do, and He gave me a Rhema word. Not all of them had the same remarkable outcome, but they all brought a sense of peace and confidence that I was obeying God and that He would work things out. They were the words I needed for the moment! Often the Rhema word that God brings is drawn from the Logos. He reminds me of a passage of Scripture, “*Do not fear, for I am with you!*” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="41" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 41:10</span>), “*By His stripes we are healed*” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="53" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 53:5</span>), “*Do not grow weary in doing good!*” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:9</span>), and many more examples continue!

<!-- p48 -->
Receiving a Rhema word not only strengthens our faith, but also deepens our relationship with God, as we trust Him more in our daily decisions. We will continue our study to learn to hear God's voice in the chapter on the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p49 -->
Our faith is also strengthened by what we see God doing, but sometimes what we see in the natural world challenges what we hear from God. Reconciling the disparity between what we see and hear is an important ministry of the Holy Spirit, who promises to guide us into all truth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span>).

### Faith greater than what we can see

<!-- p51 -->
After His resurrection, Jesus appeared multiple times to many of His disciples. Thomas (one of the Twelve) was not with the other disciples, so he missed the appearance of the risen Savior. Thomas believed in Jesus and trusted his fellow disciples, but he did not believe that Jesus had risen bodily; he insisted that he could not truly believe until he experienced Him personally.

When Jesus appeared to His disciples and calmed them (I imagine it’s quite terrifying when someone appears in your midst having come through closed doors), He spoke to Thomas. He did not rebuke Thomas or blame him or accuse him of being part of the evil and adulterous generation seeking a sign (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 16:4</span>) but revealed Himself to Thomas.

<!-- p60 -->
And Jesus goes on to say, ‘*Blessed are those who have not seen and have believed*d’. This is not Jesus encouraging a ‘blind or ignorant faith’ nor a rejection of using empirical evidence. Rather, He is saying that we are more blessed when we can believe without the need for empirical proof. Why? If our faith, our trust, is confined only to what we have seen empirically proven, we could never have confidence in future action. We would have great confidence in what has been accomplished, but we would not walk in the peace of God for our tomorrow and our future.

<!-- p61 -->
Using a relational example, how difficult would my relationship with my wife be if my stance were always ‘<u>I’ll believe it when I see it!</u>’? Instead, a trust-based relationship says: ‘I know you are a trustworthy person’.

3. The action of our faith is manifested in obedience.

<!-- p91 -->

<!-- p92 -->
In the workplace, when a manager or supervisor gives instructions, they are not “talking just to talk,” but providing guidance they intend their employees to follow.

<!-- p93 -->
When we truly listen to God, it should lead us to action—to trust Him and obey. As the Scriptures say in:

<!-- p94 -->

The counselor will teach him the principles of financial freedom and help him build a budget. This guidance will give him (confidence/faith) in what he needs to do to be debt-free. But confidence is only the first step, for the person must obey the principles he has learned through disciplined practice in order to receive the good outcome and the blessing of being debt-free.

<!-- p108 -->
The more we build our faith in God, the more it will naturally bear obedience in us. And this obedience is for our good and produces a good harvest. Returning to my personal example from this chapter, I would not have experienced peace at that time, nor the blessing of promotion, if I had not obeyed God’s word to me to *wait for Him*!

### Practical steps to grow your faith

<!-- p153 -->
Walking with God is a relationship, but there are skills you need to learn if you want good relationships. In earthly relationships, the skills that yield strong and secure relationships are built on:

<!-- p154 -->
- Open and free dialogue and communication

<!-- p155 -->
- Healthy conflict resolution

<!-- p156 -->
- Commitment

<!-- p157 -->
- Consistency

<!-- p158 -->
You need to show up consistently, to disclose (communicate) yourself (our thoughts and feelings), and to receive the other person (listen), and to walk with them, if you want a strong, vibrant relationship! All these skills apply to our relationship with God.

<!-- p159 -->
Alongside the basic relational skills, there are specific practices and disciplines that will strengthen your relationship with God:

<!-- p160 -->
- **Build and maintain a life of prayer; listen to and communicate with God.** Regular, sincere prayer is essential to a close relationship with God. Through prayer you can share your concerns, joys, and gratitude, seeking His guidance and strength. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 4:6-7</span>*) Being sensitive to the voice of the Holy Spirit helps you discern God’s will, find wisdom in decisions, and grow in spiritual maturity. Set aside time for quiet reflection to hear His guidance. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:14</span>*)

<!-- p161 -->
- **Spend time in worship.** Worship is a powerful means of drawing near to God, expressing love and awe. Through praise and singing, or quiet meditation, worship deepens your awareness of His presence. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="100" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 100:2-4</span>*)

<!-- p162 -->
- **Read, study, and meditate on the Bible.** God’s Word reveals His character, promises, and instructions for life. Setting aside time to study the Bible helps you grow in faith, understanding, and spiritual resilience. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 3:16-17</span>*)

<!-- p163 -->
- **Obey His commandments.** Your obedience to God’s teachings shows your love, respect, and trust in Him. Walking in obedience brings your life into alignment with His purpose and brings blessings. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:15</span>*)

<!-- p164 -->
- **Consistently attend a local church and serve others.** Engaging in a church community provides spiritual support, encouragement, and opportunities to serve others. Active participation helps build a sense of belonging and strengthens your commitment to God’s mission. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:24-25</span>*)

<!-- p165 -->
- **Build relationships with other believers.** Fellowship with other Christians provides accountability, encouragement, and shared wisdom. These relationships enrich your faith journey and help you grow through mutual support. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PRO" data-chapter="27" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Proverbs 27:17</span>*)

<!-- p166 -->
Our ‘faith toward God’ is not a one-time event for salvation, but an ongoing practice and relationship we are meant to cultivate throughout our lives. The benefits of a relationship based on trust and mutual benefit are very great, but we must do the good work of developing that trust and that growth for it to flourish.
