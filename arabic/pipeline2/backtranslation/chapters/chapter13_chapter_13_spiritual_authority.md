# Chapter 13 - Spiritual Authority

<!-- p1 -->
Jesus is the supreme and complete source of spiritual authority. As the victorious Christ, He has the ultimate authority over all things in heaven and on earth.

<!-- p2 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p3 -->
> **<u>Matthew 28:18-20</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And Jesus came and spoke to them, saying, "All authority has been given to Me in heaven and on earth.
>
> Go therefore and make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit,
>
> teaching them to observe all things that I have commanded you; and lo, I am with you always, even to the end of the age." Amen.
>

<!-- p4 -->
</span>

<!-- p5 -->
In this chapter we will study the authority and power of Christ, which authorities and powers He has delegated to us (His Church), and how we operate in the authority of the name of Jesus. We will also study angelic and demonic beings and examine their authority, power, and natures.

<!-- p6 -->
Authority means having the right to act. Power is the ability to carry it out. Both are essential in spiritual life. Authority is different from power. Let us look at the following table that compares authority &amp; power and consider some examples.

<!-- p7 -->
[See Table 7](../tables/table7.md)

<!-- p8 -->
Here are some examples to help understand the difference between power and authority.

<!-- p9 -->
Example 1: At work, an employee may have the talents and skills to complete the sale of the company’s assets (power). This power is legitimate if he also has the associated permission (authority) from the company’s owners. Without this authority, selling the company’s assets is considered theft.

<!-- p10 -->
Example 2: A police officer is given special training in weapons and hand-to-hand combat to learn how to arrest a criminal and subdue him physically (power). Police officers are entrusted with a duty backed by laws (authority) to enforce criminal laws.

## The source of Jesus’ authority

<!-- p74 -->
Now that we have closely examined what authority is, we need to know who is the source of that authority. Jesus’ authority is both divine and delegated. It flows from God the Father, is affirmed through His death and resurrection, and operates in perfect harmony with the Father’s will. And though it is absolute, His authority respects human free will, displaying God’s love rather than coercive control.

<!-- p75 -->
Jesus’ authority is rooted in His divine identity as the Son of God and is granted by the Father. The Father entrusted Christ with all authority, including judgment and rule over heaven and earth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 11:27</span>). Jesus’ authority reflects the unity and love within the Trinity, as the Son carries out the Father’s plan perfectly (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="35" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:35</span>).

<!-- p76 -->
And while Jesus had inherent authority as the Son of God, His victory over sin, death, and hell was fully consecrated through His death and resurrection. Christ’s obedience in His sacrifice led to His exaltation (His being seated at the right hand of the Father), which established His authority over all creation. By conquering death, Christ obtained absolute sovereignty over life and eternity.

## Laying on of hands

<!-- p116 -->
The laying on of hands is an important biblical practice used throughout both the Old and New Testaments to symbolize blessing، commissioning، healing، and the impartation of authority or spiritual gifts. It is more than just a physical act - it represents a spiritual connection and transfer under the direction of God’s authority.

<!-- p117 -->
In the Old Testament، the laying on of hands was often associated with blessing and consecration. For example، Jacob blessed his grandsons Ephraim and Manasseh by laying his hands on them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="48" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 48: 14</span>). Similarly، Moses laid his hands on Joshua to commission him as his successor، granting him the authority of leadership and wisdom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="NUM" data-chapter="27" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Numbers 27: 18-23</span>، <span class="x-scripture" data-book="DEU" data-chapter="34" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Deuteronomy 34: 9</span>). This act publicly declared God’s choice and empowerment for the task ahead.

<!-- p118 -->
In the New Testament، the laying on of hands is closely associated with the ministry of Jesus and the early church. Jesus regularly laid His hands on the sick to heal them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 6: 5</span>، <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 4: 40</span>)، also demonstrating compassion and authority over sickness and disease. The apostles continued this practice، using it for receiving the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8: 17</span>)، spiritual gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 4: 14</span>، <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 1: 6</span>)، and blessing believers. It also played a role in commissioning leaders and missionaries for service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 13: 3</span>).

<!-- p119 -->
The laying on of hands symbolizes an act of faith and obedience to God’s word. It is not a mechanical ritual، but a spiritual practice rooted in reliance on the Holy Spirit. For example، in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6: 6</span>، the apostles laid hands on those chosen for the service of deacons، consecrating them to God’s service and publicly declaring their calling. And in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 19: 6</span>، Paul laid his hands on the believers، and they received the Holy Spirit، demonstrating its role in spiritual empowerment.

<!-- p120 -->
However, the Bible also provides warnings regarding this practice:

<!-- p121 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p122 -->
> **<u>1 Timothy 5:22</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Do not lay hands on anyone hastily, nor share in other people's sins; keep yourself pure.
>

<!-- p123 -->
</span>

<!-- p124 -->
This highlights the importance of discernment and spiritual maturity when appointing individuals to leadership roles. As we studied in the chapter on the church, leaders must be tested and proven to avoid promoting unqualified or immature believers who may cause harm to the body of Christ.

<!-- p125 -->
Some additional guidelines for the laying on of hands:

<!-- p126 -->
1.  Contextually appropriate - the person on whom hands are laid should be a willing participant.

<!-- p127 -->
2.  Physically appropriate - the laying on of hands does not involve sexual or sensual touching. Practically, placing a hand on the shoulder, hand, or forehead are generally acceptable places to touch (after obtaining permission) in the Western world.

<!-- p128 -->
3.  Culturally appropriate - in some cultures, it is not appropriate for men and women to touch unless they are related by blood or marriage.

<!-- p129 -->
The laying on of hands remains a powerful expression of faith and obedience in the church today. It serves as a means of blessing, healing, commissioning, and empowerment, always pointing to the authority of Christ and the work of the Holy Spirit. Whether praying for the sick, sending missionaries, or commissioning leaders, it reminds us that believers do not operate in their own strength but under the authority of Jesus Christ and in alignment with his purposes.

<!-- p130 -->
Now that we have studied the authority of Jesus and the authority of the believer, we will discuss other beings in the spiritual realm, angels and demons.

<!-- p131 -->
## Angels and Demons

<!-- p132 -->
### Angels

<!-- p133 -->
Angels are an important part of God's creation, serving as his messengers and agents to carry out his divine purposes. The Bible reveals a spiritual realm inhabited by these beings, who are powerful, intelligent, and obedient to God's commands. Although angels are often invisible, their presence and actions affect human history and spiritual warfare. Understanding their roles, classifications, and activities grants us deeper insight into God's plan and the unseen realities that surround us.

<!-- p134 -->
The term "angel" itself means "messenger," highlighting their primary function. Angels act in obedience to God's will, whether delivering messages, providing protection, executing judgment, or engaging in spiritual battles. They are not objects of worship but serve as examples of obedience and reverence, always pointing to God's glory. This chapter explores the nature of angels, their classifications, and their roles, highlighting their significance in the Bible and in our lives today.

<!-- p135 -->
In the Scriptures, angels are seen as **messengers**, **servants**, and **warriors**. Angels often act as messengers, delivering important revelations, warnings, and encouragements. They reveal God's plans and provide guidance in times of need (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26-38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 1:26-38</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 2:13</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 1:1</span>). Angels are actively involved in protecting, strengthening, and rescuing believers. They minister to God's people in times of distress and provide supernatural help (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="91" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalms 91:11</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 12:7-11</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 4:11</span>). Angels engage in spiritual warfare, fighting demonic forces to advance God's kingdom and defend believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="DAN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Daniel 10:13</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:7-9</span>).

<!-- p136 -->
#### Nature of Angels

<!-- p137 -->
Angels are created beings, brought into existence by God before the foundation of the world. They are distinct from humans, possessing unique characteristics that equip them for their heavenly roles. The Bible teaches that angels are spiritual beings not limited by physical constraints; nevertheless, they can take on visible forms when interacting with humans.

<!-- p138 -->
Angels are described as immortal, meaning they do not die or age. They are innumerable, their vast hosts filling the heavens. Although they are often referred to with masculine pronouns, the Bible makes clear that angels are without gender and do not marry or reproduce. These attributes affirm their purpose as God's servants, wholly devoted to his will and commands. Angels possess distinctive qualities that reflect their divine purpose. They are obedient, reverent, wise, and powerful. These traits equip them to carry out God's will effectively and faithfully.

<!-- p139 -->
[See Table 8](../tables/table8.md)

<!-- p140 -->
Although the Bible highlights several roles and titles for angels, it does not present a comprehensive classification system. Nevertheless, some angels are identified by specific roles or functions:

<!-- p141 -->
- **The Angel of the Lord** - often considered a pre-incarnate appearance of Christ, appearing with divine authority. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="23" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 23:20-23</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JDG" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Judges 6:11-24</span>*)

<!-- p142 -->
- **Archangels** - high-ranking angels such as Michael, who leads the armies, and Gabriel, who delivers divine messages. (*Jude 9; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:7</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 1:19-26</span>*)

<!-- p143 -->
- **Cherubim** - guardians of God's glory and presence, as in Eden and the Ark of the Covenant. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 3:24</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 25:22</span>*)

<!-- p144 -->
- **Seraphim** - worshipers of God, continually proclaiming His holiness. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 6:1-6</span>*)

<!-- p145 -->
- **Elect angels** - faithful angels who resisted Satan's rebellion and remained loyal to God. (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 5:21</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 25:41</span>*)

<!-- p146 -->
These classifications emphasize the diversity and organization within the angelic realm, yet their primary focus always remains on accomplishing God's purposes.

<!-- p147 -->
#### Human Interaction with Angels

<!-- p148 -->
Humans often encounter angels in the Bible, but we are never commanded to direct them or to pray to them. Instead, angels operate under God's direction. They serve believers as **servants of God** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 1:14</span>). Their interactions include protection, guidance, and deliverance, always pointing to God's power and sovereignty.

<!-- p149 -->
- **Warnings against angel worship**: The Bible forbids worshiping angels or seeking encounters with them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:18</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 22:8-9</span>).

<!-- p150 -->
- **Pray to God, not to angels**: Believers ought to pray to God, who sends angels as He wills (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="91" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 91:11</span>).

<!-- p151 -->
- **Encouragement and trust**: Angels remind us of God's care, but our faith must rest wholly in God, not in angels.

<!-- p152 -->
Angels are awe-inspiring beings created to serve God and to serve His people. Though their work is often unseen, the Bible gives us glimpses of their activity, revealing their faithfulness, power, and majesty. Angels remind us of God's care and protection, yet they always point to His glory. As believers, we are called to trust in God's care, knowing that angels operate as His servants to accomplish His divine purposes.

<!-- p153 -->
### Demons

<!-- p154 -->
Just as the study of angels is essential for understanding the spiritual realm, so is the study of demons. The Bible clearly describes their activity, their influence, and their defeat through Jesus Christ. Recognizing their role in spiritual warfare helps believers to stand firm in the faith without fear.

<!-- p155 -->
#### Origin and Nature of Demons

<!-- p156 -->
Demons are fallen angels who followed Satan's rebellion against God. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:7-9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 25:41</span>).* Satan, formerly known as Lucifer (“light-bearer”), was a glorified angel who sought to exalt himself above God and was cast out of heaven along with the other rebellious angels. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 14:12-15</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EZK" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ezekiel 28:12-17</span>).* 

<!-- p157 -->
Demons are spirits without physical bodies, yet they can oppress and torment humans and animals. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 5:11-13</span>).* They seek to deceive, tempt, oppress, and destroy. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 11:14</span>). Unlike God,* they have limits and are not omnipresent (everywhere), omniscient (all-knowing), or omnipotent (all-powerful)*.* Their inevitable defeat is assured, and they will receive eternal punishment. *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 20:10</span>)*

<!-- p158 -->
The Scriptures describe Satan's work and activity as:

<!-- p159 -->
- Opposes the work of God *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Thessalonians 2:18</span>)*

<!-- p160 -->
- He deceives the world *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:9</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 4:4</span>)*

<!-- p161 -->
- He tempts believers and accuses them *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 22:31</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 12:10</span>)*

<!-- p162 -->
- He torments and harms *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JOB" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Job 1:12</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 13:16</span>)*

<!-- p163 -->
There are many names and descriptions for Satan, each of which describes a different aspect of his work.

<!-- p164 -->
[See Table 9](../tables/table9.md)

<!-- p165 -->
The Gospels and the Epistles explain the work of the enemy more deeply. Evil spirits, being disembodied, often must carry out their work through willing and open people. Just as God works through people, so does Satan. This oppression is often marked by torment, self-harm, and destructive behaviors *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 8:27-29</span>).* Their attacks cause fear, temptation, and discouragement *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 12:7</span>).* And the most common source of their power (not their authority) in a person’s life is through the influence of lies and mental strongholds *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 10:3-5</span>).*

<!-- p166 -->
It is important to note that not every lie a person believes, nor every ailment in his body, nor every stronghold in his life, is a direct result of demonic influence, but these areas of wounding are places where the enemy can exploit us and keep us in a state of discouragement and oppression.

<!-- p167 -->
#### The Authority of Jesus over the works of the enemy

<!-- p168 -->
Do not be afraid, Jesus has complete and absolute authority over the works of the enemy. Jesus demonstrated power over demons during His ministry *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 1:34</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 5:1-20</span>).* The works of the enemy, and the devil himself, were defeated by His death and resurrection *(<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:15</span>).*

<!-- p169 -->
And as believers, we have been given delegated authority from Jesus to cast out demons in His name:

<!-- p170 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p171 -->
> **<u>Mark 16:17</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And these signs will follow those who believe: In My name they will cast out demons؛ they will speak with new tongues;
>

<!-- p172 -->
</span>

<!-- p173 -->
Moreover, as heirs of salvation, we have the authority to submit to God, and resist the devil and he will flee from us! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 4:7</span>).

<!-- p174 -->
To deal with an evil or unclean spirit, the pattern expressed in Scripture is that we take authority over it in the name of Jesus and command it to come out. And the Holy Spirit promises to give us wisdom and discernment if we ask! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:5</span>). We do not need to make it a show, or a drama like in the movies, nor even to talk with them؛ that is a trick of the devil to distract us. The devil’s power stands and continues only on the lies he can make us believe.

<!-- p175 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="18" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p176 -->
> **<u>Matthew 18:18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> "Truly I say to you، whatever you bind on earth will be bound in heaven، and whatever you loose on earth will be loosed in heaven.
>

<!-- p177 -->
</span>

<!-- p178 -->
We address them in the name of Jesus and command them to come out.

<!-- p179 -->
There are two basic warnings in Scripture when dealing with demonic matters:

<!-- p180 -->
1.  **Only those who have a relationship with Jesus have authority over demons**. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 19:11-20</span> speaks about the sons of a Jewish chief priest named Sceva. They tried to intervene in the life of a person who was oppressed and tormented by the devil, and the evil spirit refused to come out because they had no relationship with Jesus. The man who was oppressed leaped on them, stripped them of their clothes, and beat them. Just as you should not impersonate a police officer, **do not impersonate someone who has a relationship with Jesus; rather, form a relationship with Jesus**!

<!-- p181 -->
2.  **When an evil spirit goes out, it goes away for a time and then tries to return**. And if it finds the place it used to inhabit empty, it comes back in a worse way. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="43-45" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 12:43-45</span>. When we minister to a person oppressed by the devil, that person needs to fill the void that the evil spirit used to occupy in their life. This means at least establishing a personal relationship with Jesus and strengthening it by obeying the Holy Spirit. Often an evil or unclean spirit is able to operate in a person’s life through choices they have made based on their beliefs. The one who has been set free from the devil needs to replace that old belief system and be transformed by the renewing of their mind (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:1-2</span>).

<!-- p182 -->
We serve a wonderful and mighty God, and we are ‘more than conquerors’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="37" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:37</span>) over the defeated enemy. We have no need to fear our defeated enemy, because Jesus has given us His authority to crush his head! The devil’s time is short, and in the end he will be judged along with every principality and authority on earth that refuses the lordship of Christ.
