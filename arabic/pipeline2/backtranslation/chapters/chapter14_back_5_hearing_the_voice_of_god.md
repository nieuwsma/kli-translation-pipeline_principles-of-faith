# Hearing the Voice of God

<!-- p1 -->
The following guest appendix by Rev. Larry Low ([<u>www.LarryLow.com</u>](http://www.larrylow.com)) completes our exploration of the role of the Holy Spirit, offering practical insights on hearing the voice of God. The following guest contribution is used by permission.

<!-- p2 -->
During prayer one morning, I asked **God** the following question: "What is the most important thing I can teach Your people for the times that are coming toward us?" He answered me very quickly, and I felt a strong urgency when He said: "**Teach them to hear My voice.**"

<!-- p3 -->
When the Lord gives me a directive like this, I always grab a pad and a pencil and start writing down what little I know, or what I think I know, and the Scriptures that support it. I love doing word studies on the topics the Lord leads me to teach. I love the anointing for preaching, but I really love teaching the Word. It is an adventure to find things that have always been there but were overlooked.

<!-- p4 -->
David must have longed to discover God's truths, for he prayed in verse 18 of Psalm 119: "Open my eyes that I may see wondrous things from Your law."

<!-- p5 -->
That is what I want to see—wondrous things from His Word.

<!-- p6 -->
As I was studying and preparing to teach the second message on hearing and discerning the voice of God, He spoke to my heart as follows:

<!-- p7 -->
"*Teach them to hear My voice,  
Teach them to honor and obey.*"

<!-- p8 -->
"*Yes, teach them to hear My voice and I will always lead them away:  
Away from the troubles that could destroy them,*"

<!-- p9 -->
"*Away from the trials they are in,  
Away to a place of peace and love and joy that only a few have experienced.*"

<!-- p10 -->
"*Away to a place of refreshing,  
To a place of divine ecstasy.*"

<!-- p11 -->
"*Oh yes, teach them to hear My voice, my son  
and these people will be free forever*.”

<!-- p12 -->
05/27/00 9:10 A.M.

<!-- p13 -->
The following eleven points should help you understand how the Lord will communicate with you:

<!-- p14 -->
1.  <u>Hearing God's voice is not an audible voice but a knowing in your heart.</u>

<!-- p15 -->
There was a time when His word came so clear and bold that it could have been audible. I answered Him speaking out loud; it was so very real. However, there was no one there but the Lord and me, so I really cannot be certain whether He spoke to me as we speak to one another.

<!-- p16 -->
There was a time when Jesus was being baptized in the Jordan River and the Father spoke: "This is My Son in whom I am well pleased," but even some who were there said it sounded like thunder, so I suppose He may have spoken to me audibly, but for now, I want you to understand that when I say God spoke to me, it is a knowing in my heart more than a voice in my ear.

<!-- p17 -->
2.  <u>This knowing is not constructed by logic in your mind; nevertheless, it is a thought or a complete understanding.</u>

<!-- p18 -->
Never has the Lord said something to me that confused me. Nor has He ever said something that left me wondering 'why.' It is always a knowing with complete understanding. God is not a God of confusion (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:33</span>).

<!-- p19 -->
3.  <u>When He speaks, He speaks in complete thoughts.</u>

<!-- p20 -->
He does not leave us to fill in the blanks. In the blink of an eye, God can fill your heart with enough information to keep you preaching for a lifetime. One touch from God is able to fill you with a fire that will keep you burning forever. He is a perfect communicator.

<!-- p21 -->
4.  <u>When God speaks to us, He never drives. Rather, He leads.</u>

<!-- p22 -->
He is not a harsh master; rather, a shepherd. Cattle drivers drive, but shepherds lead. Jesus is the great Shepherd of the sheep, and we are the sheep of His pasture.

<!-- p23 -->
5.  <u>When He speaks to His children, He never convicts; rather, He convinces.</u>

<!-- p24 -->
His children are those who have been born again (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:3</span>, 5) by confessing faith in the shed blood of **Jesus** and His work at Calvary. Who wants to listen to someone who is always convicting us of our wrong? I think most of **God’s** children are afraid to hear **God** speak to them because they have been told that **God** will convict them of this or that. Look at <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 3:19-20</span>. Who is it that says He convicts us or condemns us? It is our spirit or our heart that condemns the child of **God**, but **God the Holy Spirit**, who knows all things, is greater than our hearts. He is there to be our Comforter. Read the following verses; this will help you understand that the only ones the Holy Spirit convicts are sinners. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33-34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:33-34</span> teaches us that nothing can separate us from the **love of God**. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:16</span>, 26 teaches us that the Holy Spirit is our Comforter, not our Convictor; our Counselor, not our Disciplinarian; our Advocate and Helper, not our Hinderer. Also read <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 9:31</span> and 15:28 as examples of how the Holy Spirit (the voice of **God**) helps us, comforts us, and strengthens us.

<!-- p25 -->
6.  <u>The voice of God can be tested so that you may know it is from God and not from your thoughts nor the voice of familiar spirits.</u>

<!-- p26 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 4:1-3</span> directs us to “test every spirit” to make sure it is from God. When you think you are hearing from God, you have the right and responsibility to ask that voice a question (this is the testing or discerning of the spirit.) The question to ask is: “Has Jesus Christ come in the flesh?” The one thing familiar spirits will not confess is that Jesus came to earth in the flesh. He was fully man and fully God. No false spirit and no false religion will confess that Jesus came to this earth in the flesh and redeemed mankind from all their sins forever. Test the voice you hear. You should also know that God will never lead you outside His written Word, that is, the Bible. If you are hearing something that cannot be supported by the Bible, you are not hearing from God. Get rid of it! Test the voice you hear to see if it is from God.

<!-- p27 -->
7.  <u>The voice of God always brings glory to Jesus Christ.</u>

<!-- p28 -->
The Holy Spirit is the voice of heaven. He is the One who is here on earth to work with us, and Jesus said that He would speak only what He hears, and that He would always bring glory to Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13-14</span>). He is not here to bring glory to your ministry or your works or church programs. All those things are good, and most of them are necessary for what we do, but the Holy Spirit will speak and bring glory to the person of Jesus. If the voice you are hearing glorifies you, you are not hearing God. There is a difference between being glorified and being encouraged and strengthened. The Holy Spirit will always encourage you and strengthen you whenever He speaks to you, even when He corrects you (guides you) by the Word. But glory is when you think you are somebody. You are nothing without Jesus — He is the important person, not us.

<!-- p29 -->
8.  <u>The voice of God will always declare something to us.</u>

<!-- p30 -->
See <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:14</span>. He always takes what belongs to **Jesus** and declares it to us. The Amplified translation adds the words: declares, discloses, and transmits when looking at the word “declare.” **God** will always disclose something to us after He declares it to us, and then by your faith He transmits it to us. Also study <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 2:10</span> and 12.

<!-- p31 -->
9.  <u>The voice of God will always bring peace.</u>

<!-- p32 -->
Read <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:26</span> and 27, and <span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 3:14</span> and 15. His voice never disturbs us. It is a voice that brings perfect peace. Inner peace because there is knowledge (assurance) that gives us confidence before God, which translates into perfect peace. If the voice you are listening to is troubling you, you are not hearing from God — silence it. Jesus has promised us peace, not as the world gives, but as He gives us through the comforting words of the Holy Spirit and His constant indwelling (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:27</span>).

<!-- p33 -->
10. <u>The voice of God always brings truth.</u>

<!-- p34 -->
Read <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:17</span>, 26; 16:13. In fact, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span> calls the Holy Spirit: “the Spirit of truth.” God cannot lie! He is truth, and therefore He will speak only truth. I have heard people say things like: “I can’t believe that what I’m hearing is from God. It’s too good to be true.” And perhaps it is; therefore you should test the spirit by matching it with the Word of God to make sure it is true. Some people cannot believe because what they hear is always positive, and they have been taught that God convicts us of wrong. God says only truth, and it will always be good news. The sad truth is that most people hear only bad news from the voice they are listening to and do not expect to hear good news, so when the good news comes, they doubt it as possibly not true. **Truth is always good news!** It is **the truth that** sets us free, so how can **the truth** be anything but good news?

<!-- p35 -->
11. <u>The voice of **God** always leads us forward and upward.</u>

<!-- p36 -->
Read Deuteronomy 28:1-2, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="30" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 30:21</span>, and <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span>

<!-- p37 -->
God will never put you down! Nor will He belittle you; rather, He builds you up. When God speaks to you, He speaks from the perspective that everything the Word says about you has already been accomplished and you have accepted it. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="33" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 33:3</span> says: “Call to Me, and I will answer you, and show you great and mighty things, which you do not know.” Does that sound like it belittles you or like gloom and doom? There is no darkness and gloom in God, so He cannot say anything to you that is grim or bleak. You have to tune in to the world’s news service to hear gloom and doom.

<!-- p38 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="29" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p39 -->
> **<u>Jeremiah 29:11</u>** (NKJV)
>
> For I know the thoughts that I think toward you, says the LORD, thoughts of peace and not of evil, to give you a future and a hope.

<!-- p40 -->
</span>

<!-- p41 -->
Now, if God thinks good thoughts of peace, a future, and hope toward us, why would He speak otherwise? The answer is: He won’t, and He doesn’t! He leads us only upward and forward with great hope to grant us an expected future.

<!-- p42 -->
I hope this encourages you to “call on Him” and learn how to **hear His voice**. He has great things to tell you, but He needs your attentive ear before any of His good things can benefit you.
