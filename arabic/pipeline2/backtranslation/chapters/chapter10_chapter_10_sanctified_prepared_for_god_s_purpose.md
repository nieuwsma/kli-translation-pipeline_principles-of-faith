# Chapter 10 - Sanctified-Prepared for God's purpose

<!-- p1 -->
In the previous two chapters we discussed several important topics. We explored the role and ministry of the Holy Spirit, as well as the role and ministry of the Church.

<!-- p2 -->
We have a role to play in the Church, and although we know we need training, we also need cleansing. And in order to be truly prepared for God's purposes we must be sanctified.

<!-- p3 -->
The definition of **sanctification** is the purification of something for a special purpose. And this is closely related to the term **consecration** which means dedicating something for a special purpose.

<!-- p4 -->
Imagine a skilled sculptor preparing to work on a large block of marble. The master imagines what this raw, unshaped marble can become through his precise craftsmanship. He has a very specific design in mind, so he orders his assistants to set apart this block of marble for a special design (**consecration**). And as he refines the marble and works on it, adding texture, depth, and form, he purifies it (**sanctifies it**) for that special design. The act of setting the marble apart for his own project was quick, while the process of shaping the marble into its final design takes a great deal of patience.

<!-- p5 -->
We were set apart and consecrated to God at salvation. And by the act of purifying our souls with the help of the Spirit we become transformed. This process of purification is what sanctifies us.

<!-- p6 -->
When we accepted Jesus for salvation, we were born again. We were justified and our sins were forgiven. Our spirit was renewed, and we became alive in Christ. The family of God adopted us and we became the true temple of the Holy Spirit. A change so wonderful that Scripture says, “*There is joy in the presence of the angels of God over one sinner who repents.” (*<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 15:10</span>).

<!-- p7 -->
Our acceptance of Jesus for salvation (**justification**) saves our spirit, purifies us, and makes us acceptable before God, able to receive His Holy Spirit. However, despite this great salvation, our souls (our mind, will, and emotions) still need work!

<!-- p8 -->
Was the work of salvation incomplete? No! The work of salvation is effective, but it must continue in the life of the believer through the renewal that the Holy Spirit brings. This process of renewal and transformation is the work of **sanctification.**

<!-- p9 -->
We know that there is ongoing work to be done after conversion because our painful memories have not been resolved, many of our beliefs remained the same, and many of our habits or rituals have not faded. Moreover, conversion did not separate our minds from their carnal (fleshly) nature.

<!-- p10 -->
The word 'carnal' comes from the Latin root that means 'meat' or 'flesh.' The carnal nature is present within each one of us from birth؛ and, like animal instincts, it exists to help us survive. The way the carnal nature works is to be self-focused and self-centered. Children are natural examples of the carnal nature. For although they are innocent, their nature is primarily focused on their desires, longings, and needs؛ and 'beware!' if you do not fulfill all of their expectations.

<!-- p11 -->
Part of maturing is that we move beyond our childish ways, but when we are under pressure we easily return to a self-centered pattern of thinking and living. As James says:

<!-- p12 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p13 -->
> **<u>James 4:1</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Where do wars and quarrels come from among you? Are they not from your desires for pleasure that wage war in your members?
>

<!-- p14 -->
</span>

<!-- p15 -->
Paul details this carnal nature further in his letter to the Romans (chapters 6 - 8). He says that this carnal nature makes him keep doing what he hates and drives him to do evil. He describes this inner struggle as a war between his human nature and his spirit which is according to God.

<!-- p16 -->
This struggle between the flesh and the Spirit exists in each one of us، and it is our duty to do something about it. If we do not confront our carnal nature, our souls will remain entangled in patterns of spiritual decay, even though our spirits have been redeemed.

<!-- p17 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p18 -->
> **<u>Romans 8:6-8</u>** (NKJV)
>
> For the mindset of the flesh is death, but the mindset of the Spirit is life and peace.
>
> Because the mindset of the flesh is enmity against God؛ for it is not subject to the law of God, nor indeed can it be.
>
> So then, those who are in the flesh cannot please God.
>

<!-- p19 -->
</span>

<!-- p20 -->
These words of Paul are at once a warning and a promise to us. Although we have been born again, we still possess a carnal mind that needs to be subjected to the knowledge of Christ. And as we do that, we walk in God’s life and peace.

<!-- p21 -->
Take heart, for there is hope for us! We must walk according to the Spirit and be led by the Spirit of God. And by doing so, we will not walk according to the flesh.

<!-- p22 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="1,13,14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p23 -->
> **<u>Romans 8:1, 13, 14</u>** (NKJV)
>
> There is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus, who do not walk according to the flesh, but according to the Spirit.
>
> For if you live according to the flesh you will die; but if by the Spirit you put to death the deeds of the body, you will live.
>
> For as many as are led by the Spirit of God, these are sons of God.
>

<!-- p24 -->
</span>

<!-- p25 -->
How do we learn to walk in the Spirit? Is it *merely a conscious choice*? We learn to walk in the Spirit in the same way we learn to walk in the natural. We are driven to move and grow, so we struggle, strive, crawl, and wobble until we can stand on our feet. Our natural parents cannot walk for us, but they can support us, encourage us, help us, and empower us in our walk.

<!-- p26 -->
Likewise, God cannot walk out our faith for us. He will teach us, but we have to make the effort. As Paul said to the Philippians:

<!-- p27 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="12-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p28 -->
> **<u>Philippians 2:12-13</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Therefore, my beloved, as you have always obeyed, not as in my presence only, but now much more in my absence, work out your salvation with fear and trembling;
>
> for it is God who works in you both to will and to do for His good pleasure.
>

<!-- p29 -->
</span>

<!-- p30 -->
We must work out our own salvation. This does not mean that we go up on a wooden cross and are crucified physically, but we must crucify our carnal nature (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:24</span>). God promises that He will work in us and help us, but we must make a decision to walk this way, and to persevere in it.

<!-- p31 -->
It is right to say that ‘we’ deal with our carnal nature, but it is also right to say that ‘God’ deals with it. The work of the cross (justification) was hard on Jesus, for He had to sacrifice His life for us, but it is easy for us, for it is a free gift. But the work of the Spirit (sanctification) is easy for God, for He walks in perfect truth and is ready and willing to abide in us, but it is hard for us, because we must crucify our old nature, and our nature is not very interested in being crucified.

<!-- p32 -->
This process is cooperative. God helps, aids, guides, leads, and corrects. We listen, obey, follow, and respond. The result is growth and transformation. This is the pruning that Jesus said God would do in us. Jesus is the vine, His Father the vinedresser, and we are the branches:

<!-- p33 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p34 -->
> **<u>John 15:1-4</u>** (NKJV)
>
> "I am the true vine, and My Father is the vinedresser.
>
> Every branch in Me that does not bear fruit He takes away; and every branch that bears fruit He prunes, that it may bear more fruit.
>
> You are already clean because of the word which I have spoken to you.
>
> Abide in Me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself, unless it abides in the vine, neither can you, unless you abide in Me.
>

<!-- p35 -->
</span>

<!-- p36 -->
Pruning is extremely beneficial for the plant. Returning to our chapter on repentance, we are called to turn from dead works (those works that no longer bear fruit) and to turn to God. The result of this turning from the dead places in our lives, and to God, enables the master Gardener of our lives to prune the branches, improve growth, and produce greater spiritual fruit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:2</span>).

<!-- p37 -->
Although pruning may seem painful, it reflects God’s loving care as He shapes us into vessels for His purpose (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 12:11</span>). Some may feel a sense of loss, even fear, when submitting to the process of pruning. Nevertheless, God is gentle, and He will not break a bruised reed (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="42" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 42:3</span>). Grapevines are pruned in their dormant season to reduce stress on the plant. The gardener who tends the growth will cut back the old, woody growth that no longer produces fruit as it once did, allowing the vine to direct its energy toward the branches with greater potential for good fruit.

<!-- p38 -->
Just as the gardener cuts back branches to direct growth, God may allow seasons of correction and discipline to purify our faith and character, but His work is patient and gentle. God prunes those who abide in Him. Our focus should be more on abiding in Christ, and less on the work of pruning.

<!-- p39 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 12:11</span> reminds us that pruning “produces the peaceable fruit of righteousness” for those who have been trained by it.

<!-- p40 -->
How are we trained in righteousness? By being transformed through the renewing of our minds.

In the following section I will describe each of the memory systems, their purpose and function, and some practical steps to renew this aspect of your mind, in addition to resources for continued practice and study.

<!-- p61 -->
### **Memory Systems and How to Renew Them**

<!-- p62 -->
#### **1. Explicit Memory (Declarative Memory)**

<!-- p63 -->
**Purpose:** Stores facts, events, and experiences that we can consciously recall. It is divided into two types-**semantic** and **episodic**.

<!-- p64 -->
**Semantic memory - facts and knowledge.** Semantic memory is where we store facts, definitions, and general knowledge-things like historical dates, verses from the Bible, and practical information. It helps us anticipate outcomes, make decisions, and avoid danger (for example: recognizing poisonous plants). And while it gives us stability and order, it can also lead to rigidity-overreliance on rules instead of adapting to unexpected situations.

<!-- p65 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p66 -->
- **Study the Bible** to shape our worldview (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="119" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="105" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 119:105</span>).

<!-- p67 -->
- **Memorize facts** and teachings to be established in the faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 3:16</span>).

<!-- p68 -->
- **Read Christian books** such as *"Renewing the Mind" by Casey Treat* or *"Battlefield of the Mind" by Joyce Meyer* to cultivate godly thought patterns.

<!-- p69 -->
**Episodic memory - personal experiences.** Episodic memory stores our personal experiences, linking them to specific times and places-like the wedding day or a traumatic event. These memories provide context that helps us learn from the past and build relationships. However, episodic memory tends toward emotional bias and distortion. Past traumas can create frightening associations, making it difficult to move forward.

<!-- p70 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p71 -->
- **Reflect and confess** to bring hidden pain into God’s light (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 1:9</span>).

<!-- p72 -->
- **Reframe past experiences** through the lens of God’s grace (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:28</span>).

<!-- p73 -->
- **Seek inner healing** through prayer and counseling (*“Your Pain is Visible” by Larry F. Lo، "The Bondage Breaker" by Neil T. Anderson*, *"Emotionally Healthy Spirituality" by Peter Scazzero*).

<!-- p74 -->
#### **2. Implicit Memory (Non-declarative Memory)**

<!-- p75 -->
**Purpose:** Unconscious memory used for habits, skills, and emotional associations. It operates automatically, helping us respond quickly to situations. It is divided into **procedural memory** and **emotional conditioning**.

<!-- p76 -->
**Procedural memory - skills and habits.** Procedural memory enables us to perform tasks without thinking-like driving, typing on a keyboard, or playing a musical instrument. It is efficient and saves our mental energy and time. However, bad habits and negative patterns can become deeply rooted and hard to break.

<!-- p77 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p78 -->
- **Practice the spiritual disciplines** such as prayer and fasting to build godly habits (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 5:14</span>).

<!-- p79 -->
- **Train yourself in godliness** through repetition and obedience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 4:7</span>).

<!-- p80 -->
- **Read books like** *"Atomic Habits" by James Clear* (practical) and *"Spiritual Disciplines for the Christian Life" by Donald Whitney* (biblical) to establish habits that honor God.

<!-- p81 -->
**Emotional conditioning - emotional associations.** Emotional conditioning links sensory cues (such as smells or sounds) with emotions, triggering responses before we consciously process them. This system protects us but can also cause irrational fears or emotional reactions when past experiences are negative.

<!-- p82 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p83 -->
- **Pray for healing** from past wounds (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="41" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 41:10</span>).

<!-- p84 -->
- **Practice forgiveness** and release bitterness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-32" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:31-32</span>).

<!-- p85 -->
- **Replace fear with God's promises** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 1:7</span>).

<!-- p86 -->
- **Seek guidance from books like** *“Your Pain is Showing” by Larry F. Low, "The Bondage Breaker" by Neil T. Anderson*, “*Soul Care" by Rob Reimer* to overcome emotional triggers.

<!-- p87 -->
#### **3. Working memory (short-term memory)**

<!-- p88 -->
**Purpose:** It provides temporary storage for problem-solving and decision-making. It helps us focus, retain information briefly, and process tasks—such as remembering instructions or making quick decisions. Despite its power, it is limited, prone to distraction, and easily overloaded, especially under stress.

<!-- p89 -->
**How to renew it:**

<!-- p90 -->
- **Focus and meditate** on God's Word daily (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 1:2</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
- **Renew your mind** through deliberate reflection (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:2</span>).

<!-- p92 -->
- **Practice mindfulness** and simplify mental clutter (*"Habits of Grace" by David Mathis*).

<!-- p93 -->
- **Read** *"The Celebration of Discipline" by Richard Foster* to hone focus through spiritual practices.

<!-- p94 -->
## Conclusion

<!-- p95 -->
Renewing the mind is not a quick fix—it is a gradual, lifelong transformation process. Each memory system plays a role in shaping how we think, feel, and act. Through deliberate cooperation with the Holy Spirit, applying Scripture, and developing godly habits, we can train our minds to align with God's truth. Whether we are healing past wounds, learning new habits, or maintaining focus in the moment, God has provided both spiritual tools and practical wisdom to help us walk in renewal and freedom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 10:5</span>).

<!-- p96 -->
Our world is full of problems, tribulations, and trials. Through disciplined mind renewal, our souls are transformed, and we can live a life of freedom for God, filled with the fruit of His Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-25</span>).
