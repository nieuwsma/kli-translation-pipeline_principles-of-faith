# Chapter 2 - God-the Eternal Creator and the Loving Father

<!-- p1 -->
It is essential that we lay a firm foundation in knowing who God is. We can understand this by studying:

<!-- p2 -->
- His desires, purposes, and actions,

<!-- p3 -->
- His nature, character, and attributes,

<!-- p4 -->
- What is said about Him and what He says about Himself.

<!-- p5 -->
The Bible uniquely reveals the attributes of God, and with each facet our understanding of the unchanging, eternal God deepens.

<!-- p6 -->
Summarizing God in a single description is difficult, if not impossible, because He is too vast and multifaceted to be simplified. And though we can, and will, make great efforts to explain, describe, and portray God, in the end, like art and music, He must be experienced to be understood.

<!-- p7 -->
When someone listens to music, they describe it by expressing how it moves them-how it resonates within them, beyond mere notes and rhythms. A melody may carry warmth or sharpness, with a rhythm that pulses like a heartbeat or swells that create a sense of euphoria or longing. Music can soothe like a gentle wave or electrify like a storm, lifting the listener’s spirits, steadying their thoughts, or transporting them to somewhere else entirely. This experience is far more than a succession of sounds; it is the spirit of the music that remains, embodied in how it shapes the listener’s emotions and personal journey.

<!-- p8 -->
Similarly, a viewer of a painting looks beyond the colors or brushstrokes, exploring what these elements embody. A landscape may evoke a sense of serene escape, while an abstract work can awaken curiosity or self-reflection. Certain hues may bring warmth, while the composition may create a sense of calm, chaos, or beauty. The painting becomes a living experience, prompting a shift in perspective or a deep emotional response that words alone cannot capture. Thus music and art become more than their physical forms-they become encounters that touch something essential, changing the listener or viewer in profound and personal ways.

## Exploring God through the Old Testament

<!-- p10 -->
### Genesis: God who dwells with His creation

<!-- p11 -->
A central theme in God’s narrative is His desire to dwell with His creation. This is evident in the stories throughout Genesis. Across its fifty chapters and the genealogies from Adam (the first human) to Joseph (the last major figure in Genesis, the last significant leader of God’s people before Moses), there is a span of approximately ~2,400 years. Through these stories we can see the constancy of God’s goodness despite human evil.

<!-- p12 -->
In Genesis, God establishes a relationship with selected individuals.

<!-- p13 -->
1.  **Adam and Eve** - the first humans, created by God and placed in the Garden of Eden. God walked and conversed with them, providing guidance and establishing a relationship of mutual love and obedience. However, this relationship is affected by their disobedience in eating from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil (Genesis 1-3).

<!-- p14 -->
2.  **Cain** - Although Cain ultimately rebels by killing his brother Abel, God speaks to him directly, warning him of the danger of sin and providing guidance before the murder. And even after punishing Cain, God places a mark on him to protect him from harm (Genesis 4).

<!-- p15 -->
3.  **Noah** - God establishes a unique relationship with Noah, a righteous man, in a time filled with corruption. God commands Noah to build an ark to save his family and the animals from the flood. Afterward, God makes a covenant with Noah, promising not to destroy the earth with a flood again, and setting the rainbow as a sign of this covenant (Genesis 6-9).

<!-- p16 -->
4.  **Abraham (Abram)** - One of the most important relationships in Genesis, God calls Abram to leave his homeland and promises to make him the father of a great nation. God makes a covenant with Abram, promising land, offspring, and blessing for all nations through him. Abram’s faith and obedience become central themes, and his name is changed to Abraham as part of the covenant (Genesis 12, 15, 17, 22).

<!-- p17 -->
5.  **Hagar** - An Egyptian servant of Sarah, she encounters God in the wilderness after being mistreated. God comforts her, promises a future for her son Ishmael, and commands her to return to Abraham and Sarah. Hagar names God “El Roi” (“the God who sees me”), acknowledging His care for her (Genesis 16).

<!-- p18 -->
6.  **Isaac** - The son of Abraham and Sarah, he inherits the covenant promises given to his father. God affirms these promises directly to Isaac, including the promise of offspring and blessings. God also directs Isaac during a famine, protecting him and confirming the covenant relationship (Genesis 26).

<!-- p19 -->
7.  **Rebekah** - Although her relationship with God is indirect, God answers Rebekah’s prayer regarding her difficult pregnancy, revealing that her twins, Jacob and Esau, will become nations with distinct destinies (Genesis 25).

<!-- p20 -->
8.  **Jacob** - God establishes a personal relationship with Jacob, appearing to him in a dream and confirming the covenant promises of land, offspring, and blessings. Jacob meets God several times, including the famous wrestling in which God changes his name to Israel, a symbol of his transformation and the establishment of Israel as a people (Genesis 28, 32, 35).

<!-- p21 -->
9.  **Joseph** - Although Joseph’s relationship with God is not based on direct appearances or covenants like those of Abraham or Jacob, God is deeply involved in his life. Through dreams, divine favor, and providence, God guides Joseph’s journey from being sold as a slave to becoming a powerful leader in Egypt. Joseph’s faith in God’s sovereignty enables him to forgive his brothers, acknowledging that God used his suffering for good (Genesis 37, 39-50).

<!-- p22 -->
Throughout this narrative, God, who desires to dwell with His creation, was forced to settle for dwelling with specific individuals, and ultimately through the covenant with Abraham He establishes a people (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="17" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 17:7</span>).

<!-- p23 -->
### Exodus: God who dwells with His people

<!-- p24 -->
God’s desire to dwell with His people is also clearly revealed in the second book of the Bible, Exodus. In the book of Exodus, the people of Israel, who had lived for a time in relative peace and harmony in Egypt, are now subjected to oppression by a Pharaoh who did not know Joseph and feared the Hebrews (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 1:6-8</span>). God’s people lived in Egypt for approximately ~400 years (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40-41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 12:40-41</span>), and the latter part of that period was spent as slaves (there is no general consensus on the exact length of time Israel spent in Egyptian bondage).

<!-- p25 -->
In the intense presence of the burning bush that was not consumed, God reveals Himself to Moses, Hebrew by birth and raised as an Egyptian, the exiled murderer who was tending his father-in-law’s flock in the wilderness.

<!-- p26 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p27 -->
> **<u>Exodus 3:14</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And God said to Moses: "Ehyeh-Asher-Ehyeh." And He said: "Thus you shall say to the children of Israel: 'Ehyeh has sent me to you.' "

<!-- p28 -->
</span>

<!-- p29 -->
The phrase “I AM WHO I AM” (Ehyeh-Asher-Ehyeh) is one of the deepest self-disclosures in the Bible. When Moses asks for God’s name, God answers with this phrase, which can be translated “I am who I am,” “I will be who I will be,” or simply “I am.” This declaration seems superficially puzzling but carries profound meaning. Can you imagine introducing yourself to someone new by saying ‘I’? When God reveals Himself as ‘Ehyeh-Asher-Ehyeh’ He bears witness to His nature, His eternal presence, and His sovereignty. The ‘I am’ is infinite in meaning and intent.

<!-- p30 -->
God uses Moses to declare Himself to Pharaoh:

<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

>
> And you shall say to him: 'The Lord God of the Hebrews has sent me to you, saying, "Let My people go, that they may worship Me in the wilderness"; but, until now, you have not listened!'

</span>

<!-- p34 -->
Moses tells Pharaoh that God is ‘Ehyeh’ and that God demands the release of the children of Israel so that they may worship Him in the wilderness.

<!-- p35 -->
Plagues and misery follow in succession, but in the end Pharaoh relents and allows the children of Israel to depart.

<!-- p36 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p39 -->
In the wilderness, God miraculously delivers His people from the vengeful Egyptian army (Exodus 14), provides protection from the sun and a pillar of fire to keep them warm in the cold desert nights (Exodus 13), food from heaven (Exodus 16), and water to drink (Exodus 17).

<!-- p40 -->
God brings His people to Mount Sinai, where God commands Moses to consecrate the people and give them the Law (including the Ten Commandments) and establishes His covenant with them (the Old Covenant) (Exodus 19، 20، 24). This covenant (the Mosaic covenant) builds on the Abrahamic covenant and extends from Moses to Jesus.

<!-- p41 -->
Despite His making a covenant with His people، the relationship is that of a loving and jealous God who pursues a very stubborn and ungrateful people and remains with them (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="32" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 32:9</span>). Ignoring God's miraculous intervention، they continued to carry a rebellious attitude through large portions of the narrative. God even brought them into the Promised Land and told them they could inherit it (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="33" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 33:3</span>)، but in fear and disobedience they disregarded the supernatural deliverance God had granted them، and instead were forced to wander in the wilderness for forty years until nearly that entire generation died (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="NUM" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33-34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Numbers 14:33-34</span>).

<!-- p42 -->
### The rest of the Old Testament

<!-- p43 -->
Over roughly ~1,400 years between the death of Moses and the birth of Christ، the Israelites experienced a recurring cycle of faithfulness that brings God's favor and blessings، followed by rebellion، God's judgment، then their subsequent repentance، and finally God's deliverance. This pattern is especially evident in the books of **Judges, Kings, and Chronicles**، which recount cycles in which the people turn away from God، often by worshiping other gods، leading to periods of oppression or exile. In response to their cries، God repeatedly raised up judges، prophets، or leaders to deliver them.

<!-- p44 -->
Over these years، despite the Israelites' repeated failures to keep their covenant with Him، God remains faithful، displaying justice and mercy together. He continually saves His people and forgives them، showing the steadfastness of His commitment and His enduring love.

<!-- p45 -->
As we see in the Bible، God reveals Himself through deeds، relationships، and even names that speak of His attributes. Each name represents a specific aspect of the totality of His character. These names were given as part of the narrative of how God revealed Himself to His people. For your personal study an appendix includes a reference table: *Table 10 - The names of God in the Old Testament.* Read these names and study the Scripture references. For each reference is a story that accurately portrays how God saved، rescued، and delivered His people.

<!-- p46 -->
The appendix also includes an additional set drawn from attributes that describe His nature، and descriptions of His works and purposes: *Table 11 - The attributes of God.* These attributes، though somewhat more abstract than the names of God، are no less authentic!

<!-- p47 -->
These names help us understand God- the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit (the **Trinity**)- working together as one. And in our ongoing study we will examine the names and attributes of Jesus and the Holy Spirit in greater depth.

<!-- p48 -->
God's character and works are fully manifested in the unity of the Father and the Son and the Spirit. The Scriptures teach that God is one in essence، yet exists in three distinct persons: the Father، the Son، and the Holy Spirit. The Scriptures affirm God's oneness by saying: “There is no other God besides Me” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="45" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 45:5</span>). Yet the Godhead is understood as a compound unity- three distinct persons cooperating with one mind and purpose، united in the truest sense. Each person of the Trinity has a unique role: **the Father creates**، **the Son redeems**، and **the Holy Spirit sanctifies**. The term “Trinity” does not appear in the Bible؛ rather، it developed as a theological expression to explain this concept revealed in the Scriptures.

<!-- p49 -->
The Trinity can be illustrated with examples from nature that help clarify the idea of unity in diversity. Just as water can be liquid، ice، or vapor yet remains H₂O، so each person of the Trinity is fully God while distinct in personhood. Or consider the triangle، with three sides and three angles، it forms one complete shape؛ if one side is removed it is no longer a triangle. Similarly، the nature of God is incomplete without all three persons، who work together in perfect unity.

<!-- p50 -->
- **God the Father**: He is the Creator and Sustainer of all things، and is often understood as the initiator in theology. His love for humanity is central to the biblical story، as is evident in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:16</span>. In our study we will examine God the Father as a loving Father and a righteous Judge، who made a way for our redemption.

<!-- p51 -->
- **God the Son**: Existing with the Father and coeternal with Him، Jesus took on human nature، being conceived by the Holy Spirit and born of the Virgin Mary. He lived a sinless life، died to redeem humanity، then rose again conquering sin and death. He now sits at the right hand of the Father، interceding for us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:1-3</span>؛ <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 1:23</span>؛ <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1TI" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Timothy 2:5</span>). In our study we will examine Jesus as the perfect atoning sacrifice، the risen Lord، and the head of the church.

<!-- p52 -->
- **God the Holy Spirit**: The Holy Spirit، who is with the Father and the Son and equal to them، is sent from the Father through the Son to convict the world of sin and to prepare believers as the Bride of Christ. He empowers، comforts، and sanctifies those who are in Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:26</span>; 15:26; 16:8). In our study we will examine the Holy Spirit as the Comforter whom God has given us to equip us and empower us and sanctify us and remain with us.

<!-- p53 -->
Through these distinct persons، the triune nature of God is revealed as a complete union in purpose، will، and love، with each person playing an essential role in the divine mission of creation، redemption، and sanctification. The Trinity invites believers to experience God fully، as a loving Father and a Savior who redeems and a Spirit who transforms.

<!-- p54 -->
## Conclusion

<!-- p55 -->
We can be distinctly known by our names: husband، father، son، brother، friend. Each one of these is a declaration of who we are to someone else. We can also be known by our attributes: faithful، patient، kind، intelligent، witty. Similarly، we can know God by His attributes and His names- He is our Healer، our Provider، our Shepherd، and our Redeemer. Yet since labels and words are only a small part of us، to be truly known by others، we must be experienced. This is even more true with God. To know Him truly and fully we must experience Him.

<!-- p56 -->
God's names reveal His character، but personal encounters deepen our understanding of what these names mean. For example، saying “God is my Healer” becomes richer and deeper when we understand the depths of illness or despair or helplessness from which God delivers. And our knowledge of Him as our Provider is transformed when we feel a real need and find Him faithful.

<!-- p57 -->
The Scriptures provide a context for these encounters، showing us God's faithfulness to His people across time and circumstances، and affirming that His names and attributes are not mere titles but realities that He lives out and that are experienced in the lives of individuals. Through this interaction between personal experience and the biblical narrative we come to know God- not as a distant description only، but as a present and active relationship in every season of our lives.
