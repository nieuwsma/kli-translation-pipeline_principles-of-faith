# Chapter 11 - Baptisms - Water and Spirit

<!-- p1 -->
Baptism in our modern age, detached from its Jewish roots, is often understood as only water baptism, and the baptism of the Holy Spirit (Pentecost).

<!-- p2 -->
The Epistle to the Hebrews was written to a Jewish audience. The vast majority of Christians in our modern era have no Jewish roots or heritage. Without Jewish roots and culture, many of us lack the cultural context for matters that first-century Messianic Jews (those Jewish believers who acknowledge Jesus) would have shared. Since we were not raised in the Jewish tradition, we usually do not know much about the feasts, fasts, prayers, washings, rites, and ceremonies that Jewish believers knew.

<!-- p3 -->
The Greek word for baptisms, or washings, is: **Baptismos** (bap-tis-mos’ - **G909**) - the act of dipping or washing. It refers to ritual washings or ceremonial purification. It can denote immersion, purification rites, or washings practiced in both Jewish and Christian contexts.

<!-- p4 -->
Baptisms are among the elementary teachings mentioned in Hebrews:

<!-- p5 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p6 -->
> **<u>Hebrews 6:1-2</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Therefore, leaving the discussion of the elementary principles of Christ, let us go on to perfection, not laying again the foundation of repentance from dead works and of faith toward God,
>
> of the doctrine of baptisms, of laying on of hands, of resurrection of the dead, and of eternal judgment.
>

<!-- p8 -->
## Old Testament Washings - Historical Context

<!-- p9 -->
In the Old Testament, ritual washings played a central role in maintaining ceremonial purity and the symbolism of holiness before God. These washings were mandated in the Law of Moses and included the priests who served in the Tabernacle and the people of Israel. For example, the priests had to wash their hands and feet at the bronze laver before entering the Tabernacle or approaching the altar, as a sign of their readiness to serve in the presence of a holy God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EXO" data-chapter="30" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Exodus 30:18-21</span>). Similarly, the rites for cleansing individuals from impurity, such as touching a corpse or contracting skin diseases, involved washing with water as a sign of the restoration of purity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LEV" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Leviticus 14:8</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="NUM" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Numbers 19:17-19</span>). These acts served as outward expressions of an inner desire for holiness, underscoring the importance of being set apart for God.

<!-- p10 -->
Ritual washings in the Old Testament required regular repetition to maintain ceremonial purity. Unlike permanent solutions, these rites provided only temporary external cleansing, leaving the deeper issue of sin unresolved. The writer of Hebrews points to this shortcoming, explaining that these practices were “symbolic for the present time,” unable to perfect the conscience of the worshiper (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:9-10</span>). These washings served as a continual reminder of human imperfection and the need for something greater—a final cleansing that could purify not only the body but also the heart and soul.

<!-- p11 -->
The ritual washings of the Old Testament find their fulfillment in Christ, who offers complete and eternal purification through His sacrifice. Unlike the temporary rites of the Law, the blood of Christ cleanses completely, purifying not only the body but also the conscience (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:13-14</span>). Through His death and resurrection, Jesus accomplished what the Old Testament rituals could only symbolize—true reconciliation with God. And <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:22</span> invites believers to “draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, having our hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience and our bodies washed with pure water.” This wording links Christ’s work to the imagery of Old Testament washings while displaying its far superior effectiveness. His sacrifice does not merely cover sin; it removes it completely (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 1:7</span>). Thus, the ritual washings served as a shadow pointing to the deeper cleansing that only Christ can provide, fulfilling humanity’s need for holiness through divine intervention.

<!-- p12 -->
To underscore this point, Christians are under no obligation to perform any ritual washing. The washings did not grant salvation in the Old Testament and will not grant it in the New Testament. Ritual washing will not increase our standing with God, nor earn us favor. Paul strongly rebuked the Judaizers in Galatia (read Galatians) who tried to blend requirements of the Jewish context as a condition for salvation (such as circumcision). Salvation is by faith in Jesus alone!

<!-- p13 -->
## The Baptism of John

<!-- p14 -->
In the New Testament canon, several baptisms are mentioned. We have already discussed the baptisms and washings of the Jewish tradition.

<!-- p15 -->
John the Baptist’s ministry centered on preparing the way for the coming of Jesus Christ. His baptism was described as a baptism of repentance for the forgiveness of sins (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 1:4</span>), calling people to confess their sins, turn their hearts back to God, and prepare for the coming of the Messiah. It was an outward sign of inward repentance, symbolizing a desire for spiritual cleansing and readiness for the kingdom of God. However, John’s baptism was temporary and preparatory—it pointed to the greater work that Christ would accomplish. While John’s baptism focused on repentance, it did not grant full forgiveness or spiritual regeneration, as it lacked the indwelling and empowering of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:11-12</span>). Indeed, John explicitly declared that One coming after him, greater than he, would baptize not with water only but with the Holy Spirit and fire (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 3:16</span>).

<!-- p16 -->
John’s baptism was fulfilled and surpassed through Jesus’ death, burial, and resurrection. Christian baptism, instituted by Jesus and practiced by the apostles, points to a deeper reality—the union with Christ, death to sin, and resurrection to new life (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 6:3-4</span>). Unlike John’s baptism, Christian baptism is directly connected to faith in Jesus’ atonement and the indwelling of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:38</span>). The coming of the Holy Spirit on the Day of Pentecost marked the new covenant and the beginning of Spirit-empowered life, which John’s baptism could not impart. Because of this, John’s baptism is no longer practiced, as it has been replaced by the baptism that Christ commanded (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19</span>), which includes repentance, faith, and the sealing of believers by the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>).

<!-- p17 -->
## The Baptism of Christ

<!-- p18 -->
Christ’s baptism provided a powerful example for His followers and laid the foundation for the Christian practice of water baptism. Although Jesus was without sin, He submitted to baptism by John “to fulfill all righteousness” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:15</span>). In doing so, He identified with humanity’s need for repentance and pointed to His death, burial, and resurrection. At His baptism, the Holy Spirit descended like a dove, and the Father declared, “This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:16-17</span>). This event marked the beginning of Jesus’ public ministry and revealed the unity of the Father, the Son, and the Spirit, paving the way for the spiritual transformation that believers would experience through faith in Christ.

<!-- p19 -->
Jesus later commanded His followers to be baptized, sending them out: “Go therefore and make disciples of all the nations and baptize them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit” (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:19</span>). Water baptism, as instituted by Christ, became an **act of obedience** and a **public declaration of faith** for believers. It represents their union with Jesus’ death, burial, and resurrection (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 6:3-4</span>). Immersion under the water symbolizes dying to sin, and coming up out of the water displays resurrection to new life in Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 2:12</span>). However, it is essential to clarify that this outward act is not the source of salvation but is a **visible sign** of the **inward work of grace** that has already been accomplished by the blood of Jesus.

<!-- p20 -->
Water baptism is a **symbol** of the cleansing power of Christ’s blood and the renewal of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:26</span>؛ <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>). It signifies repentance, faith, and commitment to follow Jesus. It is an outward testimony of an internal transformation - a way by which believers publicly declare their allegiance to Christ and their participation in the body of Christ, that is, the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:41</span>). Although it is commanded by Jesus and practiced in the early church, it does not **save** a person. Salvation is by grace through faith alone (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>)، and true cleansing is accomplished spiritually by the blood of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 1:5</span>). Water baptism is a response to that salvation, an act of obedience, not a condition for it.

<!-- p21 -->
In the New Testament, water baptism is consistently associated with **repentance and faith** (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:38</span>؛ <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 16:16</span>)، both of which require a personal decision to follow Christ—which infants are unable to make. Baptism symbolizes the believer’s deliberate identification with Christ’s death, burial, and resurrection (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 6:3-4</span>)، as well as the commitment to live in obedience to him. Some denominations (such as Catholics and Lutherans) practice infant baptism as a covenant sign similar to circumcision; however, because infants are unable to express personal faith or repentance, the practice of **infant baptism** does not align with the biblical model of **believer’s baptism**. Instead, many churches practice **dedication ceremonies** for children, following the example of Hannah, who dedicated Samuel to the Lord (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1SA" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Samuel 1:27-28</span>) and Mary and Joseph when they presented Jesus at the temple (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 2:22</span>). Dedication is not a substitute for baptism, but a commitment by the parents to raise their child in the faith, teach them the Word of God, and provide a model of a godly life until the child reaches an age to decide for themselves to follow Christ and be baptized.

<!-- p22 -->
In contrast to the **ritual washings** in the Old Testament that required repeated rites for purification, Christian baptism is a one-time event reflecting the completed work of grace. The washings of the Old Testament symbolized external cleansing and prepared for the deeper spiritual cleansing that comes through Christ. New Testament baptism symbolizes the inner renewal and new birth accomplished by the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>). It points to the believer’s participation in the new covenant, where cleansing from sin is permanent and based on Christ’s finished work.

<!-- p23 -->
Water baptism is closely related to the **promise of the Holy Spirit**. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:38</span> Peter calls people to repent, be baptized, and receive the gift of the Holy Spirit. While water baptism publicly signifies a believer’s repentance and faith, true baptism happens within when the Holy Spirit washes, renews, and seals the believer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:13</span>). This spiritual washing makes us members of the body of Christ and enables us to live a holy life. The **role of the Holy Spirit** in baptism reminds us that salvation is not accomplished by rituals, but by God’s transforming power.

<!-- p24 -->
It is important to distinguish between the **symbolic act of water baptism** and the spiritual reality of salvation by the blood of Christ. The Bible clearly teaches that salvation is based on faith in Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 10:9-10</span>) and not on works, including baptism (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>). Nevertheless, water baptism is an **act of obedience** that publicly identifies the believer with Christ and the church. It displays an outward commitment to live as a disciple of Jesus, but it does not add to or complete the work of salvation.

<!-- p25 -->
Christ’s water baptism provided an example for believers to follow, demonstrating humility, obedience, and readiness for ministry. For Christians today, water baptism makes a public declaration of faith, symbolizing death to sin and resurrection to a new life. Although it is commanded by Jesus and an important step of obedience, it is not required for salvation. True cleansing is accomplished by faith in the blood of Jesus, and the empowerment to live the Christian life comes from the work of the Holy Spirit in the believer. Baptism unites us with Christ and his church, reminding us of the inner transformation and renewal that God has already accomplished.

<!-- p26 -->
Having established that water baptism is an outward sign of faith, we now move on to the baptism of the Holy Spirit, which empowers believers for ministry and service.

<!-- p27 -->
## Baptism of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p28 -->
Baptism in the Holy Spirit is a profound and transformative experience God promised to empower believers for ministry, service, and holy living. Every believer receives the Holy Spirit at new birth—thus we become spiritually alive. But later we should also receive baptism in the Holy Spirit, which grants us boldness, spiritual gifts, and effective service.

<!-- p29 -->
Water baptism is a public declaration of faith and symbolizes the cleansing of sin by Christ’s blood, whereas Spirit baptism represents God’s empowering presence in the believer’s life. Together, these two kinds of baptism form complementary aspects of the Christian walk - one an outward sign of obedience, and the other an inward source of power and renewal. Baptism in the Holy Spirit is the Spirit upon us, not within us. In salvation all believers receive the Spirit within them, but when the Spirit of God comes upon them they receive power and boldness for ministry.

<!-- p30 -->
John the Baptist prophesied about baptism in the Holy Spirit, saying,

<!-- p31 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p32 -->
> **<u>Matthew 3:11</u>** (NKJV)
>
> I baptize you with water for repentance, but the one who comes after me is mightier than I, whose sandals I am not worthy to carry. He will baptize you with the Holy Spirit and fire.
>

<!-- p33 -->
</span>

<!-- p34 -->
This promise pointed to a greater work that Jesus would accomplish after his resurrection—sending the Holy Spirit to indwell and empower his followers. Jesus confirmed this when he instructed his disciples to wait for the gift of the Spirit, explaining that they would receive power to be his witnesses (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>). This promise was fulfilled on the Day of Pentecost, when the disciples were filled with the Holy Spirit, evidenced by speaking in tongues, prophecy, and boldness to proclaim the gospel (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:1-4</span>).

<!-- p35 -->
This outpouring marked the beginning of the **church age** and showed that the power of the Spirit is now available to all believers. And this promise was not limited to the apostles, but extends to everyone who believes, as Peter declared: *“The promise is for you and your children and for all who are far off - for all whom the Lord our God will call”* (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="39" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:39</span>).

<!-- p36 -->
### The Spirit within versus the Spirit upon

<!-- p37 -->
When someone puts their faith in Jesus, the Holy Spirit grants new life within. This is the meaning of being born again (**regeneration**). At that moment of faith, the Holy Spirit dwells **within** the believer, making them spiritually alive and adopting them into God’s family (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:5-6</span>؛ <span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>). This is the new birth or being born again as Jesus described in John 3.

<!-- p38 -->
The biblical basis:

<!-- p39 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="TIT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Titus 3:5</span>* - “He saved us by the washing of regeneration and renewing of the Holy Spirit.”

<!-- p40 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>* - The Holy Spirit seals believers as a **guarantee** of their inheritance.

<!-- p41 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:9</span>* - “Anyone who does not have the Spirit of Christ does not belong to Him.”

<!-- p42 -->
This work of regeneration is for all believers at salvation and establishes the permanent indwelling of the Spirit. It is not dependent on any external act, such as water baptism, and it guarantees salvation by grace through faith.

<!-- p43 -->
In contrast, the baptism in the Holy Spirit is a separate and subsequent experience that equips believers with power for service and witness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>). While regeneration concerns new life and identity, Spirit baptism focuses on empowerment **upon** and the release of spiritual gifts. This baptism is often accompanied by signs and manifestations of the gifts of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p44 -->
Biblical basis:

<!-- p45 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p46 -->
> **<u>Acts 1:8</u>** (NKJV)
>
> But you will receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you; and you will be My witnesses in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the ends of the earth."
>

<!-- p47 -->
</span>

<!-- p48 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:15-17</span>* - The Samaritans had believed and were baptized, but they received the Holy Spirit later through prayer and the laying on of hands.

<!-- p49 -->
- *<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 19:6</span>* - Disciples in Ephesus, who were already believers, received the Holy Spirit when Paul laid his hands on them.

<!-- p50 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit does not replace regeneration but builds upon it, preparing the believer for spiritual service and boldness in ministry. Being baptized in the Holy Spirit is a free gift for the saved, and not a condition for salvation. A person is not required to display a <u>specific</u> gift of the gifts of the Holy Spirit to prove their 'baptism' in the Holy Spirit, but we should expect the baptism of the Holy Spirit today to appear as it did in the book of Acts, with accompanying signs and boldness! Some people err in thinking that if a person does not speak in tongues, they are not saved, or that they do not have the baptism of the Holy Spirit. Not everyone receives every gift or every manifestation of the gifts of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29-31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:29-31</span>).

<!-- p51 -->
## How to Receive the Baptism of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p52 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit is described in the Bible as a promised gift for believers and is received by faith. It is separate from salvation and its primary purpose is empowerment and witness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span>). The baptism of the Holy Spirit is an empowering gift for believers, promised by Jesus and fulfilled on the Day of Pentecost. It is received through repentance, faith, and seeking God. And although the laying on of hands is often used as a means, God is ultimately the one who baptizes with the Spirit.

<!-- p53 -->
Scripture presents speaking in tongues and prophecy as a common sign, but the purpose of this baptism is far greater—it provides boldness, power, and spiritual gifts for ministry. Every believer is encouraged to seek this experience as part of their spiritual journey, confident that God will equip them for His work.

<!-- p54 -->
The Bible lays out several key principles regarding how to receive this baptism:

<!-- p55 -->
### **1. The baptism of the Holy Spirit is a free gift from God**

<!-- p56 -->
The baptism of the Holy Spirit is not something believers can earn—it is a free gift God gives. God wants to give His children good gifts, and He is eager to give the Holy Spirit to those who ask in faith and with a desire for empowerment.

<!-- p57 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="11" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p58 -->
> **<u>Luke 11:13</u>** (NKJV)
>
> If you then, being evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will the heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to those who ask Him!"
>

<!-- p59 -->
</span>

<!-- p60 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="38-39" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p61 -->
> **<u>Acts 2:38-39</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Then Peter said to them, "Repent, and let every one of you be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of sins; and you will receive the gift of the Holy Spirit.
>
> For the promise is for you and for your children, and for all who are far off, as many as the Lord our God calls."

<!-- p63 -->
###  **2. Ask, Seek, and Receive by Faith**

<!-- p64 -->
The Scriptures emphasize that baptism in the Holy Spirit is received by asking for it in prayer and with an expectation full of faith. Faith, coupled with the readiness to ask and receive, is the key to experiencing the baptism of the Spirit. If you are not sure whether God wants to grant you the baptism of the Holy Spirit, ask Him for it, for He is a good Father! When He prompts you, you may feel a tangible presence of God in your body that you have not experienced before. It’s okay if you do not feel that! God is a great God, and He does not need to be confined to a single perfect formula. If you feel God drawing your heart, yield to Him!

<!-- p65 -->
- **<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:15-17</span>** - Peter and John prayed and laid hands on the believers, and they received the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p66 -->
- **<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="44-46" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 10:44-46</span>** - Cornelius and his household received the Holy Spirit while Peter was preaching-without hands being laid on them-which shows that the one who ultimately baptizes is God.

## Gifts of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p79 -->
When the Holy Spirit was poured out, and people were baptized in the Holy Spirit, manifestations of the Holy Spirit often accompanied it.

<!-- p80 -->
There are several lists of gifts in the New Testament:

<!-- p81 -->
- Motivational gifts given by God: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:6-8</span>

<!-- p82 -->
- Discussed in the chapter on the church.

<!-- p83 -->
- The fivefold ministry gifts given by Jesus: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:11-13</span>

<!-- p84 -->
- Discussed in the chapter on the church.

<!-- p85 -->
- Manifestation gifts given by the Holy Spirit: <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span>

<!-- p86 -->
- Discussed here.

<!-- p87 -->
Each set of these gifts is given for different purposes, and operates in a different way, but together they point to **diversity** and **unity** in the gifts within the **Body of Christ**. Each gift has a unique role in accomplishing God's purposes.

<!-- p88 -->
### Purpose &amp; Operations of Gifts

<!-- p89 -->
The primary aim of the gifts of the Holy Spirit is to build up the Body of Christ and bring glory to God:

<!-- p90 -->
- **Edification** - strengthening and encouraging believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>).

<!-- p91 -->
- **Unity** - fostering harmony and cooperation within the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:12-13</span>).

<!-- p92 -->
- **Evangelism** - demonstrating God's power as a testimony to unbelievers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="43" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:43</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 8:6-7</span>).

<!-- p93 -->
- **Service** - meeting the needs of others through acts of mercy and generosity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 12:6-8</span>).

<!-- p94 -->
These gifts are not for personal glory but for the common good (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:7</span>). They work best when practiced with love and humility, reflecting the character of Christ (1 Corinthians 13).

<!-- p95 -->
Paul emphasized that spiritual gifts must be exercised decently and in order (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:40</span>). This ensures that the use of gifts promotes unity, peace, and understanding, rather than confusion or division.

<!-- p96 -->
- Prophecies should be evaluated (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:29</span>).

<!-- p97 -->
- When bringing a message in tongues to the body, pray that it may be interpreted (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:27-28</span>).

<!-- p98 -->
- Gifts must always bring **edification** to the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:12</span>).

<!-- p99 -->
This order does not quench the Spirit but ensures that the gifts operate in ways that **honor God** and **benefit others**.

<!-- p100 -->
The exercise of spiritual gifts must always be rooted in love. Paul devotes an entire chapter (1 Corinthians 13) to explain that without love even the greatest spiritual gifts become meaningless. Love provides the motivation and framework for using the gifts in ways that build others up rather than tear them down.

<!-- p101 -->
Additionally, the gifts operate as the Spirit wills (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:11</span>). They cannot be manufactured or controlled by human effort- they are given and strengthened by the sovereignty of the Holy Spirit, often in moments of need or service. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 13:8</span> says that prophecy and tongues will cease in the end. Theologically, those who believe that the gifts of the Holy Spirit have ceased are known as ‘cessationists’. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="13" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 13:10</span> says: “When the governor [Jesus] comes, that wish which is in part will be the reason for it to pass away.” And since Jesus has not yet returned for His church, the gifts are still at work on earth.

<!-- p102 -->
It is essential to recognize that the gifts of the Spirit should not be separated from the fruit of the Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-23</span>). While the gifts display power, the fruit reveals character- love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control.

<!-- p103 -->
- **Gifts without fruit**: A believer may operate in the gifts yet lack maturity or character, leading to pride, misuse, or harm.

<!-- p104 -->
- **Fruit without gifts**: A believer may possess godly character but lack empowerment to minister effectively.

<!-- p105 -->
Both are necessary. The gifts equip believers for service. The fruit is a sign of their spiritual health (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 7:16</span>) and provides a ‘pleasant taste’ to those who partake of it.

<!-- p106 -->
### Explanation of the nine gifts of the manifestation of the Holy Spirit

<!-- p107 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:8-10</span> lists the nine gifts of the manifestation of the Holy Spirit:

<!-- p108 -->
1.  **Word of wisdom** - Supernatural insight into God’s purposes or His guidance for a particular situation. It applies divine wisdom to complex problems or decisions (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 6:10</span>).

<!-- p109 -->
2.  **Word of knowledge** - A supernatural revelation of information about a person, event, or circumstance that cannot be known naturally (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 4:17-18</span>).

<!-- p110 -->
3.  **Faith** - A supernatural ability to trust God for miracles, even in impossible situations. This gift inspires boldness and confidence (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 3:6-7</span>).

<!-- p111 -->
4.  **Gifts of healings** - The supernatural ability to heal physical, emotional, or spiritual illnesses and restore wholeness (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="6-8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 3:6-8</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 9:17-18</span>).

<!-- p112 -->
5.  **Working of miracles** - The ability to perform supernatural works that defy natural laws, such as multiplying food or raising the dead (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40-41" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 9:40-41</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 2:9-11</span>).

<!-- p113 -->
6.  **Prophecy** - Declaring God’s truth or will by inspiration of the Holy Spirit. It should be encouraging, edifying, and comforting (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>).

<!-- p114 -->
7.  **Distinguishing (discerning) between spirits** - The ability to discern whether a spiritual influence is from God, from the enemy, or from a human source (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 16:16-18</span>).

<!-- p115 -->
8.  **Different kinds of tongues** - Speaking in languages unknown to the speaker, often as an act of worship or prayer or as a sign to unbelievers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 2:4</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:22</span>).

<!-- p116 -->
9.  **Interpretation of tongues** - The supernatural ability to interpret a message spoken in tongues so that the church may be built up (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:27</span>).

<!-- p117 -->
Paul explains that all the gifts are valuable and should work together for the common good of the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:7</span>). However, he also encourages believers to desire the greater gifts (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:31</span>). The greater gifts are often interpreted as those that most directly build up the church, such as prophecy (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:1-5</span>).

<!-- p118 -->
- Prophecy is emphasized in particular because it strengthens, encourages, and comforts others (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:3</span>). Paul directs believers to eagerly desire this gift because it enhances clarity and understanding in the church.

<!-- p119 -->
- Gifts such as tongues are highly valued for personal edification and prayer (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:4</span>), but Paul teaches that when delivering a message in tongues to the church one should pray for interpretation to ensure that they build up the church (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27-28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 14:27-28</span>).

<!-- p120 -->
Although the Holy Spirit grants gifts, we must yield and submit to the Holy Spirit for Him to use them through us. And as with muscles and gifts, when we practice submitting to the Holy Spirit to use His gifts, we become more confident and reliable in using them.
