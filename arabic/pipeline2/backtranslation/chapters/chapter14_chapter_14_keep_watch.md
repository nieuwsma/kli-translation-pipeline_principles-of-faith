# Chapter 14 - Keep Watch

<!-- p1 -->
Since we have been given Christ’s spiritual authority on earth to establish His kingdom, we should work diligently and quickly, for Jesus will return!

<!-- p2 -->
The return of Christ is an important part of our faith. The study of Christ’s return and the end times is known as **Eschatology**. Many books have been written about eschatology, and *most of them do not agree with one another*! Specifically, they differ over interpretations (figurative, literal, symbolic), and the order and timing of events in the Scriptures. This reminds me of the saying of the great Pentecostal evangelist Reinhard Bonnke - "I will split hairs in heaven when I am done breaking chains on earth!"

<!-- p3 -->
As the author, I hesitate to dive deeply into this controversial subject since this book focuses primarily on the essentials of the faith. I will largely stick to a high-level overview of the events portrayed in the Bible and focus on what is most important - **the** **gospel message**!

<!-- p4 -->
## Universally agreed-upon points:

<!-- p5 -->
1.  Jesus will return

<!-- p6 -->
2.  The resurrection of the dead

<!-- p7 -->
3.  Judgment for all people

<!-- p8 -->
4.  Eternal destiny

<!-- p9 -->
5.  A defeated enemy

<!-- p10 -->
6.  A new heaven and a new earth

<!-- p11 -->
For each point we will review what is meant, look at the reference verses, consider why it matters, and what we should do in light of it.

<!-- p12 -->

### **3. Judgment for All People**

<!-- p37 -->
**What it is:** All people-believers and unbelievers-will face judgment before God. Believers appear before the **judgment seat of Christ** to receive rewards, while unbelievers face the **Great White Throne Judgment**.

<!-- p38 -->
#### The Great White Throne Judgment

<!-- p39 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

> **<u>Revelation 20:11-12</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Then I saw a great white throne and Him who sat on it, from whose face the earth and the heaven fled away. And there was found no place for them.
>
> And I saw the dead, small and great, standing before God, and books were opened. And another book was opened, which is the Book of Life. And the dead were judged according to their works, by the things which were written in the books.
>

<!-- p41 -->
</span>

<!-- p42 -->
As described in the chapter on sin, there will be a great and terrifying final judgment for all unbelievers. The punishment will be eternal separation from God! **This judgment is not for the believer.**

<!-- p43 -->
#### The Judgment Seat of Christ

<!-- p44 -->
As believers our names have been written in ***the Book of Life* of the Lamb** *(*see the book of *Revelation)**.** And so we will stand* *before **the Judgment Seat of Christ***. This judgment is not for condemnation, but for reward! And again**,** it is not a moment of **shame or fear** for believers, but a **celebration** of what God has accomplished through us. This reminds us that our lives matter-not only for today but for **eternity**.

These laurel crowns highlight God’s recognition of believers’ faithfulness and their service in His kingdom.

## Common points of disagreement

<!-- p89 -->
For completeness, if you're curious about common points of division in eschatology, here is a **very** brief overview of the points most often debated.

<!-- p90 -->
- **Timing of the rapture:** Before the tribulation, mid-tribulation, or after the tribulation? Views differ on **when** the rapture occurs in relation to the **tribulation**. Some denominations do not believe in the rapture.

<!-- p91 -->
- **Purpose of the tribulation:** Is it a literal judgment for Israel or a symbolic period of struggle?

<!-- p92 -->
- **The millennium:** Is it literal **(Premillennial)**, symbolic **(Amillennial)**, or spiritual **(Postmillennial)**?

<!-- p93 -->
- **The final rebellion of Satan:** Occurs after the millennium (literal event or figurative?).

<!-- p94 -->
It's wonderful to study eschatology, but let's remember, as D.L. Browning said, “Always be sure to keep the main thing the main thing!”

## **Final Thoughts**

<!-- p96 -->
The return of Christ is the climax of God's plan and the reason for which we live and strive with urgency and purpose. The parable of the ten virgins (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="25" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 25:1-13</span>) and the two men in the field (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="24" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40-42" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 24:40-42</span>) remind us to be watchful and faithful, always ready for His return. Whether through the rewards of crowns or the hope of the resurrection, our motivation is clear-Christ will return!

<!-- p97 -->
**May we be found ready, faithful, and expectant on that glorious day!**
