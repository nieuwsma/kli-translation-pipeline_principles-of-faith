# Chapter 7 - Jesus - The Foundation of Our Faith

<!-- p1 -->
Continuing our study of Jesus as the Messiah, in this chapter we continue to look at the life, person, and work of Jesus. We will see that Jesus is the mediator of a new and better covenant - and that he completely fills the roles of prophet, priest, and king. In this new covenant we believers have a right standing before God and move from a covenant based on works to a covenant based on trust (faith). Finally, we will read and study the Apostles’ Creed as a way to complete our study of Jesus.

<!-- p2 -->
## Mediator of the New Covenant

<!-- p3 -->
The concept of a mediator involves a person who stands between two parties, bridging the gap between them and facilitating a relationship or agreement. In the story of the Exodus, Moses was the first mediator of the covenant that God made with his people. Moses received the law, offered the sacrifice (priest), called God’s people to obedience (prophet), and judged among them (judge / king). In the rest of the Old Testament after Moses, the mediating role was carried out through the role of the prophet and the Levitical priesthood, then the judges and later the kings of the nation.

<!-- p4 -->
The roles of mediators in the Old Covenant:

<!-- p5 -->
1.  **Prophet:** The prophets were God’s messengers, sent to speak his heart to the people and call them back when they strayed. Moses, for example, served as a prophet mediating the Old Covenant by receiving the law from God and teaching it to Israel. The prophets also foretold the coming of the Messiah and the ultimate redemption of humanity. Notable examples of prophets include: Isaiah, Jeremiah, Elijah, Elisha, Samuel.

<!-- p6 -->
2.  **The Levitical priesthood:** The priests served as mediators by offering sacrifices for the atonement of sins and interceding for the people before God. The high priest had a pivotal role especially on the Day of Atonement, when he entered the Holy of Holies to sprinkle the blood on the mercy seat, a symbol of reconciliation with God. The priest’s ministry is to intercede for the people.

<!-- p7 -->
3.  **Judge/king:** Judges, and later kings, were entrusted with governing God’s people, applying his laws, judging among the people, delivering them from their enemies, and leading them in righteousness. The Book of Judges recounts the stories of these leaders—people like Deborah, Samson, and Gideon—whom God raised up to save and guide Israel. Israel later had a series of kings, many evil and a few good (see 1 Kings and 2 Kings, 1 Samuel and 2 Samuel, and 1<sup>st</sup> and 2<sup>nd</sup> Chronicles). Notable examples include Saul, David, and Solomon.

<!-- p8 -->
It is worth noting that all the mediators of the Old Covenant were fully human and not divine. And since they were human they could sin, and often fell into sin. Some mediators were sinners and led people away from God toward profane worship: false prophets, false and corrupt priests, and evil kings.

<!-- p9 -->
### Christ, the Mediator of the New Covenant

<!-- p10 -->
The death, resurrection, and ascension of Jesus bring the Old Covenant to completion and establish the New Covenant.

<!-- p11 -->
The New Covenant is a transformative relationship between God and his people, established through the life, death, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. Unlike the Old Covenant which focused on external laws, the New Covenant emphasizes inner transformation and grace. It was inaugurated by Jesus at the Last Supper, where he declared that his blood would establish this covenant, replacing the repeated animal sacrifices of the Old Covenant with his once-for-all atonement (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="22" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Luke 22:20</span>؛ <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:12</span>؛ <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:10</span>). Through this sacrifice, the New Covenant grants complete forgiveness and cleansing from sin, fulfilling the requirement for full and lasting reconciliation with God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 31:34</span>؛ <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:12</span>؛ <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:12-18</span>).

<!-- p12 -->
A central feature of the New Covenant is its inward nature, as God writes his law on the hearts of his people, establishing a heart-level relationship with him by the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="33" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 31:33</span>؛ <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:10</span>؛ <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 3:3</span>). This transformation enables believers to live out God’s will not merely by adhering to external commands, but by the empowering of the Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:1-4</span>). The New Covenant also grants direct access to God, removing the need for an earthly priesthood. Christ is the eternal High Priest, and believers are called a royal priesthood, able to draw near to God with confidence through Christ’s sacrifice (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24-25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 7:24-25</span>؛ <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:19-20</span>؛ <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:9</span>).

<!-- p13 -->
Unlike the Old Covenant which was exclusive to Israel, the New Covenant is universal, extending to all who believe in Christ regardless of ethnicity or background (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 3:16</span>؛ <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 3:28</span>). This covenant is entirely founded on grace, offering salvation and forgiveness freely through faith in Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 2:8-9</span>). It is not maintained by human effort, but by the empowering strength of the Holy Spirit, who transforms believers and enables them to live in obedience and reflect God’s character (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EZK" data-chapter="36" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ezekiel 36:26-27</span>؛ <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:9-16</span>؛ <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 5:22-23</span>). Moreover, the New Covenant is eternal, surpassing the temporary and conditional nature of the Old Covenant (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="32" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="40" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 32:40</span>؛ <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 8:13</span>). It represents the ultimate fulfillment of God’s redemptive plan, reconciling humanity to himself and enabling believers to live as his people under his authority forever.

<!-- p14 -->
Jesus is explicitly identified as the mediator of the new covenant in

<!-- p15 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p16 -->
> **<u>Hebrews 9:15</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And for this reason He is the Mediator of the new covenant, by means of death, for the redemption of the transgressions under the first covenant, that those who are called may receive the promise of the eternal inheritance.
>

<!-- p17 -->
</span>

<!-- p18 -->
Through his atoning death and resurrection, Christ inaugurates this New Covenant, replacing the system of sacrifices and external laws with a covenant written on the hearts of believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="31" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31-34" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 31:31-34</span>). He stands between God and humanity, not only interceding for his people, but also providing the means of reconciliation by his own blood (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:12</span>). Unlike the mediators of the Old Covenant, Jesus is sinless, eternal, and fully able to meet God’s requirements of justice and mercy, guaranteeing an enduring relationship between God and believers.

<!-- p19 -->
Jesus fulfills and completes these roles

<!-- p20 -->
1.  **Prophet:** Jesus is the supreme prophet, not merely a messenger of the word of God but the Word who became flesh (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:14</span>). He fully and completely reveals the character of God, His will, and His plan for salvation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 1:1-2</span>). His teachings, miracles, and prophetic declarations fulfill Old Testament prophecies and provide the foundation for the New Covenant.

<!-- p21 -->
2.  **Priest:** Jesus is the perfect High Priest, who offered himself as a sacrifice once for all for sin (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 7:27</span>). And unlike the Old Testament priests, who had to offer sacrifices repeatedly for their own sins and the sins of the people, Jesus, who is without sin, offered his blood to secure eternal redemption (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 9:14</span>).

<!-- p22 -->
3.  **King &amp; Judge:** Jesus is the eternal King, who reigns with righteousness and justice. And as the Son of David, he fulfills the promise of an eternal kingdom (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2SA" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Samuel 7:16</span>). His kingdom is not merely earthly but cosmic, as he rules over all creation and establishes peace through his victory over sin and death (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="9-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 2:9-11</span>). Jesus will return as King and Judge, and will judge ‘the living and the dead’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 10:42</span>).

<!-- p23 -->
In Christ, the roles of prophet, priest, and king come together and are fully completed. And as the mediator of the new covenant, he bridges the gap between a holy God and sinful humanity, enabling believers to approach God with confidence (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 4:16</span>) and securing the eternal inheritance promised in him.

## The Apostles’ Creed

<!-- p25 -->
The Apostles’ Creed is not found in the Scriptures; rather, it was created and revised based on the earliest teachings of the church. A creed is an official confession of beliefs; think of it as a pledge or oath accompanied by a statement of faith. Creeds are important because they are used to unite those who believe in a certain way and to distinguish them from those who do not. In the United States, it is common at civic occasions such as city council meetings or legislative assemblies for those present to recite the Pledge of Allegiance. The pledge is widely used in the United States, but it is not used in other countries because the citizens of those countries do not owe allegiance to the United States. And the pledge is not what makes a person «American», but it represents a basic belief important to those who are citizens.

<!-- p26 -->
The earliest defined forms of the creed were written in the fourth century AD. The creed has changed slightly over the centuries, and it has minor variations depending on denomination. Not every Christian denomination uses this creed, but it is widely accepted in some form across the whole of Christianity.

<!-- p27 -->
The Apostles’ Creed has been used as a tool for teaching and catechesis for centuries, and it expresses the deepest foundational truths of the Christian faith, that is, the core beliefs that must be confessed and held in order to be a Christian. Some denominations have a formal process of catechesis, which is the way a new believer joins that specific denomination and is instructed in its beliefs.

<!-- p28 -->
A person does not need a complete understanding of these beliefs to come to God and receive salvation; salvation is by faith, not by a doctrinal exam! Nevertheless, as we continue in that faith we should all come to understand these essential truths.

## A Brief Explanation of the Apostles’ Creed

<!-- p31 -->
The core truths in the Apostles’ Creed are many and very profound, and a full explanation of them would certainly require many volumes. Although this chapter speaks about Jesus, many of the topics extend to the other persons of the Godhead in theology. This will be a concise explanation of the creed, focusing only on the most central topics. Several of these topics have been studied in greater depth throughout the chapters of this book.

<!-- p32 -->
#### *I believe in God, the Father almighty, creator of heaven and earth.*

<!-- p33 -->
This establishes faith in God as the Father-Creator, almighty and sovereign. The title "Father" highlights God’s loving relationship with His creation, especially with His children by faith (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:12</span>). As Creator, He spoke and brought the universe into being, bringing order out of chaos (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 1:1-3</span>). His absolute power is displayed in the vastness of creation, while His fatherly care appears in His ongoing provision and governance. This statement grounds God as the source and owner of all life, worthy of worship and trust.

<!-- p34 -->
#### *I believe in Jesus Christ, his only Son, our Lord.*

<!-- p35 -->
Jesus Christ is the unique Son of God, eternally begotten, not made, and shares the divine nature (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:1-2</span>, 14). Jesus is ‘the Word’ from the beginning, and he became flesh and dwelt among us. As "the Christ," he is the Anointed One who fulfills the Old Testament promises of a Savior. And the title "Lord" acknowledges his authority over all creation and his rightful status as ruler and king (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PHP" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Philippians 2:10-11</span>). He is not only Lord, but the Lord who saves (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="31" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 16:31</span>).

<!-- p36 -->
Jesus is not merely sent from God; rather, he is part of God. Jesus abides in the Father and the Father abides in Jesus. The Gospel of John repeatedly affirms that Jesus and the Father are one (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="30" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:30</span>), that Jesus did nothing of himself (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 5:19</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="49-50" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 12:49-50</span>), that everything he did was to glorify his Father (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="17" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 17:4</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:13</span>), and that this was by his Father’s authority and power (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:10</span>; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 8:28-29</span>).

<!-- p37 -->
#### *Conceived by the power of the Holy Spirit and born of the virgin Mary.*

<!-- p38 -->
This affirms the miraculous conception of Jesus by the Holy Spirit, emphasizing his divine origin. By being born of the virgin Mary, Jesus fulfills the prophecy of <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 7:14</span>, which foretold the virgin birth of the Messiah. This event points to the incarnation, where the eternal Word of God took on human flesh (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 1:14</span>). Being fully God and fully man, Jesus bridges the gap between God and humanity, enabling him to be the perfect mediator and Savior.

<!-- p39 -->
Jesus was conceived from the seed of the Holy Spirit and did not inherit a sinful nature as we who are born of human seed do. Jesus was tempted and tested in every way, yet he was perfect and without sin. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 4:15</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 5:21</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:22</span>.

<!-- p40 -->
#### *Suffered under Pontius Pilate، was crucified، died، and was buried.*

<!-- p41 -->
The sufferings of Jesus and his death were real historical events that took place during the tenure of the Roman governor Pontius Pilate، grounding the Christian faith in verifiable history. His crucifixion was the fulfillment of Old Testament prophecies, such as <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ISA" data-chapter="53" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Isaiah 53:5</span>, which speaks of the suffering servant who bears the sins of many. Jesus’ death was a sacrifice، as he took the penalty of sin on behalf of humanity (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="24" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 2:24</span>). And his burial confirms the reality of his death and prepares for the significance of the resurrection.

<!-- p42 -->
#### *He descended to the dead.*

<!-- p43 -->
After his death، Jesus descended to the realm of the dead، which is often referred to in Scripture as *Hades* or *Sheol*. This act shows his full solidarity with humanity، even in death، and points to his victory over sin، death، and the devil (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 3:18-20</span>). By his descent he proclaimed his triumph and the completion of his redemptive work.

<!-- p44 -->
#### *On the third day he rose again.*

<!-- p45 -->
The resurrection of Jesus is the cornerstone of our Christian faith، as it confirms his divinity and the truth of his teachings. By his resurrection from the dead he defeated sin and death، granting believers the assurance of eternal life (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17-22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 15:17-22</span>). The resurrection also fulfills what is written in the Scriptures، including Jesus’ own predictions (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="21" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 16:21</span>). This miraculous event turned the disciples’ despair into bold faith، forming the foundation of the early church’s witness.

<!-- p46 -->
#### *He ascended into heaven، and is seated at the right hand of the Father.*

<!-- p47 -->
Jesus’ ascension points to his return to the Father’s presence in glory. Seated at the right hand of the Father (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:20</span>)، he takes up the role of our eternal High Priest، interceding for believers (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="25" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 7:25</span>). His royal position also indicates his exaltation as King، the ruler over all creation and the director of the church’s mission (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:22</span>) so that it may do greater works (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:12</span>) and fulfill the Great Commission he entrusted to us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 28:18-20</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="MRK" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Mark 16:15-18</span>). The ascension gives believers the assurance of his abiding presence and power through the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p48 -->
#### *He will come again to judge the living and the dead.*

<!-- p49 -->
This points to the future return of Christ when he brings final justice and restoration. Jesus will judge all people، rewarding the faithful and bringing justice upon the unrepentant (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 4:1</span>). His return will accomplish God’s ultimate purpose for creation، ushering in his eternal kingdom of righteousness and peace. This hope encourages believers to live with faithfulness and expectation، knowing that their labor in the Lord is not in vain (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="58" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 15:58</span>).

<!-- p50 -->
#### *I believe in the Holy Spirit.*

<!-- p51 -->
The Holy Spirit، the third person of the Trinity، is the active presence and power of God in the world today. He convicts of sin، renews hearts، and empowers believers to live a holy life (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:8-13</span>). The Spirit unites believers as one body، that is، the church، and equips them with spiritual gifts for service (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 12:4-11</span>). He is the Comforter، Advocate، and Guide، indwelling believers as a seal of their eternal inheritance (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:13-14</span>).

<!-- p52 -->
#### *The holy catholic church، the communion of saints.*

<!-- p53 -->
"The holy catholic church" refers to the universal body of Christ، which includes all believers across time and space. The church is dedicated to God’s purposes، called to reflect his holiness and share the gospel. "The communion of saints" affirms the unity of all Christians، living and dead، as members of the body of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-6" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 4:4-6</span>). This fellowship transcends earthly divisions، displaying the shared hope and mission of believers.

<!-- p54 -->
Jesus is the head of the church. The church is called the Bride of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="25-27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 5:25-27</span>) and also the Body of Christ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="COL" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Colossians 1:18</span>).

<!-- p55 -->
#### *The forgiveness of sins.*

<!-- p56 -->
This highlights the heart of the gospel: through Jesus’ sacrificial death، God offers full forgiveness of sins to all who repent and believe (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Ephesians 1:7</span>). This forgiveness restores the broken relationship between humanity and God، removing guilt and shame. It also calls believers to extend forgiveness to others، reflecting the grace they have received (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14-15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 6:14-15</span>).

<!-- p57 -->
#### *The resurrection of the body.*

<!-- p58 -->
Like Jesus، we will experience a bodily resurrection at the end of the age. This resurrection will transform perishable، corruptible bodies into glorified، imperishable bodies، fit for eternal life (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="42-44" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 15:42-44</span>). It also affirms the Christian hope of restoration، not only for individuals but for all creation، as God makes all things new (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="REV" data-chapter="21" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Revelation 21:4-5</span>).
