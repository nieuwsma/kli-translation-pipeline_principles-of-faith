# Chapter 8 - The Holy Spirit—the Comforter, the Guide, and the Power

<!-- p1 -->
The Holy Spirit is not a new spirit but is from God and has been with God from the beginning. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GEN" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Genesis 1:2</span> the Spirit of God was hovering over the waters during creation. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="NEH" data-chapter="9" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Nehemiah 9:20</span>, the Scriptures say that it was the Spirit who led the people in the wilderness. By this same Holy Spirit God anointed kings, judges, and prophets in the Old Testament. In <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JOL" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="28-29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Joel 2:28-29</span> it is promised that the Spirit will be poured out on all people. And by this same Holy Spirit God raised Jesus from the dead, and He now dwells in us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:11</span>). There is only one Holy Spirit, and He has always been the same. The way He works with people may seem different at different times, but His heart has never changed.

<!-- p2 -->
Jesus gave His disciples, and all of us, the powerful promise of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p3 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p4 -->
> **<u>John 14:16-18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And I will ask the Father, and He will give you another Comforter, to remain with you forever—
>
> the Spirit of truth, whom the world cannot receive, because it neither sees Him nor knows Him; but you know Him, for He dwells with you and will be in you.
>
> I will not leave you orphans; I am coming to you.
>

<!-- p5 -->
</span>

<!-- p6 -->
While Jesus walked the earth in His physical body, He was limited as we are, for His body needed the same things our bodies need: rest, water, food, sleep, etc. These limitations affected not only Jesus but also His followers, since He could be in only one place at a time. Often, as Jesus traveled, He was pressed by a large crowd trying to call to Him and touch Him. And Jesus often needed to spend time with His heavenly Father to be refreshed and to pray.

<!-- p7 -->
In order for the Father’s work to multiply, Jesus needed to raise up disciples to carry that work. Jesus knew that a critical aspect of His ministry on earth depended on His connection with and abiding in His Father. In fact, Jesus said that He could do nothing unless the Father did it through Him and showed Him to do it (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 5:19</span>). In His wisdom God sent His Holy Spirit to dwell in us, to lead and guide us, enabling us to do the work of the Father.

<!-- p8 -->
## When was the Holy Spirit sent to all believers?

<!-- p9 -->
Jesus said:

<!-- p10 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p11 -->
> **<u>John 16:7</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Nevertheless, I tell you the truth. It is for your good that I go away; for if I do not go away, the Comforter will not come to you; but if I go, I will send Him to you.
>

<!-- p12 -->
</span>

<!-- p13 -->
After Jesus was crucified, His death made it possible for the Spirit of God to dwell within us. Until that moment, the tangible Spirit of God was largely veiled behind a thick curtain in the temple. From time to time, God would choose certain individuals and anoint them (consecrating and appointing them to a unique service), and they would receive the Holy Spirit coming upon them. God’s anointing, and the Spirit coming upon them, was specific, temporary, and conditional. David prayed after his sin with Bathsheba, '*Do not take Your Holy Spirit from me!*' (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="PSA" data-chapter="51" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Psalm 51:11</span>), because David had seen the Spirit depart from King Saul for his acts of disobedience.

<!-- p14 -->
We (humanity) could not receive the Holy Spirit within us because of our sinful nature, but when Jesus dealt with the issue of sin through His sacrifice, by faith there became a way for us to become children and heirs of God (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:17</span>).

<!-- p15 -->
In John 20, after His resurrection, Jesus appears to His disciples in the middle of a locked room and breathes on them, saying, '*Receive the Holy Spirit*' (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="20" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 20:22</span>). The theology here is somewhat debated regarding exactly what happened at that moment, but most Pentecostal interpretations see this as the disciples receiving the Spirit of God within them, indicating the new birth, that is, being born again. This was not, however, when they received the Spirit of God upon them, which empowered them with boldness for ministry. Rather, this was when they received the Holy Spirit within them, which allowed them to abide and have fellowship with God. This will be discussed in greater depth later in this chapter as well as in the chapters on sanctification and baptism.

<!-- p16 -->
Later, at the conclusion of the 40-day period between the resurrection and the ascension (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:3</span>), Jesus gives the promise of the Holy Spirit:

<!-- p17 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="4-9" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p18 -->
> **<u>Acts 1:4-9</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And being assembled together with them, He commanded them not to depart from Jerusalem, but to wait for the Promise of the Father, "which," He said, "you have heard from Me;
>
> for John truly baptized with water, but you shall be baptized with the Holy Spirit not many days from now."
>
> Therefore, when they had come together, they asked Him, saying, "Lord, will You at this time restore the kingdom to Israel?"
>
> And He said to them, "It is not for you to know the times or seasons which the Father has put in His own authority."
>
> "But you shall receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you; and you shall be witnesses to Me in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the ends of the earth."
>
> Now when He had spoken these things, while they watched, He was taken up, and a cloud received Him out of their sight.
>

<!-- p19 -->
</span>

<!-- p20 -->
The disciples had to wait for the promise of the outpoured Spirit, and this was to enable them to be witnesses to the ends of the earth. The Holy Spirit is an integral part of God’s plan for humanity, and He is essential for us as believers to fulfill our mission, and for the world.

<!-- p21 -->
## Getting to Know the Holy Spirit

<!-- p22 -->
The Holy Spirit is first and foremost from God and of God. The Scriptures refer to the Spirit as the Spirit of God, the Spirit of the Lord, or the Spirit of Christ; although they highlight different aspects, they are all one and the same. And the Holy Spirit, being fully God, is also eternal and holy (blameless, innocent, pure, undefiled). Some translations refer to the Holy Spirit as the Holy Ghost.

<!-- p23 -->
The Greek word for spirit is **Pneuma** (**NYOO-mah** - **G4151**) and means breath or wind. The Hebrew word for spirit means the same thing. Breath is significant because we need it constantly to live. It was the breath of God that He breathed into Adam that made him alive, and similarly it was the breath of Jesus that He exhaled finally before He died on the cross. Without breath there is no life. And breath, like wind, can be felt and experienced, but it is not as easily observed as the other “elements”: water, fire, earth.

<!-- p24 -->
There are several other essential attributes to describe the Holy Spirit. Each of these attributes is used with “Spirit of,” which means these characteristics are fundamental in defining the Spirit. For example, “Spirit of truth” or “Spirit of life.”

<!-- p25 -->
Spirit of:

<!-- p26 -->
- **truth** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:17</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span> points to the Spirit’s role in guiding believers into all truth, revealing God’s will and the reality of Christ.

<!-- p27 -->
- **life** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="2" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:2</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 6:8</span> highlights the Spirit’s role in granting spiritual and eternal life to believers, freeing them from the law of sin and death.

<!-- p28 -->
- **grace** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="HEB" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="29" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Hebrews 10:29</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ZEC" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="10" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Zechariah 12:10</span> reflects the Spirit’s work in imparting God’s favor, forgiveness, and unmerited grace to humanity.

<!-- p29 -->
- **glory** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1PE" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Peter 4:14</span> indicates the Spirit’s role in declaring and manifesting the glory of God, especially in times of suffering and persecution.

<!-- p30 -->
- **adoption** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="15" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:15</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="GAL" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Galatians 4:5</span> points to the Spirit’s work in making believers children of God, granting them the status of sons and daughters.

<!-- p31 -->
- **holiness** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="4" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 1:4</span> refers to the Spirit’s work in sanctification, that is, setting believers apart for God’s purposes.

<!-- p32 -->
- **power** - <span class="x-scripture" data-book="2TI" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="7" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Timothy 1:7</span>, <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 1:8</span> describes the Spirit’s empowering of believers with boldness, service, and the ability to live out their faith effectively.

<!-- p33 -->
In all these attributes the Holy Spirit is also rightly known as «the Helper» or «the Comforter». Jesus refers to the Holy Spirit as the coming Helper, and uses the Greek word ***Paraklētos** (*pah-RAH-klay-tos - **G3875**), which means advocate, helper, one called alongside.

<!-- p34 -->
The advocate speaks on behalf of another, like a defense attorney. He intercedes and helps the one entrusted to him.

<!-- p35 -->
The helper helps. He assists in enabling the work, but his task is primarily to aid the work. The helper is not solely responsible for performing the work, but comes alongside us to help accomplish it.

<!-- p36 -->
The Holy Spirit has several distinct purposes and roles in His ministry. His work is inward (in the hearts and lives of believers), upward (in serving and acknowledging God), and outward (toward the field of our mission, that is, the world).

<!-- p37 -->
## The Roles of the Holy Spirit-Inward, Upward, Outward

<!-- p38 -->
### Inward-the Holy Spirit unites us with Jesus

<!-- p39 -->
Before Jesus performed any miracle or began His public ministry, He was baptized by John the Baptist and the Spirit of God descended upon Him, and a voice from heaven declared, ‘This is My beloved Son in whom I am well pleased’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="3" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Matthew 3:17</span>).

<!-- p40 -->
When we receive Jesus by faith, we receive salvation and the seal of the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p41 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="EPH" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="13-14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p42 -->
> **<u>Ephesians 1:13-14</u>** (NKJV)
>
> In Him you also, when you heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation; in whom also, having believed, you were sealed with the Holy Spirit of promise,
>
> who is the guarantee of our inheritance until the redemption of the purchased possession, to the praise of His glory.
>

<!-- p43 -->
</span>

<!-- p44 -->
This seal is not figurative but literal. It serves as a sign of our redemption and is a down payment of the full inheritance we receive as children of God. Romans 8 describes that through the new birth we receive the Spirit of God Himself, who raised Christ from the dead, in our mortal bodies, and we become the literal temple of the Holy Spirit (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2CO" data-chapter="6" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Corinthians 6:16</span>).

<!-- p45 -->
This receiving of the Spirit of God changes us and causes rivers of living water to flow from us (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="7" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="38" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 7:38</span>). This renewal is immediate and complete, yet it continues throughout our lives. The river of the Holy Spirit should not cease flowing in our lives!

<!-- p46 -->
Through salvation and the indwelling of the Holy Spirit we are able to abide in Christ and He in us. The Spirit of God is no longer confined outside, but can dwell deeply with us. We can walk in fellowship and harmony with God from now into eternal life.

<!-- p47 -->
There is much to be said about the inward work of the Holy Spirit and His role in our sanctification, but this will be discussed in the chapter on sanctification.

<!-- p48 -->
### Upward - The Holy Spirit testifies about Jesus

<!-- p49 -->
The Holy Spirit is from God and is for God. And just as Jesus said He does nothing except what the Father shows Him (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="5" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="19" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 5:19</span>), so the Holy Spirit does nothing unless directed by the Father. And because the Holy Spirit is the Helper, He does not act on His own, but works in complete harmony with the Father.

<!-- p50 -->
The Spirit bears witness to Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="15" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 15:26</span>), glorifies Jesus, and declares and makes known what is ours from Jesus (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="14" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:14</span>).

<!-- p51 -->
The Holy Spirit will bear witness to Jesus, glorify Him, teach, remind us of Jesus’ teachings (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="26" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:26</span>), and guide us into all the truth (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 16:13</span>). Jesus told His disciples that He had many things to teach them, but they were not yet able to bear them, and that the Holy Spirit would guide them into all the truth. This is another fine point in theology. These teachings that Jesus needed His disciples to know were conveyed in several ways. Jesus taught them Himself before His crucifixion, as well as after the resurrection and before His ascension.

<!-- p52 -->
Jesus also promised that the Spirit of truth, who was poured out on them on the Day of Pentecost, would lead them into all the truth through divine revelation. Is there still divine revelation today? Absolutely yes! The gifts of the Holy Spirit, including prophecy and words of knowledge and wisdom, have not ceased! However, no prophecy nor word from any spirit is for private interpretation (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="2PE" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">2 Peter 1:20</span>). This is critical. Some people, mistakenly, claim that they are listening to the Holy Spirit, but their actions and words do not bear witness to Jesus. <span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 John 4:3</span> warns against any spirit that does not bear witness to Jesus, saying that such a spirit is of the antichrist. And this bears repeating: If any spirit deviates from the teaching of Christ, it is not the Holy Spirit, but an unclean spirit!

<!-- p53 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1JN" data-chapter="4" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="1" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p54 -->
> **<u>1 John 4:1</u>** (NKJV)
>
> Beloved, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits, whether they are of God; because many false prophets have gone out into the world.
>

<!-- p55 -->
</span>

<!-- p56 -->
Throughout the Scriptures, we see examples of false claims, often from false prophets or well-intentioned individuals who spoke contrary to God’s will. For example, Jeremiah warned of prophets who spoke the visions of their hearts, proclaiming peace while destruction was looming (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="23" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-17" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 23:16-17</span>). Similarly, in <span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="16-18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Acts 16:16-18</span>, the words of a slave girl about Paul and Silas were accurate, but they were inspired by a demonic spirit, not by the Holy Spirit. These examples remind us that even words that seem true may come from sources that do not please God.

<!-- p57 -->
Every claim of the sort "God told me!" or a "new" teaching should be measured by the biblical tests of truth. For proper discernment, we must ask:

<!-- p58 -->
- Does this align with the Bible?

<!-- p59 -->
- Does this glorify Jesus Christ?

<!-- p60 -->
- Does this produce divine fruit?

<!-- p61 -->
By applying these tests, we can discern the true guidance of the Holy Spirit from errors or deception.

<!-- p62 -->
God is accustomed to providing us with ample &amp; abundant wisdom and guidance (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JAS" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">James 1:5</span> &amp; <span class="x-scripture" data-book="JER" data-chapter="33" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="3" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Jeremiah 33:3</span>). God’s divine wisdom will not violate Scripture, but it will go beyond the scope of Scripture. His *entolē* *(*the commandments*)* are immediate orders that must be obeyed, and they are *rhema* words (as discussed in the chapter on faith).

<!-- p63 -->
This wisdom, understanding, and guidance will often not be words that match the Bible verbatim. For example, the Bible gives us great general direction about what to look for in a life partner, but it does not give specific prescriptions for a particular person. God promises to direct our path and to guide us into all truth, and we can be comforted knowing that this wisdom and guidance from the Holy Spirit will not violate any teaching of Scripture.

<!-- p64 -->
### Externally-the Holy Spirit empowers the mission

<!-- p65 -->
Jesus gave us His message, namely the Great Commission:

<!-- p66 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="28" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="18-20" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p67 -->
> **<u>Matthew 28:18-20</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And Jesus came and spoke to them, saying, "All authority has been given to Me in heaven and on earth.
>
> Go therefore and make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit,
>
> teaching them to observe all things that I have commanded you; and behold, I am with you always, to the end of the age." Amen.
>

<!-- p68 -->
</span>

<!-- p69 -->
The Holy Spirit has a vital role in the mission, as He convicts the world of sin, righteousness, and judgment:

<!-- p70 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="8-11" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p71 -->
> **<u>John 16:8-11</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And when He has come, He will convict the world of sin, and of righteousness, and of judgment:
>
> of sin, because they do not believe in Me;
>
> of righteousness, because I go to My Father and you see Me no more;
>
> of judgment, because the ruler of this world is judged.
>

<!-- p72 -->
</span>

<!-- p73 -->
And digging deeper, the Spirit brings conviction of these three truths:

<!-- p74 -->
1.  **Conviction of sin**: exposing the reality of humanity’s rebellion against God, and drawing people’s attention to the corruption of their own lives.

<!-- p75 -->
2.  **Conviction of righteousness**: highlighting Jesus’ perfect fulfillment of the Father’s will, and that Jesus’ sacrifice has provided the once-for-all atonement necessary for the forgiveness of sins.

<!-- p76 -->
3.  **Conviction of judgment**: affirming the defeat of Satan, the certainty of God’s justice, and that all will stand to be judged for the work of their lives and their deeds.

<!-- p77 -->
The Holy Spirit also empowers the mission. The Holy Spirit gives believers power to be bold witnesses in the face of persecution to share the gospel. Boldness does not mean being loud; rather, it means having courage despite fear. Power is determination, ability, and capability. The Holy Spirit gives the power and ability to be a witness to the gospel despite threats and fear of punishment.

<!-- p78 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ACT" data-chapter="1" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="8" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p79 -->
> **<u>Acts 1:8</u>** (NKJV)
>
> "But you shall receive power when the Holy Spirit has come upon you; and you shall be witnesses to Me in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the end of the earth."

<!-- p80 -->
</span>

<!-- p81 -->
The entire book of Acts is full of stories and narratives that follow this general pattern:

<!-- p82 -->
1.  **Prayer**: Believers ask for boldness and God’s intervention.

<!-- p83 -->
2.  **Empowerment**: The Holy Spirit fills them with courage, power, and guidance.

<!-- p84 -->
3.  **Proclamation**: They proclaim the gospel with boldness, often accompanied by signs, wonders, and miracles.

<!-- p85 -->
4.  **Opposition**: Resistance appears, leading to persecution or challenges. Sometimes believers are martyred, and sometimes they are imprisoned or beaten.

<!-- p86 -->
5.  **Renewal**: The church returns to prayer, seeking fresh empowerment, and the cycle continues.

<!-- p87 -->
The New Testament church grew greatly in the face of determined opposition. Around the world the church is subjected to violence at the hands of persecutors and evil rulers determined to suppress the knowledge of the truth of Jesus.

<!-- p88 -->
God knew that this opposition would happen, and Jesus prophesied about it:

<!-- p89 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="LUK" data-chapter="12" data-lang="en" data-type="quote" data-verses="11-12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p90 -->
> **<u>Luke 12:11-12</u>** (NKJV)
>
> "Now when they bring you to the synagogues and magistrates and authorities, do not worry about how or what you should answer, or what you should say.
>
> For the Holy Spirit will teach you in that very hour what you ought to say."
>

<!-- p91 -->
</span>

<!-- p92 -->
Do not be afraid! Jesus promised us that:

<!-- p93 -->
<span class="x-scripture" data-book="MAT" data-chapter="16" data-lang="en" data-type="fragment" data-verses="18" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">

<!-- p94 -->
> **<u>Matthew 16:18</u>** (NKJV)
>
> And I also say to you: You are Peter, and on this rock I will build My church, and the gates of Hades shall not prevail against it.
>

<!-- p95 -->
</span>

<!-- p96 -->
This is an amazing and comforting promise. Gates are fixed defensive fortifications. When Jesus says that the *gates of hell will not prevail*, He paints the picture of a conquering army breaking down the gates and taking the city!

<!-- p97 -->
The Holy Spirit brings a supernatural boldness to declare the testimony of the gospel in the face of persecution. The result of this empowerment can be described, but it is best experienced. It is a supernatural (greater-than-human) boldness that drives believers to share. And this boldness in preaching is often accompanied by supernatural works as a demonstration of the Spirit and of power (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1CO" data-chapter="2" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="1-5" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Corinthians 2:1-5</span>). People are healed physically and emotionally, the dead are raised, those tormented by evil spirits are set free, and the hard-hearted repent.

<!-- p98 -->
Jesus said that we will do these works and greater! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="14" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="12" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 14:12</span>). This collective work is supernatural and divine, and it is urgently needed in a world that is tearing itself apart, miserable, full of pain, waiting for the mature sons of God to be clearly revealed! (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="ROM" data-chapter="8" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="22-23" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">Romans 8:22-23</span>)

<!-- p99 -->
The instrument Jesus chose to bring change on the earth is His church, empowered, filled with boldness, and guided by the Holy Spirit.

## Building a Relationship with the Holy Spirit

<!-- p101 -->
Every relationship needs time and experience to deepen. The Holy Spirit is our advocate, our comforter, our helper, and our guide. He strengthens us and sanctifies us.

<!-- p102 -->
Start thinking of the Holy Spirit as you would think of a person. In our relationship, both of us can work to build it or do things that weaken our relationship. He can be ignored, quenched, and blasphemed, or He can be listened to, honored, and obeyed.

<!-- p103 -->
Do not ignore the voice of the Holy Spirit. He is always speaking to us and to the Church. If you do not yet know how to discern the voice of the Holy Spirit, this takes time, willingness, and effort (like all healthy and beneficial relationships). But don’t worry—you already have within you all the parts you need to begin cultivating a personal relationship with the Holy Spirit.

<!-- p104 -->
The fastest and most reliable ways to build an effective relationship with the Holy Spirit are to

<!-- p105 -->
1.  Call on Him **individually** in prayer, pause, and wait for Him to speak.

<!-- p106 -->
2.  Call on Him as part of a **group** of people who know Him (the Church), pause, and wait for Him to speak.

<!-- p107 -->
I use the term ‘calling’ to mean it literally—with your mouth, or in whatever way you speak (with your hands if you cannot speak audibly with your mouth). Why speak? For several reasons:

<!-- p108 -->
1.  We are speaking spirits, created in the image of God. God speaks, and so do we.

<!-- p109 -->
2.  We use speech as a primary way to communicate. You can also write what you want to say (journaling) and speak it out loud.

<!-- p110 -->
3.  Speaking is an intentional act and signals to the one you are addressing your desire to engage them in a conversation.

<!-- p111 -->
#### What should you say?

<!-- p112 -->
If you have never spoken to God before, try introducing yourself. It will feel a little strange, to be sure, but that discomfort is acceptable and temporary. Besides introducing yourself, tell God that you want to talk to Him and that you want Him to answer you.

<!-- p113 -->
Consider asking Him a question. It does not have to be a deep theological question; it can start simply. Could You show me Your love for me? Why do I have a certain feeling? What would You like to tell me?

<!-- p114 -->
#### Then what?

<!-- p115 -->
Well, in every effective relationship you speak a little, then you stop and listen. God will speak.

<!-- p116 -->
God speaks in many ways, but He wants to do so in a personal, inward way. It will most likely not be an audible voice (as if your best friend shouted your name out loud) but an inner voice that speaks to your heart and mind. Elijah refers to this as the ‘still small voice’ (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="1KI" data-chapter="19" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="11-13" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">1 Kings 19:11-13</span>).

<!-- p117 -->
The most common ways I have personally seen God speak to people—and I have been blessed to help dozens of people learn how to speak to God directly—are a mixture of the following:

<!-- p118 -->
- Insight - the person speaking to God receives an immediate understanding that helps them.

<!-- p119 -->
- Image - also known in the Scriptures as a vision or a dream (a vision when you are awake, and a dream when you are asleep), God will give you an image in your mind. It may be a memory, or a place you have been or know. Explore the image—what do you see? What do you feel as a result? What is happening?

<!-- p120 -->
- Inner voice - like an audible voice, but a voice that only you hear, and it speaks to your heart and mind.

<!-- p121 -->
#### What will God say?

<!-- p122 -->
I’m not sure! But that’s the fun of it. I can say that it is not a spirit of fear, but a spirit of perfect love, and that it will never violate the teaching of the Bible. For example, God will never tell you to enter into a relationship outside of marriage.

<!-- p123 -->
And now what?

<!-- p124 -->
Keep the dialogue going! It really is that simple.

<!-- p125 -->

<!-- p126 -->
- *Authentic Christianity* by Larry Low ([<u>www.larrylow.com</u>](http://www.larrylow.com))

<!-- p127 -->
- *Good Morning, Holy Spirit* by Benny Hinn ([<u>www.bennyhinn.org</u>](http://www.bennyhinn.org))

<!-- p128 -->
### Why build a relationship?

<!-- p129 -->
The Holy Spirit is the Helper. And the Helper helps us! Work goes much more smoothly if we have a common language and a relationship with Him. Can you imagine trying to work with someone at your job without having a relationship between you, not speaking the same language, and being unable to communicate?!

<!-- p130 -->
We worship a God who wants to communicate with us. Jesus said that His sheep know His voice (<span class="x-scripture" data-book="JHN" data-chapter="10" data-lang="en" data-type="ref" data-verses="27" data-version="NKJV" dir="ltr" style="text-align: left;">John 10:27</span>), and all the natural abilities we need have already been deposited in us, but we may lack experience-based mastery.

<!-- p131 -->
From personal experience, a personal and effective relationship with the Holy Spirit is one of the best parts of being a Christian. His presence brings us peace, comfort, and guidance, but only if you know how to seek Him, listen to Him, and obey Him.

<!-- p132 -->
Develop sensitivity to the Holy Spirit. As many couples do over time, you can communicate with your spouse in a unique way and sometimes with just a glance. That one look tells you everything you need to know about what just happened and what will happen. Build that kind of sensitivity and intimacy with the Holy Spirit. Involve Him in your whole life; this is a wonderful aspect of your relationship with Him that will give your faith journey great depth and richness.

<!-- p133 -->
The Holy Spirit has come to lead and guide us, to enable us to be the people of God, and to see the peoples of the earth radically transformed, rescued from the kingdom of darkness into the kingdom of light. Getting to know Him and building trust with Him are essential for daily life and for accomplishing the work that Jesus has prepared for us to do.

<!-- p134 -->
Our discussion of the work of the Holy Spirit does not end in this chapter. His work is discussed at length across the remaining chapters of this book, especially the chapters on sanctification and baptism.
